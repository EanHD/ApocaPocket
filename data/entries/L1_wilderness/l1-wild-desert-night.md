# Desert Night Survival

## Temperature Management
- Pack changes 1-2 hours before sunset
- Add all layers before you feel cold
- Ground sucks heat: insulate from below
- Rock radiates stored heat after dark
- Wind increases cold: find windbreak

## Shelter for Cold
- South-facing rock wall: warm zone
- Boulders retain solar heat well
- Debris and dry sand insulate
- Dig into sand: stable temperature
- Brush pile: effective dead air layer

## Night Navigation
- Stars: most reliable in desert sky
- Polaris: north, always
- Moon: rises east, sets west
- Shadows: cast sharp in moon/starlight
- Move on ridgelines: better visibility

## Nocturnal Hazards
- Scorpions: most active at night
- Sidewinder rattlesnakes: nocturnal
- Check ground with flashlight: always
- Shake boots: every time before wearing
- Headlamp keeps hands free to check

## Conserving Water at
- Reduce exertion to minimum at night
- Cool temp means less sweat loss
- Travel long distances at night
- Eat small amounts: conserve water
- Sleep with mouth covered: moisture out

## Dawn Preparation
- Begin travel early before sun high
- Collect dew: rocks and plants
- Assess water and shade plan for day
- Mark travel objective before sun rises
- Shade identified before 10am arrival
