# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Hurricane storm surge: 20+ ft possible
- Extreme humidity: heat illness fast
- Alligators in any standing water
- Venomous snakes in marsh areas
- Saltwater flooding contaminates wells
- Mosquito-borne illness after flooding

## Water Sources
- Freshwater difficult post-hurricane
- Rainfall catchment: high in region
- Look for elevated freshwater springs
- Avoid bayou water: highly contaminated
- Stored water is the first priority

## Food Sources
- Blue crabs in brackish water
- Freshwater catfish in rivers
- Muscadine grapes: late summer
- Fiddler crabs: edible near coast
- Deer and feral hog inland
- Mullet fish: abundant in bays

## Shelter Priorities
- Get to high ground before storm surge
- Concrete structures only in surge zone
- Elevated slab foundations are safer
- Post-storm debris: move carefully
- Insect protection critical in marsh

## Navigation
- Coast runs E-W; north is inland
- Levees mark water control boundaries
- Bridge structures are key landmarks
- I-10 runs along the coast E-W
- Natural bayous flow southward

## Evacuation Routes
- Move inland and north for hurricane
- I-10, I-12, US-90: coast routes
- Contraflow often activated for storm
- Bridge crossings may close: go early
