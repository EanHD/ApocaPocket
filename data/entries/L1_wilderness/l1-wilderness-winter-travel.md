# Winter Travel Safety

## Why It Matters

- Winter travel exhausts faster
- Wet snow adds weight rapidly
- Post-holing wastes critical energy
- Avalanche terrain is everywhere
- Frostbite occurs before you feel it
- Route choice determines survival

## Energy Management

- Break trail in rotation with partner
- Trail-breaker goes 5-10 min, rotates
- Snowshoes reduce post-holing greatly
- Travel in morning hard crust is faster
- Avoid travel in new deep snow alone
- Rest before sweating through layers

## Route Selection

- Avoid convex slope rolls in avalanche
- Stay on ridgelines in heavy snow
- Travel in trees on gentle slopes
- Cross avalanche paths quickly
- Never travel below cornice edges
- Identify run-out zones before entering

## Avalanche Awareness

- Avoid 30-45 degree slope angles
- Recent avalanche debris = active terrain
- Cracking or settling sounds = warning
- Avoid leeward (wind-loaded) slopes
- Travel one person at a time in terrain
- If buried: make space, spit to find down

## Frostbite Prevention

- Check partner faces every 30 min
- White or waxy skin = early frostbite
- Rewarm in armpit immediately
- Do not rub affected area
- Keep extremities dry above all else
- Vapor barrier socks help feet

## What Can Go Wrong

- Overheating and sweating in wool
- Crossing avalanche path as group
- Ignoring white spot on partner face
- Post-holing into deep hidden water

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Route from A to B avoiding
  all avalanche terrain on a map
- Sign of mastery: 3-day winter
  travel with zero frostbite incidents
