# Southwest Hazards

## Rattlesnake Safety
- Most bites occur on hands and ankles
- Wear boots and probe with a stick
- Do not handle even dead snakes
- Bite: immobilize limb at heart level
- No tourniquet, no cutting, no suction
- Seek medical care within 4 hours

## Scorpion Awareness
- Shake out boots every morning
- Check bedding before sleeping
- Bark scorpion is most dangerous
- Child sting: treat as emergency
- Adult sting: monitor for symptoms
- Gila River area: highest density

## Heat Stroke Signs
- Stopped sweating is a danger sign
- Confusion, red dry skin, high pulse
- Move victim to shade immediately
- Cool neck, armpits, groin with water
- Fan the person constantly
- Life-threatening emergency: act fast

## Flash Flood Danger
- Forms miles away without local rain
- Canyon walls funnel water rapidly
- Escape to high ground immediately
- Never camp in wash or dry riverbed
- Sound: roaring before water arrives

## Dust Storm Protocol
- Pull off road, turn off headlights
- Set brake, keep seatbelt on
- Wait out storm; often just minutes
- If on foot: cover face, face away
- Eye protection prevents cornea damage
