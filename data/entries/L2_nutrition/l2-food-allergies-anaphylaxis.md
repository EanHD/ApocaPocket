# Food Allergies

**CRITICAL**: Anaphylaxis can be fatal in 15 - 30 minutes. Early epinephrine saves lives. 32 million Americans have food allergies.

## Big 9 Allergens

- Milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts (almonds/walnuts/cashews/pecans/pistachios), fish, shellfish, wheat, soy, sesame
- Together these cause 90% of all food allergic reactions
- Hidden sources: milk = casein/whey/lactose; wheat = semolina/spelt/kamut; soy = lecithin/miso/tofu; peanut = groundnuts/monkey nuts

## Anaphylaxis Signs

**Early signs (5 - 30 min after exposure):**
- Skin: hives, itching, flushing, lip/tongue/face swelling
- Throat: tightness, hoarseness, difficulty swallowing
- Respiratory: shortness of breath, wheezing, chest tightness
- GI: nausea, vomiting, cramping
- Cardiovascular: rapid pulse, dizziness, fainting
- Neurological: sense of doom, confusion

**Life-threatening signs:**
- Stridor (high-pitched wheeze), unable to speak, blue lips
- Weak or rapid pulse above 120, loss of consciousness, shock
- BIPHASIC RISK: 20% have second reaction 4 - 12 hours later  -  monitor minimum 4 - 6 hours

## EpiPen Use

**Use when ANY of these occur:**
- Respiratory symptoms (wheezing, throat tightness, difficulty breathing)
- Cardiovascular symptoms (dizziness, rapid pulse, fainting)
- Multiple body systems affected simultaneously

**Steps:**
1. Remove from carrier tube
2. Form fist  -  orange tip DOWN, blue safety UP
3. Pull blue safety release straight up (do not bend)
4. Swing and push orange tip firmly into outer thigh (through clothing is OK)
5. Hold firmly 3 seconds (hear two clicks)
6. Remove and massage injection site for 10 seconds
7. Call 911 and evacuate immediately (epinephrine lasts only 15 - 20 min)
8. Keep person lying flat with legs elevated

**Dosing:** adults/children above 66 lbs = EpiPen 0.3 mg; children 33 - 66 lbs = EpiPen Jr 0.15 mg

**Second dose:** no improvement after 5 - 10 min -> give second EpiPen; always evacuate regardless

## Post-EpiPen Care

- Expected effects: easier breathing, reduced wheezing, fading hives within 5 - 10 min; side effects = rapid heart rate, tremors, anxiety (normal, temporary)
- Keep flat with legs elevated 12 inches; if vomiting, turn on side but keep legs elevated
- Do NOT let person sit up or stand (cardiovascular collapse risk)
- Do NOT assume cured  -  evacuate for 4 - 6 hour monitoring (biphasic reaction risk)

## Antihistamines

- Diphenhydramine (Benadryl): 25 - 50 mg after epinephrine; helps hives and itching but does NOT stop anaphylaxis
- **NOT a substitute**: works too slowly (20 - 30 min vs 5 min), no bronchodilation, no cardiovascular protection
- Give epinephrine FIRST, then antihistamine

## If No EpiPen

- Benadryl 50 mg immediately + albuterol inhaler if wheezing + lay flat with legs elevated + oxygen if available
- Anaphylaxis without epinephrine has 1 - 3% mortality  -  EVACUATE URGENTLY

## Prevention

- Read labels every time (manufacturers change recipes); cook at home for more control
- Keep EpiPen accessible (not locked away); check expiration monthly (expires 18 - 24 months)
- Eating out: inform manager (not just server); avoid buffets, bakeries, ice cream shops (high cross-contamination risk)
- Travel: pack two EpiPens (one in bag, one in carry-on); research hospitals at destination

## EpiPen Storage

- Room temperature 59 - 86 degF; protect from light (keep in carrier); do NOT refrigerate or freeze
- Expired EpiPen: still use if nothing else available (may retain 70 - 80% potency); replace before expiry
- Inspect through window: solution must be clear and colorless; discard if brown, cloudy, or has particles
