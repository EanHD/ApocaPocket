# Wood ID by Grain

## Overview

- Grain pattern unique to each species group
- Color, smell, weight, and texture all help
- End grain most diagnostic; reveals pores
- Field test with fingernail and water drop

## Ring Pattern Reading

- Growth rings: one per year in temperate trees
- Wide rings = fast growth (soft, weak wood)
- Narrow rings = slow growth (dense, stronger)
- Consistent rings = even growing conditions

## End Grain Pores

- Ring porous: large pores in spring; oak, ash
- Diffuse porous: even pores throughout; maple
- Non-porous: conifers; no visible pores
- Pore pattern identifies hardwood species

## Color and Smell

- Red heartwood: cedar, cherry, redwood
- Yellow-tan: oak, hickory, ash
- White/pale: ash, maple, birch sapwood
- Smell test: cedar aromatic; pine resinous

## Hardness Field Test

- Fingernail test: dents soft pine easily
- Knife test: hard = resists scratching
- Bounce test: dense wood rings clearly
- Weight: dense hardwood heavy for size

## Water and Float Test

- Dense woods (oak, hickory) sink in water
- Light softwoods (pine, cedar) float easily
- Partially sinking = intermediate density
- Density guides use: structural vs. carving
