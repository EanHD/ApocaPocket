# Hickory Wood

## Overview

- Hardest, toughest native North American wood
- Exceptionally high shock resistance
- Ring-porous; distinct growth rings visible
- Heavy; high BTU fuel and charcoal wood

## Physical Properties

- Density: 48-52 lbs per cubic foot
- Very high Janka hardness (1820+)
- Light tan to white sapwood; brown heartwood
- Stiff, strong, and tough simultaneously

## Tool Handle Uses

- Top choice for axes, sledges, and hatchets
- Absorbs shock better than any alternative
- Splitting mauls, picks, and adzes
- Handle length: 24-36 inches for long handles

## Smoking Food

- Produces strong, distinctive smoke flavor
- Classic for smoking pork and red meat
- Burns slowly; long even heat output
- Mix with fruitwood for milder flavor

## Charcoal Quality

- Highest BTU charcoal of any North American wood
- Dense charcoal; burns longest of hardwoods
- Preferred for high-temp smelting operations
- Low ash; clean long-lasting coals

## Working Notes

- Must be worked green; dries like iron
- Use sharp draw knife and spoke shave
- Quarter-split for straight-grain blanks
- Air dry 1 year per inch of thickness
