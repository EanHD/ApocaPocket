# Flint and Chert

## Overview

- Microcrystalline quartz (cryptocrystalline)
- Conchoidal fracture allows predictable flaking
- Flint: typically dark grey to black
- Chert: wider color range; same properties

## Identification

- Smooth waxy surface when freshly broken
- No visible crystals; glassy appearance
- Rings when tapped with another stone
- Found in limestone beds and gravel deposits

## Quality Assessment

- Best: uniform fine texture; no inclusions
- Look for ripple marks on fresh break face
- Avoid: grainy texture, fossilized pockets
- Heat treating: improves inferior material

## Where to Find

- Riverbed gravels and exposed stream banks
- Chalk and limestone outcrops and bluffs
- Plowed agricultural fields after rain
- Beach cobbles at many coastal locations

## Knapping Properties

- Thin flakes with sharp cutting edge
- Edge retains sharpness; cuts hide and meat
- Fractures predictably from platform angle
- Can be retouched to restore cutting edge

## Flint vs Chert

- Flint: typically finer; more predictable break
- Chert: variable; some poor, some excellent
- Both used the same way; same techniques
- Test each piece before investing time
