# Green vs Seasoned Wood

## Overview

- Green wood: freshly cut; high moisture content
- Seasoned: air-dried; moisture below 20%
- Kiln-dried: fastest method for low moisture
- Each has specific advantages for different tasks

## Green Wood Advantages

- Much easier to carve, bend, and shape
- Steam bending not usually required
- Splits cleanly along grain with hand tools
- Preferred for bowls, spoons, handles (rough)

## Seasoned Advantages

- Dimensionally stable; will not shrink or warp
- Stronger for structural applications
- Better for joinery; tight joints stay tight
- Required for finished bows and tool handles

## Moisture Effects

- Freshly cut: 40-100% moisture content (MC)
- Air-dried 1-2 years: 12-20% MC
- Equilibrium with indoor air: 6-10% MC
- Each percent drop causes slight shrinkage

## Drying Methods

- Air dry stacked with spacers for airflow
- Stack in shade; avoid direct sunlight cracking
- End grain sealing (mud, tar) prevents checking
- 1 year per inch of thickness is rule of thumb

## Green vs Dry Uses

- Green: baskets, spoons, rough handles, bending
- Dry: bows, fine tools, structural joints
- Dry: saw cuts (green gums saw blade)
- Green: axe and hoe handle fitting (shrinks tight)
