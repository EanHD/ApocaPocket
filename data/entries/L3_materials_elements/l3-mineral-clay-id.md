# Clay Identification

## Overview

- Clay: fine-particle hydrous aluminum silicate
- Forms from weathering of feldspar minerals
- Found in stream banks, roadcuts, lake beds
- Plasticity is the key workability indicator

## Field Identification

- Pick up moist soil; roll between fingers
- Clay: smooth, slippery; no gritty feeling
- Sandy soil: gritty and crumbly when wet
- Silt: smooth when wet; crumbles when dry

## Plasticity Test

- Roll moist clay into pencil-thin coil
- Clay coils without cracking = workable
- Cracks immediately = too sandy; add more clay
- Test: bend coil in U-shape; good clay holds

## Ribbon Test

- Push clay between thumb and forefinger
- Squeeze to form ribbon from fist
- Long smooth ribbon (>5cm) = high plasticity
- Short crumbling ribbon = low plasticity clay

## Settling Purity Test

- Add soil to water in jar; shake; let settle
- Gravel settles first (seconds); sand (minutes)
- Silt (hours); clay stays suspended days
- Pour off top layer; clay settles last = pure

## Firing Temperature

- Low-fire earthenware: 900-1100C (bonfire)
- Higher fire stoneware: 1200C+ (kiln)
- Test: small test piece in fire before committing
- Pinkish-red after firing = earthenware clay
