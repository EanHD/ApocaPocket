# Sulfur

## Overview

- Native sulfur deposits near volcanic vents
- Bright yellow crystalline mineral
- Low melting point 115C; ignites at 160C
- Burns blue with toxic SO2 gas production

## Identification

- Bright yellow to pale yellow color
- Distinctive rotten-egg smell (sulfurous)
- Burns with blue flame; distinct smell
- Lightweight; brittle crystals

## Where Found

- Fumarolic deposits near volcanic vents
- Geothermal areas and hot spring margins
- Coastal volcanic islands worldwide
- Near iron pyrite deposits (after oxidation)

## Fire-Starting Use

- Ignites from spark or friction fire coal
- Match-head equivalent when powdered
- Coat kindling with sulfur for quick ignition
- Combined with saltpeter and charcoal: black powder

## Preservation Uses

- Burn in sealed container to fumigate
- SO2 preserves fruit, grain, and wine
- Sulfur candle in storage room kills insects
- Kills mold and mildew in sealed spaces

## Medical and Other Uses

- Topical fungicide for skin conditions
- Dust on plants to control mold and mites
- Flux in metal smelting (absorbs impurities)
- Component in rubber vulcanization process
