# Wood Knot Types

## Overview

- Knots are cross-sections of branch attachments
- They interrupt wood grain; create weak zones
- Live knots: intergrown; moderate strength
- Dead knots: encased; fall out when dried

## Live Knots

- Tightly grown into surrounding wood
- Branch was alive when wood grew around it
- Grain distorts around knot but stays connected
- Lower strength than clear wood; not zero

## Dead Knots

- Branch died before wood encased it
- Black ring encircles knot; no wood bond
- Falls out when dried; leaves hole in board
- Zero structural value; do not use in load path

## Spike Knots

- Cut end-on from branch; elongated oval
- Common in quarter-sawn and rift-cut lumber
- Less disruptive than flat-cut round knots
- Still weaker than clear grain but not as bad

## Safe Knot Positions

- Compression zone: top of a beam is OK
- Interior sections where not bearing load
- Decorative craft items where strength less critical
- Avoid: tension face, edge of tool handles

## Knot-Free Selection

- Split (rive) rather than saw for clear grain
- Split material follows grain; sawn does not
- Select bow staves and handles from clear sections
- Check all four faces before use in critical parts
