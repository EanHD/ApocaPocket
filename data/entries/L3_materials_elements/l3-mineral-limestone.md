# Limestone

## Overview

- Sedimentary rock; mostly calcium carbonate
- Burns to make quicklime above 900C
- Common and widespread globally
- Softer than granite; easier to work

## Identification

- White, grey, tan, or cream colored
- Reacts with acid (vinegar bubbles vigorously)
- Acid test is definitive field identification
- Layered or massive; often contains fossils

## Lime Production

- Burns at 900C+ to produce quicklime (CaO)
- Shells and coral are same compound
- Quicklime slaked with water = lime putty
- Best kiln fuel: charcoal or hardwood

## Building Uses

- Softer than granite; can be hand-cut with saw
- Cut blocks for walls; ancient standard
- Weathers moderately in wet climate
- Lime mortar: bonds limestone blocks well

## Field Tool Uses

- Too soft for sharp cutting tools
- Adequate for hammering and rough shaping
- Use as grindstone for soft materials
- Good weight for pressing and pressing

## Acid Test Details

- Drop vinegar on suspect stone
- Limestone: vigorous visible bubbling
- Marble (metamorphic limestone): also bubbles
- Granite: no reaction; does not contain carbonate
