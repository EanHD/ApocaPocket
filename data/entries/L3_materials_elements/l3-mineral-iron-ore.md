# Iron Ore Field ID

## Overview

- Iron most abundant metal in Earth's crust
- Three main ores: magnetite, hematite, limonite
- Identifying feature: heavy weight for size
- Streak test, magnet test, and color identify type

## Magnetite (Fe3O4)

- Black to dark grey; metallic luster
- Strongly magnetic; test with compass needle
- Heaviest iron ore; very high iron content
- Found in igneous and metamorphic terrain

## Hematite (Fe2O3)

- Red to silver-grey; earthy to metallic luster
- Red-brown streak on unglazed ceramic tile
- Not magnetic; much more common than magnetite
- Most important commercial iron ore globally

## Limonite (Goethite)

- Yellow to brown to orange color
- Yellow-brown streak on streak plate
- Rust-colored staining in soil nearby
- Lower grade ore; widely available in bogs

## Bog Iron

- Limonite deposited by bacterial action in bogs
- Nodules form in bog soil over decades
- Harvest nodules from bog floor or stream bed
- Lower grade but historically important

## Field Tests

- Heavy weight test: iron ores feel heavy
- Streak test: red = hematite, yellow = limonite
- Magnet test: sticks = magnetite
- All three produce iron when reduced in charcoal
