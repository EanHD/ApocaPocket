# Maple Wood

## Overview

- Dense, hard, fine-grained hardwood
- Diffuse-porous; uniform texture throughout
- Light cream to pale tan color
- Sap tappable in late winter for syrup

## Physical Properties

- Density: 44-47 lbs per cubic foot
- Janka hardness 1450 (hard maple)
- Fine grain; very smooth when worked
- Strong in bending; good floor material

## Sap and Syrup

- Tap in late winter (freeze-thaw cycles needed)
- Insert spile into tree 2-3 inches deep
- Collect sap: 40:1 ratio sap to syrup
- Boil down outdoors; very large volumes steam

## Wood Uses

- Excellent for bowls; fine grain turns well
- Cutting boards and utensils (food safe)
- Mallets and light tool handles
- Flooring and interior furniture

## Burning Properties

- Good fuel; produces steady even heat
- Dense charcoal; good cooking wood
- Mild smoke flavor; good for light smoking
- Not as much resin as pine; cleaner fire

## Identification

- Opposite leaf arrangement (like all maples)
- Paired winged seeds (samara) in fall
- Light-colored bark; shallow furrowed ridges
- Leaf shape classic 5-lobed maple pattern
