# Elm Wood

## Overview

- Distinctive interlocked grain pattern
- Almost impossible to split with axe or wedge
- This makes it ideal for wheel hubs and mallets
- Excellent friction fire hearthboard wood

## Physical Properties

- Density: 35-40 lbs per cubic foot
- Medium to dark brown; often grayish tones
- Ring-porous; distinct growth rings
- Cross-grained structure resists splitting

## Friction Fire Use

- Among the best hearthboard woods available
- Works with willow, cedar, or cottonwood drill
- Produces fine dark dust at low drill speed
- Pre-dry hearthboard thoroughly before use

## Why It Cannot Be Split

- Grain interlocks in alternating spiral pattern
- Grain never runs out parallel to any face
- No straight grain plane to split along
- Must be sawn; cannot rive easily at all

## Working Methods

- Saw cross-cuts work; splitting does not
- Work with sharp chisels and gouges
- Plane along grain direction carefully
- Carve bowls and spoons from branch sections

## Other Uses

- Wheel hubs: withstands spoke rotation force
- Chopping blocks; resists impact splitting
- Boat planks; holds nails despite wet cycles
- Bowls: dense and durable for long use
