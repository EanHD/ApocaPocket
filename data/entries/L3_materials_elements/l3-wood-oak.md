# Oak Wood Properties

## Overview

- Hardest and densest common temperate hardwood
- Ring-porous; visible pores on end grain
- High tannin content in bark and heartwood
- Most splits along straight radial grain

## Physical Properties

- Density: 45-50 lbs per cubic foot
- Janka hardness: 1290 (red oak) typical
- Color: light tan to brown; darker heartwood
- Strong; resists compression and bending

## Splitting and Working

- Splits along straight radial grain readily
- Use wedge or froe to split fence rails
- Difficult to work cross-grain; plan cuts
- Quarter-sawn shows distinctive ray fleck

## Charcoal Quality

- Among best charcoal woods; high BTU
- Dense charcoal; long slow burn
- Preferred for iron smelting and forging
- Even heat output; low ash residue

## Bark and Tannin Uses

- Inner bark: highest tannin for leather
- Boil bark for leather tanning solution
- Oak galls contain highest tannin content
- Bark tea: astringent for wound care

## Other Uses

- Tool handles: strong and shock-resistant
- Barrel staves: watertight and flavor-neutral
- Structural timbers: lasts centuries
- Acorns: food source after leaching tannin
