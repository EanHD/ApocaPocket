# Wood Rot Resistance

## Overview

- Heartwood extractives (oils, tannins) resist fungi
- Sapwood has near-zero rot resistance in all species
- Species rated: very durable, durable, moderate, poor
- Use durable species for ground contact or wet use

## Very Durable Species

- Black locust: best in North America by far
- Osage orange: extremely durable in ground
- Eastern red cedar: excellent fence post wood
- Pacific yew: very durable; limited size

## Durable Species

- White oak: good for outdoor use 10-20 years
- Chestnut: excellent but mostly disease-killed
- Black walnut: good outdoor durability
- Teak: best tropical outdoor timber

## Moderate Durability

- Red oak: moderate; not for ground contact
- Douglas fir: moderate; widely used in West
- Larch: moderate; often used for outdoor use
- Cherry: moderate; limit to above-ground

## Poor Durability

- White pine, ash, maple: poor durability
- Birch, alder, basswood: rot quickly in soil
- Any sapwood from any species: avoid outdoor
- Treat poor species with tar or charring

## Extending Durability

- Char end-grain of posts before planting
- Coat with pine tar to repel moisture
- Raise wood off ground with stone bases
- Keep roof covering to prevent prolonged wet
