# Bow Wood Selection

## Overview

- Bow needs compression (belly) and tension (back)
- Back = wood facing target; must resist tension
- Belly = wood facing archer; must resist compression
- Growth ring as back prevents delamination

## Top Tier Bow Woods

- Osage orange (hedge apple): best in North America
- English yew: traditional; back and belly combo
- Black locust: excellent where osage scarce
- All three rarely take set; ideal strength:weight

## Second Tier Options

- Ash: good compression; readily available
- Black walnut: moderate; better belly wood
- Red oak: most available substitute overall
- White oak: slightly better than red oak

## Growth Ring Rule

- Follow one growth ring as the back of bow
- Never cut through rings on the back face
- Rings on belly may be cut through freely
- Violation causes delamination under tension

## Stave Preparation

- Quarter-split log to get straight sections
- Air dry for 1 year minimum; 2 years preferred
- Leave back ring intact; shape belly only
- Work slowly; leave stave thick at first

## Testing and Tillering

- Bend stave over leg before shaping
- Mark stiff spots; remove wood from belly only
- Tiller evenly both limbs from nock to handle
- Draw 25 lbs max until fully tillered and cured
