# Quartz

## Overview

- Most common mineral on Earth's surface
- Hardness 7 on Mohs scale; scratches steel
- Clear to white; also pink, purple, smoky
- Found in granite, pegmatites, and veins

## Identification

- Clear to white glassy crystals common
- Six-sided prism shape in well-formed crystals
- Hardness test: easily scratches glass
- Conchoidal fracture on broken surfaces

## Spark Production

- Strike quartz against high-carbon steel
- Produces sparks for fire-starting
- Less reliable than flint but widely available
- Quartz vs quartz also produces sparks

## Cutting Use

- Freshly fractured quartz has sharp edges
- Improvised cutting edge for emergency use
- Not as controllable as flint or obsidian
- Sharp enough to cut rope, gut, and skin

## Abrasive Applications

- Sand of quartz used for grinding and polishing
- Quartz gravel: sharpen tools on wet piece
- Coarser than fine sandstone; good initial work
- Polishing with progressively finer grades

## Other Properties

- Piezoelectric (pressure produces voltage)
- Optical: clear varieties transmit light
- Heat-stable; does not crack from fire heat
- Used as fire starting with metal striker
