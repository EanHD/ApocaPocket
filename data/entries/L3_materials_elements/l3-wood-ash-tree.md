# Ash Tree Wood

## Overview

- Best tool-handle wood in temperate forests
- Outstanding shock and impact resistance
- Ring-porous; distinct grain pattern
- Bends well when steam-heated; ideal for bows

## Physical Properties

- Density: 40-44 lbs per cubic foot
- Light tan to white; minimal color contrast
- Tough, elastic; absorbs impact energy
- Janka hardness about 1320; tough not brittle

## Shock Resistance

- Best of all northern hardwoods for impact
- Preferred for axes, hammers, mauls, hoes
- Absorbs vibration; reduces hand fatigue
- Does not split suddenly; gives warning flex

## Bow Making Properties

- Good working back and belly combination
- Bends without set when properly tillered
- Inferior to osage orange but widely available
- Kiln or air-dry before shaping stave

## Splitting and Working

- Splits along grain cleanly with froe
- Very workable with edged tools when green
- Dries to hard surface; work green if possible
- Quarter split sections make best staves

## Other Uses

- Steam-bent snowshoe frames
- Canoe ribs and gunwales
- Chair frames and cabinet parts
- Paddle blades and oar shafts
