# Field Rock Testing

## Overview

- Three key tests identify most common minerals
- Hardness scale: Mohs 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond)
- Acid test: carbonate minerals bubble with vinegar
- Streak test: color of powdered mineral on tile

## Mohs Hardness Scale

- 1-2: fingernail scratches it (talc, gypsum)
- 3: copper coin scratches it (calcite)
- 4-5: knife blade scratches it (fluorite, apatite)
- 6-7: scratches glass (feldspar, quartz)

## Field Hardness Kit

- Fingernail: hardness ~2.5
- Copper coin: hardness ~3
- Steel knife blade: hardness ~5.5
- Quartz piece: hardness ~7

## Acid (Vinegar) Test

- Calcium carbonate minerals effervesce
- Limestone and marble: vigorous bubbling
- Dolomite: slow bubbles with cold acid
- Silicate minerals: no reaction at all

## Streak Test

- Rub mineral on unglazed ceramic tile
- True color of mineral shows in streak
- Hematite: red-brown streak (despite grey)
- Pyrite: black streak (despite gold color)

## Specific Gravity Test

- Heavy for size = likely metallic ore
- Iron ore, lead, copper all notably heavy
- Compare to same-size common rock
- Light despite solid look = silicate mineral
