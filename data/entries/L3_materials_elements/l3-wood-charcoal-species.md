# Best Charcoal Woods

## Overview

- Charcoal quality mirrors original wood density
- Dense hardwoods produce dense long-burning char
- Softwood charcoal is light; burns fast and hot
- Metal smelting demands dense high-carbon char

## Top Tier Woods

- Hickory: densest; highest BTU charcoal
- Oak (all species): consistent quality
- Black locust: very dense; rot-resistant tree
- Osage orange: extremely dense; very hot char

## Second Tier Options

- Hard maple: good density; widely available
- Beech: excellent charcoal; consistent quality
- Hornbeam (ironwood): dense; small diameter
- White ash: slightly lower density but good

## Softwood Charcoal

- Burns fast and hot but not long
- Good for quick forge heats
- Not suitable for sustained smelting operations
- Pine charcoal: excess resin affects metal quality

## BTU Relative Ranking

- Hickory and locust: 100% reference standard
- Oak: approximately 90-95% of hickory value
- Maple: approximately 85-90% of hickory value
- White pine: approximately 50% of hickory value

## Production Notes

- Dry wood thoroughly before burning to char
- Slow burn produces denser charcoal
- Dense charcoal from dense wood; no shortcuts
- Mound or retort method; retort is more consistent
