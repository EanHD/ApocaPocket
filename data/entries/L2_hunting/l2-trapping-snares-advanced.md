# Advanced Snares

**Key advantage**: Passive 24/7 hunting. 20 - 30 snares = 5 - 10 animals/day. Works while you sleep; silent; no ammo; reusable. In long-term survival, snares are primary food source  -  active hunting is the backup.

## Snare Types

- **Simple loop**: rabbits/squirrels; 3 - 4 ft wire or cordage; loop 4 - 6 inch diameter; hang 3 - 4 inches off ground; strangles animal in 30 - 60 sec
- **Spring snare**: rabbits/marten/fox; bent sapling 8 - 15 ft + trigger + loop at trail; trigger releases, spring yanks animal up, breaks neck instantly; animal lifted off ground (predator-proof)
- **Drag snare**: fox/coyote/beaver; loop attached via 6 - 10 ft wire to 15 - 30 lb log; animal drags log, exhausts in brush; use when stakes pull out
- **Squirrel pole**: lean 8 - 12 ft pole against tree at 45 deg; attach 3 - 5 loops (2 - 3 inch diameter) along pole; squirrels run up, head enters loop; 50%+ capture rate per pole

## Materials

- **Wire** (best): 22 - 24 gauge for rabbits, 18 - 20 gauge for larger game; smooth sliding lock; reusable 20+ times; darken with mud or smoke before setting
- **Cordage**: braided plant fiber, sinew, paracord, tarred bankline; quieter than wire; replace after 5 - 10 catches
- Wire lock: thread wire through small fixed loop at one end to create sliding noose

## Placement

- Read sign: fresh tracks (sharp edges, moist soil), fresh scat, worn trails (compressed vegetation), runs under brush, hair on fences
- Best locations: game trails, natural funnels (between trees/rocks/brush), water source approaches, den entrances, berry patches
- Do NOT modify the trail (disturbed vegetation alerts animals); camouflage wire with mud; set 5 - 10 snares per area; check every 24 hours

## Trigger Types

- **Figure-4**: 3 notched sticks that form a "4" shape; holds spring/deadfall under tension; sensitive, adjustable; ~10 min to build
- **Paiute deadfall**: stick + cordage toggle; most sensitive trigger (catches mice/small birds); no carving needed; ~5 min to build
- **Spring pole release**: bent sapling held by trigger stick; animal entering loop disturbs trigger; yanks animal upward

## Ethics & Safety

- Check traps daily (minimizes suffering); dispatch trapped animals immediately; match loop size to target animal (too large = escape, too small = body catch)
- Map all trap locations; mark with natural cairns; harvest before predators steal catches
- Spring snares: use safety stick during setup (can break fingers if triggered); wear gloves with wire; never reach under set deadfalls
- Release non-target or undersized animals; know local protected species

## Legal Notes

- Most US states require trapping license; specific snare types vary by state (neck snares generally legal, leg snares often banned); check 24-hour check interval requirements
- Survival situations: emergency use typically not prosecuted; always verify regulations before non-emergency trapping
