# Crayfish Trapping

## Overview

- Crayfish are abundant in streams,
  lakes, ponds across North America
- High protein, easy to catch
- Method: hand-turning rocks, bottle
  traps, or simple woven basket traps
- Best collected at night or dawn

## Finding Crayfish

- Under flat rocks in streams
- Along lake edges in rocky shallows
- In weed beds along banks
- Active at night -- easiest after dark
- Look for claw marks in mud flats

## Hand Collection

- Turn rocks gently from downstream side
- Crayfish swim backward -- scoop fast
  from below with cupped hands
- Grab body, not claws
- Drop immediately into container
- Work methodically upstream

## Bottle Trap

- Cut plastic bottle as described in
  Fish Trap entry
- Bait inside with chunk of fish,
  meat scraps, or bread
- Submerge at rocky shoreline
- Check morning and evening
- Multiple traps = good yield

## Woven Basket Trap

- Same funnel principle as fish trap
- Smaller scale: 6 in diameter
- Funnel opening: 1.5 in
- Anchor at rocky bank edge
- Bait with strong-smelling food

## Cooking

- Boil live in salted water 3-5 min
- Red/pink color = cooked
- Eat body meat and tail meat
- Small claws edible: crack with rock
- Discard any that smell off
