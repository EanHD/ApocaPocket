# Alaska Fish Species

**Key Strategy**: July - August salmon runs = 90% of annual fish protein. Net fishing = 50 - 200 lbs/day. Ocean-bright fish = best quality; dark/hooked = edible but lower quality.

## Five Pacific Salmon

- **King/Chinook** 20 - 50 lbs, May - Jul  -  black gums, fully spotted tail, deep red flesh
- **Sockeye/Red** 5 - 8 lbs, Jul peak  -  NO spots, brightest red flesh, highest fat; BEST for preservation
- **Coho/Silver** 8 - 12 lbs, Aug - Sep  -  white gums, upper-half spotted tail, aggressive biter
- **Pink/Humpy** 3 - 5 lbs, Jul - Aug  -  large black spots, smallest; males get huge hump when spawning
- **Chum/Dog** 8 - 15 lbs, Jul - Sep  -  no distinct spots, deeply forked tail; tiger stripes when spawning
- Harvest ocean-bright fish only; dark/hooked fish still edible but flesh is softer

## Run Indicators

- Fish jumping in river; eagles and bears congregating; locals fishing
- Position at river mouth or lower river during July - August peak

## Salmon Fishing

- **Net (most efficient)**: gill net 50 - 100 ft, 6-inch mesh; set perpendicular to current; check every 2 - 4 hours; 10 - 50 fish/day at peak
- **Rod & reel**: heavy rod 10 - 20 lb line; spinners/spoons/salmon eggs; target deeper pools; 2 - 10 fish/day
- **Snagging**: large treble hook, heavy weight; cast into school, yank to hook body; legal in designated areas

## Other Species

- **Arctic Grayling** 1 - 2 lbs  -  HUGE dorsal sailfin, purple-gray body, smells like thyme; easy on dry flies; clear cold streams
- **Northern Pike** 5 - 15 lbs  -  torpedo body, duck-bill snout, RAZOR TEETH (use pliers); spoons near weeds; year-round; Y-bones in fillet
- **Rainbow Trout** 1 - 5 lbs  -  pink lateral stripe, black spots; flies/spinners; year-round freshwater
- **Arctic Char** 2 - 8 lbs  -  light spots on dark body, orange/red belly; fall spawning run = best eating
- **Lake Trout** 5 - 20 lbs  -  light spots on dark, deeply forked tail; deep cold lakes; ice jigging in winter
- **Dolly Varden** 1 - 5 lbs  -  light spots, square tail; coastal streams; often caught incidentally while salmon fishing

## Seasonal Guide

- Jan - Mar: lake trout, pike via ice fishing  -  moderate yield
- Apr - May: grayling, Dolly Varden at ice-out  -  moderate yield
- Jun: early king salmon, grayling, trout  -  high yield
- **Jul - Aug: ALL SALMON peak  -  use nets  -  VERY HIGH yield**
- Sep: coho (late run), char spawning  -  high yield
- Oct - Dec: lake trout, pike ice-up  -  moderate yield

## Field Prep

- Bleed (cut gills) and gut within 1 - 2 hours of catch; cool below 40 degF
- Spoilage: 32 - 40 degF = 3 - 5 days; 50 degF+ = 6 - 12 hours
- **Hot smoking** (best for salmon): 225 degF, 4 - 6 hours, lasts 2 - 4 weeks; use alder or birch (not spruce/pine)
- **Drying/jerky**: thin strips, salt cure, air dry 1 - 3 days at 120 - 140 degF
- **Salting**: pack in salt layers, lasts months
- See l2-hunting-game-preservation-alaska for full protocols

## Species ID Summary

- Trout: dark spots on light background, slightly forked tail, 1 - 5 lbs, year-round freshwater
- Char: light spots on dark background, square tail, orange belly, 2 - 8 lbs
- Salmon: anadromous (die after spawning), 5 - 50 lbs, deeply forked tail
