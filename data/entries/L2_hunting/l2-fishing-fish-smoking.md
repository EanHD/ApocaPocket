# Fish Smoking Method

## Overview

- Two types: hot smoke and cold smoke
- Hot smoke: cooks and flavors, 1-4 hrs
  at 160-225F -- short-term preservation
- Cold smoke: below 90F, flavors and
  partially dries -- longer shelf life
  but requires more time
- Both extend fish beyond raw shelf life

## Fish Preparation

- Clean and fillet or butterfly fish
- Salt lightly or brine 1-2 hours:
  2 tbsp salt per quart water
- Air dry 30-60 min until tacky
  (pellicle forms on surface)
- This step crucial -- locks in moisture
  and helps smoke adhere

## Hot Smoking Setup

- Build small fire, let burn to coals
- Build rack or hang fish over coals
  at 6-12 inch height
- Add green/wet wood for smoke:
  alder, apple, hickory, oak
- Temp goal: 160-225F near fish
- Time: 1-4 hours depending on size

## Cold Smoking Setup

- Fire box separate from smoke chamber
- Route smoke through long tube or
  trench to cool before reaching fish
- Temperature at fish: below 90F
- Time: 6-24+ hours
- Requires sustained attention

## Wood Selection

- Hardwoods best: hickory, oak, alder,
  fruit woods
- Avoid: pine, cedar, green softwoods
  (resin = bitter taste, toxic)
- Wet wood chips on coals add smoke

## Doneness Signs

- Hot smoked: flakes easily, opaque
- Surface: golden brown, firm to touch
- Properly smoked: shelf life 3-5 days
  without refrigeration in cool weather
