# Throwing Stick

**Function**: Rotating stick sweeps an 18–24 inch arc — far larger strike zone than a thrown stone. Easier to learn than sling (5–10 hrs vs 20+). Aboriginal Australians routinely brought down kangaroos with these.

## Design & Build

- Length: 18–24 inches; slight banana curve (20–40°); weight 8–16 oz
- Cross-section: flat on one side (blade), rounded on other — creates aerodynamic lift for stable flat flight
- Wood: hardwood with natural curve (oak, hickory, mesquite); look for curved branches at tree junctions
- Steps: strip bark; carve one flat side; round all edges; heavier end leads in flight (1/3 from grip); sand smooth; char in fire optional (hardens wood); seal with animal fat
- Test flight: should spin flat and parallel to ground; travels 40–60 yards with moderate effort

## Throwing

- **Sidearm** (primary, 30–60 yds): sideways stance; stick horizontal; snap arm forward with wrist flick; stick spins like frisbee
- **Overhand** (10–30 yds): overhead throw; stick rotates end-over-end; full weight on impact
- **Underhand** (dense cover): minimal windup; pendulum swing between legs; low trajectory under branches

## Hunting

- **Rabbits**: lead target by 3–5 ft at 30–50 yds; throw at the running path
- **Squirrels**: 20–40 yds; throw when animal pauses on branch or ground
- **Ground birds** (grouse, quail): throw into flight path when flushed
- Carry 2–3 sticks for follow-up throws without retrieval
- Benchmark: Aboriginal hunters achieved 70%+ hit rate on rabbits at 40–50 yds

## Advantages

- Vs sling: larger strike zone; less practice (5–10 hrs vs 20+); stick is visible in flight for correction
- Vs bow: build in 30 min vs 10–20 hrs; no arrows to lose or string to replace; carry 3–4 in belt
- Vs atlatl: instant deployment; works in thick brush; easy to find after throw

## Safety

- Sticks travel 60+ yards — verify backstop and 50 ft clearance on all sides
- Inspect for cracks before every throw (failure mid-flight = uncontrolled tumble)
- Start practice at 15 ft before progressing to distance work

## Troubleshooting

- Tumbles in flight: unbalanced weight or poor release — re-carve for better balance, practice wrist snap
- Flies short distance: too heavy or insufficient spin — carve thinner, increase snap
- Returns and hits thrower: too much curve — reduce curve angle or round the flat side slightly
