# Sling (Slinging)

**Function**: Launches 2 - 4 oz stones at 100+ mph; lethal at 200 yards. Roman legions maintained dedicated sling units. Infinite ammunition, zero maintenance, fits in pocket.

## Construction

- Pouch: leather or woven fabric, diamond shape, 4 inches long x 3 inches wide; punch holes at pointed ends
- Retention cord: loops over middle finger permanently; never releases during throw
- Release cord: held between thumb and forefinger; releases at throw; tie knot at ideal grip point for consistency
- Total cord length: 3 - 4 feet per cord (6 - 8 feet end to end); adjust to arm length
- Wet leather and stretch before drying for tight, durable pouch

## Ammunition

- Weight: 2 - 4 oz, about egg-sized
- Shape: smooth ovoid or spherical (reduces tumble, increases accuracy)
- Material: river stones best; limestone acceptable; avoid flint (shatters unpredictably); sort by weight for consistency

## Throwing

- **Overhead** (max power, 40 - 100+ yds): swing vertically overhead 2 - 3 rotations; release at 45 deg for range
- **Sidearm** (accuracy, 30 - 50 yds): swing horizontally; release at target line; easier to aim at close range
- **Underhand** (under 30 yds): swing low beside hip; quick release; minimal windup needed
- Release timing is the critical skill  -  too early = high-left; too late = low-right; requires 100+ throws to develop muscle memory

## Hunting

- Small game: rabbits and squirrels at 30 - 50 yds; instant end with head shot
- Birds: grouse, ducks at expert level; must lead the target
- Benchmark: Balearic slingers hit a man's head at 200 yards; trained from childhood

## Advantages

- Ammunition is everywhere  -  infinite supply; zero maintenance; weighs 4 oz; fits in pocket; costs nothing
- Once learned: muscle memory retained for life

## Safety

- Misses travel 100 - 200 yards  -  verify backstop and open area; 50 ft minimum clearance all sides
- Inspect cords before each session; replace if frayed; start with 1 - 2 oz stones until technique is solid

## Troubleshooting

- Stone falls out of pouch: too loose or stone too small  -  soak leather and re-dry tighter
- Inconsistent accuracy: variable stone weight or rushed release  -  sort stones; slow down rotations
- Cord breaks: frayed or over-rotated  -  inspect before every use; reduce spin speed
