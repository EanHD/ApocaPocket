# Game Preserve in Alaska

**CRITICAL**: Alaska summer moose (600 lbs) spoils in 48 hours. Chris McCandless had hundreds of pounds of meat and lost it all to rot. Act within 2 - 4 hours or lose everything.

## Hour 0 - 4 Protocol

- Hour 0 - 2: field dress completely; quarter to maximize cooling surface; remove hide (holds heat); hang in shade with airflow
- Hour 2 - 4 (CRITICAL WINDOW): fire/wood -> smoke; salt -> jerky cure; cold weather -> cache; 60 degF+ -> use all methods simultaneously
- Begin cutting strips/chunks; protect from flies with smoke or cloth covering

## Hot Smoking

- Target: 225 - 250 degF; duration: 4 - 6 hrs per 1-inch strips; shelf life: 2 - 4 weeks unrefrigerated
- Best wood: alder (traditional, sweet), birch, willow  -  avoid spruce/pine (resinous, bitter taste)
- Meat prep: cut 1-inch strips across grain; trim ALL fat (goes rancid); salt rub 2 tbsp/lb; cure 30 min
- Temperature test: hand 6 inches above heat  -  4 - 5 sec = correct (225 - 250 degF); 2 - 3 sec = too hot
- Done when: dark mahogany color, firm but bendable
- Mistakes: fat left on = rancid in days; thick white smoke = creosote bitter; too hot = case-hardened outside/raw inside

## Jerky/Drying

- Best conditions: low humidity, 60 - 80 degF, windy; shelf life 1 - 2 months
- Salt cure: 1/3 cup salt per 5 lbs meat; pack completely; cure 12 - 24 hrs; rinse; dry at 120 - 140 degF
- Hang 6+ feet high (bear deterrence); cover at night; 1 - 3 days air drying or 12 - 24 hrs near fire
- Safe jerky: dark red/brown, firm leathery, bends without breaking, no moisture when squeezed
- Discard if: green/black/white spots, slimy texture, sour or ammonia smell

## Cold Storage

- Target below 40 degF
- **Snow cache**: dig 3 - 4 ft pit; line with spruce boughs; layer snow between packages; 18+ inch snow cap; mark location; log cap (bear deterrence)
- **Stream cooling**: glacier-fed stream below 50 degF; wrap in waterproof bags; weight with rocks; check every 12 hrs
- **Underground**: dig 3 - 4 ft below frost line; line with rocks; cover with moss then soil; heavy rock cap

## Spoilage Recognition

- Fresh: bright red/purple, firm, clean metallic smell  -  safe
- Early: brownish-gray edges, soft spots  -  trim and preserve immediately
- Advanced: green/black patches, slimy film  -  DISCARD ENTIRE PIECE
- Spoiled: putrid/liquefying, rotten eggs or ammonia smell  -  do not touch bare-handed
- **Temperature window**: 32 - 40 degF = 7 - 10 days; 50 - 60 degF = 24 - 36 hrs; 60 - 70 degF = 12 - 24 hrs; 70 degF+ = 6 - 12 hrs
- Alaska summer (Jun - Aug): assume 60 - 70 degF  -  act within 12 - 24 hours

## Yield Estimates

- Moose 1000 lbs -> 400 - 500 lbs edible -> 3 - 5 days full-time preservation work
- Caribou 300 lbs -> 120 - 150 lbs -> 1 - 2 days
- Deer 150 lbs -> 50 - 75 lbs -> 8 - 16 hours
- Prioritize high-value cuts (backstrap, tenderloins); accept partial loss over total loss

## Bear Safety

- Work 100+ yards from camp; keep firearm accessible
- Hang finished product 15 ft high, 10 ft from trees; never preserve meat near sleeping area
- Active smoking/drying strongly attracts bears; move processing site daily if possible
