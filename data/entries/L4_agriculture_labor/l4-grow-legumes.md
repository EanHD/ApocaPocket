# Legume Growing

## Why Legumes

- High protein: 20-25% by dry weight
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen (self-fertilizing)
- Store for years when dried
- Calorie-dense: 300-350 kcal per 100g dry
- Seed is also next year's planting stock
- Grow in poor soils where other crops fail

## Best Survival Legumes

- Fava bean (broad bean): cold-hardy,
  can be sown in autumn, very productive
- Bush beans: fast (55 days), easy
- Pole beans: higher yield, needs support
- Peas: early spring crop, cold-tolerant
- Lentils: drought-tolerant, high protein
- Soybeans: highest protein, warm season

## Planting

- Sow directly, not transplanted
- Peas: 3cm deep, 5cm apart, in rows
- Beans: 3-5cm deep, 15-20cm apart
- Fava: 5cm deep, 20-25cm apart
- Lentils: broadcast, rake in 2cm
- Inoculate with rhizobium if first planting

## Growing Conditions

- Peas: cool weather (below 20C) preferred
- Beans: warm soil, frost-tender
- Fava: tolerates frost, bolt in heat
- Lentils: dry, hot conditions are fine
- All: full sun, good drainage
- Watering: consistent until pod fill

## Harvesting for Storage

- Leave pods on vine until brown and dry
- Harvest before rain (mold risk)
- Shell into clean container
- Continue drying indoors if damp
- Store in airtight container when bone dry
- Test: bite a bean - should not dent

## Drying and Storage

- Dry at room temperature 2-3 weeks
- Grains must crack cleanly, not bend
- Store in sealed containers
- Oxygen absorbers extend storage to 25+ years
- Cool, dark location best
- Seal with a vacuum if possible

## Nitrogen to Soil

- Leave roots in ground after harvest
- Nodules release nitrogen as they decay
- 1 sq m of beans adds ~10g pure nitrogen
- Follow beans with heavy-feeding brassicas
- Rotate legumes around garden annually
