# Root Vegetables

## Best Root Crops

- Turnip: fastest (45 days), tolerates frost
- Parsnip: very cold hardy, sweetens with frost
- Beet: dual-purpose (root + greens)
- Carrot: long storage, high nutrition
- Celeriac: cold-hardy, very calorie-dense
- Swede/rutabaga: large, excellent storage

## Soil Requirements

- All prefer loose, deep, stone-free soil
- Stones cause forked, deformed roots
- Deep loosen to 30-40cm before planting
- Sandy loam is ideal
- Add compost but not fresh manure
  (causes forked roots)

## Depth and Spacing

- Carrots: 0.5cm deep, thin to 5cm apart
- Beets: 2cm deep, thin to 10cm apart
- Turnips: 1cm deep, thin to 15cm apart
- Parsnips: 1cm deep, thin to 15cm apart
- Thin ruthlessly for good root size
- Thinnings are edible microgreens

## Timing

- Carrots, beets: sow 4 weeks before last frost
- Parsnips: sow as early as possible (slow germinate)
- Turnips: sow in late summer for fall harvest
- Beets: fast maturing, 3 successions possible
- All can be left in ground through frost

## Harvesting and Storage

- Harvest before ground freezes solid
- Leave 2cm of tops on storage roots
- Never wash before storage
- Store in damp sand or sawdust
- Temperature: 0-4C, humid
- Good storage: 3-6 months for most roots

## Succession Planting

- Spring crop: beets, carrots (sow early)
- Summer gap: none needed (previous stored)
- Fall crop: turnips, beets (sow late summer)
- Leave parsnips in ground all winter
- Mark rows with stakes before snow

## Common Problems

- Forked carrots: stones or fresh manure
- Splitting: irregular watering
- Bolting: too hot, sow earlier next year
- Carrot fly: companion with onions
- Small roots: too crowded, thin harder
