# Fan Li Polyculture System

## Overview

- Fan Li (517-448 BC), statesman turned merchant
- Developed integrated aquaculture c. 460 BC
- Wrote world's oldest aquaculture manual
- Core idea: outputs of one system feed another
- Fish ponds + rice fields + animals = closed loop
- Nothing wasted; all waste becomes input
- Achieved wealth through surplus, not extraction

## Philosophy

- Nature operates in cycles, not lines
- Stack functions: one pond does many jobs
- Diversity prevents total crop failure
- Small ponds outperform large ones per area
- Water, land, air layers all productive
- Mimic wetland ecosystems deliberately

## Core System Components

- Fish pond as nutrient cycling center
- Flooded rice paddies with fish inside
- Mulberry trees on pond banks
- Silkworms fed mulberry leaves
- Silkworm waste fed to fish
- Ducks patrol rice for pests

## Why It Works

- Fish fertilize rice with waste
- Rice provides shade, insects for fish
- Ducks eat weeds, pests, snails
- Duck waste fertilizes pond/rice
- Mulberry roots stabilize pond banks
- Every organism earns its keep

## Land Efficiency

- 1 mu (~667 sq m) can feed a family
- Fish yield 2-5x land-only protein
- No synthetic inputs required
- Soil improves each season
- System strengthens over years

## Starting Small

- Begin with one small pond
- Add rice paddy adjacent first
- Introduce fish before ducks
- Plant mulberry on banks second year
- Add silkworms when mulberry matures
- Full system takes 2-3 years to establish

## Common Mistakes

- Stocking too many fish too soon
- Neglecting bank vegetation
- Monoculture fish species
- Ignoring seasonal transitions
- Draining ponds completely in winter

## Modern Relevance

- Used widely in China and SE Asia
- FAO promotes integrated rice-fish systems
- Proven 2,500+ year track record
- Adaptable to temperate and tropical zones
- Requires no electricity or chemicals
