# Biochar Soil Amendment

## What is Biochar

- Wood or biomass charred without oxygen
- Creates stable carbon that lasts centuries
- Porous structure holds water and nutrients
- Hosts soil microorganisms in pore spaces
- Terra preta soils in Amazon: biochar base
- Still fertile after 2,000+ years

## Making Biochar

- Load wood or biomass into metal drum
- Burn top layer, cap loosely to limit O2
- Goal: char not ash (black not white)
- Watch for blue-grey smoke to turn clear
- Clear smoke = pyrolysis complete
- Quench with water immediately after

## Simple Cone Pit Method

- Dig shallow cone-shaped pit in ground
- Build fire in bottom, add material steadily
- Keep flames at top, char builds below
- When pit is full of char, quench with water
- No equipment needed beyond a shovel
- Good for processing brush and crop residue

## Charging Biochar

- Raw biochar absorbs nitrogen from soil
- Must be charged before application
- Method 1: mix with equal volume compost
  and let sit for 2-4 weeks (turns black)
- Method 2: soak in diluted urine 1-2 weeks
- Method 3: mix into compost pile center
- Charged biochar smells earthy, not smoky

## Application Rates

- Apply 1-5% by volume of topsoil
- Per 100 sq m garden: 50-200 liters biochar
- Mix into top 15-20cm of soil
- One-time application (no annual reapplication)
- Can apply annually at lower rates to
  build up over time

## Benefits Over Time

- Year 1: minimal visible improvement
- Year 2: water retention noticeably better
- Year 3: plant vigor improves clearly
- Year 5: soil biology fully colonizes biochar
- Year 10+: permanent structural improvement
- Combined with compost: synergistic effect

## What Biochar Fixes

- Sandy soil: water and nutrient retention
- Clay soil: aeration and microbial habitat
- Acid soil: slight pH buffering effect
- Depleted soil: nutrient holding capacity
- All soil types benefit from biochar
