# Vermicomposting

## Why Worm Castings

- Most nutrient-dense compost available
- Plant-available nutrients, not raw
- Suppresses some soil pathogens
- Improves soil structure dramatically
- Odorless when done correctly
- Can be done indoors, any season

## Getting Started

- Species: Eisenia fetida (red wigglers)
- NOT earthworms from garden
- Start with 500g-1kg of worms
- Purchase from bait shop or online
- Container: any box 30-45cm deep
- Wood or plastic both work

## Bedding Preparation

- Shredded cardboard or newspaper
- Moisten to wrung-out sponge level
- Fill container 2/3 full of bedding
- Add worms on top
- Cover with moist paper to reduce light
- Wait 1 week before adding food

## Feeding

- Bury food under bedding surface
- Rotate feeding spots each addition
- Good: vegetable scraps, coffee grounds
  fruit, garden waste, paper
- Avoid: citrus (too acidic), onion/garlic
  meat, dairy, oily foods
- Feed every 1-2 weeks, not daily

## Moisture Management

- Bedding should feel like wrung-out sponge
- Too wet: add dry shredded paper
- Too dry: mist with water
- Leachate (liquid) draining out: dilute 10:1
  and use as liquid fertilizer (worm tea)
- Bad smell = too wet or wrong food added

## Harvesting Castings

- Harvest every 3-4 months
- Push contents to one side of bin
- Fill other side with fresh food and bedding
- Worms migrate to new side over 2-3 weeks
- Harvest the worm-free casting side
- Or use light: worms flee darkness, scoop top

## Application Rates

- Use as potting mix addition: 20% castings
- Top-dress garden beds: 1-2cm annually
- Seed-starting mix: 10-20% castings
- Worm tea: dilute 1:10, drench roots
- Small amounts go a long way
