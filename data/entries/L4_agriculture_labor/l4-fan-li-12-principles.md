# 12 Polyculture Principles

## Principles 1-3: Design

- 1. Stack functions: each element serves
  multiple purposes
- 2. Use edges: bank, shore, fence line
  are most productive zones
- 3. Small and slow: small ponds managed
  well beat large ponds neglected

## Principles 4-6

- 4. Use diversity: monoculture fails,
  polyculture endures
- 5. Each niche filled: no food source
  wasted - stock the layer that uses it
- 6. Integrate animals: animals do work
  and produce fertility simultaneously

## Principles 7-9: Flow

- 7. Catch and store: hold water, seeds,
  animals, fertility on-site
- 8. No waste: every output is input for
  another part of the system
- 9. Cycle nutrients: what leaves the pond
  must return to the pond

## Principles 10-12

- 10. Observe and interact: visit daily,
  systems speak if you listen
- 11. Work with seasons: do not fight cold
  or drought, design around them
- 12. Begin small: one pond, one rice paddy,
  one flock - master before expanding

## Application

- Start with principles 1, 4, 8 first
- Design the system on paper before digging
- Identify your available inputs and wastes
- Map where water flows naturally
- Stock layers that match your food wastes
- Expand only when each stage is working

## Why They Last

- Not tied to any specific technology
- Work at household or village scale
- Apply in any climate with adaptation
- Each principle is independently valid
- Together they create resilient systems
- 2,500 years of proven results

## Common Violations

- Principle 4 broken: single fish species
- Principle 8 broken: dump waste offsite
- Principle 3 broken: over-expand too fast
- Principle 10 broken: ignore pond signals
- Principle 12 broken: start with 10 systems
