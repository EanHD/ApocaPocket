# Beekeeping Basics

## Why Keep Bees

- Honey: high-calorie sweetener, antibacterial
- Beeswax: candles, waterproofing, salves
- Pollination: increases all crop yields
  by 20-30% in nearby garden
- One hive produces 10-30kg honey/year
- Low labor: 1-2 hours per week in summer

## Hive Types

- Langstroth: industry standard, removable frames
- Warre: simpler, minimal intervention
- Top-bar: horizontal, cheapest to build
- Skep: woven straw dome, traditional
- Log hive: hollow log, most natural
- For beginners: top-bar or Warre recommended

## Capturing a Swarm

- Spring swarms are free bees
- Swarm hangs in ball on branch for hours
- Place box below swarm, shake branch
- Queen usually falls into box with bees
- Close entrance at dusk, move at night
- Open entrance at new location next morning

## Basic Equipment

- Veil and gloves minimum protection
- Smoker: wet burlap or pine needles
- Hive tool: pry bar for frames
- Spray bottle with water or sugar syrup
- Observation window ideal for beginners

## Seasonal Management

- Spring: check for queen, room to expand
- Summer: add space as population grows
- Late summer: assess honey stores
- Fall: leave 15-20kg honey for winter
- Winter: do not open below 10C
- Spring: first check in February-March

## Honey Harvest

- Harvest only surplus above winter stores
- Uncap comb with hot knife
- Extract by spinning (extractor) or crush
- Filter through cloth to remove wax
- Store in sealed jars, does not spoil
- Never harvest all: minimum 15kg must remain

## Common Problems

- Varroa mite: treat with oxalic acid vapor
- American foulbrood: serious, notify authorities
- Swarm prevention: add space in spring
- Queenless hive: no eggs for 2+ weeks
