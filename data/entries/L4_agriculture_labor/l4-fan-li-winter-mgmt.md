# Polyculture Winter Mgmt

## Overview

- Fish metabolism slows when water cools
- Below 10C: stop feeding entirely
- Fish survive on body reserves in winter
- Deep zone (1.5-2m) stays above freezing
- Oxygen exchange through ice must continue
- Correct fall preparation prevents losses

## Fall Preparation

- Harvest all marketable fish in late autumn
- Leave only breeders and juveniles
- Feed heavily in September-October to
  build fish body condition
- Remove excess aquatic vegetation
- Check and repair all bank sections
- Clear inlet/outlet screens of debris

## Winter Stocking

- Reduce stocking density by 50% for winter
- Overcrowding + low oxygen = mass die-off
- Keep only next year's brood stock
- Common carp most cold-tolerant species
- Goldfish and crucian survive near freezing
- Tilapia cannot survive below 10C - harvest

## Ice Management

- Allow ice to form naturally on surface
- Insulates water below from extreme cold
- Maintain one small hole for gas exchange
- Tap gently with stick, do not smash
- Never use salt in freshwater fish pond
- Snow on ice is good insulation - leave it

## Oxygen in Winter

- Decomposition continues under ice
- Consumes oxygen from sealed water
- If pond is small (<500 sq m) watch closely
- Fish gasping at ice hole = oxygen crisis
- Emergency: add fresh water through hole
- Aerator or waterwheel in one area helps

## Spring Transition

- Resume feeding when water reaches 10C
- Start with small amounts, increase slowly
- Overfeeding in cold water fouls pond
- Add new fingerlings after water reaches 15C
- First feed: rice bran or duckweed
- Inspect all fish for disease as ice melts

## Records to Keep

- Date of ice formation and melt
- Any fish losses and likely cause
- Water clarity before and after winter
- First feeding date each spring
- Weight of fish harvested in fall
- These patterns guide next year's plan
