# Seed Viability Test

## Why Test Seeds

- Old or improperly stored seeds may fail
- Find out before planting whole bed
- Adjust seeding rate based on results
- Identify seeds to replace this year
- Prevent wasted bed space and time
- Takes 7-10 days to get clear answer

## The Rag Doll Test

- Count out exactly 10 seeds
- Wet a cloth or paper towel thoroughly
- Lay seeds on half, fold other half over
- Roll loosely, put in warm place (20-25C)
- Check after 5, 7, and 10 days
- Count sprouted seeds = germination %

## Reading Results

- 9-10 germinated: excellent (90-100%)
- 7-8 germinated: good (sow normally)
- 5-6 germinated: acceptable (sow twice
  as densely to compensate)
- 3-4 germinated: poor (sow very densely
  or source fresh seed)
- 0-2 germinated: discard and replace

## Seed Storage Life

- Onion: 1-2 years (save fresh each year)
- Parsnip: 1-2 years (always save fresh)
- Leek, carrot: 2-3 years
- Corn, pepper, tomato: 3-5 years
- Bean, pea, beet: 4-6 years
- Squash, cucumber, melon: 5-8 years

## Extending Viability

- Every 1% moisture reduction doubles life
- Every 10F temperature reduction doubles life
- Seeds at 4C, 6% moisture last 50+ years
- Home: silica gel + sealed jar + fridge
- Freezer: viable for decades if very dry
- Never store seeds in warm, damp locations

## Float Test

- Pour seeds into bowl of water
- Floaters: often hollow or dead
- Sinkers: usually viable
- Not reliable for all species
- Best for peas, beans, corn
- Always confirm with rag doll test

## Old Seed Prep

- Soak large seeds (beans, peas) 8-12 hrs
- Speeds germination of viable seeds
- Discard any seeds that float after soak
- Plant in warm soil for best germination
- Mark beds planted with old seed
  in case replanting needed
