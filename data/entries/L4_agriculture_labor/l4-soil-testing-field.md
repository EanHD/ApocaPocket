# Field Soil Testing

## Why Test

- Soil type determines water retention
- pH determines nutrient availability
- Drainage predicts root rot risk
- Organic matter shows fertility baseline
- 30 minutes of testing saves years of failure
- Match crop to soil, not soil to dream crop

## Jar Test

- Fill jar 1/3 with soil, fill with water
- Shake vigorously, let settle 24 hours
- Sand settles in first 2 minutes (bottom)
- Silt settles within 1 hour (middle)
- Clay remains cloudy for days (top layer)
- Ideal loam: roughly equal visible layers

## Ribbon Test

- Take handful of moist soil
- Squeeze into ribbon between thumb and finger
- Sandy: crumbles, no ribbon forms
- Loam: ribbon 2-4cm before breaking
- Clay: ribbon 5cm+ smooth and plastic
- Clay loam: most crops prefer this range

## Drainage Test

- Dig hole 30cm deep, fill with water
- Return after 15 minutes
- Fast drain: sandy soil, needs organic matter
- Slow drain (still full): clay, needs amendment
- 2-5cm/hr ideal for most vegetable crops
- Standing water = drainage problem, raised beds

## pH Indicators

- Turmeric paper turns red in acid soil
- Red cabbage juice: red=acid, green=alkaline
- Vinegar on soil: fizzing = alkaline (lime)
- Commercial pH strips work fine in water test
- Purple flowers often indicate acid soil
- Healthy earthworms suggest neutral pH

## Compaction Test

- Push wire flag into soil with consistent force
- Stops at hardpan depth
- Less than 15cm: serious compaction problem
- 30cm or more: good rooting depth
- Compacted layers need deep tillage or
  daikon radish as bio-drill

## Quick Fertility Check

- Count earthworms in one shovelful
- 10+ worms: good organic matter
- 0-5 worms: low organic matter
- Smell soil: earthy petrichor = active biology
- Grey color: anaerobic conditions
- Dark brown-black: high organic matter
