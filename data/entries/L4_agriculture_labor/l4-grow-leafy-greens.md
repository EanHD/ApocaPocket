# Leafy Greens

## Why Grow Leafy Greens

- Fastest harvest: 25-45 days from seed
- Highest nutrition density per sq m
- Cut-and-come-again: one plant, many harvests
- Vitamins A, C, K often lacking in survival diet
- Grow in partial shade (unlike most crops)
- Many grow in cool weather when other crops fail

## Best Varieties

- Kale: extremely cold-hardy, multi-year plant
- Chard: tolerates heat and cold, large leaves
- Spinach: fast, but bolts in heat
- Lettuce: fast, many varieties, no cooking needed
- Arugula: fast, spicy, self-seeds freely
- Mustard greens: very fast, spicy, cold-hardy

## Planting

- Broadcast seed thinly, rake in 1cm
- Or transplant seedlings 20-30cm apart
- Thin to final spacing; thinnings are edible
- Succession sow every 2-3 weeks
- Spring and fall are peak seasons
- Summer: use heat-tolerant varieties only

## Cut-and-Come-Again

- Cut outer leaves only, leave center intact
- Or cut plant to 5-8cm above ground
- New leaves regrow in 1-2 weeks
- Continue harvesting for months
- Plant produces until heat triggers bolting
- Bolted plant: harvest seeds for next crop

## Managing Bolting

- Bolting = plant sends up flower stalk
- Happens in heat or drought stress
- Harvest before bolting if eating leaves
- Let one plant bolt per variety for seeds
- Remove bolted plants: leaves become bitter
- Replace with heat-tolerant variety in summer

## Year-Round Plan

- Early spring: cold frame or polytunnel starts
- Spring: outdoor direct sow (kale, chard, lettuce)
- Summer: heat-tolerant chard, perpetual spinach
- Fall: kale, arugula, mustard thrive in frost
- Winter: cold frame kale, tunnel spinach
- Overlap plantings to avoid harvest gap

## Nutritional Value

- Kale: highest iron, calcium among vegetables
- Chard: high magnesium, potassium
- Spinach: high iron (oxalates reduce absorption)
- All high in vitamins A and C
- Essential for deficiency prevention
