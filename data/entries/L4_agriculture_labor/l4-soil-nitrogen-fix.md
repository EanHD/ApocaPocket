# Nitrogen Fixing Plants

## How It Works

- Rhizobium bacteria live in legume roots
- Bacteria convert N2 gas to plant-usable NH3
- Plant provides sugar, bacteria provide nitrogen
- Root nodules visible as small white/pink bumps
- Pink nodules = active nitrogen fixation
- Grey/brown nodules = inactive, no fixing

## Annual Nitrogen Fixers

- Crimson clover: 100-200 kg N/ha per year
- Hairy vetch: aggressive, high N output
- Field peas: edible and nitrogen source
- Soybeans: dual-purpose food + nitrogen
- Fava beans: cold-tolerant, high N
- Lentils: dry-climate tolerant

## Perennial Fixers

- White clover: living mulch under fruit trees
- Alfalfa: deep roots, very high N output
- Comfrey: not a legume but high N leaves
  (dynamic accumulator)
- Black locust tree: 50-100 kg N/ha/yr
- Alder tree: grows in wet areas, fixes N
- Autumn olive: pioneer plant, nitrogen tree

## Inoculation

- Rhizobium bacteria may not be in local soil
- Inoculant packets available (cheap)
- Coat seeds with inoculant before planting
- Or mix inoculant in planting hole
- Critical for clover, vetch in new gardens
- Established gardens usually have bacteria

## When to Incorporate

- For green manure: till under at bud stage
- Maximum N before seed is set
- After flowering: N locked in seed, not soil
- Chop and drop: cut and leave as mulch
- Roots also release N as they decompose
- No-till: roll-crimp at flowering stage

## Companion for N

- Plant nitrogen fixers between heavy feeders
- Clover under corn fixes while corn grows
- Beans adjacent to brassicas works well
- Alder windbreak fixes N for whole garden
- Integrate into food forest understory
- Clover lawn: self-fertilizing grass area

## Deficiency Signs

- Pale yellow-green lower leaves first
- Stunted growth across planting
- Solution: add legume green manure or
  apply compost with high N content
