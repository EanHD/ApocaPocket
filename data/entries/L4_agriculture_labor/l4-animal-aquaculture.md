# Freshwater Aquaculture

## Species Selection

- Common carp: most adaptable, any climate
- Tilapia: warm only (>15C), fast growing
  Cannot survive below 10C
- Catfish: warm climate, excellent flavor
  Tolerates low oxygen well
- Trout: cold water only (<18C preferred)
  High value, delicate management
- Bluegill + bass: native N. American combo

## Tank vs Pond

- Pond: lower labor, natural feed inputs
  Lower stocking density
- Tank (IBC or concrete): higher control
  Higher density, must provide all feed
- Recirculating (RAS): most efficient but
  requires pump and filter (power-dependent)
- For off-grid: pond system preferred

## Stocking Density

- Carp pond: 2-5 fish per 10 sq m
- Tilapia tank: 1 fish per 50 liters
- Catfish pond: 3-6 fish per 10 sq m
- Trout: cold water requires more space
- Overcrowding: most common mistake
  Oxygen runs out, fish die overnight

## Feeding

- Ponds: natural food + supplemental
- Rice bran, duckweed, garden waste
- Commercial fish pellets if available
- Feed once daily, only what eaten in 20min
- Leftover feed rots, depletes oxygen
- Fan Li: silkworm frass, kitchen waste

## Water Quality Basics

- Dissolved oxygen: minimum 5mg/L
- Signs of low O2: fish surfacing, gasping
- pH: 6.5-8.5 acceptable range
- Clear water: healthy (see 30cm down)
- Green algae bloom: too much nutrient input
- Foul smell: anaerobic, needs aeration

## Harvest Methods

- Seine net across pond (most effective)
- Hook and line for selective harvest
- Trap net at outlet when draining
- Drain to refuge trench, harvest by hand
- Leave 20-30% breeders after harvest
- Spring restocking from own breeders

## Preserving Fish

- Salt-cure or smoke within hours of harvest
- Hot-smoke at 75C+ for 2-4 hours
- Properly smoked fish keeps 2-4 weeks
- Dried fish: lasts months without salt
