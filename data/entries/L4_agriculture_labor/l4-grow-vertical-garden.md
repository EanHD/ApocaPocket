# Vertical Gardening

## Why Go Vertical

- Triple or quadruple yield per sq m
- Better air circulation reduces disease
- Easier harvest (no bending)
- Sun penetrates to lower plants
- Shade created below for heat-sensitive crops
- Uses fence lines and walls productively

## Best Vertical Crops

- Pole beans: very productive, 2-3m climbers
- Climbing peas: early season, prolific
- Cucumbers: heavy yield, needs strong trellis
- Small squash: butternut, delicata work
- Indeterminate tomatoes: need sturdy support
- Melons: small varieties with fruit support

## Trellis Construction

- Simplest: two posts, horizontal wires 20cm apart
- String trellis: posts + vertical twine
- Cattle panel: near-permanent, very strong
- Bamboo A-frame: lash with cord or vine
- Fence line: use existing structures
- Height: 1.8-2.5m for most climbers

## Training Techniques

- Tie main stem loosely to support
- Use soft ties (strips of cloth, not wire)
- Check and retie weekly as plant grows
- Pinch side shoots on cucumbers
  (keeps plant manageable)
- Cucumbers and squash: train to single stem
- Beans and peas: self-attach with tendrils

## Supporting Heavy Fruit

- Melons, large squash need hammock support
- Cut fabric strip, form cradle under fruit
- Tie cradle to trellis so fruit hangs
- Check daily as fruit grows heavier
- Do not let fruit pull vine off trellis

## Under-Trellis Planting

- Shade below trellis usable for cool crops
- Lettuce and spinach thrive in trellis shade
- Summer: shade from trellis extends season
- Companion plant below: basil, flowers
- Effective doubling or tripling of production

## End-of-Season Care

- Annual trellises: remove, compost plant waste
- Permanent: inspect and repair each spring
- Store bamboo poles dry to prevent rot
- Wire trellises last many years
