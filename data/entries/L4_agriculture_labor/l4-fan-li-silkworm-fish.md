# Silkworm-Fish System

## Overview

- Pearl River Delta system, refined c. 500 BC
- Mulberry trees planted on pond embankments
- Mulberry leaves fed to silkworms
- Silkworm frass and pupae fed to fish
- Pond mud dredged annually, spread on berms
- Mud fertilizes mulberry trees
- Complete closed-loop system, zero waste

## The Four-Stage Cycle

- Stage 1: Mulberry grows on pond bank
- Stage 2: Leaves feed silkworms in shed
- Stage 3: Frass, dead worms, pupae to pond
- Stage 4: Pond mud dredged back to bank
- Cycle repeats annually, building fertility
- System produces silk, fish, and food crops

## Mulberry Cultivation

- Plant mulberry on pond banks and berms
- Roots stabilize bank, prevent erosion
- Space 2-3m apart along bank edges
- Prune heavily each spring for leaf yield
- Harvest leaves 3-4 times per season
- Morus alba (white mulberry) preferred

## Silkworm Rearing

- Bombyx mori: domesticated silk moth
- Rear in dry shed near pond for convenience
- Hatch eggs in spring when mulberry leafs out
- Feed fresh leaves 4-6x daily
- 4-6 week cycle from egg to cocoon
- Each batch produces frass, pupae, dead worms

## Feeding Fish

- Silkworm frass = high nitrogen fish feed
- Dead worms and failed pupae fed directly
- After silk reeling, dump spent pupae in pond
- Fresh mulberry trimmings also fed to fish
- Grass carp love mulberry leaves
- Carp eat frass and pupae enthusiastically

## Pond Mud Management

- Dredge pond bottom each winter
- Accumulated mud = high-fertility compost
- Apply to mulberry bank 10-15cm thick
- Improves mulberry vigor significantly
- Deepens pond each year (adds capacity)
- Also apply to vegetable beds nearby

## Economic Output

- Silk thread from cocoons (trade value)
- Fish harvest from pond
- Mulberry fruit edible (bonus harvest)
- Compost for additional crop beds
- One hectare supports a family with surplus
