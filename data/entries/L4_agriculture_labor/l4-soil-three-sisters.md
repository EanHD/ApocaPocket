# Three Sisters Garden

## Overview

- Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) origin, 1000+ years
- Corn, beans, squash are the Three Sisters
- Each supports the other two
- Self-fertilizing, weed-suppressing system
- No external inputs needed after setup
- Works on any well-drained, sunny site

## Why It Works

- Corn: tall structure for beans to climb
- Beans: fix nitrogen fed to corn/squash
- Squash: large leaves shade ground,
  block weeds, retain moisture
- Squash prickly leaves deter pests
- Three different root depths, no competition
- Three canopy levels, full light use

## Site Preparation

- Full sun location minimum 6 hours
- Well-drained soil, not waterlogged
- Amend with compost before planting
- Mound soil 30cm high, 60cm diameter
- Space mounds 1 meter apart in grid
- Water well before planting

## Planting Sequence

- Week 1: plant corn in center of mound
  (4-6 seeds per mound, thin to 2-3)
- Week 2-3: corn sprouted and 15cm tall
- Week 3: plant beans around corn
  (4-6 bean seeds 10cm from corn)
- Week 4: plant squash on mound edges
  (2-3 seeds, 30cm from center)
- Do not plant all at same time

## Varieties to Use

- Corn: flint corn, dent corn, or sweet corn
- Beans: climbing pole beans only
  (not bush beans - cannot climb)
- Squash: any large squash or pumpkin
- Traditionally: native heritage varieties
- Save seed from each crop each year

## Maintenance

- Water at base of mounds, not overhead
- Hand-weed only until squash spreads
- Once squash canopy covers: weeding done
- Corn pollinates in blocks (plant 4+ rows)
- Single row of corn: poor pollination

## Harvest and Soil Care

- Harvest beans fresh or let dry on vine
- Harvest squash before hard frost
- Leave corn stover and squash vines
- Chop and incorporate for green manure
- Bean roots: leave in soil, release nitrogen
- Rotate mound locations next season
