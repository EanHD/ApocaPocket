# Composting

## Green vs Brown Ratio

Target 30:1 carbon-to-nitrogen by volume.
Use 3 parts browns to 1 part greens.

Browns (carbon): dry leaves, straw, shredded cardboard,
sawdust, corn stalks. Dry, pale, crunchy.

Greens (nitrogen): food scraps, fresh grass clippings,
coffee grounds, fresh manure, fresh plant trimmings. Wet, soft.

Squeeze test: pile should feel like a wrung-out sponge.
Too dry: add water while turning.
Too wet: add more browns.

## Turning Schedule

Hot composting requires oxygen. Turn to restart heat.
- Day 3-5: first turn when temperature drops from peak
- Day 7-10: second turn
- Day 14-17: third turn
- Weekly turns after that until pile stops reheating

Technique: move outer material to center, break up clumps,
check moisture, rebuild compactly.

Minimum pile size for heat: 3 x 3 x 3 feet (1 cubic yard).
Smaller piles will not retain enough heat.

## Troubleshooting

Pile will not heat:
- Too small: add more material to reach minimum size
- Too dry: add water while turning
- Wrong ratio: add nitrogen (fresh greens or manure)

Smells like ammonia: too much nitrogen.
Add dry browns and turn the pile.

Smells rotten: too wet and anaerobic.
Add dry browns, turn pile, cover from rain.

Attracts pests: food is exposed on surface.
Bury food scraps in center, cover with thick layer of browns.

## Compost Timeline

Hot compost (turning weekly): 3-6 months.
Cold compost (low effort, no turning): 12-24 months.

Finished compost looks like:
- Dark brown to black, crumbly like coffee grounds
- Earthy forest smell, no heat remaining in center
- Original materials no longer recognizable

Still seeing recognizable chunks: not done. Keep turning.

## What NOT to Compost

Never add to any compost pile:
- Meat, fish, bones, dairy, cooking oils (rodents, pests)
- Human or pet waste (pathogens survive cold compost)
- Diseased plants (disease spreads back to garden)
- Weeds with mature seeds (unless hot pile confirms 140F+)
- Treated lumber, glossy or coated paper (chemicals)
- Coal ash (toxic heavy metals)

Fresh chicken or pig manure: compost separately for 6 months
before applying to food-producing beds.
