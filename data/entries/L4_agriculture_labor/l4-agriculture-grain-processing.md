# Grain Processing

**Field -> flour without electricity. 1 bushel wheat (60 lb) -> ~50 lb flour -> ~400 loaves bread.**

## Threshing

Goal: break grain kernels free from stalks. Dry grain to <12% moisture first.

- Flail: beat dried stalks on hard surface with flail (4-5 ft handle + 2-3 ft striker on 6 in rope/leather); 30-60 min/bushel
- Tarp/sack: stuff grain in pillowcase, beat against hard surface or stomp; 45-90 min/bushel; gentler on grain
- Hand rubbing: rub seed heads between palms over bucket; 2-3 hr/bushel; no tools, best for small batches
- Vehicle (large scale): drive over grain on clean tarp 15-20 passes; 15-30 min; food-grade tarp, clean dry tires only

## Winnowing

Goal: remove chaff (light husks) from grain (heavy kernels) using wind.

- Stand with wind perpendicular; pour grain slowly from 3-5 ft height into downwind container
- Wind carries chaff away; grain falls; repeat 3-5 times until clean
- Ideal wind: 5-10 mph. Indoor: fan on low setting across airstream
- Completion test: blow handful  -  chaff flies away = done
- Normal yield loss: 10-20% by weight

## Milling

Hand-crank stone mill: 1-2 lb flour per 10-15 min; adjustable gap; $50-300 new.
Pedal-powered mill: 3-5x faster than hand crank; ~1 lb per 3-5 min.
Mortar and pestle: 1-2 cups/hr; emergency only, exhausting.

Gap settings: wide 1-2 mm = cracked grain | medium 0.5-1 mm = coarse flour | narrow 0.2-0.5 mm = fine flour.
Multi-pass (wide -> medium -> narrow): easier on mill, more consistent, less heat buildup.
Pause if stones too hot to touch  -  overheating damages flour nutrients.
Sift with fine mesh: keep everything (whole grain, most nutritious) or sift bran for white flour (longer storage).

Emergency DIY mill: two flat granite/river rocks 8-12 in diameter; center hole in top stone for dowel pivot; handle attached. Slow but functional.

## Grain Types

- Wheat: easy thresh/winnow; 1 lb -> 3-4 cups flour; hard red = bread, soft white = pastry, durum = pasta
- Oats: de-hull required  -  light pound then re-winnow; 1 lb -> 3.5 cups; oatmeal, baking
- Rye: easy thresh/winnow/mill; 1 lb -> 4 cups; **inspect for ergot  -  discard any purple/black kernels (TOXIC)**
- Corn: twist dried cobs to release kernels; heavy-duty mill needed; nixtamalize (lye/lime soak) for masa/tortillas
- Quinoa: hand-rub thresh; **rinse 3-5x until no foam to remove saponins (bitter, toxic in quantity)**
- Amaranth: beat seed heads over container (tiny seeds); cook whole or pop; high protein, drought-tolerant
- Buckwheat: easy thresh; de-hull with light pounding; cook as groats or mill to flour; grows in 60-90 days

## Flour Storage

- Whole grain (unmilled): 10+ years at 50-60 degF, <10% moisture, dark, airtight (mylar bags + O2 absorbers)
- Freshly milled: 1-3 months room temp; 6-12 months refrigerated
- Best practice: store whole grain, mill as needed (maximum nutrition + shelf life)
- Spoilage: rancid/oily smell, bitter taste, mold, or insect infestation -> discard

## Safety Warnings

- **Ergot (rye/wheat)**: purple/black kernels = TOXIC. Causes convulsions, gangrene, death. Inspect every batch.
- **Quinoa saponins**: rinse until zero foam or causes GI illness in quantity
- Flour dust explosion risk in enclosed spaces  -  mill outdoors or ventilate well; no open flames
- Repetitive strain: break every 20-30 min, alternate hands when hand-cranking

## Common Mistakes

- Threshing damp grain  -  must dry to <12% moisture first or mold and poor separation
- Storing milled flour long-term  -  store whole grain and mill as needed
- Milling too fast  -  overheating damages nutrients; slow steady pace
- Single-pass milling  -  multiple passes produce finer flour with less effort and heat
- Using tap water for quinoa rinse  -  rinse until water runs clear with no foam
