# Soil pH Adjustment

## Why pH Matters

- pH scale 1-14: 7 is neutral
- Below 7: acidic; above 7: alkaline
- pH controls nutrient availability
- pH 6.0-7.0: most nutrients available
- Very acid or alkaline locks out nutrients
- Even fertile soil fails at wrong pH

## pH by Crop

- Most vegetables: 6.0-7.0
- Blueberries: 4.5-5.5 (very acid)
- Potatoes: 5.0-6.0 (slightly acid)
- Legumes: 6.0-7.5
- Brassicas: 6.5-7.5
- Corn, grains: 6.0-7.0

## Raising pH

- Use agricultural lime (calcium carbonate)
- Rate: 2-3 kg per 10 sq m for slight acid
- Rate: 5 kg per 10 sq m for very acid
- Mix into top 15cm of soil
- Results take 3-6 months to show
- Wood ash also raises pH (use 0.5kg/sq m)

## Lowering pH

- Use elemental sulfur (slow, stable)
- Bacteria convert sulfur to sulfuric acid
- Rate: 0.5-1 kg per 10 sq m per point
- Takes 6-12 months for full effect
- Aluminum sulfate works faster (months)
  but damages soil biology over time
- Pine needle mulch slowly acidifies

## Post-Treatment Test

- Wait 3-6 months after lime application
- Test again before planting
- Adjust again if needed (go slow)
- Better to under-treat and retreat than
  over-treat and have hard-to-fix problem
- Keep records of treatments and results

## Natural Indicators

- Healthy blueberries/azaleas = acid soil
- Good clover growth = near-neutral
- Abundant earthworms = 6.0-7.0 range
- Stunted legumes = too acid (below 5.5)
- Yellow leaves despite fertilizer = check pH

## Emergency Fixes

- Wood ash: raises pH, adds potassium
- Compost: buffers both acid and alkaline
- Limestone gravel in soil: slow release
- Vinegar drench: temporarily lowers pH
- Coffee grounds: mild acidifier (compost)
