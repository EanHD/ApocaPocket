# No-Till Growing

## Why Avoid Tilling

- Tilling destroys fungal networks in soil
- Exposes weed seeds buried below surface
- Breaks up soil aggregates (structure)
- Accelerates carbon loss from soil
- Creates hardpan below till depth
- Disturbs earthworm tunnels and habitat

## Core No-Till Method

- Apply 5-10cm compost to surface each year
- Do not dig or incorporate it
- Worms and organisms do the mixing
- Plant through compost into undisturbed soil
- Weeds managed by surface hoeing only
- Soil improves continuously without digging

## Sheet Mulch Transition

- Smother existing weeds with cardboard
- Overlap cardboard edges to seal gaps
- Wet cardboard thoroughly first
- Layer 10-15cm compost on top
- Can plant immediately in compost layer
- By second year, soil underneath is ready

## Raised Bed No-Till

- Build raised bed on top of existing soil
- Do not dig or remove existing vegetation
- Layer cardboard, then soil/compost mix
- Bed depth 30cm minimum
- Undisturbed soil below activates slowly
- After 2 years: rich worm activity below

## No-Dig Weed Control

- Hoe shallowly to sever weed roots
- Do not dig up and expose new seeds
- Thick mulch suppresses most weeds
- Hand-pull perennial weeds at root
- Never let weeds set seed
- Persistent weeds: smother with cardboard

## No-Till Cover Crops

- Roll-crimp cover crops at flowering stage
- Leaves flat, mulch layer forms in place
- Plant cash crops through rolled mulch
- No-till drill or dibble stick for planting
- Crimped cover crop decays as weed mulch
- Roots decompose adding organic matter

## Results to Expect

- Year 1: similar yield, easier weeding
- Year 2: soil structure noticeably better
- Year 3: earthworm count doubles
- Year 5+: self-maintaining system
- Watering needs decrease each year
- Soil never crusts or compacts
