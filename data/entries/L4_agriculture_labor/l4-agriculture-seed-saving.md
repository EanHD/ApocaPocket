# Seed Save

## Open-Pollinated Only

Only save seeds from open-pollinated (OP) or heirloom plants.
Hybrid (F1) seeds do not breed true. Offspring revert to
unpredictable parent traits. Do not save them.

GMO seeds may be sterile or legally restricted. Do not save.

Identify by: seed packet labeled OP, heirloom, or variety
name in use for 50+ years. If unlabeled hybrid: discard.

## Dry Processing

For: beans, peas, lettuce, brassicas, herbs.
- Let pods dry fully on the plant until brown and brittle
- Harvest before pods shatter open
- Spread indoors on screen or paper for 1-2 more weeks
- Thresh by rubbing or shaking to release seeds
- Winnow: pour between containers in breeze, chaff blows off
- Dry one more week before storage

Pods should rattle when shaken before harvest.

## Wet Processing

For: tomatoes, cucumbers, melons, squash.
- Pick fully ripe fruit from the healthiest plants
- Scoop seeds and pulp into a jar with equal water
- Label jar with variety name
- Ferment 2-4 days at 70-75F, bubbles form, film on surface
- Add water: good seeds sink, floaters are bad, discard
- Rinse seeds in fine strainer
- Dry on wax paper, stirring daily
- Ready when seeds snap when bent, not flex

## Storage Conditions

Temperature: 32-41F. Refrigerator ideal. Freezer for long-term.
Humidity: below 10 percent. Use silica gel packets.
Light: complete darkness.
Container: airtight glass jars or mylar bags.

Seed viability by crop (room temp vs cold storage):
- Beans, peas: 3-4 years / 10+ years
- Tomatoes, peppers: 4-5 years / 10+ years
- Lettuce: 1-3 years / 5-7 years
- Squash, cucumber: 5-6 years / 10+ years
- Onions: 1-2 years / 3-5 years
- Corn: 2-3 years / 10+ years

Replace silica gel packets when they turn pink.

## Viability Testing

Test before committing seeds to planting:
- Place 10-20 seeds on a damp paper towel
- Fold and seal in a plastic bag
- Keep at 65-75F for 7-10 days
- Count sprouted seeds at day 10

Results:
- Over 80 percent: excellent, plant at normal density
- 60-80 percent: acceptable, plant 25 percent more densely
- Under 60 percent: replace the stock

Test annually for critical crops.

## Labeling

Label every packet before sealing. Minimum required:
- Variety name and crop type
- Date saved
- Germination rate if tested

Store labeled paper envelopes inside the sealed jar.
Keep a separate written log away from the seed storage.
If the container is lost, the log survives.
