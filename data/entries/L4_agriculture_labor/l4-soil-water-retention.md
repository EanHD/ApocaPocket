# Water Retention Methods

## Core Principle

- Slow water, spread water, sink water
- Every drop that leaves your land is wasted
- Landscape stores water better than tanks
- Design water flow before planting
- Observe where water goes in a rainstorm
- Intercept it before it leaves the site

## Swale Design

- Swale = level channel dug on contour
- Runs exactly horizontally across slope
- Water fills channel, spreads evenly
- Soaks in rather than running off
- Downhill berm planted with perennials
- Berm roots access the stored water

## How to Find Contour

- A-frame level: two equal legs, central weight
- Walk slope, mark level points with stakes
- Connect stakes: that is your contour line
- Water level on a hose: check across slope
- Clinometer: simple angle measurement
- Smartphone level apps work adequately

## Keyline Plowing

- Developed by P.A. Yeomans (Australia)
- Key line: contour line at valley head
- Plow just above keyline across ridges
- Water diverted from valley to ridge
- Distributes water to driest areas
- Requires only subsoil plow to implement

## Berms and Swales

- Swale on uphill side catches water
- Berm on downhill side holds it
- Plant trees/shrubs on berm immediately
- Berm plants stabilize structure
- Space swale-berm pairs every 5-10m
- Stack multiple swales on longer slopes

## Mulch and Ground Cover

- 10cm mulch holds 2-3cm of rain
- Prevents surface crusting (increases infiltration)
- Reduces evaporation by 50-70%
- Living plants: roots open soil channels
- Each 1% more organic matter holds
  170,000 liters extra water per hectare

## Small-Scale Techniques

- Sunken beds (sunken below grade): hold rain
- Ollas (clay pots): slow subsurface release
- Hugelkultur: buried wood holds water
- Dig hole 1m deep, fill with logs and brush
- Cover with soil: logs absorb and release
  water slowly over 5-10 years
