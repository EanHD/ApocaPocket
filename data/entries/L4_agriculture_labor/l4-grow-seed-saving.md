# Seed Saving Basics

## OP vs Hybrid

- Open-pollinated (OP): breeds true from seed
- Heirloom: OP variety >50 years old
- Hybrid (F1): bred from two parents
  F1 seeds produce inconsistent offspring
- Save seed ONLY from OP/heirloom varieties
- Label varieties clearly to avoid mixing

## Easiest Seeds to Save

- Tomatoes: self-pollinating, easy
- Beans and peas: self-pollinating, very easy
- Lettuce: self-pollinating, easy
- Peppers: usually self-pollinating
- Squash/cucumbers: insect-pollinated,
  need isolation or hand pollination
- Brassicas: cross-pollinate easily,
  need 500m+ isolation

## Selection Principles

- Save from healthiest, most productive plants
- Save from plants that matured first
  (adapts crop to your local season)
- Never save from weakest plant
- Mark best plants early with ribbon/stake
- Maintain 6+ plants per variety to preserve
  genetic diversity in the population

## Harvest and Dry Seeds

- Harvest seeds when fully mature on plant
- Beans/peas: leave until pods turn brown
- Tomatoes: fruit overripe, seeds ferment
- Lettuce: stalks dry, seeds visible
- Dry seeds in warm, ventilated place
- Spread thinly on paper, not plastic

## Tomato Fermentation

- Squeeze tomato seeds into water
- Allow to ferment 2-3 days
- Good seeds sink, bad seeds/gel float
- Pour off floating material
- Rinse remaining seeds well
- Dry on non-stick surface (not paper towel)

## Storage Conditions

- Dry completely before storage (critical)
- Store in paper envelope inside sealed jar
- Add silica gel packet to absorb moisture
- Store in cool, dark location (below 15C)
- Freezer: seeds last decades if very dry
- Label with variety, date, location grown

## Pre-Plant Test

- See seed viability entry
- Count on 50%+ germination from stored seeds
- Increase seeding rate if old seed
- Refresh stock every 3-5 years for best results
