# Mulching Methods

## Why Mulch

- Reduces water loss by 50-70%
- Suppresses 80-90% of annual weeds
- Moderates soil temperature extremes
- Feeds soil organisms as it breaks down
- Protects soil from compaction by rain
- Foundation of no-dig and no-till systems

## Straw Mulch

- Best for annual vegetable gardens
- Apply 10-15cm depth around plants
- Breaks down in one season
- Free from grain fields and threshing
- Avoid hay (contains seeds)
- Pull back in spring to warm soil fast

## Wood Chip Mulch

- Best for perennials, trees, paths
- Apply 10-15cm depth
- Takes 2-3 years to fully break down
- Creates excellent soil biology habitat
- From tree trimming operations often free
- Not ideal directly under annual crops

## Leaf Mold

- Shred fallen leaves, pile for 1-2 years
- Excellent weed-free, fine-textured mulch
- High in trace minerals from deep roots
- Best mulch for seedbeds and transplants
- Free and available everywhere in autumn
- Mix with compost for ideal seedbed mix

## Living Mulch

- Low-growing plants as ground cover
- Clover: fixes nitrogen, feeds bees
- Creeping thyme: aromatic, edible
- Strawberry: edible living mulch
- Space main crop to allow living mulch
- Mow or trim to manage competition

## Sheet Mulch

- Lay cardboard directly on weeds
- Overlap edges 15cm, no gaps
- Layer 15-20cm compost on top
- Plant directly in compost layer
- Cardboard smothers existing weeds
- Decomposes into soil over one year

## Seasonal Timing

- Spring: pull back mulch to warm soil
- Late spring: re-apply after planting
- Summer: maintain depth, add if needed
- Fall: apply heavy layer before frost
- Perennial beds: mulch all year round
