# Soil Health Indicators

## Earthworm Count

- Dig one cubic foot (30x30x30cm) of soil
- Count all earthworms found
- Less than 5: poor soil health
- 5-10: moderate health
- 10+: good health
- 20+: excellent, thriving ecosystem
- Do this test in moist soil, not dry

## Soil Smell

- Healthy soil smells earthy and sweet
- Petrichor smell = geosmin from bacteria
- That smell = active Streptomyces bacteria
- Sour or sulfur smell = anaerobic (waterlogged)
- Ammonia smell = fresh manure, nitrogen rich
- No smell at all = low biology, depleted

## Soil Structure

- Squeeze moist soil in fist, release
- Healthy: crumbles into aggregates
- Unhealthy: falls as powder (no structure)
- Or stays solid lump (compacted clay)
- Aggregates = soil particles glued by fungi
- Good aggregates hold air and water together

## Soil Color

- Dark brown to black = high organic matter
- Rich red = iron-rich, often well-drained
- Pale tan/grey = low organic matter
- Mottled grey-orange = seasonal waterlogging
- Green algae on surface = compacted, wet
- Bright white patches = salt accumulation

## Plant Indicators

- Plantain, dock: compacted, poorly drained
- Nettles: high nitrogen, disturbed soil
- Dandelion: low calcium, compacted
- Clover growing well: neutral pH
- Bracken fern: acidic soil
- Chamomile: slightly acid, good drainage

## Infiltration Test

- Pour 1 liter water onto soil surface
- Measure time to absorb completely
- Less than 30 seconds: excellent drainage
- 1-5 minutes: good to moderate
- Over 10 minutes: compaction or drainage issue
- Standing water after 30 min: serious problem

## Stability Test

- Drop clod of dry soil into water
- Watch what happens over 5 minutes
- Good soil: holds shape, settles slowly
- Poor soil: explodes, disperses in water
- Dispersal = low organic matter or
  sodium-dominant soil
