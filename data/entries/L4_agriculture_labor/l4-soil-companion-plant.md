# Companion Planting

## How It Works

- Plants emit volatile compounds that repel pests
- Strong-scented herbs mask crop odors
- Flowers attract predatory insects
- Legumes fix nitrogen for neighbors
- Tall plants shade low heat-sensitive crops
- Root depths differ: share soil layers

## Classic Combinations

- Tomato + basil: basil repels aphids and whitefly
- Brassica + dill: dill attracts parasitic wasps
- Carrot + onion: carrot fly/onion fly confused
- Rose + garlic: garlic repels aphids
- Bean + squash: squash shades weed competition
- Corn + beans: beans fix nitrogen for corn

## Three Sisters

- Corn, beans, squash together
- Corn: trellis for beans
- Beans: fix nitrogen for corn
- Squash: living mulch, spiny leaves deter pests
- Inter-plant at specific spacings
- Native American polyculture system

## Pest-Repelling Plants

- Marigolds: repel nematodes in soil
- Nasturtium: trap crop for aphids
- Wormwood: repels many insects nearby
- Tansy: repels ants, flea beetles
- Lavender: repels moths, attracts bees
- Chives: repel carrot fly, aphids

## Insect Attractors

- Dill, fennel: attract parasitic wasps
- Phacelia: excellent bee plant, fast growing
- Buckwheat: attracts hoverflies (aphid eaters)
- Yarrow: attracts ladybirds (aphid eaters)
- Sweet alyssum: ground-level beneficial habitat
- Any umbellifera family plant works

## Antagonistic Pairs

- Fennel inhibits most vegetables nearby
- Garlic near beans and peas: growth stunted
- Brassicas near strawberries: antagonistic
- Onions near beans: inhibit bean growth
- Dill near carrots: cross-pollination risk

## Planning Beds

- Mix 3-4 species per bed
- Include one nitrogen fixer always
- Include one pest repeller
- Include one beneficial attractor
- Rest of bed: food crops
