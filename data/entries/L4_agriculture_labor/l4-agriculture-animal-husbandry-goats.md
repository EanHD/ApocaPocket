# Goat Husbandry

**Priority: HIGH.** Milk, meat, brush clearing, leather, manure. 3-5 does feeds a family in dairy.

## Why Goats

- 1/10 the size of a cow. Efficient browsers on marginal land other animals ignore.
- 1-2 quarts milk/day from dairy breeds, 10 months/year.
- Fast reproduction: 5-month gestation, does breed at 7-9 months old.
- Multiple products: milk, meat, leather, fiber, and pelleted manure for the garden.
- Thrive on brush, weeds, and brambles. No need for good pasture.

## Best Breeds

**Dairy**
- Nubian: 1-1.5 gal/day, 4-5% fat. Vocal. Best butterfat for cheese and soap.
- Alpine: 1-1.5 gal/day. Hardy and adaptable. Good all-around choice.
- LaMancha: 1-1.5 gal/day. Calm, tiny ears. Very hardy. Beginner-friendly.
- Nigerian Dwarf: 1-2 qts/day, 6-10% fat. Small. Backyard and urban-viable.

**Meat**
- Boer: 200-340 lbs. Fast growth. Purpose-bred for maximum meat yield.
- Kiko: 180-250 lbs. Hardy, parasite-resistant, minimal intervention needed.

**Dual-purpose:** Nubian and Alpine (good milk yield plus decent meat from culled animals).

## Housing

- 15 sq ft per goat indoor minimum. Three-sided structure acceptable. Dry bedding.
- Ventilation critical. No drafts at goat level. Poor airflow causes respiratory disease.
- Separate kidding pen: 10×10 ft, quiet and clean, available at kidding time.

**Fencing — goats escape, this is non-negotiable**
- Woven wire 4-5 ft high. Small mesh openings. Bury 6" or add ground apron.
- Add electric wire on top to stop jumping and climbing.
- Electric fence works if goats are trained to respect it from a young age.
- Never use barbed wire (injury). Chicken wire (goats break through easily).
- Gate latches: Carabiner or chain. Simple hook latches fail.

## Feeding

- Goats are browsers, not grazers. Prefer brush, leaves, weeds, and branches over grass.
- Hay: 2-4 lbs/day. Alfalfa for dairy does. Grass hay for meat/maintenance animals.
- Grain: Only for lactating does, last 2 months of pregnancy, and growing kids.
- Amount: 1/2 to 1 lb grain per 100 lbs body weight when actively milking.
- Minerals: Loose goat-specific minerals free-choice. Do NOT use sheep minerals — blocks copper and is toxic to goats.
- Baking soda free-choice: Prevents bloat. Always available.
- Water: 2-4 gallons/day per goat. They refuse dirty water. Heated in winter.

## Milk Production

- Cycle: Breed in fall → kid in spring → milk 10 months → 2-month dry period → repeat.
- Peak production: 1.5-2 gal/day at 6-8 weeks post-kidding.
- Milk 2x daily exactly 12 hours apart. Consistency is essential for production.
- Strain immediately after milking. Chill within 30 minutes. Prevents "goaty" flavor from bacteria.
- Keep bucks at least 100 ft upwind of milk does at all times. Buck odor taints milk flavor.

**Hand milking:** Wrap thumb and forefinger around top of teat to trap milk. Squeeze down with remaining fingers. 5-10 min per doe.

## Breeding

- Season: Fall (Sep-Dec). Does cycle every 18-21 days. Shorter days trigger estrus.
- Signs of heat: Tail wagging, bleating, pink swollen vulva, decreased appetite, restlessness.
- Buck odor during rut (breeding season) is extremely strong. Separate housing required.
- Gestation: 150 days (5 months).
- Normal presentation: Front hooves plus nose. Call vet if no progress after 30 min of hard labor.
- Colostrum within 1 hour of birth. Critical for kid immune system. Bottle-feed if kid won't nurse.

## Health

**Preventive**
- Hoof trimming every 6-8 weeks. Overgrown hooves cause lameness and foot rot.
- CDT vaccine annually. Pregnant does get booster 1 month before kidding.
- FAMACHA eye check: Pale or white = anemia = heavy parasite load = deworm. Red/pink = healthy.
- Rotational grazing prevents parasite buildup in pasture.

**Common problems**
- Bloat (distended left side, discomfort): Baking soda, walk the goat. Vet if severe.
- Mastitis (hot udder, clots in milk): Antibiotics, frequent stripping. Vet required.
- Urinary stones in bucks: Hay-based diet prevents it. Vet surgery to treat.
- Foot rot (limping, foul odor): Trim hooves, antibiotic spray, zinc sulfate footbath.
- Pregnancy toxemia (lethargy, grinding teeth in late pregnancy): Propylene glycol. Vet urgently.
