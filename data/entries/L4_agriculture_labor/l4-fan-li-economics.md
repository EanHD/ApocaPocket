# Polyculture Economics

## Economic Method

- Feed your household first, trade second
- Polyculture creates multiple income streams
- Fish, silk, rice, vegetables all saleable
- Diversification prevents total income loss
- Fan Li became one of richest men in China
- His wealth came from systems, not labor

## Subsistence Stocking

- 1 adult needs ~50g protein/day from fish
- 200 sq m pond yields ~20 kg fish/year
- Family of 5 needs ~500 sq m of pond
- Plus rice paddy for carbohydrate base
- Plus small animal flock for eggs/dairy
- 2000 sq m total provides food security

## Output Streams

- Fish harvest (primary protein)
- Rice harvest (primary calories)
- Duck eggs and meat (secondary protein)
- Silk from silkworms (trade commodity)
- Mulberry fruit (supplemental food)
- Pond mud compost (value to neighbors)

## Reducing Inputs

- System needs no purchased fertilizer
- No pesticides required
- Feed mostly from on-system waste
- Seeds saved from own harvest
- Breeding stock maintained on-site
- Cash needs drop dramatically each year

## Trading Surplus Wisely

- Fan Li advocated keeping 1 year reserve
- Sell only surplus above reserve
- Trade processed goods (silk > worms)
- Diversify trade partners, not just one
- Store grain and dried fish as currency
- Never sell breeding stock in hard times

## Scaling Up

- Master 200 sq m pond before adding more
- Replicate proven systems, do not reinvent
- Each new pond funds the next
- Hire help for harvest, not management
- Keep records of yields by season
- Compare year-over-year performance

## Wealth Building

- Year 1-2: establish, minimal surplus
- Year 3: first reliable surplus appears
- Year 5: system matures, output peaks
- Year 10: expand or share knowledge
- Teaching the system creates goodwill
- Goodwill is Fan Li's third form of wealth
