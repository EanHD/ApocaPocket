# Pig Keeping Basics

## Why Keep Pigs

- Feed conversion: 3:1 (3kg feed = 1kg gain)
- One pig provides 70-80kg of pork
  from 5-6 months of raising
- Eats food waste, garden scraps, whey
- Excellent land clearing and tilling
- Lard for cooking, skin for leather
- Manure for compost

## Breeds for Small-Scale

- Berkshire: excellent flavor, heritage breed
- Tamworth: excellent forager, hardy
- Ossabaw: very small, forager, fatty
- Gloucestershire Old Spot: orchard pig
- Large White: fast-growing, feed-efficient
- For beginners: Berkshire or Tamworth

## Housing

- Simple three-sided shelter from rain/wind
- 4-6 sq m per pig inside shelter
- 20-40 sq m outdoor area minimum
- Straw bedding, replaced weekly
- Wallow (mud hole): essential for cooling
- Pigs cannot sweat; overheating is fatal

## Fencing

- Pigs are escape artists and root under fences
- Best: 2 strands electric wire
  - Strand 1: 20cm high
  - Strand 2: 40cm high
- Or heavy-gauge welded wire buried 30cm
- Rotational paddocks: move pigs regularly
- Roots up paddock, then plant and rest it

## Feeding

- Pigs are omnivores: feed almost anything
- Kitchen scraps, garden waste, whey
- Grain: barley, wheat, corn (primary energy)
- Forage: pannage (mast, roots, grubs)
- Water: 10-15 liters per day minimum
- Sows lactating: 15-20 liters per day

## Growth Timeline

- Piglet birth: 1-1.5 kg
- 8 weeks (weaning): 15-20 kg
- 4 months: 50-60 kg
- 5-6 months: 90-110 kg live weight
- Dress out at 70-75% of live weight
- Hang carcass 24-48 hours before butchering

## Manure Value

- Pigs produce 2-3 kg manure per day
- High nitrogen, excellent for compost
- Do not apply fresh to vegetable beds
- Compost 6 months before use
