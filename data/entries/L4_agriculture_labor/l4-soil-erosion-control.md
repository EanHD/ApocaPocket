# Erosion Control

## Understanding Erosion

- Rain impact on bare soil splashes particles
- Sheet erosion: thin layer washed downhill
- Rill erosion: small channels form
- Gully erosion: channels deepen, very serious
- Wind erosion: top 5cm of soil removed
- Each cm of topsoil takes 100-1000 yrs to form

## Contour Planting

- Plant rows across the slope, not down
- Follow contour lines (same elevation)
- Slows water runoff across entire slope
- Each row acts as small dam for soil
- Use A-frame level to find true contours
- Most effective first line of defense

## Swales on Contour

- Dig shallow channel on contour line
- Spoil goes on downhill side as berm
- Swale catches and holds water in place
- Water soaks in, does not run downhill
- Plant swale berm with perennials
- Spaced every 5-10m on steep slopes

## Ground Cover

- Never leave soil bare for more than 2 weeks
- Cover crop or mulch immediately after harvest
- Thick mulch absorbs rain impact
- Living mulch of low plants fills gaps
- Grass on paths between beds
- Annual crops: transplant, not direct seed

## Windbreak Design

- Trees/shrubs on prevailing wind side
- Dense evergreen base, taller trees above
- Windbreak protects 10x its height
  downwind from erosion
- Also moderates temperature extremes
- Hawthorn, elder, alder excellent species
- Plant as soon as site is established

## Slope Plants

- Deep-rooted species hold slope structure
- Comfrey: deep taproot, fast biomass
- Black locust: pioneer tree, fixes nitrogen
- Vetiver grass: roots to 3m, erosion-proof
- Willow: fast-growing, loves wet slopes
- Wild roses: thorny, wildlife habitat too

## Emergency Repair

- Active rill: fill with rocks, plant crown vetch
- Bare slope: cover immediately with straw
  held down by netting or pegs
- Gully: stone check dams across channel
- Unstable bank: stake willow cuttings every 30cm
