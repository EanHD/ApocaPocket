# Hawthorn Universal

## Universal ID Points

- Thorny tree or shrub with hard spines 1-3in
- Lobed toothed alternate leaves
- White 5-petal flower clusters in spring
- Red to dark red berries in fall
- All Crataegus species worldwide are edible

## Spring Harvest

- Young leaves: eat raw as trail nibble
- Leaves: mild flavor, slightly chewy
- White flowers: eat raw; mild, sweet
- Steep flowers in water for pleasant tea

## Summer: Young Leaves

- Leaves toughen but remain edible
- Use as bulk green in boiled soups
- Leaves have mild astringent quality
- Add to any foraged green recipe

## Fall Berry Harvest

- Harvest when deep red and slightly soft
- Berries persist on tree into winter
- After frost: sweetness improves
- Major advantage: available when other fruits gone

## Preparing Berries

- Eat flesh raw; spit out hard seeds
- Boil and strain through cloth for juice
- Boil down for thick paste (seed-free)
- Dry whole berries; rehydrate in soup

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C in berries and young leaves
- Bioflavonoids, polyphenols, antioxidants
- Heart-supportive properties in all parts
- ~52 cal per 100g flesh

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes anywhere
- All Crataegus species are safe to eat
- Thorns + lobed leaves + red berries = hawthorn
- Most reliable universal wild food
