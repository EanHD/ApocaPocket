# Salt Extraction

## Overview

- Salt is critical for preservation
  and electrolyte replacement
- Sources: seawater, salt springs,
  rock salt deposits, salt licks,
  saltwater plants, wood ash
- Survival need: 1-3g per day minimum

## Seawater Evaporation

- Collect seawater in container
- Boil or sun-evaporate until dry
- White salt crystals remain
- Scrape up and store in dry container
- 1 quart seawater = 1 tsp salt approx

## Salt Springs and Licks

- Animals worn paths to mineral licks
  = salt spring or mineral seep nearby
- Scoop water, evaporate to crystals
- Taste test: saltiness confirms
- Lick rock surface at salt seep

## Rock Salt Deposits

- White or clear crystalline deposits
  on cliff faces or dry rock outcrops
- Dissolve in water, filter, evaporate
- Found in arid and semi-arid regions
- Test: distinctive salty taste

## Plant Ash Salt

- Burn saltgrass, marsh plants,
  seaweed, or hickory
- Dissolve ash in water, filter out
  charcoal particles
- Evaporate: remaining minerals
  include sodium and potassium
- Alkaline: use sparingly

## Using Salt Substitutes

- Salt lick mineral water: high mineral
- Sea plants (kelp, dulse): salty
- Animal blood: contains sodium
- Crickets and insects: naturally salty
