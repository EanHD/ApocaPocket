# Bone Broth

## Overview

- Extract maximum nutrition from
  bones after meat is removed
- Rich in gelatin (protein), minerals,
  and residual fat
- Rehydrating, warming, calorie-dense
- Can simmer in bark vessel (stone
  boiling) or clay pot

## Bone Preparation

- Crack large bones with rock or knife
  butt to expose marrow
- Roast bones first: improves flavor,
  kills surface contaminants
- Any bones: deer, rabbit, bird, fish
- Fish bones: full broth in 1-2 hours
- Large mammal bones: 12-24 hours

## Making the Broth

- Cover bones with water
- Bring to boil, reduce to simmer
- Add wild onion, garlic if available
- Skim foam during first 30 min
- Add small amount of vinegar or
  acidic plant to improve mineral
  extraction
- Low simmer throughout -- not boil

## Time by Bone Type

- Fish bones: 1-2 hours
- Bird bones: 3-6 hours
- Rabbit/small game: 6-12 hours
- Deer/large game: 12-24 hours
- Longer time = more gelatin extracted

## Signs of Good Broth

- Gels when cooled: gelatin extracted
- Golden or tan color
- Rich smell, full flavor
- Fat layer on top: skim or eat

## Using the Broth

- Drink as warming nutrition
- Cook grains and greens in broth
- Rehydrate dried foods in broth
- Thicken with flour or starch plant
