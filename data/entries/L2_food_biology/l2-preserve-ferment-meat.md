# Fermented Meat

## Overview

- Traditional preservation: salt +
  lactobacillus bacteria lower pH
- Acidic environment stops pathogen
  growth
- Examples: traditional sausage,
  indigenous fermented fish
- HIGH RISK without experience --
  do not attempt in first week of
  survival situation

## Basic Principle

- Salt inhibits harmful bacteria
- Lactobacillus (always present)
  ferments meat sugars
- Acid produced lowers pH to 4.5-5.0
- At this pH most pathogens die
- Process takes 2-6 weeks

## Fermented Fish

- Layer fish and salt in container
- 1 part salt to 5 parts fish by weight
- Press under weight
- Ferments in sealed container
- Strong smell is normal
- pH drop = success

## Safe Ferment Signs

- Sour, tangy smell (not rotten)
- Liquid (brine) is clear or slightly
  cloudy -- not black or discolored
- Meat is firm, not slimy
- pH below 5 (taste: distinctly sour)

## Danger Signs

- Sweet or putrid smell (not sour)
- Black or unusual discoloration
- Gas bubbles forming rapidly in
  sealed container (botulism risk)
- Unusual texture: mushy or slimy

## Bottom Line

- Start with salt curing + smoking
- Fermented meat requires experience
  to identify safe product reliably
