# Midwest US Hazards

Region: ND SD NE KS MN IA MO WI IL IN OH MI

## Seasonal Risks

Dec-Feb: blizzards, extreme cold, ice storms. Wind chill to -50°F in Dakotas and Minnesota.
Mar-May: tornado season peak (Tornado Alley). Spring flooding from snowmelt plus rain.
Jun-Aug: heat waves, severe storm season, second tornado peak, drought.
Sep-Nov: early blizzards possible. Flooding continues. Harvest season farm hazards.

## Tornadoes

Midwest is the core of Tornado Alley. Kansas, Oklahoma, Nebraska have highest frequency.
EF scale: EF0 (65-85 mph) to EF5 (200+ mph). EF3 and above = total structural loss.
Warning: average 13-minute lead time from sirens. Sirens not audible in all conditions.
Shelter: below ground is the only safe option. Basement over interior room. Away from all windows.
Mobile homes: always evacuate to a solid structure before a tornado watch or warning. No exceptions.
After: do not re-enter shelter until all-clear. Secondary tornadoes follow in the same storm system.
Signs: dark green sky, large hail, freight-train roar, rotating debris cloud at ground level.

## Flooding

Mississippi and Missouri rivers: major flooding gives 1-2 weeks warning. Monitor NWS river stage forecasts.
Flash floods: 0-6 hour warning. Especially dangerous in loess hills, river bluffs, and paved urban areas.
Spring peak: rivers reach highest stage when snowmelt and rain combine. Highest risk March through May.
Contamination: floodwater carries agricultural chemicals, sewage, and livestock waste. Treat all water.
Never drink untreated surface water after any flooding event in agricultural regions.

## Winter Storms

Blizzard: 35+ mph winds plus blowing snow plus near-zero visibility. Do not drive under blizzard warning.
Wind chill at -30°F: exposed skin freezes in 10 minutes. Stay indoors and monitor frostbite risk.
Ice storms: 0.5 inch or more of ice accumulation drops power lines. Roads impassable 24-72 hours.
Shelter-in-place kit: 72 hours minimum of water, food, heat source, carbon monoxide detector.
Vehicle kit: sleeping bag, candles, emergency food, water. Blizzard stranding is a survival situation.

## Heat Waves

Midwest summer heat: 90-100°F with high relative humidity creates dangerous heat index.
Corn belt effect: corn transpiration increases local humidity 10-15% above regional average.
Heat index above 103°F: limit all outdoor exertion. Work in 20-minute intervals with shade breaks.
High-risk groups: elderly, children, outdoor workers, anyone without air conditioning.
Cooling centers: libraries, malls, community centers. Locate the nearest one before any heat event.

## Ag Hazards

Grain entrapment:
Grain bin entry is an extreme hazard. Flowing grain acts exactly like quicksand.
Rescue requires a grain vacuum truck and trained responders. Do not enter alone to rescue anyone.
Victim action: stop moving. Keep breathing. Call 911 immediately. Every movement sinks faster.

Pesticides and herbicides:
Skin contact: remove all contaminated clothing. Flush with water for 15-20 minutes.
Inhalation: move immediately to fresh air. Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
Eye exposure: flush with clean running water for 15-20 minutes. Do not rub eyes.

Post-flood risks: ag chemical-contaminated water on skin, crops, and livestock is common after floods.
Anhydrous ammonia (common fertilizer): leak causes severe chemical burns. Evacuate upwind immediately.
Escaped livestock on roads after flooding: large animals at night cause fatal vehicle collisions.
