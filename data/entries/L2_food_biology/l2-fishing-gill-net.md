# Improvised Gill Net

## Overview

- Passive method: intercepts fish at
  night or across a channel
- Most effective across narrow water
- Labor intensive to build but works
  while you do other tasks
- Mesh size matches target fish size

## Materials

- Cordage: paracord, plant fiber, wire
- Two horizontal lines: float line,
  weight line
- Floats: sections of dry wood
- Weights: stones tied on weight line
- Net mesh panels: woven cordage

## Mesh Size Guidelines

- Trout/panfish: 1.5-2 in mesh
- Bass/walleye: 2-3 in mesh
- Large fish (carp, pike): 3-4 in mesh
- Mesh too small: fish cannot enter
- Mesh too large: fish swim through

## Basic Construction

- Stretch two parallel lines 3 ft apart
- Tie vertical cords every 2 in along
  top line (these become net body)
- Knot adjacent cords at mesh intervals
  to form diamond mesh pattern
- Attach stones along bottom line
- Attach wood floats along top line

## Setting the Net

- Stretch across stream or inlet
- Float line on surface, weight line
  on bottom -- net hangs vertical
- Anchor ends to banks with stakes
- Net faces fish travel direction
- Mark location clearly

## Checking

- Check every 4-6 hours minimum
- Remove fish immediately: gills tangle
  -- extract carefully or cut free
- Dry net after use to prevent rot
