# Hen of the Woods

## Identification

- Cluster of overlapping gray-brown caps
- Grows at base of oak trees
- Each cap 2-8 in; fan-shaped, wavy
- White to cream pore surface underneath
- Firm aromatic mushroom; earthy smell

## Where to Find

- Base of large oak trees in fall
- Often returns to same tree yearly
- September-November in eastern US
- Mark oak trees for annual return

## Confirming Identity

- Pore surface (tiny holes), not gills
- Gray-brown top; white underside
- At oak base; not on trunk high up
- Many overlapping wavy caps together

## How to Harvest

- Cut entire cluster at base
- Leave 20% to spread spores
- Inner portions can be tough; use outer
- Bring paper bag; heavy and moist

## Preparation

- Brush clean or rinse quickly
- Slice and saute in any fat available
- Excellent in soup; holds texture
- Dry: slice thin and dehydrate for storage

## Nutritional Value

- Immune-modulating beta-glucans (high)
- Iron, zinc, niacin, vitamin D
- Protein: ~4g per 100g
- ~31 cal per 100g; nutrient-dense

## Medical Significance

- Beta-glucan content highest of any mushroom
- Immune system support; anti-tumor research
- Blood sugar modulating properties
- Eat fresh or dried; both retain benefits

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Gray-brown overlapping caps at oak base
- Berkeley's polypore: similar but tan colored
- Berkeley's also edible; no safety concern
