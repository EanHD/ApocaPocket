# Wild Echinacea

## Identification

- Purple cone-shaped flower head
- Drooping purple-pink petals around cone
- Rough hairy stems and leaves
- Alternate lance-shaped leaves
- Chew root or leaf: strong tingling/numbing

## The Tingle Test

- Chew small piece of root or fresh leaf
- True echinacea: strong tingling numbness
- Like slight electric buzz on tongue
- This is definitive ID confirmation
- No other common plant has this property

## Where to Find

- Dry prairie grasslands and open woods
- Eastern US and Great Plains
- Echinacea purpurea: most common species
- Blooms July-September

## How to Harvest

- Root: dig in fall after flowering
- Flowers: pick at peak bloom for tea
- Root at least 3 years old for medicinal value
- Take root of only large mature plants

## Preparation

- Root: dry and grind for capsule/tea powder
- Root tea: boil 20 min, steep 10 more
- Flower tea: steep in hot water 10 min
- Tincture: steep in spirits for 2-4 weeks

## Immune Support Use

- Drink tea at onset of cold/infection
- Most effective in first 2-3 days of illness
- Stimulates white blood cell production
- Traditional Plains tribes: wound infection

## Nutritional Note

- Not a caloric food source
- Alkylamides are active compounds
- Root most potent; flowers moderate
- Use medicinally; not as regular food
