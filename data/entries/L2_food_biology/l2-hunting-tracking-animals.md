# Animal Tracking

## Overview

- Tracking reads story of animal movement
- Look at: track shape, gait, pressure,
  substrate disturbance, related sign
- Fresh tracks: sharp edges, moist soil,
  no debris in print
- Old tracks: dry, crumbled, filled edges

## Common Track Shapes

- Deer: two curved toes, heart-shaped
- Rabbit: two large ovals (back), two
  small (front), asymmetric set
- Squirrel: four toes, small pairs
- Raccoon: hand-like, five fingers
- Coyote: four toes, oval, claws show
- Fox: similar to coyote, narrower

## Gait Patterns

- Walk: diagonal pairs (deer, coyote)
- Bound: back feet land ahead of front
  (rabbit, squirrel)
- Gallop: asymmetric four-print sets
- Trot: evenly spaced pairs
- Stride length shows size and speed

## Pressure Readings

- Deep heel = walking slowly
- Deep toe = running, accelerating
- Slipped soil = turning
- Flat pressure = standing, feeding
- Uneven depth = limp or injury

## Following a Trail

- Mark last known track before moving
- Grid-search when trail goes cold
- Look for disturbance at edges:
  broken stems, scuffed bark, hair
- Use low angle light to see shadows
- Never walk on the trail itself

## Age Estimation

- Fresh under 1 hr: crisp, moist
- Several hours: edges soften
- 12-24 hrs: dried out, dusty
- Rain or frost accelerates aging
