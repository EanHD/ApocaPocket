# Southeast US Regional

Region: VA, NC, SC, GA, FL, AL, MS, LA, AR, TN, KY

## Top 5 Hazards

Hurricane (Jun-Nov, peak Aug-Oct): CATASTROPHIC. Storm surge kills more than wind. Cat 3+ coastal = evacuate inland 40+ miles. Never shelter in basement near coast.

Heat stroke (May-Sep, peak Jul-Aug): EXTREME. Brain damage in 15-20 min. Hot DRY skin + confusion = emergency. Cool aggressively: ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, wet skin + fan. Do not give fluids if unconscious.

Flooding (year-round, peak summer): HIGH. 6 inches moving water knocks you down. 12 inches moves a car. Never drive through flowing water. If swept: float on back feet-downstream, angle toward shore.

Venomous snakes (Apr-Oct): HIGH. Four types: cottonmouth (water), copperhead (forest), rattlesnake (varied), coral (pine/scrub). Step ON logs not over them. No tourniquet. No cutting/sucking. Immobilize, seek antivenom within 6 hours.

Ticks/tick-borne disease (Mar-Nov, peak May-Aug): MODERATE but chronic. Lyme and RMSF both present. Check entire body within 2 hours of exposure. Remove with tweezers at skin level, pull straight. Attached over 24 hrs = antibiotic prophylaxis if available.

## Seasonal Timing

- Jan-Feb: Ice storms possible, minimal snake/insect risk
- Mar-May: Tornadoes, snakes emerging, ticks active
- Jun-Aug: Peak heat/hurricane/lightning/flood/snake/fire ant season
- Sep-Oct: Hurricane peak, snakes still active, cooling
- Nov-Dec: Cold fronts, secondary tornado risk

## Key Actions by Hazard

Hurricane: Monitor NHC at nhc.noaa.gov. Track 72 hrs out. Fill bathtubs (backup water). 7-day food/water before storm arrives. Evacuate Cat 3+ if coastal.

Heat: Work only dawn-10am and 4pm-dusk in summer. Drink before thirsty, minimum 1 gallon/day. Dark urine = already dehydrated.

Flood: Camp 10+ ft above any waterway. Never camp in dry creek bed. Leave area if rain reported upstream even if skies are clear.

Snakes: 90% of bites are feet/ankles/hands. Boots + long pants reduce severity. Cottonmouth will stand its ground near water. Back away, do not approach.

Ticks: Permethrin-treat clothing (lasts 6 washes). Light-colored pants tucked into socks. Full body check every evening. Bull's-eye rash = start doxycycline immediately if available.

## Fire Ant Emergency

Silent swarm: ants climb then sting simultaneously. Move away fast, brush ants off with sweeping motion. Anaphylaxis in 1-5% of people. Swelling beyond sting site + breathing difficulty = use epinephrine if available, get medical care.

## Monthly Danger Peaks

Most dangerous month: August (heat + hurricane peak + alligators + fire ants)
Most dangerous condition: Heat stroke (15-60 min to death without treatment)
Second: Storm surge during hurricane landfall
Third: Flash flood crossing
