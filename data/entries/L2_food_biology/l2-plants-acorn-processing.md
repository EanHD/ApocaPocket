# Acorn Processing

## Identification

- Round to oval nut in cap
- Cap stays on tree; nut falls when ripe
- All Quercus (oak) species produce acorns
- White oak group: rounder, sweeter
- Red oak group: pointier, more tannin

## Selecting Best Acorns

- White oaks (Q. alba): lowest tannin
- Harvest tan-brown fallen acorns
- Check for mold and weevil holes
- Float test: good sink, bad float

## Cold Water Leaching

- Crack and grind acorns to coarse meal
- Place in container with running water
- Change water 2-4x per day; 2-4 days
- Test: taste small piece; less astringent?

## Hot Water Leaching

- Boil cracked acorns in water 15 min
- Pour off dark water; add fresh water
- Repeat 3-6 times until mild tasting
- Loses some nutrients but much faster

## Making Acorn Flour

- Fully leached acorns: dry in sun or heat
- Grind with two rocks (metate technique)
- Progressively finer grinding
- Store dry; use within weeks

## Nutritional Value

- 357 cal per 100g dry flour
- Fat: 24g (mostly unsaturated)
- Protein: 6g; carbohydrates 41g
- Calcium, magnesium, phosphorus

## Cooking Acorn Flour

- Mix with water for porridge (easiest)
- Pat into cakes and bake on hot rock
- Combine with other flour sources
- Acorn porridge: traditional worldwide
