# Chickweed

## Identification

- Small delicate plant; oval opposite leaves
- Single line of hairs along one side of stem
- Tiny white star-shaped 5-petal flowers
- Grows in low mats on disturbed soil
- Stem snaps cleanly; juicy texture

## The Hair Line Test

- Roll stem between fingers
- One line of fine hairs on one side only
- This is definitive chickweed identification
- No milky sap when stem is broken

## Where to Find

- Disturbed soil, garden beds, lawns
- Shaded moist areas preferred
- Spring and fall; dies in summer heat
- Often first green plant of early spring

## How to Harvest

- Grab handfuls of stems and leaves
- Scissors-cut above root for quick harvest
- Large patches yield substantial quantity
- Harvest before flowers for best taste

## Preparation

- Eat raw in salads: mild, slightly sweet
- Wilt in hot water as simple pot herb
- Mix with stronger-flavored greens
- Very tender; short cooking time

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamins C and A; calcium, iron
- ~20 cal per 100g
- High water content for mild hydration
- Best eaten in volume for real nutrition

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Spurge nearby: always check for milky sap
- Mouse-ear chickweed: hairy all over; edible
- Star-of-Bethlehem: bulb plant; avoid bulb
