# Feral Pig Hunting

## Overview

- Invasive in Southeast, Southwest US
- 100-300+ lbs, excellent meat yield
- Dangerous when cornered or wounded
- Roam widely -- not territorial
- Peak activity: dawn, dusk, night

## Finding Pigs

- Wallow: mud pit near water source
- Rooting: earth turned up, large area
- Rubs: mud on trees at 2-3 ft height
- Tracks: cloven hoof, 2-3 in wide,
  dew claws often show in soft ground
- Strong musky odor near fresh sign

## Approach and Harvest

- Circle downwind before approaching
- Pigs hear/smell well, poor vision
- Shot placement: shoulder/heart area
- Broadside at base of ear works
- Never approach from downhill

## Danger Signs

- Pig facing you, tail erect = charge
- Snorting, pawing ground = agitated
- Back hair raised = defensive posture
- Do not run -- climb a tree if charged
- Wounded pigs are always dangerous

## Field Dressing

- Use heavy gloves -- bristles sharp
- Remove skin quickly to cool meat
- Check lymph nodes: enlarged or spotted
  nodes = discard entire animal
- Same cavity process as deer

## Meat Safety

- Cook all pork to 160F minimum
- Trichinella common in feral pigs
- Do not eat raw or undercooked meat
- Cook until juices run completely clear
