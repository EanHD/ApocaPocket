# Stone Boiling

## Overview

- Boil water with no metal pot needed
- Vessel: birch bark, folded bark,
  large shell, turtle shell
- Rocks heated in fire, dropped in water
  -- heat transferred to water
- Boiling point reached in 10-15 min
- Indigenous method used for millennia

## Vessel Construction

- Birch bark: fold into box shape,
  hold with wooden pegs or green
  cordage lashings
- Large leaves: line depression in
  ground with overlapping leaves
- Clay pot: fashion from clay, let
  dry 2+ days before use
- Bark vessels: flame won't burn below
  water line (water absorbs heat)

## Rock Selection

- Dry granite: best option
- Dry basalt or smooth lava: OK
- Sandstone: works but crumbles slowly
- River stones: avoid (often saturated)
- Avoid: quartz, white or glassy rocks,
  shale or layered stone
- Test: if rock hisses or sizzles when
  placed in cold water = too wet

## Process

- Heat rocks in fire 20-30 min
- Use two sticks like tongs to handle
- Carry hot rock from fire to vessel
- Drop gently to avoid splashing
- Use 3-4 rocks at once for fast boil
- Swap out cooled rocks for hot ones

## Uses

- Boil water for drinking
- Cook soups, stews, broths
- Cook plant materials
- Melt fat for rendering
