# Mushroom ID Safety

## The Core Rules

- RULE 1: If in doubt, do not eat it
- RULE 2: Identify before eating, not after
- RULE 3: Eat only one new species at a time
- RULE 4: Eat small amount first; wait 2 hours
- RULE 5: Never eat based on appearance alone

## Amanita Check

- Dig up entire base before deciding
- Check for cup (volva): buried membrane
- Check for ring (skirt) on stem
- Both present = Amanita = assume toxic
- No cup + no ring = safer to proceed

## Toxic Mushroom Myths

- MYTH: Silver turns dark near toxic mushrooms
- MYTH: Toxic mushrooms peel easily
- MYTH: Toxic mushrooms taste bad
- MYTH: Cooking removes all toxins
- ALL FALSE; never rely on these

## Beginner Safe Species

- Chanterelle: false gills + fruity smell
- Chicken of woods: orange shelf on wood
- Lion's mane: white teeth on hardwood
- Giant puffball: pure white interior
- Oyster: fan shape on dead hardwood

## Spore Print Method

- Place cap gills-down on paper overnight
- Spore print color confirms family/genus
- White print + white gills = potential Amanita
- Brown-black print = Coprinus (shaggy mane ok)

## If Poisoning Suspected

- Save a sample of the mushroom eaten
- Seek medical help immediately
- Do not wait for symptoms; call early
- Activated charcoal if within 1-2 hours

## The Conservative Rule

- In survival: skip mushrooms if uncertain
- Plants easier to ID and safer overall
- Unless you know it: find another food
- Risk-reward: deadly mistakes change nothing
