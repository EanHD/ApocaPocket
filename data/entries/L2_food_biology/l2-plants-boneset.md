# Boneset

## Identification

- Opposite leaves that wrap around stem
- Stem appears to grow through leaf base
- White flat-topped flower clusters
- Tall plant 2-5 ft; fuzzy stems
- Eupatorium perfoliatum: common species

## Distinctive Feature

- Leaf base wraps around stem (perfoliate)
- Appears as if stem pierces leaf
- This is the key identification feature
- No other common plant has this exactly

## Where to Find

- Moist areas, stream banks, wetland edges
- Open woods and woodland edges
- Eastern US; summer through fall bloom
- Often in large patches

## Traditional Flu Use

- Named for use during influenza epidemics
- Intensely bitter hot tea induces sweating
- Traditional: break fever, relieve bone pain
- Historically called flu weed or fever weed

## Making Boneset Tea

- Small handful of fresh or dry leaves
- Boil in water; steep 15 minutes
- Very bitter; extremely unpleasant taste
- Drink hot, small amounts (1/4 cup)

## Medicinal Properties

- Immunostimulant compounds
- Diaphoretic: promotes sweating
- Anti-inflammatory for joint and muscle pain
- Traditional use: flu-related body aches

## Caution

- Do not eat raw plant; sesquiterpene lactones toxic
- Tea is safe in small amounts
- Very large doses cause vomiting (traditional emetic use)
- Use only for fever/flu; not daily
