# Desert Marigold

## Identification

- Bright yellow daisy-like flowers
- White woolly/hairy stems and leaves
- Low growing 1-2 ft; multiple flower stems
- Blooms nearly year-round in desert
- Very common roadside plant in SW

## THIS PLANT IS TOXIC

- Contains sesquiterpene lactones
- Toxic to sheep, cattle, and humans
- Eating causes weakness, tremors, staggers
- Chronic exposure causes liver damage
- Do NOT eat flowers, leaves, or stems

## Why It's Included Here

- Very common and prominent in SW desert
- Bright flowers may attract hungry forager
- Easy to mistake for edible desert plants
- Important AVOID identification entry

## Telling Apart Edibles

- True sunflowers: taller, bigger seed heads
- Brittlebush (Encelia): similar but smoother
- Desert marigold: distinctly woolly/white hair
- No edible desert plants have woolly stems

## If Eaten by Accident

- Symptoms: weakness, trembling, neurological
- Onset within hours to days
- Seek shelter; rest and hydrate
- Symptoms may persist days to weeks

## Safe Alternatives

- Mesquite pods in same habitat
- Prickly pear pads and fruit
- Desert willow flowers nearby water
- Prioritize known-safe plants in SW
