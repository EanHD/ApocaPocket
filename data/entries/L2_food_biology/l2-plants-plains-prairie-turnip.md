# Prairie Turnip

## Identification

- Low plant 6-12 in tall; hairy stems
- Compound leaf: 5 leaflets in palm shape
- Purple pea-like flowers in May-June
- Deep taproot: turnip-shaped, starchy
- Above-ground part dies and blows away in summer

## Finding After Die-Back

- Flower stalk breaks off and rolls
- Dig where dead stalks accumulate
- Mark locations during flowering in June
- Shallow taproot: 4-8 in deep in soil

## Where to Find

- Dry prairie grassland and hillsides
- Well-drained rocky or sandy soil
- Northern Great Plains; Dakotas, Nebraska
- May-July when above-ground and findable

## How to Harvest

- Pull or dig taproot straight down
- Root is brittle; dig carefully
- Peel outer skin before eating
- Best before plant flowers for tender root

## Preparation

- Eat raw: sweet, starchy, turnip-like
- Boil 10-15 min for soft texture
- Dry and pound to flour for winter
- Traditional: peel, braid, and dry for storage

## Nutritional Value

- Starch: ~300 cal per 100g dried
- Protein: 12% by dry weight
- Major Plains carbohydrate source
- Dried flour lasts a year or more

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Locoweed (Astragalus): similar pea flowers
- Locoweed: narrow linear leaflets, not palmate
- Prairie turnip: distinctly 5-palmate leaflets
- Confirm: 5 finger-spread leaflets
