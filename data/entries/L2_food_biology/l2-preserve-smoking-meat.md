# Smoking Meat Basics

## Overview

- Smoking: preserves by drying meat
  surface and depositing antimicrobial
  compounds from wood smoke
- Hot smoke: cooks AND preserves
- Cold smoke: dries and flavors only
  -- does not fully cook
- Salt before smoking significantly
  improves preservation

## Hot Smoking

- Temperature at meat: 160-225F
- Time: 2-6 hours depending on size
- Result: fully cooked, shelf stable
  3-5 days without refrigeration
- Build small fire, allow to coals
- Add hardwood chips or green wood
  for smoke

## Cold Smoking

- Temperature at meat: below 90F
- Time: 6-24+ hours
- Separate fire from smoke chamber
- Requires sustained attention
- Result: concentrated, dried surface
- Must still cook before eating

## Improvised Rig

- Four-stake framework with crossbars
- Hang meat strips or lay on wire rack
- Fire box below: small fire, lots
  of smoke-producing wood
- Cover with tarp or bark to contain
  smoke around meat

## Best Woods

- Hardwoods: hickory, oak, alder,
  apple, cherry, maple
- Avoid: pine, spruce, cedar
  (resins = bitter and potentially toxic)
- Green/wet hardwood chips: more smoke
- Bark of hardwood trees: good smoke

## Done Signs

- Mahogany to dark brown surface
- Dry, firm texture on outside
- Smoke ring visible in cross-section
- Clean smoky smell, not rancid
- Shelf life: 3-7 days hot smoked
