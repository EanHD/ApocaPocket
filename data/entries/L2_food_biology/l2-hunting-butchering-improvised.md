# Improvised Butchering

Your ancestors processed mammoths with stone, bone, and patience. A sharp rock and knowledge is enough.

## Stone Selection

**Best stones in order of quality**
- Obsidian: Black, glassy, volcanic. Sharper than steel at microscopic level. Volcanic regions only.
- Flint or chert: Gray, black, tan. Fine-grained. Conchoidal fracture (glass-like break). Most widely available.
- Quartzite: White or pink or gray. Coarser edge but functional for butchering.
- Glass: Broken bottles. Excellent sharpness. Wear cloth on the grip end.

**Finding flint:** Riverbeds, gravel bars, exposed rock layers. Fine-grained, smooth, rings when struck.

## Making Stone Blades

**Percussion flaking (rough shaping)**
- Select fist-sized stone with no visible cracks.
- Strike edge at 30-45 degree angle with hammerstone (smooth rounded river rock).
- Flake breaks off, leaving sharp edge.
- Repeat around perimeter to shape into a blade form.

**Simple method (fastest for beginners):** Break flint with hammerstone. Use the resulting sharp flakes directly. No further shaping needed.

Wrap grip end with cloth or leather before use. Stone cuts as deep as steel.

**Lifespan:** Very sharp but dulls in 10-20 cuts. Re-sharpen by striking off the dull edge with hammerstone. One fist-sized stone yields 5-10 usable blades through re-flaking.

## Bone & Antler Tools

- Bone scraper: Split leg bone lengthwise. Sharpen broken edge on coarse rock. Use for fleshing hides.
- Bone awl: Rib splinter or leg bone. Sharpen one end to a point. Piercing hide for sewing.
- Bone needle: Fine rib splinter. Grind to needle shape. Polish smooth. Sewing sinew thread.
- Antler pressure flaker: Pointed antler tine tip. Press against stone edge to pop small flakes off. Fine sharpening.

## Butchering Process

**Hide removal**
- Stone blade works the same as steel. Use shallow cuts to avoid piercing the hide.
- No knife method: Initial cuts around legs and neck with stone. Punch fist through membrane while peeling hide away from carcass.
- Fresh kill: Hide peels with moderate effort. After 12+ hours: membrane toughens significantly.
- Time comparison with deer: Steel 20-30 min, stone blade 40-60 min, stone plus fist 60-90 min.

**Quartering without a saw (use joints not bone)**
- Find joint by bending limb until you feel the gap between bones.
- Cut through ligaments and tendons around joint with stone blade.
- Twist and pull to separate. No need to cut through bone.
- Shoulder joint is easiest (only muscle, no bone-to-bone connection).

**Backstraps and tenderloins**
- Feel along the spine for the long muscle on either side.
- Cut shallow along spine. Slice under backstrap. Pull muscle away from carcass.

**Rib meat**
- Slide stone blade between ribs. Strip meat in scraping motion. Do not waste.

## Sinew Extraction

- Best sinew locations: Leg tendons (Achilles area, 12-18 inches long), back sinew (under backstrap, 2-4 feet long).
- Cut above and below tendon. Pull free from surrounding muscle.
- Dry 1-3 days hanging. Pound flat with rock on rock. Pull apart into fine fibers.
- Uses: Sewing thread (stronger than plant cordage), bowstring, lashing, fishing line.

## Bone Tool Yield

- Long leg bones: Marrow inside (crack open, scoop out, eat). Split lengthwise for scrapers.
- Rib bones: Carve into fishing hooks, use as scrapers.
- Scapula (shoulder blade): Use as a shovel or large scraper.
- Skull: Brain for hide tanning. Every animal has enough brain to tan its own hide.

## Brain Tanning Overview

- Flesh hide completely. All meat and fat removed with bone scraper.
- Soak hide 24 hours. Apply brain-water mixture. Work hide by stretching and pulling 2-4 hours while it dries.
- Smoke is optional but waterproofs and preserves the leather.
- Result: Soft supple leather for clothing, bags, or shelter material.
- Time: 1-3 days total labor.

## Time Comparison

- Full deer butcher: Steel knife 2.5-3 hrs. Stone tools 4-6 hrs. No tools 6-8 hrs.
- Stone tools are 2-3x slower but completely functional.
- Our ancestors processed 1,000 lb bison with stone in 2-3 hours as a team.

## One Animal Yields

- Bone tools: 4-6 scrapers, 2-4 awls, 10-20 needles from rib splinters.
- Sinew: 50-100 ft of cordage, 1 bowstring, sewing thread supply.
- Hide: Clothing, bag, shelter material, rawhide strips for lashing.
- Antlers (if present): Pressure flaker, knife handle, digging tool.
