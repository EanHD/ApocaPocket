# Ash Salt Preservation

## Overview

- Wood ash is alkaline (high pH)
- Alkaline environment kills or inhibits
  many food spoilage bacteria
- Traditional preservation in cultures
  without salt access
- Works as surface treatment for meat,
  nuts, corn (nixtamalization)

## Ash Selection

- Hardwood ash preferred: hickory,
  oak, maple
- Softwood ash: functional but weaker
- Use clean white/gray ash only
- Avoid: ash from painted wood,
  treated lumber, plastics
- Sift to remove charcoal chunks

## Meat Preservation

- Coat meat surface with dry wood ash
- Press ash into all cuts and surfaces
- Hang or rack in cool, dry location
- Ash dries surface and raises pH
- Extends fresh meat shelf life by
  1-2 additional days in warm weather

## Nixtamalization

- Boil dried corn in ash-water solution
  (1 tbsp ash per quart water)
- Simmer 20-30 min, soak overnight
- Rinse thoroughly in clean water
- Increases protein bioavailability
  and adds minerals

## Making Ash Lye Water

- Fill container with hardwood ash
- Pour water through ash slowly
- Collect alkaline liquid (lye water)
- Use diluted as brine for curing
- Test: should feel slippery on skin

## Limitations

- Not as reliable as salt curing
- Alkaline taste remains on food
- Rinse foods well before eating
- Best combined with drying or smoking
