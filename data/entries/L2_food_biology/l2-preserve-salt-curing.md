# Salt Curing Meat

## Overview

- Salt draws water from meat through
  osmosis: bacteria cannot survive
  in low-moisture environment
- Two methods: dry cure and wet brine
- Best combined with smoking or drying
  for maximum shelf life
- Salt available: use it -- essential

## Dry Cure Method

- Apply salt generously to all surfaces
- 1 oz salt per pound of meat (minimum)
- Rub into all cuts and surfaces
- Stack meat pieces with salt between
- Allow 12-24 hours per inch thickness
- Refrigerate (or cold location) ideally

## Wet Brine Method

- Mix: 1 cup salt per quart of water
- Submerge meat completely
- Weight down to keep fully submerged
- Cure 12-48 hours depending on size
- Temperature: below 40F if possible
- Change brine if it clouds heavily

## Signs of Curing

- Meat firms up noticeably
- Surface becomes glossy and tacky
- Slices: pale pink, firm texture
- Salt crystals may appear on surface

## After Curing

- Rinse excess salt (if over-cured)
- Air dry 30-60 min: surface dries,
  sets a pellicle for smoking
- Smoke or air dry to finish
- Or store cured in cool dry location

## Shelf Life

- Lightly cured (24 hrs): +2-3 days
- Heavily cured + smoked: 1-2 weeks
- Country cure (heavy): 1-3 months
  in cool, dry conditions
