# Honey Preservation

## Overview

- Raw honey: extremely low water
  activity, high antimicrobial
  properties (H2O2, low pH, enzymes)
- Almost indefinitely shelf stable
- Used to preserve fruit, meat, nuts
- Finding wild honey requires locating
  bee trees (see tracking skills)

## Finding Wild Honey

- Follow bees on hot days toward
  water source -- water = bee activity
- Watch bee flight lines: follow
  direction of travel
- Bee tree: hollow, constant bee
  traffic, buzzing sound
- Smoke bees: green vegetation smoke
  calms them for harvest

## Preserving in Honey

- Ensure honey fully covers food
- No air pockets -- moisture from food
  can dilute honey and cause spoilage
- Dry the food first before coating
- Works best with partially dried meat,
  fruits, or cooked grain

## Honey-Preserved Fruit

- Layer honey and cleaned dried fruit
- Ensure all pieces are fully coated
- Seal container
- Shelf life: months if fully covered
- Eat fruit and use honey for cooking

## Wound Care Properties

- Honey: antimicrobial wound covering
- Apply to clean wound, cover with leaf
- Change daily
- Not for deep puncture wounds

## Honey Storage

- Store in sealed container
- Does not need refrigeration
- Crystallized honey: still good,
  warm gently to re-liquify
