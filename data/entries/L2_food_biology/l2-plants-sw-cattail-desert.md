# Desert Cattail

## Identification

- Same as all Typha species: brown hot-dog head
- Flat strap leaves 4-8 ft tall
- Grows in standing water or mud
- Green pollen spike above seed head in spring
- Typha domingensis common in SW

## Desert Water Sign

- Cattail = guaranteed water presence
- Visible cattails = accessible water nearby
- Green patch in dry canyon = check for water
- Follow desert washes for cattail colonies

## Where to Find

- Desert springs and seeps
- Seasonal and permanent desert streams
- Stock ponds and irrigation canals
- Cienegas (desert marshes) in sky islands

## Desert Harvest

- Spring: young shoots if water is present
- Spring-early summer: pollen harvest
- Fall-winter: rhizomes in mud
- Year-round plant where water persists

## How to Harvest

- Pull young shoots from base in water
- Shake pollen clusters into container
- Dig rhizomes with stick in muddy bottom
- Check water source quality first

## Preparation

- Same as PNW: shoots boiled or raw
- Pollen as flour supplement
- Rhizomes: pound in water, strain starch
- High priority harvest when in desert

## Water Source Caution

- Desert water can carry bacteria and chemicals
- Boil or filter all water from cattail sites
- Agricultural runoff: avoid heavily farmed areas
- Livestock ponds: risky; boil required
