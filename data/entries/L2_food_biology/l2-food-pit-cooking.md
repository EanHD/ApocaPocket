# Pit Cooking

## Overview

- Underground oven: slow, even, moist
- Ideal for large animals, tubers,
  and mixed one-pot meals
- Self-tending: set and forget 2-4 hrs
- Result: tender, fully cooked food
- Excellent for tough meat

## Digging the Pit

- Dig 2x the volume of food, approx
  12-18 in deep for small game
- 18-24 in for large roasts
- Cylindrical shape, 12-24 in diameter
- Sloped sides work well to hold rocks
- Remove loose dirt from bottom

## Heating the Rocks

- Fill pit with dry rocks
- Build fire on top of rocks in pit
- Burn 1-2 hours until rocks red-hot
- Remove burned wood and ash
  with green stick tools
- Work quickly once fire removed

## Loading the Pit

- Layer: green vegetation (leaves,
  fresh grass) on hot rocks
- Add food on top of vegetation
- Add more green vegetation on top
- If available: wrap food in leaves
  before placing

## Sealing the Pit

- Cover with dirt or flat rocks
- Seal edges: no steam should escape
- Mark location clearly
- Cooking time: 2-3 hrs for small game,
  4-6 hrs for large game
- Longer is safer -- hard to overcook

## Opening the Pit

- Stand back: steam burns
- Remove dirt/rock cover carefully
- Internal temp should be very hot
- Meat falls from bone when done
- Vegetables: soft and fully cooked
