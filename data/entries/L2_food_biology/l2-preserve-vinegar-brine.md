# Vinegar Brining

## Overview

- Vinegar (acetic acid) creates acidic
  environment hostile to bacteria
- Works for: vegetables, cooked meat,
  fish, eggs, wild plants
- Shelf life: 2-4 weeks in cool location
- Requires vinegar (see Improvised
  Vinegar entry) or acidic plant juice

## Brine Ratio

- Standard: 1 part vinegar to 1 part
  water minimum (5% acidity minimum)
- For short-term (1-2 weeks): 1:1 mix
- For longer (3-4 weeks): straight
  vinegar, no dilution
- Add salt: 1 tsp per cup of brine

## Preparing Vegetables

- Clean and cut into manageable pieces
- Blanch briefly in boiling water
  (1-2 min): softens slightly,
  kills surface bacteria
- Cool before placing in brine

## Preparing Cooked Meat

- Cook fully before brining
- Slice thin (1/4 inch pieces)
- Pack into container
- Pour hot brine over completely
- Fully submerge -- air = spoilage

## Container and Sealing

- Use clay pot, bark container, or
  any non-reactive vessel
- Food must be fully submerged
- Weight down if floating
- Cover opening with cloth to keep
  insects out while allowing gas out

## Signs of Success

- Vinegar smell maintained throughout
- No unusual odors develop
- Brine remains clear or slightly
  cloudy from proteins
- Black or putrid smell: discard
