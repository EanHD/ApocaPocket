# Chanterelle ID

## Identification

- Golden yellow to egg-yolk color
- Cap: wavy irregular, 2-6 in diameter
- Underside: forked ridges not true gills
- Ridges run down the stem (decurrent)
- Fruity apricot aroma; firm white flesh

## False Gill Test

- Turn cap over; look at underside
- True chanterelle: forked blunt ridges
- Ridges look like folded flesh, not sharp blades
- Run finger across: ridges are blunt, rubbery
- True gills = sharp blade-like; cuts finger

## Where to Find

- Mixed hardwood and conifer forest
- Near oaks, beeches, and conifers
- Summer through fall after rain
- Often partially buried under leaf litter

## How to Harvest

- Slice at base with knife
- Do not pull; damages mycelium
- Leave small ones to mature
- Firm specimens last days in cool air

## Preparation

- Clean with dry brush; avoid water-soaking
- Saute in fat: excellent rich flavor
- Do not overcook; becomes rubbery
- Dry for preservation; rehydrates well

## Nutritional Value

- Iron, potassium, vitamins B and C
- ~38 cal per 100g fresh
- Low fat but excellent flavor value
- High in vitamin D when sun-dried

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Jack-o-lantern (Omphalotus): TRUE sharp gills
- Jack-o-lantern: glows faint green at night
- False chanterelle (Hygrophoropsis): orange, thin true gills
- RULE: blunt forked ridges = safe; sharp gills = stop
