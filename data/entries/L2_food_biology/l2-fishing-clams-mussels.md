# Clams and Mussels

## Overview

- Marine shellfish: readily available
  on rocky and sandy shores
- Freshwater mussels: rivers and lakes
- Excellent nutrition: protein, minerals
- CRITICAL: Paralytic Shellfish Poisoning
  (PSP) is extremely dangerous
- No PSP in freshwater mussels

## PSP Safety Rules

- Never harvest during red tide events
  (water appears reddish-brown)
- Avoid harvest May through October
  in Pacific waters
- Safe months on Atlantic: winter
- If in doubt: do not eat marine
  shellfish (PSP has no antidote)

## Freshwater Mussels

- Found in rivers, streams, lakes
- No PSP risk in freshwater
- Bivalve shell, dark exterior
- Collect from clean water only
- Do not collect near agriculture
  runoff or sewage

## Harvesting

- Clams: dig in sand 4-6 in below
  surface at low tide
- Mussels: pry off rocks, logs
- Only harvest live: shell closes when
  tapped -- open shells = dead, discard
- Collect in mesh or breathable bag

## Cooking

- Steam or boil until shells open
  (3-5 minutes after water boils)
- Do not eat any that stay closed
- Remove from shell with fork or knife
- Eat meat directly or add to stew
- Broth from cooking is nutritious

## Signs of Bad Shellfish

- Shell stays open when tapped
- Off/sulfur smell
- Meat is gray or unusual color
- Do not eat: discard entire batch
