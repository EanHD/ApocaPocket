# Wild Ramps

## Identification

- Broad flat smooth leaves 6-12 in long
- Two leaves per plant; deep green
- Reddish-purple stem base below ground
- Small white bulb at base; spring only
- Intense garlic-onion smell when crushed

## Smell Test Critical

- ALWAYS crush a leaf and smell
- True ramp: strong garlic-onion odor
- No smell = lily of the valley (TOXIC)
- No smell = false hellebore (TOXIC)
- Smell test is mandatory, every time

## Where to Find

- Rich moist forest floors in Appalachians
- Often in large colonies under hardwoods
- March-May; disappears by early summer
- Look for broad smooth leaves on forest floor

## How to Harvest

- Dig bulb with fingers or small stick
- Pull bulb, roots, and leaves together
- Sustainable: take max 1/3 of colony
- Strong smell; bag immediately

## Preparation

- Eat whole plant raw: bulb, stems, leaves
- Chop and add to any cooked dish
- Sauté bulbs in fat as onion substitute
- Pickle bulbs in vinegar for long storage

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamins A, C; selenium, chromium
- Strong flavor = small amount goes far
- Carbohydrates in bulb for energy
- Traditional Appalachian spring tonic

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Lily of the valley (Convallaria): DEADLY
- False hellebore (Veratrum): DEADLY
- Both grow same habitat, same time
- RULE: no onion smell = do not eat
