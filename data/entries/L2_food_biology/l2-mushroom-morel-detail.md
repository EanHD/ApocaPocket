# Morel Detail

## Identification

- Honeycomb pitted cap attached at base
- Cap appears: attached directly to stem
- Hollow inside when sliced lengthwise
- Cap and stem in one hollow chamber
- Colors: tan, gray, black depending on species

## Mandatory Slice Test

- Cut mushroom in half top to bottom
- True morel: single hollow cavity inside
- Cap and stem walls thin; fully open
- No cotton/fiber inside = true morel
- False morel: chambered or cottony inside

## True vs False Morel

- True morel cap: attached at base of cap
- False morel (Gyromitra): cap attached only at top
- Cap of false morel hangs free like a skirt
- True morel: ridges AND pits; honeycomb look

## Where to Find

- Near dead elms, ash, and old orchards
- Burned areas previous year (burn morels)
- Spring: when apple trees bloom
- Morel season: 2-4 week window only

## How to Harvest

- Cut at base with knife
- Do not pull; disturbs mycelium
- Brush clean; they hold dirt in pits
- Check hollow immediately after harvest

## Preparation

- ALWAYS cook morels; never eat raw
- Saute in fat: nutty, earthy rich flavor
- Dry for preservation; rehydrates excellently
- Soak briefly in salt water to remove bugs

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Gyromitra esculenta (false morel): TOXIC
- Gyromitra: brain-like wrinkled; NOT honeycomb pits
- Gyromitra: cottony chambers inside
- RULE: honeycomb pits + hollow = safe morel
