## Identification
- Fan or oyster-shaped cap on wood
- White to cream-gray or tan colored
- Gills run down small off-center stem
- No ring or volva on stem
- Fresh anise-almond or neutral smell
- Spore print: white to pale lilac

## Where to Find
- Dead or dying deciduous trees and logs
- Especially elm, beech, oak
- Fall through spring; cooler temperatures
- Also summer after cool wet periods
- Never on ground; always on wood

## Confirming Identity
- Growing on wood, not ground
- Gills run down onto stem (decurrent)
- No ring on stem; no cup at base
- White to cream color when fresh

## Harvest and Prep
- Twist and pull at base or cut with knife
- Harvest whole clusters when small-medium
- Leave very old yellowed specimens
- Saute in fat: delicate seafood-like flavor
- Dry for preservation; powder or whole

## Nutritional Value
- Protein: ~3g per 100g fresh
- B vitamins, vitamin D, iron, potassium
- ~33 cal per 100g
- Beta-glucans for immune support

## Lookalikes — DANGER
- Angel wings (Pleurocybella): on conifers, smaller, white, no stem
- Reported fatalities in Japan from angel wings
- RULE: large oyster cluster on hardwood = safe
