# Purslane

## Identification

- Succulent thick rounded leaves
- Reddish-green trailing stems
- Tiny yellow 5-petal flowers
- Grows flat along ground in mats
- Stem snaps cleanly; no milky sap

## Where to Find

- Disturbed soil, gardens, roadsides
- Thrives in full sun and heat
- Common in SW summer after rains
- Appears in cracks in pavement and soil

## How to Harvest

- Pinch stems and leaves from plant
- Take top 3-4 inches of trailing stems
- Morning harvest: most water content
- Whole stems, leaves, and flowers edible

## Preparation

- Eat raw: cool, slightly sour, crunchy
- Boil briefly as spinach substitute
- Mix with other greens to bulk salad
- Pickle in vinegar for preservation

## Nutritional Value

- Omega-3 fatty acids: highest of land plants
- Alpha-linolenic acid: 400mg per 100g
- Vitamins A, C, E; magnesium, potassium
- ~16 cal per 100g but nutrient-dense

## The Milky Sap Test

- Snap any stem: clear sap = purslane (safe)
- White milky sap = spurge (Euphorbia)
- Spurge causes skin irritation and GI upset
- Always break stem and check sap color

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Spurge (Euphorbia maculata): MILKY SAP
- Spurge: much smaller leaves, spotted
- Always do sap test before eating
- Purslane: clear sap, thick juicy leaves
