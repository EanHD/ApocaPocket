# St. John's Wort

## Identification

- Bright yellow 5-petal flowers
- Black dots on petal edges (look closely)
- Opposite leaves with transparent dots
- Hold leaf to light: translucent spots visible
- Stems with two raised ridges running down

## The ID Tests

- Hold leaf up to light: clear gland dots
- Crush flower buds: red-purple oil on fingers
- Red oil = definitive St. John's wort ID
- Black dots on petal margins = confirm

## Where to Find

- Roadsides, disturbed areas, open woods
- Common throughout eastern US; introduced
- Blooms June-August
- Forms large patches in old fields

## Medicinal Uses

- Wound oil: infuse flowers in oil 4-6 weeks
- Apply oil to bruises, burns, nerve pain
- Tea: steep flowers for mild mood support
- Traditional: depression, nerve damage

## Making Infused Oil

- Pack fresh flowers in container with oil
- Olive or any plant oil works
- Leave 2 days in sun; oil turns red
- Apply red oil to wounds and bruises

## Tea Preparation

- Steep fresh or dry flowers 10 minutes
- Mild sweet floral taste
- Drink 1-2 cups; relaxing effect
- Anti-inflammatory properties

## Caution

- Photosensitivity in high doses
- Avoid direct sun after large consumption
- Significant drug interactions
- Safe for external wound use always
