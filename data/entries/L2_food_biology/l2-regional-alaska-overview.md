# Alaska Regional Survival

663,300 sq mi. 2.5x Texas. 730,000 people (80% in 3 cities). 200+ fly-in-only villages.

## Climate Zones

Interior (Fairbanks, Denali): -80°F to +90°F range. 100+ days below 0°F per winter. Semi-arid (10-16 in/yr). Best firewood, worst cold.

Arctic (North Slope): -50°F to +50°F. Near-zero precipitation. Polar night 65+ days. No trees. Oil lamp or no heat.

Southeast (Juneau, Sitka): 30-65°F. 60-200+ in rain/yr. Temperate rainforest. Mild but perpetually wet = hypothermia risk.

Southcentral (Anchorage): Moderate, occasional -20°F. 16 in/yr. Most services available.

## Permafrost Basics

Permafrost = ground frozen year-round, starting 6 in to 3 ft below surface.

- Cannot dig foundations into permafrost (will sink as heat melts it)
- Build on pilings with air gap between floor and ground
- Permanent natural freezer: dig 4-6 ft cache for meat storage
- Identifies itself in summer as boggy, waterlogged tundra (muskeg)

## Subsistence Skills

Summer window is 10-12 weeks. Everything must happen then.

Salmon fishing (Jun-Sep): One good week = entire winter protein. Catch hundreds. Smoke or dry into strips. Sockeye best for drying. King best calories.

Berry harvest (Jul-Aug): Blueberry, salmonberry, lingonberry, crowberry. Dry for winter. Bears compete. Harvest aggressively.

Wild potato (Jul-Aug ONLY): Eat ROOTS ONLY. Seeds contain ODAP neurotoxin (paralysis, death). Chris McCandless died from seeds in 1992. If seeds are visible on plant, do not harvest at all.

Firewood (Jul-Sep): Cut, split, stack 8-12 cords before freeze. Birch = highest BTU. Spruce = most available. Store 2+ weeks indoors before winter.

Moose harvest (Sep-Oct): One moose = 500-800 lb meat = 6-12 months food. Hunt during rut when bulls are vocal. Requires pack frame, raft, and cold weather to preserve.

## Emergency Contacts

Alaska Rescue Coordination Center is primary SAR authority for all of Alaska.

1. AKRCC: 1-800-420-7230 (all SAR)
2. Alaska State Troopers: 907-269-5511
3. USCG District 17: 907-463-2000 (coastal)
4. NWS Alaska: weather.gov/alaska (before you go)

File a float plan. Leave it with someone reliable. State your return date and what to do if you don't check in.

## Winter Gear Minimums

These are not luxuries. Each item has a specific death-prevention role.

Shelter: 4-season tent rated to -40°F or ability to build snow shelter. A tarp alone is not enough.

Sleeping: Bag rated to -40°F minimum. Liner adds 15°F. Sleeping pad R-value 6+ (ground conducts cold faster than air).

Fire: Ferrocerium rod + char cloth + birch bark. Keep in chest pocket (body heat). Backup waterproof matches in sealed container.

Communication: Satellite communicator (Garmin InReach or SPOT) minimum. Satellite phone preferred. PLB as last resort.

Navigation: Paper topo map + compass. GPS fails at -40°F without battery management.

Clothing layers: Wool or synthetic base (no cotton). Insulating mid (down or synthetic). Wind/waterproof shell. Vapor barrier liner in extreme cold. Spare dry gloves.

Fuel: White gas or alcohol stove (propane fails below -10°F). Carry 20% more fuel than calculated. Melting water uses 30-50% of daily fuel.

Food: 3,500-4,500 cal/day minimum in winter (cold increases metabolic demand). High fat content (fat = most calories per oz). Freeze-dried + nuts + hard cheese + chocolate.
