# Pine Inner Bark Bread

## Overview

- Pine cambium (inner bark): edible,
  calorie-containing carbohydrate
- Found between rough outer bark and
  wood -- thin white/cream layer
- Can be eaten raw, cooked, or dried
  and ground to flour
- Best in spring when cambium is moist
  and sweet
- All true pines (genus Pinus) edible

## Harvesting

- Choose large tree, work one side
  only -- do not circle the tree
- Cut small window through outer bark
  with knife
- Peel outer bark away to expose
  white/cream inner layer
- Scrape cambium with spoon or knife
  held perpendicular to trunk
- Harvest from 2-3 foot section max

## Eating Fresh

- Scrape and eat directly (spring best)
- Slightly sweet, moist, pine flavor
- Can be eaten as-is for emergency
- Texture like wet fibrous paper
- Chew well before swallowing

## Making Flour

- Spread scraped cambium flat on rock
- Dry in sun or over fire until
  brittle (1-2 days)
- Grind to powder with two stones
- Sift and regrind for finer texture

## Bread Making

- Mix flour with water to thick paste
- Form thin patties
- Cook on hot flat rock beside fire
- Or wrap around stick and roast
- Bake until firm and slightly brown
