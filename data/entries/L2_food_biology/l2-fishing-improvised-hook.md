# Improvised Fish Hooks

## Overview

- A hook and line is the simplest and
  most effective survival fishing method
- Many materials can become a hook
- Key features: point, bend, barb
- Attach firmly to strongest line
  you can make

## Safety Pin Hook

- Open safety pin fully
- Bend the clasp end to form hook shape
- Use as-is or add a barb notch
- Best for small fish
- Two safety pins linked = bigger hook

## Bone Hook

- Small mammal bone, bird bone, fish bone
- Shape with stone: grind to point
- Cut notch for barb
- Notch end for line attachment
- Soak in water before use: flexible
- Breaks on large fish -- use for small

## Wood Hook

- Dense hardwood: oak, osage, hickory
- Carve hook shape with knife
- V-notch at top for line
- Fire-harden: brown over coals,
  do not char
- Thorns from hawthorn work as hooks
  for very small fish

## Wire Hook

- Stiff wire from fencing, gear
- Bend 90 degrees for hook bend
- Create point with file or stone
- Wrap at shank for line attachment
- Most durable improvised option

## Rigging the Hook

- Lash line to hook with multiple wraps
- Use tight half-hitch knots
- Test before fishing: pull hard
- Place bait to hide hook point
- Check connection after each fish
