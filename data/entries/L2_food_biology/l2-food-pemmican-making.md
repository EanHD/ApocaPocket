# Pemmican Making

## Overview

- Traditional high-energy food of
  Indigenous North Americans
- 3,500+ calories per pound
- Shelf life: months to years if made
  correctly
- Three components: dried meat, fat,
  optional dried berries

## Ingredients and Ratios

- Dried lean meat: 50% by weight
- Rendered beef or bison fat: 50%
- Dried berries: optional, 10-20%
- No water, no salt required
- Lean meats best: venison, bison, elk

## Drying the Meat

- Slice meat thin: 1/4 inch or less
- Slice against grain
- Dry until hard and brittle: 3-5 days
  sun dried or low fire smoke
- Should snap when bent fully dry
- Pound to powder in cloth with rock

## Rendering the Fat

- Leaf fat or kidney fat preferred
- Dice small, heat slowly in pot
- Cook on low until fat is liquid
  and solids (cracklings) settle
- Strain through cloth
- Cool until just liquid but not set

## Combining

- Mix equal weight: meat powder to fat
- Add dried berries if available
- Pour just-liquid fat over meat
- Mix thoroughly while warm
- Press into rectangular blocks or
  pack into gut casing/clean bag

## Storage

- Wrap in cloth or pack in container
- Keep cool and dry
- If made correctly: room temperature
  stable for months
- Test: no sour smell, firm texture
