# Scene Safety Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Rescuers become casualties regularly
- Second victim doubles the problem
- Most hazards are visible if you look
- 10 seconds of assessment = safety
- Emergency tunnel vision is dangerous
- Stop, look, then approach

## The 10-Second Look

- Stop at scene perimeter first
- Scan for ongoing hazard source
- Look up, down, all sides
- Is the hazard contained or active?
- Traffic, fire, structural, chemical
- Only advance when hazard is managed

## Common Scene Hazards

- Moving vehicle traffic
- Live electrical wires
- Unstable structure above or below
- Fire, smoke, fumes present
- Water current still pulling
- Hostile person or animal near

## Assessing Before Entry

- Fire: is smoke color normal?
- Vehicle: is fuel leaking, spark risk?
- Water: current still running?
- Structure: any sounds of shifting?
- Chemical: are others unconscious?
- Cliff: is rock stable above victim?

## Making Scene Safe

- Turn off ignition on wrecked vehicle
- Move victim away from traffic zone
- Cut power before electrical rescue
- Avoid smoke entry without mask
- Shore unstable structure minimally
- Subdue or contain animal threat

## What Can Go Wrong

- Rushing in and inhaling toxic fumes
- Standing under unstable wall
- Entering still-moving water alone
- Touching electrocuted victim

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Complete scene safety check
  verbally in under 10 seconds
- Sign of mastery: Zero unsafe
  approaches in 5 scenario drills
