# Documentation Methods

## Why Document

Memory fails under stress, injury, and age. Write it down or lose it.

- Individual knowledge in one head = knowledge at risk
- Patterns emerge only from recorded data
- Costly mistakes repeat without records

**Core principle:** If it is worth knowing, it is worth recording.

## Writing Materials

**Charcoal on bark:**
- Burn hardwood in smoldering fire, cool, break into sticks
- Best surface: smooth birch bark, peel gently, do not kill the tree
- Best for: temporary notes, maps, sketches

**Clay or wax tablets:**
- Knead clay, form 6 x 4 inch tablets, dry to leather-hard
- Or fill wooden frame with beeswax for reusable surface
- Fire clay tablets for permanence

**Ink on paper or fabric:**
- Oak gall ink: crushed galls + water + iron nails soaked in vinegar = black
- Soot ink: fine soot + water + tree sap
- Tools: carved reed pen, sharpened quill, hair brush on a stick

**Modern priority:** Waterproof notebooks (Rite in the Rain type). Worth the weight.

**Digital rule:** Always back up to physical. Electronics fail.

## Maps and Diagrams

**Every map needs:**
- North arrow, scale in paces or meters
- Landmarks, water sources, hazards, resource caches, safe routes

**Diagrams beat text for:**
- Tool construction with numbered steps and arrows
- Plant anatomy: label edible vs. toxic parts
- Knot stages, trap mechanisms, wound documentation

**Plant ID:** full page per species. Overall shape, leaf detail, flower or seed, scale reference.

## Symbols and Shorthand

Agree on 20-30 symbols. Post reference in common area. Everyone uses the same set.

- Triangle = landmark, tilde = water, X = hazard, plus-circle = cache
- Square = structure, circle = camp, arrow = route, plus = medical
- Checkmark = verified, exclamation = urgent, warning = caution

Update only by group consensus.

## Weather Logs

**Daily entry (2 min, 2-3x per day):** temperature, sky, wind, precipitation.

**Monthly review:** average highs and lows, total precip, storm frequency.

**Result:** Predictive calendar for planting, travel, and safety planning.

## Medical Records

**Individual file:** name, DOB, blood type, allergies, chronic conditions, daily meds.

**Incident log:** date, issue, treatment, provider, outcome, notes.

**Vital signs baseline:** record when healthy. Compare when sick to assess severity.

## Inventory

**Each item:** name, location, quantity, date acquired, expiration, source, condition.

**Update schedule:**
- Weekly: check quantities
- Monthly: full audit
- Immediately: large additions or withdrawals

Rotate stock. Use oldest first. Flag items expiring within 3 months.

## Construction Notes

**Log each project:**
- Materials list with quantities
- Measurements and sketches
- Assembly sequence, numbered
- What worked, what failed, next time notes

Photos or diagrams at each major stage. Future builders need this.

## Preserve Records

- **Water:** sealed container, elevated off floor, silica gel packets inside
- **Light:** UV fades ink, store in dark or opaque container
- **Pests:** insects eat paper, rodents shred it, sealed container is essential

**3-2-1 rule:**
- 3 copies minimum
- 2 formats: paper plus digital
- 1 offsite location

## Quick Reference

**Priority order:**
1. Medical records: allergies, blood type, treatments
2. Resource inventory: what you have and where
3. Maps: where things are
4. Weather logs: predict patterns
5. Construction notes: replicate success

**Write at minimum:**
- Who: name, blood type, allergies
- Where: coordinates or landmarks
- What: core resources
- How: fire, water, first aid

**Memory fails. Paper persists. Document now, survive later.**
