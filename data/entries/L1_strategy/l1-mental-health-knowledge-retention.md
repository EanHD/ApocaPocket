# Knowledge Retention

In survival scenarios, you cannot Google things. What you remember is what you have. Master retention now.

## The Forgetting Curve

Ebbinghaus (1885): Humans forget predictably without reinforcement.
- 24 hours: 50% gone.
- 48 hours: 70% gone.
- 1 week: 90% gone.

Each review dramatically slows the curve. After 4-5 reviews, information moves to long-term memory.

Plan to review. Front-load reviews early. Review before you forget, not after.

## Spaced Repetition

Review too soon = wasted time. Review too late = already forgotten. Space reviews optimally.

Leitner System (paper flashcards):
- Set up 5 boxes: Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, Day 30, Mastered.
- New cards start in Box 1.
- Got it right -> move to next box.
- Got it wrong -> back to Box 1.
- Review each box on its schedule.

Time investment: 10-20 cards in 15 minutes. Retention: 85-95% long-term.

What to put in cards:
- Critical identification (plants, threats, landmarks)
- Procedures (medical protocols, radio codes)
- Facts (dosages, measurements, formulas)
- Language vocabulary and phrases

## Teaching Others

Teaching = forced retrieval + organization + simplification. All three cement knowledge.

The Feynman Technique:
- Choose a concept you want to retain.
- Explain it in simple terms as if to a child.
- Identify where you struggled to explain.
- Go back, fill the gaps, simplify further.

Teaching gives 90% retention vs 50% from reading alone.

Study buddy system: Partner with someone. Alternate teaching each other. 15 min teach, 5 min questions, 15 min swap.

## Practice Schedules

Consistency beats intensity. 15 minutes per day outperforms 2 hours per week.

Daily practice (15 min):
- 5 min: Review yesterday's material.
- 5 min: New material.
- 5 min: Flash through older cards.

Weekly deep dive (60 min):
- Review entire week's material.
- Practice hands-on application.
- Identify and drill weak spots.

Monthly audit (2 hours):
- Review all flashcards from the month.
- Test real-world application.
- Update or remove outdated information.

## Testing Mastery

Test at three levels:

Level 1  -  Recognition: Can you identify it when you see it? Standard: 90%+ correct.

Level 2  -  Recall: Can you produce it from memory? Standard: 80%+ correct.

Level 3  -  Application: Can you use it in the field under realistic conditions? Standard: 3 successes in a row.

Stress testing: Test under time pressure, fatigue, and distraction. Knowledge under stress is truly retained knowledge.

## Physical Practice

Motor skills require physical repetition, not just mental review. Different neural pathways.

Deliberate practice structure:
- Slow motion: Execute at quarter speed to build correct pattern.
- Full speed: Execute at normal pace.
- Variable conditions: Execute in different circumstances.
- Automaticity: Execute while distracted (talking, under stress).

Time to automaticity:
- Simple skill: 50-100 reps.
- Complex skill: 500-1000 reps.

Maintenance schedule:
- Critical or complex skills: Practice weekly.
- Moderate skills: Practice monthly.
- Simple or stable skills: Practice quarterly.

## Triage System

Not all knowledge deserves equal attention.

Tier 1  -  Critical (review weekly):
- Life-or-death skills (first aid, threats)
- Core survival (water, fire, shelter)

Tier 2  -  Important (review monthly):
- Foraging, hunting, navigation
- Tool maintenance

Tier 3  -  Useful (review quarterly):
- Comfort improvements
- Optimization techniques

## Review Cycles Summary

Daily (5-10 min): Critical current learning. Flashcard queue.

Weekly (30-60 min): All daily items. Hands-on skill practice.

Monthly (2-3 hours): Full skill testing. Update documentation.

Yearly (8-12 hours): Comprehensive review. Full practical test.

## Key Principle

The difference between knowing and remembering is the review schedule. Build it. Follow it. Use it or lose it.
