# Children in Disaster

## Age-Appropriate Roles

Under 5: play, rest, stay near a trusted adult. No tasks.
Ages 5-7: simple fetch tasks, carry light items, sort supplies.
Ages 8-11: monitoring tasks, prep work, help younger children.
Ages 12+: adult-level tasks under supervision, navigation, first aid.
Always explain WHY. Children cooperate better when they understand.
Assign jobs as dignity  -  it helps them feel useful and safe.

## Keeping Children Calm

Your emotional state is contagious. Regulate yourself first.
- Calm, steady voice even when you don't feel it
- Maintain predictable routines: meals, bedtime, activities
- Honest simple answers: "We're staying safe. Here's our plan."
- No graphic details about the threat
- Physical contact: hugs, holding hands  -  this anchors them
- Let them ask questions. Never shut them down.

## Signs of Trauma

Watch for these in days after an event:
- Regression: bedwetting, thumb-sucking, baby talk in older children
- Extreme clinginess or complete withdrawal
- Nightmares, sleep refusal, or night terrors
- Explosive anger or total emotional flatness
- Stomachaches or headaches with no physical cause
These are normal responses. Do not punish. Provide safety.

## Educational Tasks

Occupied minds reduce anxiety.
- Reading, writing, drawing  -  anything structured
- Teach practical skills: water purification, fire safety, plant ID
- Math with real objects: counting rations, measuring distances
- Story-telling: describe today's events in order
- Assign an older child as teacher for younger ones
Learning restores agency when the world feels out of control.

## When to Evacuate

Evacuate immediately if:
- Location is structurally unsafe or will become unsafe
- Food or water for children runs out within 24 hours
- Medical care the child needs is not available here
- Security threat is imminent and movement is safer than staying
Do NOT move through unknown terrain at night with small children.
Know each child's weight. Plan how each is carried if they cannot walk.
