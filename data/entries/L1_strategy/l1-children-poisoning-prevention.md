# Poison Prevention

## Common Poisons

Survival settings increase children's access to dangerous substances:
- Medications: iron supplements, pain relievers, heart meds
- Cleaning chemicals: bleach, ammonia, drain cleaner
- Fuels: kerosene, gasoline, lighter fluid, lamp oil
- Plants: unknown berries, mushrooms, leaves (assume toxic)
- Rodent bait, pesticides, antifreeze (sweet taste attracts children)
Lock or height-secure ALL of these. Out of sight is not enough.

## Signs of Poisoning

- Sudden drooling, vomiting, or strange breath odor
- Pinpoint or extremely large pupils
- Extreme drowsiness, unresponsiveness, or seizures
- Burns, redness, or swelling around mouth (chemical ingestion)
- Skin color change: very pale, blue lips, or flushed
Act immediately. Do not wait for symptoms to worsen.

## Immediate Response

1. Stay calm. Identify what was taken and how much.
2. Unconscious and not breathing: begin CPR.
3. Conscious and breathing: call Poison Control now.
4. Do NOT give food, milk, or water unless Poison Control directs.
5. Do NOT induce vomiting. (See final section.)
6. Bring the container or plant sample to medical care.
7. Note the exact time of ingestion.

## Poison Control

US: 1-800-222-1222. Save this number offline now.
Tell them: child's age and weight, substance name and amount,
time since ingestion, and current symptoms.
Follow their instructions exactly. They are the authority.
If no phone access: go to the nearest emergency facility immediately.

## Do NOT Induce Vomiting

Default: NEVER induce vomiting.
- Caustic substances (bleach, drain cleaner) burn worse coming back up
- Petroleum products (fuel, lamp oil) can enter lungs if vomited
Exception: induce ONLY if Poison Control or a doctor explicitly directs.
If vomiting occurs on its own: turn child on their side to prevent choking.
