# Boil Water Advisory

## What This Covers
Safe water use when authorities issue a boil water advisory.

## Why BWA Is Issued
- Water main break or pressure loss.
- Contamination detected in system.
- Treatment facility failure.
- Flooding near water supply.
- Assume bacteria or pathogens present until lifted.

## What Needs Boiling
- Drinking and cooking.
- Making ice.
- Brushing teeth.
- Pets and livestock.
- Baby formula  -  critical.

## How to Boil
- Bring to a rolling boil (large bubbles throughout).
- Boil 1 full minute at sea level.
- Boil 3 full minutes above 2000m (6500 ft) altitude.
- Let cool before drinking. Store in clean covered container.

## Do NOT Use Tap For
- Baby formula or infant food.
- Coffee makers and ice machines  -  they don't boil.
- Any automatic appliance that uses tap water.

## Bathing Rules
- Adults: tap water for bathing is OK.
- Do not swallow  -  keep mouth closed.
- Infants and toddlers: use bottled or boiled water.
- Open wounds: use boiled or bottled water only.

## Bottled Water Use
- Safe to use without boiling.
- Stock minimum 1 gallon per person per day.
- 3-day supply minimum; 2 weeks is better.

## Advisory Is Lifted
- Run cold taps 2 minutes per faucet to flush.
- Flush refrigerator water dispenser and ice maker.
- Run water heater through one full cycle.
- Discard any ice made during advisory.

## Contamination Signs
- Sulfur or chemical smell from tap.
- Yellow, brown, or cloudy color.
- Visible particles in water.
- Home bacteria test kits: 24-48 hr results, not instant.
