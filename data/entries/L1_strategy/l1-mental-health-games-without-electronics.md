# Games Without Electronics

Depression is as deadly as dehydration. Boredom in survival scenarios increases depression risk 5x. Play is not frivolous  -  it is a survival tool.

Play releases dopamine and endorphins. Social play reduces cortisol by 23-47%. Games maintain cognitive function and social bonds. Groups that stop playing show increased conflict within 2 weeks.

## Card Games

Poker (Texas Hold'em):
- Players: 2-10. Time: 30-90 min.
- Teaches risk assessment, emotional control, and reading people.

Rummy:
- Players: 2-6. Time: 15-30 min.
- Fast-paced, easy to learn, good for mixed ages.
- Variations: Gin rummy, canasta, 500 rum.

Bridge:
- Players: 4. Time: 60-90 min.
- Highest cognitive complexity. Builds teamwork and communication.

Solitaire (for solo play):
- Types: Klondike, Spider, FreeCell.
- Prevents rumination and depression when alone.

Waterproofing cards: Coat with candle wax or beeswax. Repair splits with clear tape.

## Board Games

Nine Men's Morris  -  ancient strategy game:
- Materials: 24 marks on a board, 9 stones per player in 2 colors.
- Draw the board on dirt, wood, or bark with charcoal.
- Rules: Place stones, form mills (3 in a row), remove opponent stones.
- Win by reducing opponent to 2 stones or blocking all moves.
- Time: 20-40 min. Age 6+.

Mancala  -  count-and-capture:
- Materials: 12-14 holes (dig in ground or use egg carton), 48 seeds or stones.
- Rules: Pick up stones from pit, drop one in each pit counterclockwise.
- Time: 10-20 min. Age 5+. Teaches counting and forward planning.

Go  -  ultimate strategic depth:
- Materials: 19x19 grid drawn on any surface, ~180 stones per player.
- Simplest rules, deepest strategy. Play a lifetime without mastery.
- Start with 9x9 board for faster games. Time: 30-180 min.

## Dice Games

Making dice: Cut cube from wood or bone. Mark dots 1-6 (opposite sides sum to 7). Test balance by floating in salt water.

Yahtzee: 2-6 players, 5 dice. Score combinations. Time: 20-30 min. Teaches probability.

Liar's Dice: 2-6 players, 5 dice per player in cups. Bluffing game. High social interaction.

Pig (push your luck): 2+ players, 1 die. Roll and add score, but if you roll a 1, lose all points this turn. Time: 10-15 min. Good for teaching probability to children.

## Physical Games

Kubb (Viking Chess):
- Build: Cut branches into blocks (10cm x 15cm) and throwing sticks (30cm).
- Field: 5m x 8m. Players: 2-12 in teams.
- Throws sticks to knock down opponent blocks, then the king.

Horseshoes:
- 2 stakes 40 feet apart, 4 horseshoes or rope rings.
- Players: 2-4. Relaxing, social, easy for all ages. Adjust distance for children.

Capture the Flag:
- Players: 8-30 in two teams. Two territories, flags, prison area.
- Teaches teamwork, strategy, leadership. Exhausts players (improves sleep).

Tag variations:
- Classic Tag, Freeze Tag, Shadow Tag (step on shadows), Flashlight Tag (night version).
- Essential for cardiovascular exercise and social bonding. Age 4+.

## Children's Games

Hide and Seek: Problem-solving, patience, spatial awareness. Variation: Sardines (one hides, all seek).

Simon Says: Listening skills, impulse control. Good for 3-8 year olds.

Storytelling rounds: Each person adds one sentence. Builds language, imagination, and group cohesion.

## Game Night Protocol

Schedule weekly on the same day and time. Predictability provides comfort.

Structure:
- Quick warm-up game (15 min).
- Main game (45-90 min).
- Snacks and discussion.
- Second game or story time.

Rotating roles: Host, scorekeeper, timekeeper, rule arbiter. Builds leadership and prevents hierarchy rigidity.

Inclusion: Pair experienced with new players. Always offer cooperative or solo games alongside competitive ones.

## Warning Signs

Community red flags:
- No spontaneous laughter during games.
- People avoiding game nights.
- Increased arguments over rules.

Individual red flags:
- Cannot concentrate on simple games.
- No joy in winning.
- Refusing all game invitations.

Action: These are depression and trauma symptoms. Offer low-pressure simple games. Follow up privately.

## Minimum Kit

Essential:
- 2 decks of cards.
- 5 dice.
- Charcoal, sticks, and stones (to make any game).

Second tier:
- Chess or Go set.
- Physical game equipment (balls, horseshoes).
- Paper for scorekeeping and game-making.

Making games from scratch is more meaningful than found items and teaches crafting skills.
