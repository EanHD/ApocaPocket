# Night Survival

## Why It Matters

- Night is disorienting and dangerous
- Most survival injuries happen at night
- Dark travel leads to getting more lost
- Night sounds give critical information
- Knowing when to stop is a skill
- Pre-dark shelter is a top priority

## Dark Adaptation

- Full dark adaptation takes 30 minutes
- Use red light to preserve night vision
- Do not look at flames before travel
- Close one eye near bright light source
- Peripheral vision is better at night
- Slow down and use it

## Travel vs Stay

- Default is STAY until daylight
- Travel only if: hypothermia risk now
- Travel only if: water or rescue near
- Do not travel if injured or uncertain
- Night travel: triple injury risk
- Mark direction before starting

## Reading Night Sounds

- Rushing water = stream/waterfall
- Traffic sounds at distance = road
- Dog barking = farmstead or village
- Train sounds = track within 2 miles
- Insects silencing = large animal near
- Wind through gap = canyon or ridge

## Night Camp Safety

- Keep fire small and controlled
- Use headlamp only when needed
- Keep footwear inside shelter
- Secure loose gear before sleep
- Know shelter exit direction
- Keep boots accessible for emergency

## What Can Go Wrong

- Travel in dark after wrong turn
- Campfire flare ruins adaptation
- Missing path edge: ankle/leg injury
- Wild animal approaches dark camp

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Navigate 500 m at night
  using stars and sound only
- Sign of mastery: Camp established
  and secured 1 hr before dark
