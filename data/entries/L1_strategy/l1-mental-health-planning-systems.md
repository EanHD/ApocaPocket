# Planning Systems

## Why Planning Works

- Reactive = survive; proactive = thrive
- Spend 10% of time planning to make the other 90% effective
- Good planning converts crises into foreseeable problems with prepared responses

## SMART Goals

- **Specific:** What exactly? Who? Where?
- **Measurable:** How will you know you succeeded?
- **Achievable:** Do you have the resources and realistic timeline?
- **Relevant:** Does this serve your current priorities?
- **Time-bound:** When does it start and finish?
- Vague: "Get better at foraging" -> SMART: "Identify 10 local edible plants by month-end (1 hr every 3 days)"

## Goal Hierarchy

- Mission (5-10 yr) -> Yearly -> Quarterly -> Monthly -> Weekly tasks
- Each level breaks down the one above; completing weekly goals = hitting monthly goals

## Daily Planning

- Spend 10 min the night before; list all tasks; mark top 3 priorities
- Time-block specific tasks to specific hours; include meals, breaks, buffer time
- The 50% rule: tasks take twice as long as estimated — plan 4-6 productive hours/day, not 12
- Top 3 rule: if top 3 are done, the day is a success; everything else is bonus

## Weekly Planning

- Sunday review (1 hr): what went well, what didn't, what are next week's priorities
- Set 3-5 measurable weekly goals; assign big tasks to specific days
- Identify resource needs early in week; schedule training and maintenance (prevent crises)

## Eisenhower Matrix

- Urgent + Important -> DO NOW (crisis)
- Not Urgent + Important -> SCHEDULE (where growth happens)
- Urgent + Not Important -> DELEGATE or minimize
- Neither -> ELIMINATE ruthlessly
- Trap: living in crisis forever. Fix: invest time in scheduling and prevention

## Project Management

- Large project -> phases -> tasks -> subtasks, each with owner and deadline
- Identify the critical path (longest sequence) and optimize it first
- Sequential tasks must wait (construction before testing, planting before harvesting)
- Parallel tasks run simultaneously (lumber cutting + stone gathering at same time)
- Set milestones at 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% — celebrate each one (morale matters)

## Contingency Plans

- Identify single points of failure: sole water source, sole fire-starter, key person
- For each: likelihood x severity = priority; have Plans B, C, D with clear trigger conditions
- Two is one, one is none — three is comfortable
- Worst case needs a minimum viable response, not obsession

## Resource Allocation

- High-risk phase: 90% immediate / 10% short-term / 0% long-term
- Stable phase: 50% immediate / 30% short-term / 20% long-term
- Energy follows time: morning (high) = hardest tasks; evening (low) = routine/planning
- Track each critical resource: current stock, consumption rate, resupply plan

## Group Coordination

- Daily stand-up (10 min): what I did, what I'm doing, what's blocking me
- Weekly meeting (1 hr): wins, challenges, next-week priorities, task assignments
- Document decisions (who, what, deadline) — memory fails; written record = clarity
- Rotate roles: cross-training prevents bottlenecks and single points of failure

## Quick Reference

- Daily: review yesterday -> top 3 priorities -> time-block -> prep tools
- Weekly: review wins/fails -> set 3-5 goals -> assign to days -> identify resource needs
- Resource split: 70% immediate / 20% short-term / 10% long-term (adjust to risk level)
- Best plan: the one you actually follow — start simple, improve over time
