# Fermentation & Brewing

## Alcohol Risk

Alcohol can boost morale and social bonds. It can also destroy communities. Read this before brewing.

**Safe use:**
- Maximum 1-2 standard drinks per day
- Not daily (3-4 days per week max)
- Zero during active crisis: impaired judgment kills
- Zero for children and pregnant people

**Warning signs of problem drinking:**
- Drinking alone regularly
- Drinking to cope with stress
- Need more for the same effect (tolerance)
- Shaking or anxiety when not drinking
- Neglecting work or family

**Survival heightens risk:** chronic stress, trauma, isolation, and boredom all increase addiction risk.

**If someone develops a problem:** community addresses it, support not punishment, gradual reduction (cold turkey from severe alcoholism can be fatal).

**Bottom line:** Brew as a cultural and social tool. Alcoholism is a community killer. Use mindfully.

## Why Ferment

Fermentation predates agriculture. Every culture does it.

- Preservation: fermented food lasts months to years
- Nutrition: increases B vitamins, probiotics, digestibility
- Safety: alcohol and acid kill pathogens
- Social: shared fermentation = bonding ritual
- Trade: skill plus time plus desirability = real barter value

## Simple Beer

Lower alcohol (3-6%), nutritious, easiest to make. Historical workers' staple.

**Ingredients for 5 gallons:**
- Malt: 8-10 lbs malted grain (sprouted then dried = starch converted to sugar)
- Hops: 1-2 oz for bitterness; substitute yarrow or mugwort if unavailable
- Yeast: wild (capture from air) or save from previous batch
- Water: 5-6 gallons, clean and boiled

**Process:**
1. Mash: heat 3 gallons water to 150-160 F, add crushed malt, hold 60-90 min. Result: sweet wort.
2. Lauter: strain out grain, rinse with hot water, collect 6 gallons wort.
3. Boil: boil wort 60-90 min, add hops early for bitterness, late for flavor.
4. Cool: cool to 60-70 F quickly using cold water bath or cold stream.
5. Ferment: transfer to vessel, add yeast, seal with airlock or cloth.
6. Wait: primary fermentation 7 days (vigorous bubbling), secondary 7-14 days (slows, clarifies).
7. Package: bottle with priming sugar for carbonation, or drink fresh.

**Total time: 2-4 weeks. Wild yeast works but flavors are unpredictable.**

## Wine and Mead

**Wine (1 gallon):**
- 3-4 lbs fruit (grapes ideal, any fruit works), 2-3 lbs sugar, water to fill
- Crush fruit, combine all, ferment 2-4 weeks (stir daily, strain fruit after week 1)
- Secondary 2-3 months in sealed vessel, bottle when bubbling stops
- Age 3-12 months for smoother flavor

**Mead (1 gallon):**
- 3-4 lbs honey + water to 1 gallon + yeast
- No boil needed. Mix, pitch yeast, ferment 2-4 weeks, secondary 1-3 months.
- Age 6-12 months. Improves dramatically with time.
- Oldest alcohol known, evidence from 7000 BCE.

## Non-Alcoholic Ferments

Equally valuable. No addiction risk. Excellent gut health.

**Kombucha:**
- Brew sweet tea (4 cups water, 2 tea bags, 1/4 cup sugar), cool, add SCOBY with starter liquid
- Cover with cloth, ferment 7-10 days until tangy
- SCOBY reproduces continuously, share with others

**Kefir:**
- Add 1-2 tbsp kefir grains to 2 cups milk, ferment 12-24 hours
- Strain out grains (reuse immediately), drink the kefir
- Grains grow and multiply, very forgiving

**Lacto-fermented vegetables (sauerkraut, kimchi):**
- Shred vegetables, mix with 2% salt by weight, pack tightly into jar
- Weight down under brine, cover with cloth
- Ferment 1-4 weeks at room temperature
- Result: probiotic, shelf-stable vegetables

## Community Protocols

**Occasions for alcohol:** celebrations, communal meals, rituals, trade.

**Avoid alcohol during:** active emergency, conflict, early acute grief, solo regular drinking.

**Serving norms:**
- Beer: 12 oz servings. Wine: 4-5 oz. Mead: 2-3 oz.
- Toasts and shared vessels build group cohesion
- Elders monitor and intervene if someone is over-consuming
- No alcohol for children

**Gut health goal:** include daily lacto-fermented food and weekly probiotic drink.
70% of the immune system is in the gut. Gut bacteria affect mood and anxiety directly.

## Mission

1. Learn one alcoholic ferment: beer, wine, or mead
2. Learn one non-alcoholic ferment: kombucha, kefir, or sauerkraut
3. Establish community protocols: when, how much, what triggers intervention
4. Monitor for problems: early intervention saves lives
5. Practice moderation and model it visibly

**Brew with wisdom. Drink with caution. A drunk community is a dead community.**
