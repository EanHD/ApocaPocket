**Rule of 3s:** 3 min without air -> 3 hrs without shelter (harsh conditions) -> 3 days without water -> 3 weeks without food.

## Decision Check
- STOP  -  sit down, stop moving
- BREATHE  -  3 deep breaths
- OBSERVE  -  weather, terrain, injuries, threats (5 min minimum)
- PLAN  -  what will end me fastest? Address that first
- ACT  -  complete one priority task before starting another

## Minutes 0-60

**Minutes 0-5  -  Injury triage:**
- Severe bleeding: tourniquet or direct pressure now
- Not breathing: CPR if viable
- Chest wound: seal with plastic and tape
- Shock (pale, cold, rapid pulse): lay flat, keep warm

**Minutes 5-15  -  Environmental threat:**
- Cold/wet: hypothermia possible in 1-6 hours
- Heat/sun: heat stroke in 3-8 hours without shade and water
- Nightfall: hours until dark = hard deadline for shelter

**Minutes 15-60  -  Stay or move?**
- Stay if: weather threatening, injured, lost, search party likely
- Move if: know location of help <2 hours away, current site is immediately deadly
- **Default: stay put**  -  moving people are harder to find and often get more lost

## Hours 1-3: Shelter
Build minimum viable shelter  -  not perfect, just enough to survive the night.

**Cold/wet:** floor insulation 1-2 ft dry debris - wind-blocking walls - waterproof roof - small to trap heat - entry away from wind (1-2 hrs solo)
**Hot/dry:** shade structure, ventilated, near water

- Location: flat, dry, away from dead trees and flood zones
- Test size (lie down inside to verify)
- Do not abandon shelter to start another task

## Hours 3-6: Water
Dehydration is critical at 72 hrs; cognitive decline starts at 24 hrs.
- Find: scan valleys, green vegetation, converging animal trails, sound of running water
- Clean stream or spring: drink to restore function
- Questionable source: plan purification (boil requires fire)
- Risk calculation: giardia in 1-2 weeks vs death in 3 days

## Hours 6-12: Fire
Fire is not urgent if weather is warm, water is clean, and shelter is complete.
- Gather tinder first: dry grass, inner bark, pine needles  -  fist-sized bundle
- Kindling: pencil-thick dry twigs  -  two armfuls
- Fuel: wrist-to-arm-thick dry wood  -  large pile
- Lighter/matches first - ferro rod second - friction fire only if experienced
- Do not spend hours on fire if daylight is running out and shelter is incomplete

## Hours 12-24
- 3 of anything = universal distress (whistle blasts, fires, shouts)
- Ground signal: SOS in rocks or logs, letters 3+ meters tall
- Separate fire, food prep, and sleep areas; hang food in bear country
- Latrine 200+ feet from water
- Set micro-goals; assign roles in groups; check in on emotional state

## Decision Matrix
- Arctic/winter: shelter -> fire -> water (melt snow, never eat snow) -> signal -> food
- Desert: shade/shelter + water (equal priority) -> signal -> food
- Temperate: shelter if cold rain -> water -> fire -> signal -> food
- Coastal: shelter from tides -> freshwater (saltwater is poison) -> fire -> food

## Fatal Mistakes
1. Panic or freeze paralysis  -  force STOP-BREATHE-OBSERVE protocol
2. Wrong priority sequence  -  hypothermia is fatal in hours; food waits weeks
3. Task-switching before completion  -  finish shelter before starting fire
4. Postponing water  -  dehydration impairs judgment fast
5. Fire obsession at expense of shelter  -  night + no shelter + no fire = hypothermia
6. Sweating in cold (overexertion)  -  pace yourself, remove layers before hard work
7. Moving while lost  -  stay put, signal, wait
8. Suppressing mental state  -  acknowledge fear, allow brief release, refocus
