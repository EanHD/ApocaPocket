# Well Digging

## Overview

- Hand-dug well: 3-4 ft diameter, dug by hand
- Reach water table: depth varies by region
- Typical depth: 10-50 ft; may be more
- Casing prevents collapse and contamination
- Cover and apron prevent surface water entry
- Uphill distance from latrine: 100 ft min
- Never dig alone; always have surface crew

## Site Selection

- Downhill from latrines, barnyards, graves
- Uphill from buildings if possible
- Away from surface water drainage paths
- Test soil for clay layer above water table:
  clay layer protects from contamination
- Consult locals for historic well locations
- Check neighbors' wells for depth guide

## Gas Testing

- Before entry, lower a lit candle on cord
- If flame goes out: dangerous gas present
- Ventilate by pumping air down with bellows
- Retest before each entry into deep well
- Do not enter if candle extinguishes
- Assistant at surface monitors at all times

## Digging Method

- Start 4 ft diameter to allow casing
- Bucket on rope hauls spoil to surface
- Tripod and windlass for deep work
- Dig in morning when cooler
- Shore walls with timber sets if soil soft
- Install casing as you descend, not after

## Casing Options

- Dry-stone or mortared stone ring
- Fired clay rings (purpose-made)
- Split wood staves (barrel method)
- Lay stones as you dig; dig ahead of casing
- Last 2 ft of casing left open for water
  inflow; no mortar at water zone

## Finishing

- Apron: concrete or clay 3 ft around well
- Slope apron away from well
- Well cover: wood or stone, tight-fitting
- Rope and bucket or install hand pump
- Disinfect new well: add chlorine solution
  stir, let stand, pump out before use

## Long-Term Value

- Independent water supply
- Reduces waterborne disease dramatically
- Community well serves many households
- Inspect and clean well annually
- Yields can be increased by deepening
