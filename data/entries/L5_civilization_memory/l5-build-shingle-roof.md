# Wood Shingle Roof

## Overview

- Shingles: sawn; shakes: split by hand
- Shakes thicker, more rustic, longer life
- Best wood: western red cedar, pine,
  white oak, black locust, cypress
- Minimum pitch: 4:12 (4 in rise per 12)
- Triple coverage at eave; double above
- Life span: 20-50 years depending on wood
- No metal fasteners needed; wood pegs work

## Splitting Shakes

- Use straight-grained bolt of wood
- Bolt: section 18-24 in long, split from log
- Froe and mallet: split along grain
- Thickness: 3/8 to 3/4 in at butt
- Width: 4-8 in; irregular is fine
- Stack to dry before installation

## Roof Structure

- Rafters at 16-24 in on center
- Sheathing: spaced boards or solid deck
- Spaced sheathing allows air circulation;
  reduces rot under shakes
- Space boards 1.5 in apart for shakes
- Solid deck needed for shingles
- Fascia board at eave for starter row

## Installation

- Starter row: two layers at eave, offset
- Exposure: how much of each shingle shows
- 18-in shingle: 5.5 in exposure typical
- 24-in shake: 8-10 in exposure typical
- Offset joints: each shingle bridges gap
  below by at least 1.5 in (no straight runs)
- Fasten 2 nails per shingle, above exposure

## Ridge

- Alternate overlapping ridge cap shingles
- Cut shingles to desired cap width
- Alternate overlap direction each row
- Last course nails covered by cap
- Seal any gaps with clay or pitch if needed
- Ridge is first to fail; inspect yearly

## Maintenance

- Remove moss: dilute vinegar or scraping
- Replace cracked or missing shakes
  promptly; water enters fast
- Inspect flashings at chimney and walls
- Allow airflow in attic; reduces moisture
- Cedar oils naturally resist rot; pine less so

## Long-Term Value

- Entirely forest materials, no mining
- Riven shakes need only froe and mallet
- Every skilled carpenter can install
- Beautiful and proven over centuries
- Fallen shakes: useful kindling
