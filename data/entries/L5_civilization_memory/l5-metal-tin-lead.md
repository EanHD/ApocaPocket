# Tin and Lead Uses

## Overview

- Tin melts at 450F; very low melting point
- Lead melts at 621F; soft, dense, flexible
- Both easily cast without complex furnace
- Solder: 60% tin, 40% lead (melts 370F)
- Neither hardens steel; alloyed for use
- Lead: radiation shielding, fishing weights,
  musket balls, roofing, flashing
- Tin: food can lining, bronze alloy addition

## Tin Uses

- Tin plate: dip iron in molten tin to coat
- Prevents rusting on food containers
- Solder component (see below)
- Bearing alloy (babbitt): see bronze entry
- Tin cups and utensils: safe food contact
- Tin is food-safe unlike lead
- Pewter: tin + small lead/antimony

## Lead Uses (Non-Food)

- Roof flashing: hammered into channels
- Pipe seals: poured lead caulking
- Fishing weights: poured in molds
- Wheel balancing weights
- Musket balls and shot: poured in molds
- Battery plates if galvanic cells needed
- Never for water supply pipes or food

## Soldering

- Solder: 60% tin, 40% lead; low melt
- Flux required: pine resin, zinc chloride
- Apply flux to clean metal surface
- Heat metal with iron (not flame directly)
- Touch solder to joint when hot enough
  for solder to flow freely
- Wipe clean immediately; cool; test pull
- Clean flux residue to prevent corrosion

## Casting Shot

- Lead shot for firearms: drop shot method
- Melt lead; add 1% arsenic for hardness
- Pour through perforated pan 20 ft high
- Drops form spheres as they fall
- Land in water tank at bottom; round shot
- Slow drip: larger shot; fast pour: smaller
- Sieve to sort sizes after drying

## Lead Sheet and

- Roll or hammer lead to flat sheet
- Lead roof flashing: very long lasting
- Pound lead sheet around irregular joints
- Lead is soft; shapes to any contour
- Lasts 100+ years when not flexed
- Oxidizes to grey: self-sealing surface
- Use only for non-potable water contact

## Long-Term Value

- Solder enables metal joining without forge
- Lead and tin available from local ores
- Shot casting: simple skill, critical use
- Pewter tableware practical and safe
- Both metals salvageable from scrap
