# Thatching a Roof

## Overview

- Materials: water reed, long straw, wheat,
  cattail, heather, palm fronds
- Minimum roof pitch: 45 degrees (1:1)
- Steeper pitch sheds water faster
- Layers overlap; only tips exposed to rain
- Thickness at eave: 12-16 in minimum
- Properly done: 20-40 year lifespan
- Most practiced roofing in human history

## Materials

- Water reed (Phragmites): best lifespan
- Long straw: widely available, shorter life
- Wheat or rye harvested before full thresh
- Bundles (yealms): 6-8 in diameter
- Harvest at full height before frost
- Store dry; damp thatch rots before use

## Roof Structure

- Rafters spaced 18-24 in on center
- Battens horizontal across rafters
- Batten spacing: 8-10 in vertically
- Battens bear weight of thatch bundles
- Ridge must be continuous and strong
- Roof plane must be flat; humps show

## Laying Thatch

- Start at eave, work toward ridge
- Fix bundles butt-end down at eave
- Subsequent courses: butt over tips below
- Each course advances 6-8 in up slope
- Fix with hazel sways (rods) and spars
- Spars: twisted hazel pins, S-shaped
- Drive spars through sway into battens

## Ridge Work

- Bundle and shape ridge separately
- Cap ridge with straw or sedge bundles
- Secure with legget and pins
- Ornamental patterns possible at ridge
- Ridge is highest-wear area; inspect yearly
- Ridge may need renewal before main coat

## Dressing

- Legget: paddle tool to align thatch face
- Dress each course before moving up
- Even face sheds water uniformly
- Trim eave and verge edges after laying
- Check for gaps; birds exploit them
- Net over new thatch in spring: bird deterrent

## Long-Term Value

- Excellent insulation: R-value rivals
  modern insulation at 12 in thickness
- Entirely local materials in most climates
- Beautiful and culturally meaningful
- Skilled thatcher trains apprentice in
  one to two seasons
