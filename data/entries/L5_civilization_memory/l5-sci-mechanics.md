# Applied Mechanics

## Overview

- Mechanics: study of force and motion
- Six simple machines: lever, wheel/axle,
  pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw
- All reduce force needed or change
  direction of force; energy conserved
- Work = force x distance (always equal)
- Understanding mechanics multiplies
  human capability with no fuel required

## Force and Work

- Force: push or pull (pounds or Newtons)
- Work: force times distance moved
- Work in = work out (ignoring friction)
- Lift 100 lb with lever: apply less force
  over longer distance; same total work
- Friction is always present; reduces
  efficiency; lubricate all moving parts

## The Lever

- Three classes based on fulcrum position
- Class 1: fulcrum between load and effort
  (pry bar, seesaw, scissors)
- Class 2: load between fulcrum and effort
  (wheelbarrow, nutcracker)
- Class 3: effort between fulcrum and load
  (tweezers, fishing rod)
- Mechanical advantage = effort arm / load arm

## Pulley and Block

- Single pulley: changes direction only
- Two pulleys (block and tackle): halves
  force needed; doubles rope travel
- Add more pulleys: further multiplication
- 5:1 block: one person lifts heavy log
- Account for rope friction loss
- Used for: raising framing, lifting loads

## Inclined Plane

- Ramp: trade distance for force reduction
- Load / slope fraction = force needed
- 1 ft rise per 3 ft run: need 1/3 of
  load weight as pushing force
- Shallower angle: less force, more travel
- Screw: inclined plane wrapped around rod
- Wedge: two inclined planes back to back

## Wheel and Axle

- Large wheel drives small axle: force
  multiplied (ratio = wheel/axle diameter)
- Windlass, capstan, winch all this class
- Gear pairs: tooth counts give ratio
- Large gear driving small: speed increase
- Small gear driving large: force increase
- Critical for mills, pumps, machinery

## Long-Term Value

- Every machine analyzed as combinations
  of six simple machines
- Understanding prevents foolish design
- Design machines on paper before building
- Friction calculations save maintenance
- Physics does not change after collapse
