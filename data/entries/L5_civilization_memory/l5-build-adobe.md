# Adobe Brick Making

## Overview

- Adobe: clay-rich soil + straw or grass fiber
- No kiln required; sun-dried only
- Standard brick: 10x14x4 in (varies regionally)
- Bricks laid with mud mortar, same mix
- Walls typically 12-18 in thick
- Excellent thermal mass for hot climates
- Fragile when wet; protect from rain

## Soil Testing

- Squeeze ball, drop 3 ft; cracks OK, not shatters
- Ribbon test for clay content (see Cob entry)
- Target 20-30% clay; amend as needed
- Too sandy: bricks crumble at corners
- Too much clay: shrinkage cracks appear
- Mix test bricks before full production

## Making the Mix

- Add water to subsoil until mud forms
- Mix in straw or dry grass 4-6 in long
- About 1 part straw to 4 parts mud by vol
- Stomp or hoe until fully homogeneous
- Consistency: stiff but mold-able
- Optional: add manure as binder

## Molding Bricks

- Wooden form: 10x14x4 in, no bottom
- Wet form before each use
- Pack mix firmly, strike top flat
- Lift form straight up, do not rock
- Lay on flat, dry, leveled ground
- Space 2 in apart for air circulation

## Drying Process

- Cover first 24 hours from direct sun
- Turn bricks on edge after 2-3 days
- Total dry time: 2-4 weeks depending
  on heat and humidity
- Test: strike with knuckle; sharp ring
  means dry; dull thud means wet inside
- Stack on edge when fully dry

## Laying Bricks

- Mud mortar same consistency as bricks
- Lay mortar 1/2 to 3/4 in thick
- Stagger joints like any masonry
- Plumb walls; check every course
- Leave buck openings for doors/windows
- Protect fresh work from rain each night

## Long-Term Value

- Zero-cost materials in arid regions
- Unskilled labor can produce bricks
- Repairs trivial with same mix
- Good fire resistance
- 1000-year-old adobe structures exist
- Insulating and quiet when thick
