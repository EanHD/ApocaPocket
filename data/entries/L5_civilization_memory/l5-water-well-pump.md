# Well Hand Pump

## Overview

- Piston pump: up-stroke lifts water column
- Works to 25 ft with suction pump
- Below 25 ft: needs force pump below water
- Force pump: piston in cylinder below water
- EMAS pump: DIY PVC version proven in
  developing world; all plastic, low cost
- Lift: determined by suction or push cylinder
  position relative to static water level

## Suction Pump Basics

- Atmospheric pressure lifts water 25 ft max
- Piston up-stroke: vacuum forms below
- Atmospheric pressure pushes water up
- Two check valves: foot valve and piston
- Foot valve: at pipe bottom, opens on lift
- Piston valve: opens on down-stroke
- Both one-way; water always moves up

## Materials Needed

- Pump cylinder: 2 in to 3 in diameter pipe
- Piston: leather cup washer on wooden disk
- Handle: long lever arm for mechanical
  advantage; 3-4 ft above pivot
- Drop pipe: same diameter as cylinder
- Foot valve: flap of leather or rubber
- Pipe thread connections or cord-wrapped

## Making Piston Seals

- Leather cup washer: cut from thick leather
- Soak in water or oil before installing
- Fits snug in cylinder; must compress
  slightly at edges to seal
- Replace when pump loses prime easily
- Rubber from inner tube works as backup
- Check fit: smooth pull with resistance

## Installation

- Lower cylinder and drop pipe into well
- Foot valve must be 2 ft below water level
- Secure pump head to well casing
- Prime pump: pour water into cylinder
- Pump handle quickly to establish suction
- If no flow: check foot valve and prime

## Maintenance

- Grease moving pivot points with tallow
  or rendered fat
- Check leather seals every 3 months
- Keep pump covered when not in use
- Inspect drop pipe connections annually
- Keep spare seals cut and stored dry
- Community pump: assign daily oversight

## Long-Term Value

- Eliminates rope-and-bucket contamination
- Much faster water delivery than bucket
- Single pump serves 200+ people per day
- EMAS design buildable from PVC in 4 hours
- Skills teachable to non-engineers
