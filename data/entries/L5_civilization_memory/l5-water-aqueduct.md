# Gravity Aqueduct

## Overview

- Water flows downhill at precise grade
- Grade too steep: erosion and turbulence
- Grade too shallow: silts up, stops flow
- Target grade: 1:500 to 1:1000 (gentle)
- Open channel, covered pipe, or tunnel
- Acequia: Spanish term; community-managed
- Romans achieved 60-mile aqueducts

## Survey and Planning

- Source must be higher than destination
- Use hand level, staff, and transit rod
  to measure elevation changes
- Calculate total drop over total distance
- Divide: this gives your available grade
- Must exceed minimum flow grade needed
- Map route avoiding major obstacles

## Channel Types

- Clay-lined earthen channel: simplest
- Stone-lined channel: more durable
- Split-log channel: forests regions
- Ceramic pipe: for pressurized systems
- Covered channel: reduces contamination
- Pipe under pressure: siphon principle
  can cross valleys if source is higher

## Construction

- Dig or build channel along survey route
- Line with puddled clay or mortar
- Slope bottom uniformly at design grade
- Check grade every 50 ft during build
- Cross-section: trapezoidal is stable
- Cover with stone slabs where possible

## Flow Calculation

- Flow (gal/day) = cross-section area
  x velocity x seconds per day
- Velocity increases with steeper grade
- Manning formula for precise calculations
- Size for peak demand plus 20% margin
- Include spillway for overflow

## Maintenance

- Inspect full length after each storm
- Clear sediment from settling basins
- Repair breaches immediately; erosion
  grows exponentially once started
- Assign maintenance responsibility
  to downstream users; classic model
- Tree roots destroy channels over decades

## Long-Term Value

- Transforms dry settlements into viable
  agricultural communities
- Acequia culture: community water law,
  shared maintenance, equitable access
- Ancient technology still in daily use
  in New Mexico, Spain, Andes
