# Charcoal Production

Without charcoal, you cannot smelt iron, forge steel, cast bronze, or make glass. Wood fires max at 1800°F. Charcoal reaches 2700°F with forced air. No charcoal = no Iron Age.

Temperature comparison:
- Wood fire (open flame): 1100-1800°F
- Charcoal fire (with air blast): 2000-2700°F

## What Is Charcoal

Wood heated with low oxygen (pyrolysis). Heat drives off water, gases, and tar, leaving 85-95% pure carbon.

Why it burns hotter: Wood is 50% volatile compounds that burn fast and cool. Charcoal is 85% fixed carbon that burns slow and extremely hot.

Energy density:
- Wood: 8,000-9,000 BTU/lb
- Charcoal: 12,000-14,000 BTU/lb

## Wood Selection

Use hardwoods. High density produces best charcoal.

Best woods:
- Oak: 25-30% yield, gold standard, burns hottest.
- Hickory: 24-28% yield, very hard, high heat.
- Maple (hard): 25-28% yield, dense, long burn.
- Ash: 23-26% yield, easy to split.
- Beech: 25-28% yield, European standard.

Avoid softwoods (pine, spruce, fir): High resin, lower yield (15-20%), more sparks.

Preparation: Split logs 3-6 inches diameter. Air-dry 6-12 months. Wet wood wastes energy and lowers yield.

Conversion ratio: 4 lbs wood → 1 lb charcoal (typical).

## Earth Pit Kiln

Yield: 20-25%. Simplest method. No equipment needed.

Build:
- Flat ground away from trees and buildings.
- Dig pit 3-4 feet diameter, 2-3 feet deep.
- Dig 3-4 air trenches from pit bottom to edge (3 inches wide, 3 inches deep).
- Place a 4-inch center stake (creates chimney channel).

Fire and cover:
- Stack split wood around center stake in teepee formation.
- Remove center stake carefully once loaded.
- Light fire at bottom through air vents.
- Burn vigorously 1-2 hours until wood glows.
- Cover with sheet metal or flat stones, then 6 inches of dirt.
- Leave small vents initially.

Monitor smoke:
- White/gray smoke: Water and volatiles escaping — normal early phase.
- Thin blue smoke: Charcoal forming — good sign.
- Yellow flames: Too much oxygen — seal vents tighter.

Cook time: 12-24 hours. Cool time: 24-48 hours. Do NOT uncover early — charcoal burns to ash.

## Drum Kiln

Yield: 25-30%. More efficient than pit.

Materials: 55-gallon steel drum, chimney pipe (3-4 inch diameter), lid with center hole, stand.

Setup:
- Burn out drum first (removes toxic coatings).
- Drill 8-12 half-inch holes around drum bottom for air intake.
- Raise drum on bricks 6-12 inches off ground.
- Insert chimney through lid hole.

Operation:
- Load drum 80-90% full with split wood.
- Light kindling on top, leave lid off initially.
- Burn 2-4 hours until smoke thins and turns blue.
- Seal lid, close bottom vents 50-75%.
- Cook 8-12 hours watching for thin blue smoke.
- When smoke nearly stops, seal all vents completely.
- Cool 12-24 hours before opening.

## Quality Check

Good charcoal: Lightweight, black, metallic ring when tapped, breaks cleanly.

Overcooked: Gray ash, crumbles to dust (too much oxygen).

Undercooked: Brown, heavy, smells like wood (insufficient heat or time).

## Safety

FIRE HAZARD: Clear 30-foot radius. Keep 20 gallons water nearby. Never leave unattended during burn phase. Underground fires can spread through roots for days.

CO HAZARD: Carbon monoxide is odorless and lethal. Never operate indoors or in enclosed space. Stay upwind. Symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea. Move to fresh air immediately.

BURN HAZARD: Charcoal stays hot for 24-48 hours after it looks cold. Wear gloves and long sleeves when harvesting.

EXPLOSION HAZARD: Never seal drum completely during burn. Chimney or vent must remain open.

## Uses

Metallurgy: Bloomery iron smelting (3:1 charcoal to ore), blacksmithing, casting bronze and aluminum.

Water filtration: Crush charcoal, layer with sand and gravel for DIY filter.

Soil amendment (biochar): Improves fertility, retains moisture, lasts centuries.

Gunpowder: Charcoal (15%) + sulfur (10%) + potassium nitrate (75%). Requires ultra-fine charcoal.

## Common Errors

- Charcoal turns to ash: Too much oxygen. Seal vents better.
- Wood does not char: Not enough heat or time. Cook longer, use drier wood.
- Low yield under 20%: Wet wood or too much airflow. Use dry hardwood, reduce oxygen.
- Drum bulges: Sealed too tight. Always leave chimney open during burn.
