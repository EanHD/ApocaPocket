# Heat and Thermodynamics

## Overview

- Heat: energy transferred from hot to cold
- Three modes: conduction, convection,
  radiation; always all three together
- Temperature: measure of average molecular
  motion; Fahrenheit or Celsius scale
- Thermodynamics laws govern all engines,
  furnaces, and heat systems
- No engine converts all heat to work;
  efficiency is always less than 100%

## Conduction

- Heat flows through solid materials
- Slow through: wood, clay, wool, air
- Fast through: metal, wet soil, stone
- Insulation: traps still air
- Dead air space: excellent insulator
- Double walls with air gap: good design
- Wet insulation loses effectiveness fully

## Convection

- Heat carried by moving fluid or gas
- Hot air rises; cool air sinks; creates loop
- Chimney draft: convection in action
- Passive solar: convection heats rooms
- Convective heating in pots: bottom heats,
  circulation mixes
- Wind increases convective heat loss

## Radiation

- Heat emitted as infrared from any object
- Does not require contact or medium
- Sun's heat: radiation across vacuum
- Shiny surfaces reflect radiation
- Dark surfaces absorb and emit more
- Radiant heat: campfire felt at distance
- Thermal mass absorbs radiation in day

## Temp Measurement

- Liquid thermometer: alcohol or mercury
- Make: sealed glass tube, calibrate
  with boiling water (212F) and ice (32F)
- Mark scale by interpolation between
- Bimetallic: two metals bend at different
  rates; useful for oven thermometers
- Blacksmith color guide: see steel entry

## Practical Applications

- Building insulation: stops conduction
- Chimney design: maximizes convection
- Solar gain: optimize radiation capture
- Cooking: control all three modes
- Kiln and furnace: maximize combustion heat
- Cold storage: minimize all heat transfer

## Long-Term Value

- Reduces fuel consumption by 50-80%
  when applied to building design
- Efficient furnaces smelt more per fuel
- Cooking efficiency multiplied with
  understanding of heat transfer
- Core physical principle; never changes
