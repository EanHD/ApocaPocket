# Rammed Earth Walls

## Overview

- Pise: French term for rammed earth
- Moist subsoil compacted in forms
- Achieves near-brick density when done
- Walls 18-24 in typical
- No straw needed; differs from cob/adobe
- Stronger than adobe; better than cob
  for structural walls under heavy load
- Less water-resistant than fired brick

## Soil Requirements

- Best mix: 70% sand/gravel, 30% clay
- Slightly damp: handful compresses and
  holds shape but does not smear hands
- Too wet: slumps in form, weak result
- Too dry: does not bind, crumbles
- Test batch first; adjust moisture
- Avoid topsoil with organic matter

## Building Formwork

- Boards or plywood, min 2 in thick
- Height of form: 12-18 in per lift
- Clamp or bolt sides to resist pressure
- Slope form 1 in per 12 for wall batter
- Align carefully; walls hard to adjust
- Move forms vertically after each lift

## Ramming Process

- Fill form to 6 in depth; ram thoroughly
- Rammer: 10-15 lb flat-bottomed tool
- Strike overlapping blows across surface
- Layer compresses to roughly 4 in
- Continue in 6 in lifts to top of form
- Correct sound: solid thud not hollow ring

## Compaction Standards

- A proper layer rings when struck with rod
- Surface should not dust easily
- Layers bond well if lightly scarified
  before adding next lift
- Corners need extra attention; ram in
  from both sides
- Allow 24 hr before moving forms

## Finishing

- Leave natural or plaster with lime
- Natural surface shows beautiful layers
- Lime plaster adds moisture protection
- Wide roof overhangs critical
- Seal any cracks before they grow
- Sill height 12 in above grade minimum

## Long-Term Value

- No manufactured materials required
- Very high thermal mass
- Fire proof; earthquake-resistant if thick
- Structures last 1000+ years (China, Yemen)
- Community can build with basic training
