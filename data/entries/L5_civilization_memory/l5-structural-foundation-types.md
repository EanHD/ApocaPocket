# Foundation Types

## Foundation Types

Slab-on-grade:
- Concrete poured directly on compacted soil
- Good for: flat stable ground, dry climates
- Weak point: cracks if soil settles unevenly
- Do not use where ground freezes, frost heave will crack it

Crawl space:
- Foundation walls with 18-36 inch clearance under floor
- Good for: sloped sites, moderate moisture, utility access
- Allows plumbing and wiring repairs from below
- Must be ventilated to prevent rot

Basement:
- Full depth below grade, minimum 7-foot ceiling
- Good for: cold climates (extends below frost), storage
- Risk: water intrusion if drainage is inadequate
- Most expensive to build

Pier and post:
- Concrete piers or posts carry beams above ground
- Good for: slopes, flood zones, wet soils, light structures
- Easiest to repair if one pier fails
- Requires cross-bracing between posts for lateral stability

## Signs of Failure

Stop using the structure and investigate:
- Cracks wider than 1/4 inch in foundation walls
- Horizontal cracks in masonry (lateral soil pressure, serious)
- Water seeping through foundation walls
- Floor joists resting on air (pier has sunk or rotted)
- Doors and windows suddenly sticking (frame is racking)
- Foundation visibly tilting or leaning from plumb

Diagonal stair-step cracks in brick or block: usually settling.
Horizontal cracks in masonry walls: serious, act now.

## Simple Repairs

Pier replacement (if accessible underneath):
- Jack up the beam off the failed pier
- Remove pier, install pressure-treated post on concrete pad
- Size pad for the load, 12x12 inch minimum for light buildings
- Lower beam carefully onto new pier

Surface foundation cracks (non-structural):
- Clean crack, fill with hydraulic cement or epoxy injection
- Improve drainage: slope grade away from foundation
- A crack that reopens after repair is still moving

Water intrusion:
- Grade soil to slope away from building (6 inches per 10 feet)
- Extend downspouts at least 4 feet from foundation
- Interior drain tile and sump pump as last resort

## New Construction

Assess soil before digging:
- Clay: poor drainage, expands when wet, needs drainage and wide footings
- Sand: good drainage, may shift, compact and widen base
- Rock: excellent bearing, shallow footings on clean rock acceptable
- Fill dirt: worst, unknown behavior, requires deep footings or piers

Footing depth rules:
- Must extend below local frost line in freezing climates
- Minimum 12 inches in no-freeze climates
- Width should be twice the wall thickness above it

Field concrete mix: 1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts gravel.
Cure 28 days before loading. Keep damp for the first 7 days.
