# Rainwater Harvest Sys

## Overview

- Roof collects rain; gutters carry to tank
- First-flush diverter discards initial
  dirty water before storage
- Covered tank prevents contamination
  and mosquito breeding
- Simple filter before use for safety
- Yield: 0.62 gal per sq ft per inch of rain
- 1000 sq ft roof, 1 in rain = 620 gal

## Catchment Surface

- Metal roofing: best, minimal leaching
- Fired clay tile: good, second choice
- Thatch: usable but adds organics
- Avoid lead paint or treated wood shingles
- Clean roof before rainy season
- Bird deterrents improve water quality

## Gutters

- Wood box gutter or split bamboo
- Slope 1/4 in per foot toward downspout
- Screen inlet: keeps leaves and debris
- Clean gutters at start of each season
- Secure well; heavy rain load is large
- Lead to downspout at tank inlet point

## First-Flush Diverter

- Pipe volume: 1 gal per 100 sq ft roof
- First flow fills this standpipe; diverts
  away from tank (slow drip to drain)
- Once full, subsequent flow enters tank
- Clean standpipe after each significant rain
- Reduces contamination dramatically
- Simple ball-float version is DIY-able

## Storage Tank

- Covered completely; no light penetration
- Light grows algae; open tanks breed
  mosquitoes carrying malaria and dengue
- Minimum: 1000 gal for family
- Access hatch with tight lid
- Overflow to secondary storage or garden
- Clean and disinfect annually

## Treatment Before Use

- Drinking water: pass through slow sand
  filter then boil or add chlorine
- Non-potable use: gutters and storage
  without further treatment is fine
- Test if possible for bacterial count
- Turbid water: settle then filter
- Maintain records of tank cleanings

## Long-Term Value

- Water independence from rivers or wells
- Essential in areas with seasonal dry spells
- Reduces waterborne disease if covered
- Full system buildable with local materials
- Scales from single barrel to 10,000 gal
