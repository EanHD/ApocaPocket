# Earthen Floor

## Overview

- Materials: subsoil, sand, straw, linseed oil
- No concrete, tile, or lumber needed
- Final surface is hard, smooth, warm
- Linseed oil seals and waterproofs surface
- Comparable hardness to soft wood when done
- Zero cost in most regions
- Repairable with same mix at any time

## Base Preparation

- Excavate to firm subsoil or compacted fill
- Depth of floor system: 4-6 in total
- Remove all organic material from base
- Install drainage layer if moisture present:
  gravel or rubble below floor mix
- Compact subgrade firmly before building up
- Rough level with long board screed

## Base Layer Mix

- 1 part clay subsoil to 2 parts sharp sand
- Add chopped straw: 2-3 in long pieces
- Mix with water to stiff consistency
- Consistency: holds shape, not soupy
- Apply 2-3 in thick; tamp firmly
- Level with screed board, then let cure

## Finish Layer Mix

- Same as base but fine; no straw
- Screen clay through 1/8 in mesh
- Mix smooth: like thick peanut butter
- Apply 1/2 to 3/4 in thick
- Compress and smooth with wet hand and
  wood float in circular strokes
- Let dry to leather-hard before sealing

## Linseed Oil Sealing

- Raw linseed oil: thin with turpentine 50/50
  for first coat; helps penetration
- Apply warm (slightly heated) oil liberally
- Let soak in; wipe off excess at 1 hour
- Repeat 3-5 times over 2 weeks
- Final coats: undiluted raw linseed
- Full cure: floor darkens and hardens

## Maintenance

- Sweep or damp mop; avoid wet mopping
- Reseal with linseed oil annually
- Patch cracks with same clay mix
- Oil finish protects against light water
- Avoid standing water; edge protection
  needed near walls where water runs

## Long-Term Value

- Ancient technology; still superior in
  many respects to poured concrete
- No mining, manufacturing, or transport
- Comfortable underfoot; warm thermal feel
- Biodegradable; leaves no waste
