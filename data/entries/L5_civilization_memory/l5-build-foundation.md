# Foundation Basics

## Overview

- Foundation transfers load to stable soil
- Failure cause: frost heave, settlement,
  erosion, or organic matter compression
- Four types: rubble trench, pad, strip,
  full perimeter wall
- Depth must reach below frost line
- Frost line varies: 0 in tropics to 60 in
  northern climates; know your region
- All earthen walls need raised stone base

## Site Assessment

- Avoid low spots; water collects there
- Test soil: step hard, observe deflection
- Clay soils: drain poorly, heave in frost
- Sandy soil: drains well, may shift
- Bedrock: ideal if reachable
- Never build on fill without compaction

## Rubble Trench

- Best low-tech general-purpose choice
- Dig trench below frost line, 18-24 in wide
- Fill with clean rubble: rocks, no fines
- Top 12 in: dry-stacked stones or gravel
- Allows drainage; no concrete needed
- Cap with stone sill for wall base

## Pad Foundation

- For post bases; each post on own pad
- Dig hole, fill with compacted rubble
- Set flat capstone for post to rest on
- Keep wood post 6 in above grade
- Prevents rot without heavy perimeter work
- Good for timber frame on stable soil

## Strip Foundation

- Continuous trench under all walls
- Fill with mortared rubble or dry-stack
- Bring above grade 12-18 in
- Level top surface carefully
- Check corners for square
- Allow mortar to cure before loading

## Drainage

- Grade ground away from building
- French drain: gravel-filled trench
  diverts upslope water around building
- Water under foundation destroys all types
- Check drainage after every heavy rain
- Dry foundation = long-lasting structure

## Long-Term Value

- Correct foundation doubles building life
- Rubble trench needs no manufactured
  materials; stones available everywhere
- Easy to inspect and repair if exposed
- Most foundation failures preventable
  by careful siting and drainage
