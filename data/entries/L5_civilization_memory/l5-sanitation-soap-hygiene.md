# Soap and Hygiene

## Overview

- Handwashing with soap cuts diarrhea 50%
- Cholera, typhoid, dysentery: fecal-oral
- Soap disrupts lipid layer of bacteria
  and viruses; mechanical removal key
- Ash or sand substitute if no soap
- Soap made from fat and lye; ancient tech
- Community soap production is viable

## Lye Production

- Hardwood ash: best lye source
- Stack ash in barrel with perforated base
- Pour water through; collect dark liquid
- Test strength: raw egg floats = correct
- Birch, hickory, oak ash: high potassium
- Boil down weak lye to concentrate
- Product: potassium hydroxide solution

## Basic Soap Making

- Fat: tallow (beef), lard, or plant oil
- Ratio: roughly 1 lb fat to 1/2 cup lye
- Heat fat to liquid; cool to room temp
- Add lye solution slowly, stirring
- Mix until it traces: pudding consistency
- Pour into mold; let harden 48 hours
- Cut; cure 4-6 weeks before use

## Ash Soap Shortcut

- Paste ash soap: no need to cure
- Mix fat and strong lye water directly
- Heat on low; stir until thick paste
- Use immediately or store in covered pot
- Washes hands well; highly alkaline
- Test on skin patch first; may irritate

## Handwashing Protocol

- Wet hands with any water
- Apply soap or ash (2 tsp ash works)
- Scrub all surfaces: minimum 20 seconds
- Between fingers, under nails, wrists
- Rinse thoroughly with clean water
- Dry on clean cloth; not shared towel
- Key moments: before eating, after latrine

## Community Hygiene

- Central handwashing station at latrine
- Tippy-tap: foot-operated hand wash rig
  made from stick, jug, and string
- Soap production monthly; distribute
- Teach children first; they spread habits
- Track illness rates to measure effect
- Hygiene education: ROI exceeds vaccines

## Long-Term Value

- Cholera prevention without medicine
- Fat and ash: available in all settlements
- Soap making skill preserves public health
- Teaches chemistry through practice
- Life expectancy impact is measurable
