# Wire Drawing

## Overview

- Wire drawing: pull rod through tapered hole
- Each hole reduces diameter slightly
- Annealing: soften wire between draws
- Drawplate: hard steel plate with holes
- Drawbench: mechanical pulling device
- Produce: fence wire, springs, needles,
  binding wire, fish hooks, snares

## Drawplate Making

- Thick steel plate: 1/4 in minimum
- Drill and file holes in series: large to small
- Holes tapered: cone-shaped on entry side
- Entry cone angle: 12-15 degrees
- Holes range from 1/8 in down to 1/32 in
- Harden the plate fully after drilling
- Polish hole interiors smooth

## Starting Stock

- Begin with drawn rod from blacksmith
- Taper end with hammer to fit first hole
- Start 20% larger than target diameter
- Work down in small steps; not jumps
- Each step: reduces diameter 10-15%
- Multiple passes needed for fine wire
- Taper end between plates as needed

## Drawing Process

- Lubricate wire: tallow, lard, or beeswax
- Thread tapered end through hole from back
- Grip with drawing tongs or pliers
- Pull firmly and steadily; no jerking
- Coil drawn wire as it comes through
- Keep lubricant on wire throughout

## Drawbench

- Mechanical advantage speeds drawing
- Long bench with fixed drawplate at end
- Sliding carriage with tongs grips wire
- Chain or rope pulls carriage
- Windlass or lever provides pulling force
- One operator draws; one manages coil
- Speeds output 10x over hand drawing

## Annealing

- Heat wire to bright red in charcoal fire
- Allow to cool slowly in ash or lime
- Wire becomes soft and ductile again
- Anneal every 3-5 drawing passes
- Failure to anneal: wire becomes brittle
  and snaps under tension suddenly
- Final product can be left work-hardened
  for spring applications

## Long-Term Value

- Wire enables: snares, fishing hooks,
  springs, telegraph, binding, needles
- Drawplate is small, portable, invaluable
- Iron and copper both drawble
- Skilled wire drawer produces miles per day
