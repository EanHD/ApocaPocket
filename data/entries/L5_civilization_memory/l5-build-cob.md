# Cob Construction

## Overview

- Cob: clay subsoil + sharp sand + straw fiber
- No forms needed; sculpted by hand and foot
- Walls 18-24 in thick for thermal mass
- Fire-resistant, rot-resistant when dry
- Suitable for non-seismic regions
- Build in warm dry season only
- Widely used in UK, Africa, Middle East

## Test Your Soil

- Ribbon test: squeeze soil in fist, extrude
  between thumb and finger; aim for 2 in
- Jar test: shake soil in water; clay sinks
  last; sand first; note proportions
- Target 20-30% clay content in mix
- Too much clay: cracks when drying
- Too little clay: crumbles, no strength
- Amend with sand or clay accordingly

## Mix Preparation

- Ratio: roughly 1 part clay to 2 parts sand
- Lay tarp on ground, add dry materials
- Wet until workable but not sticky
- Stomp with bare feet 20-30 minutes
- Add dry straw: 2-4 handfuls per batch
- Fold and stomp to distribute fiber
- Test ball: drop from 3 ft, holds shape

## Building Walls

- Start on rubble trench or stone base
- First course: thumb-sized test batch
  applied, let cure 24 hours minimum
- Apply cob in lifts no more than 12 in
- Let each lift firm before adding next
- Trim edges with spade for clean face
- Slope inward slightly (batter) for strength

## Doors and Windows

- Buck: rough frame set before cobbing
- Cob built around and over bucks
- Lintels required over all openings
- Wood, stone, or cob arch can serve
- Rough openings must carry wall load
- Seal edges where cob meets wood

## Finishing

- Plaster walls with lime or clay slip
- Three coats: scratch, brown, finish
- Lime plaster sheds water well
- Rounded corners resist cracking
- Earthen floor works well with cob walls
- Roof overhang of 18+ in protects walls

## Long-Term Value

- Entirely local materials in most regions
- No skilled labor; community can build
- Repairs made with same mix
- Thermally comfortable in hot and cold
- Can last centuries with maintained roof
