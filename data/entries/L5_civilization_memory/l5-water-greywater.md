# Greywater Systems

## Overview

- Greywater: wash water (sink, bath, laundry)
- NOT blackwater: toilet waste is separate
- Greywater contains soap, skin cells,
  some bacteria; much safer than sewage
- Must be dispersed; not pooled or stored
- Use within 24 hours; bacteria multiply
- Simple mulch basins: safest low-tech method

## Mulch Basin System

- Pipe from sink/wash area to garden
- Basin: 3x3 ft pit, 18 in deep
- Fill with coarse wood chip mulch
- Greywater soaks in; mulch filters and
  aerates; bacteria break down organics
- Refill mulch annually or when slow
- Plant fruiting trees over basin; roots
  uptake nutrients safely

## Three-Bucket System

- No plumbing: collect in bucket at source
- Bucket 1: rinse (cleanest)
- Bucket 2: soapy wash water
- Bucket 3: transport to distribution point
- Pour into mulch basin; no pooling
- Good for temporary or minimal setup
- Most common technique worldwide

## Constructed Wetland

- For larger flows: community wash station
- Trench filled with gravel: 2 ft deep
- Planted with cattail, reed, iris
- Water flows through slowly; plants filter
- Overflow to additional wetland cell
- Minimum 2 cells for continuous operation
- Effluent suitable for orchard irrigation

## Soap Selection

- Biodegradable soap breaks down fastest
- Avoid antibacterial soaps in wetlands
- Castile or ash-lye soap both work well
- Bleach and harsh cleaners kill wetland
  biology; handle separately
- Sodium content high in many soaps;
  rotate basins to prevent salt buildup

## Siting Rules

- No greywater within 100 ft of any well
- No ponding or runoff to neighbors
- No spray; surface application only
- Keep away from paths and play areas
- Do not apply during heavy rain events
- Document system location for future

## Long-Term Value

- Reduces total water demand by 30-50%
- Feeds trees and shrubs without irrigation
- Prevents yard contamination from pooling
- No manufactured components needed
- Essential in water-scarce conditions
