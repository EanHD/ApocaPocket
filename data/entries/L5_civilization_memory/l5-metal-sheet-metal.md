# Sheet Metal Work

## Overview

- Sheet metal: thin flat stock for forming
- Copper, bronze, soft iron all formable
- Techniques: hammering, raising, seaming,
  riveting, soldering
- Products: pots, pans, buckets, pipes,
  funnels, cups, roofing, gutters
- Tinsmithing: flat sheet cut and seamed
- Coppersmithing: hammered over forms

## Tools

- Stakes: steel anvil shapes for forming
- Mushroom stake: for doming and raising
- Creasing iron: folds and seams
- Mallet: wooden or rawhide for soft blows
- Snips or cold chisel for cutting
- Planishing hammer: smooth finished surface

## Cutting Sheet

- Mark with scriber on flat surface
- Cut with metal snips, shears, or chisel
- Anneal copper/bronze before cutting
- Iron sheet: cut hot with hot chisel
- File all cut edges smooth
- Deburr: sharp edges cause cuts in use

## Seaming (Tinsmith)

- Lay out flat development from 3D shape
- Fold edges with bending brake or stake
- Lock seam: fold each edge, interlock
- Hammer seam flat to lock permanently
- Seam strength often exceeds sheet
- Solder seam for waterproof vessel
- Rivets as alternative to soldering

## Raising (Coppersmith)

- Start with flat disk of sheet
- Work from center outward with mallet
- Over mushroom stake: bowl shape grows
- Anneal frequently; copper hardens fast
- Final form: planish smooth over stake
- True up rim on turning or filing
- Deep forms: multiple raising sessions

## Riveting

- Drill or punch rivet holes
- Rivet: soft iron or copper rod
- Set rivet in hole; trim to 1.5x diameter
  protruding on each side
- Support head on anvil; peen other end
- Spread peen into mushroom head shape
- Waterproof with pitch or lead-tin solder

## Long-Term Value

- Sheet metal enables cookware production
- Cookware dramatically improves nutrition
  and food safety
- Copper pipe and gutters from local ore
- Tinsmithing teachable in weeks
- Every village needs a metalworker
