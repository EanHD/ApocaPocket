# Botany Basics

## Overview

- Botany: study of plants
- Plants: producers; base of most food webs
- Vascular plants: have xylem and phloem
  (tubes to move water and nutrients)
- Non-vascular: moss, liverwort; no tubes
- Flowering plants (angiosperms): most crops
- Conifers (gymnosperms): most forest trees
- Understanding growth improves farming

## Plant Structure

- Roots: anchor, absorb water and minerals
- Stem: transport, support, storage
- Leaves: photosynthesis; gas exchange
- Flowers: reproduction structures
- Fruit: seed vessel
- Seed: embryo plus food reserve
- All parts useful; know each function

## Photosynthesis

- Chlorophyll in leaves absorbs sunlight
- CO2 enters through stomata (leaf pores)
- Water absorbed by roots; transported up
- Sun + CO2 + water = sugar + oxygen
- Sugar: plant food and structure (cellulose)
- Oxygen: byproduct; what we breathe
- Green color = chlorophyll = photosynthesis

## Growth Requirements

- Light: full sun, partial, shade adapted
- Water: drought tolerance varies widely
- Nutrients: N (leaf), P (root), K (fruit)
- Temperature: germination to frost tolerance
- Soil pH: most crops prefer 6.0-7.0
- Spacing: competition reduces yield
- Airflow: reduces fungal disease

## Reproduction Types

- Annual: germinate, grow, set seed, die
  in one season (corn, beans, most crops)
- Biennial: two seasons to complete cycle
  (carrot, parsnip, many herbs)
- Perennial: regrow each year from roots
  (fruit trees, berries, herbs, asparagus)
- Vegetative: division, cutting, layering
- Saving seed: select best specimens

## Plant Classification

- Leaf shape: simple vs compound
- Leaf arrangement: alternate, opposite,
  whorled on stem
- Flower structure: petals, sepals, stamens
- Fruit type: berry, drupe, capsule, pod
- Using a dichotomous key: yes/no choices
  lead to species identification
- Always cross-check with multiple features

## Long-Term Value

- Classification prevents poisoning
- Growth knowledge improves yields
- Medicinal plant ID requires botany
- Breeding improved varieties: requires
  understanding of reproduction
- Community botanist is critical role
