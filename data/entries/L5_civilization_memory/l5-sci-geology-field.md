# Field Geology

## Overview

- Geology: study of earth's materials and
  processes
- Three rock types: igneous, sedimentary,
  metamorphic
- Rock cycle: rocks transform between types
  over geological time
- Field geology: read landscape for
  resources, hazards, water, and soils
- No instruments needed for basic field work
- Critical for: siting, mining, soil assessment

## Rock Types

- Igneous: cooled from magma
  Granite: coarse; slow cool underground
  Basalt: fine; fast cool at surface
- Sedimentary: layered from deposits
  Sandstone, limestone, shale, coal
- Metamorphic: changed by heat/pressure
  Marble (limestone), quartzite (sandstone),
  slate (shale); harder than originals

## Mineral Identification

- Hardness scale (Mohs 1-10):
  Talc 1, fingernail 2.5, glass 5.5,
  quartz 7, diamond 10
- Scratch test reveals hardness
- Color: unreliable alone; streak test better
- Streak: mineral powder color on ceramic
- Luster: metallic, glassy, dull, pearly
- Cleavage: breaks along flat planes

## Reading Landscape

- V-shaped valley: active stream erosion
- U-shaped valley: glacial erosion
- Outwash plain: flat glacial deposit
- Escarpment: exposed sedimentary layers
- Spring line: where water table meets slope
- Talus slope: fallen rock below cliff
- River meander: deposits on inside bend

## Water Indicators

- Willows, cottonwood: high water table
- Dry creek bed: seasonal flow; dig for water
- Green vegetation in dry season: spring
- Limestone karst: underground drainage
- Granite: springs emerge at contacts
- Follow valleys downhill for water
- Rock seep: reliable in drought

## Resources from Geology

- Iron ore: red or black heavy rock
- Flint and chert: dark, glassy, fractures
  conchoidal; flintknapping and fire-making
- Clay: smooth, plastic when wet; near
  stream beds and slopes below sand
- Limestone: building and lime production
- Coal seams: visible in cliff exposures
- Copper ore: green stain on rock surface

## Long-Term Value

- Siting decisions depend on geology
- Finding water, ore, clay all field geology
- Hazard assessment: slides, floods, karst
- Soil origin from parent rock; guides crops
- Skills preserved by observation habit
