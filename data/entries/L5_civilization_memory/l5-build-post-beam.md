# Post and Beam Framing

## Overview

- Load travels post to beam to foundation
- Posts: vertical, transfer compressive load
- Beams: horizontal, carry floor/roof weight
- Bents: paired post-beam frames, raised together
- Connect bents with plates and girts
- No nails needed; pegged mortise/tenon joints
- Plan bay spacing: 8-16 ft typical

## Site Layout

- Establish batter boards outside corners
- String lines define building footprint
- Check square with 3-4-5 or diagonals
- Mark post hole centers precisely
- Grade site before raising frame
- Ensure posts land on solid footings

## Timber Selection

- Posts: 6x6 or 8x8 minimum for one story
- Beams: size by span and load (6x10 typical)
- Use straight-grained, dry or green timber
- Oak, Douglas fir, pine all acceptable
- Crown (slight bow) faces up on beams
- Avoid large knots at joint locations

## Joinery Basics

- Mortise: rectangular hole cut in post
- Tenon: tongue cut on beam end to fit
- Mark with pencil, cut mortise with chisel
- Tenon shoulder must seat flush
- Test fit dry before drawboring
- Peg with 1 in hardwood drawbore peg

## Raising the Frame

- Pre-cut and label all members in shop
- Assemble bents flat on ground first
- Raise bents with pike poles and crew
- Brace each bent plumb before next
- Install connecting plates and girts
- Frame is stable when fully connected

## Roof and Floors

- Rafters notch over top plate (birdsmouth)
- Ridge beam or ridge board at peak
- Floor joists mortise into beams or rest
  on ledger strips nailed to beam face
- Diagonal bracing prevents racking
- Fill walls: cob, stud, wattle/daub

## Long-Term Value

- Frame can last 500+ years if dry
- Repairable without special tools
- Allows interior wall reconfiguration
- Skills transferable across generations
- Entire community can raise a frame
