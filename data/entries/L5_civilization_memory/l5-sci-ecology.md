# Ecology Basics

## Overview

- Ecology: relationships between organisms
  and their environment
- Ecosystem: community of life + its
  physical environment (soil, water, air)
- Biome: large ecosystem type (forest,
  grassland, desert, wetland, tundra)
- Humans are part of ecosystems; not apart
- Extractive practices without restoration
  destroy the base of survival
- Understanding ecology prevents fatal errors

## Nutrient Cycles

- Carbon cycle: CO2 to plant via photosynthesis;
  back via respiration and decay
- Nitrogen cycle: N2 fixed by bacteria
  in soil and legume roots; recycled
  through dead matter and decomposers
- Phosphorus: released from rock; cycles
  through soil, plants, animals, back to soil
- Water cycle: evaporation, precipitation,
  infiltration, runoff, transpiration

## Food Web

- Producers: plants; make food from sunlight
- Primary consumers: herbivores eat plants
- Secondary consumers: eat herbivores
- Apex predators: top of chain
- Decomposers: fungi and bacteria break
  down dead matter; return nutrients
- Remove any level: cascade effects follow
- Healthy food web has redundancy

## Keystone Species

- Species with disproportionate impact
  on ecosystem relative to abundance
- Examples: wolves regulate elk; elk
  overgraze without wolves; rivers change
- Beavers: create wetlands that support
  many other species
- Identify local keystone species and
  protect them actively

## Succession

- Primary succession: bare rock to forest
  takes centuries (glacier retreat)
- Secondary succession: disturbed land
  recovers in decades if protected
- Stages: pioneer weeds, shrubs, young
  forest, mature climax forest
- Farming reverses succession; active
  management required to maintain
- Allow succession in unneeded areas

## Biome Guide

- Temperate forest: deciduous; 4 seasons
- Boreal (taiga): conifer; long cold winter
- Grassland/prairie: dry; fire-maintained
- Desert: <10 in rain per year; extremes
- Tropical: high rainfall; year-round warm
- Wetland: high productivity; water-based
- Know your biome; others have different rules

## Long-Term Value

- Land mismanagement destroys civilizations
- Ecology knowledge prevents that pattern
- Guides forestry, farming, and fishing
- Teaches sustainable extraction limits
- Recovery management after disturbance
