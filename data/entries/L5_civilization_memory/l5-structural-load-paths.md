# Structural Load Paths

## How Loads Transfer

Every structure carries load from roof to ground.
Three load types:
- Dead load: weight of the structure itself
- Live load: people, furniture, stored goods
- Environmental: wind, snow, earthquake, water

Load path: roof to wall to floor to foundation to soil.
Every joint in this path is critical.
Break any link and the structure fails, immediately or
delayed by days.

## Key Failure Modes

Most common causes of structural collapse:
- Connection failure: fasteners corrode, joints rot
- Foundation settlement: soil moves under load
- Overloading: weight exceeds original design capacity
- Lateral forces unbraced: walls rack and lean
- Progressive collapse: one failure triggers the next

Signs of active failure, stop using space immediately:
- Diagonal cracks from door or window corners
- Floor sloping more than 1 inch per 8 feet
- Walls leaning visibly from plumb
- New gaps at roofline or foundation level
- Creaking or cracking under normal occupancy load

## Structural Walls

Removing the wrong wall can collapse a building.

Likely structural, do not remove without replacement:
- All exterior walls
- Interior walls running perpendicular to floor joists
- Walls directly below a beam or ridge above
- Walls where floor above shows no open span

Likely non-structural, safer to modify:
- Interior walls running parallel to floor joists
- Partition walls not visible from floor above
- Walls stopping short of ceiling without beam above

When uncertain: cut a small inspection hole in the wall.
If a beam sits on top of the wall plate, it is load-bearing.
Do not remove load-bearing walls without shoring first.

## Safe vs Do Not Touch

Do not modify without temporary shoring:
- Any wall where floor above shows visible sag
- Roof ridge supports and king posts
- Corner posts of any structure
- Any member with visible rot, insect damage, or cracks

Safe to modify with care:
- Confirmed non-structural partitions
- Door and window openings if proper header is in place
- Additions if the new load path is fully continuous

Always install a temporary support wall before cutting any
opening. Temporary walls must bear on solid floor structure.
