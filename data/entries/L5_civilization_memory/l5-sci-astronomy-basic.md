# Astronomy Basics

## Overview

- Astronomy: study of celestial objects
- Stars: distant suns; fixed relative to each
  other; move across sky nightly
- Planets: wander against star background
- Moon: monthly cycle; affects tides
- Sun: drives seasons and daily time
- Navigation, calendars, and agriculture
  all depend on astronomy

## Finding North

- Northern Hemisphere: Polaris (North Star)
  marks due north within 1 degree
- Find Polaris: Big Dipper pointer stars
  extend 5 times their gap to Polaris
- Southern Hemisphere: Southern Cross
  with pointer stars for south estimation
- Polaris altitude = your latitude
- No compass needed with clear sky

## Key Star Patterns

- Orion (winter N hemisphere): bright;
  belt points to Sirius (S) and Aldebaran (N)
- Ursa Major (Big Dipper): circumpolar N;
  year-round visible above 40 N latitude
- Scorpius (summer): opposite Orion
- Cassiopeia: W-shape; circumpolar N
- Pleiades: planting indicator in many
  traditions; appear in spring

## Moon Phases

- New moon: dark; no moonrise at sunset
- First quarter: half lit; sets at midnight
- Full moon: opposite sun; all night visible
- Last quarter: half lit; rises at midnight
- Cycle: 29.5 days (synodic month)
- Planting by moon: ancient tradition;
  root crops at waning; leaf at waxing

## Seasons

- Caused by earth's axial tilt (23.5 deg)
- Summer solstice: longest day of year
- Winter solstice: shortest day
- Equinox: day and night equal length
- Solstice marker: gnomon shadow length
- Track with standing stone; build calendar
- Seasons determine all agricultural timing

## Planetary Visibility

- Venus: bright; morning or evening star
- Mars: reddish; varies in brightness
- Jupiter: very bright; steady white
- Saturn: creamy white; moves slowly
- All planets on or near ecliptic (sun's path)
- Planets do not twinkle; stars do

## Long-Term Value

- Calendar made from astronomical observation
- Navigation requires astronomy
- Planting calendar optimized by star timing
- No instruments required; naked eye enough
- Oldest human knowledge preserved here
