# Dry Stone Walling

## Overview

- No mortar; friction and gravity hold wall
- Principles: two wythes, infill, batter
- Batter: walls taper inward toward top
- Through stones tie the two faces together
- Coping caps the top; sheds water
- Walls can last 500+ years if built right
- Used globally for enclosure and structure

## Stone Selection

- Flat stones best; round stones roll out
- Hard dense stone: granite, limestone,
  sandstone all work well
- Sort by size before starting
- Footing stones: largest and flattest
- Throughstones: long enough to span wall
- Coping: heavy flat tops for final course

## Foundation Course

- Excavate 2-4 in; remove soft soil
- Set two parallel lines of largest stones
- Fill gap between with hearting (infill)
- Footing width: 2x intended wall width
- Level each footing stone carefully
- Do not rush this; everything depends on it

## Building Courses

- Two faces built up simultaneously
- Each stone bridges the joint below it
- No continuous vertical joints (runs)
- Pack hearting tightly after each course
- Every 3-5 courses: lay throughstones
- Throughstones span full wall width

## Batter and Plumb

- Batter: 1 in inward per 1 ft of height
- Use a batter frame guide or string
- Each face leans slightly inward
- Plumb each face separately
- Consistent batter prevents leaning
- A wall leaning outward will fall

## Coping

- Final course: heavy flat stones set on edge
- Or large flat capstones laid flat
- Coping must overhang both faces slightly
- Sheds rain off wall top; reduces erosion
- Weight of coping adds stability
- No mortar; coping must be heavy

## Long-Term Value

- Zero manufactured inputs
- Fully repairable with original stones
- Can serve as foundation, wall, or fence
- Excellent habitat for wildlife
- Techniques learnable in a single day
- Failure modes visible; easy to diagnose
