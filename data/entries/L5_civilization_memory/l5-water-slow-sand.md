# Slow Sand Filter

## Overview

- Schmutzdecke: biological slime layer
  on top of sand; does the real filtering
- Removes bacteria, protozoa, viruses
- No chemicals needed; no moving parts
- Flow rate: 0.1-0.4 m/hr (very slow)
- Output is safe for drinking after boil
  or UV; dramatically reduces pathogen load
- 19th century technology; still most
  cost-effective filter for communities

## Filter Components

- Container: barrel, tank, concrete box
- Gravel layer bottom: 4-8 in coarse gravel
- Fine gravel: 2-4 in over coarse layer
- Sand layer: 24-36 in fine clean sand
- Supernatant water: 8-16 in above sand
- Outlet: pipe through side at gravel level
- Overflow: pipe at supernatant level

## Sand Selection

- Fine, clean, washed sand
- Grain size: 0.15 to 0.35 mm effective
- Uniformity coefficient: less than 3
- NO beach sand with shells; shells
  dissolve and raise pH, kill biology
- River sand cleaned by repeated washing
- Test: clear water after 5 minutes settle

## Establishing

- Flood with source water; start flowing
- Do not use filtered water for 2-4 weeks
- Test: water becomes clearer and odorless
- Mature schmutzdecke is grey-brown slime
- Never let filter run dry; kills biology
- Restart time after drying: 2-3 weeks

## Operation

- Flow rate control via outlet valve
- Target: 1 gal per sq ft per hour
- Check outlet turbidity daily; clear = good
- Backfill supernatant water if level drops
- Never disturb sand surface during operation

## Cleaning

- Scrape top 1-2 in of sand when flow
  rate drops to unacceptable level
- Wash scraped sand; return to top
- Schmutzdecke re-establishes in 1-2 weeks
- Typically needed every 1-6 months
- Do not over-clean; biology is the filter

## Long-Term Value

- Filter lasts decades with maintenance
- Community scale: 3 sq ft per person
- Effluent: still boil for drinking safety
- No manufactured inputs after build
- Documented since 1804 in practice
