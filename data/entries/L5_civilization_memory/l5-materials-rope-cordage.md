# Rope & Cordage

**Level 5: Materials & Manufacturing**

Rope enables shelters, tools, hunting, fishing, transport, and construction. One rope-maker can supply an entire community.

---

## Plant Fiber Sources

**Bark fibers** (strong, rot-resistant):
- Cedar bark: excellent strength, outstanding rot resistance. Strip inner cambium in spring. Dry 2-4 weeks, beat with mallet.
- Willow bark: good strength, very flexible. Strip young shoots in spring/summer.
- Basswood/linden: very good strength, soft. Ret 1-2 weeks in water to separate inner bark.

**Leaf fibers** (very strong):
- Yucca/agave/sisal: exceptional (1000-1200 lbs/inch). Scrape leaves, ret 1-2 weeks, dry.
- Flax (linen): very good (700-900 lbs/inch). Pull plant at seed maturity. Ret 1-2 weeks.

**Root fibers**:
- Spruce roots: good strength, excellent flexibility. Boil 1 hour, peel bark, split lengthwise.

**Grass/reed** (moderate, 200-400 lbs/inch):
- Cattail, bulrush, reed grass. Dry thoroughly. Best for mats, baskets, light cordage.

---

## Fiber Preparation

**1. Retting** (separate fibers from plant tissue):
- Bundle plant, submerge in water 5-14 days. Rinse thoroughly. Dry 2-3 days.
- Or dew ret: spread on grass, turn daily, 2-4 weeks. Better quality, no smell.

**2. Breaking**: Bend stalks over log edge or beat with mallet to crack woody tissue.

**3. Scutching**: Scrape broken bits away with dull knife or wooden blade. Leave clean fibers.

**4. Hackling** (optional): Pull fibers through nail board to align and remove short pieces.
- Long fibers (12"+): rope and fine cordage.
- Short fibers (<6"): stuffing or coarse work.

---

## Twisting Technique

**Critical rule**: Twist individual strands one direction. Ply them together in the opposite direction. Counter-twist locks strands and prevents unraveling.

**Two-ply rope**:
1. Hold two fiber bundles anchored at one end.
2. Twist strand A clockwise, then cross it over strand B.
3. Twist strand B clockwise, then cross it over strand A.
4. Repeat — twist, cross, twist, cross.
5. Add new fibers by overlapping 4-6 inches. Keep consistent tension.

**Three-ply rope** (stronger, rounder):
1. Make three two-ply strands.
2. Twist each strand tightly (same direction, e.g., clockwise).
3. Ply all three together in the opposite direction (counterclockwise).
4. Rotate — each strand stays twisted while plying.

---

## Eye Splice

1. Untwist rope end 6-8 inches.
2. Form desired loop size.
3. Tuck each strand: over one standing strand, under one standing strand.
4. Repeat 3-4 tucks per strand.
5. Taper and trim ends.

---

## Strength Reference

- 1/4" three-ply hemp: breaking 300 lbs, working load 50 lbs.
- 1/2" three-ply hemp: breaking 1200 lbs, working load 200 lbs.
- 3/4" three-ply hemp: breaking 2700 lbs, working load 450 lbs.

**Use 1/6 of breaking strength as working load (6:1 safety factor).**
**Wet rope is 10-15% weaker than dry.**

---

## Common Errors

- Rope unravels: wrong twist direction — use counter-twist when plying.
- Fibers break during twist: too dry or over-twisted — dampen fibers, twist gentler.
- Weak spots: fibers not overlapped enough — overlap new fiber 6+ inches.
- Rope rots quickly: moisture trapped — dry thoroughly after use, hang for airflow.
- Splices pull apart: too few tucks — minimum 3 tucks per strand.

---

## Applications

- Shelter: lashing, guy lines, thatch binding.
- Hunting: snares, bowstrings, trap triggers.
- Fire: bow drill cordage.
- Construction: hoisting, scaffolding, structural lashing.
- Water: well draw rope (use rot-resistant fiber — cedar, agave).
- Fishing: lines, nets.

---

## Preservation

- Dry thoroughly after use — prevents rot.
- Coil without kinks, hang or lay flat.
- Store in cool, dry, dark place with airflow.
- Inspect regularly for fraying and weak spots.
- Working rope lifespan: 1-3 years with hard use.
