# Bridge Construction

## Overview

- Assess crossing before building
- Ford if possible: safest, no maintenance
- Log bridge: up to 20 ft span, simple
- Stringer and plank: common walking bridge
- Suspension: up to 100 ft, more complex
- Abutments must be on stable ground
- Annual inspection essential; floods shift

## Ford Assessment

- Check depth at normal and flood stage
- Bottom: solid gravel or rock is safe
- Soft mud bottom: risk of miring
- Mark ford with stakes visible from bank
- Improve ford with compacted gravel fill
- Depth limit: knee-high for loaded pack
  animal; waist-high for unloaded adult

## Log Stringer Bridge

- Two or more logs span the full gap
- Logs minimum 12 in diameter at narrow end
- Notch into abutment to prevent rolling
- Cross-pin with drift pins if available
- Deck planks: 3 in thick, edge to edge
- Nail or peg to stringers; level surface
- Width: 4 ft for foot traffic, 8 ft for cart

## Abutments

- Stone abutment: dry-stone or mortared
- Wood crib: log cage filled with rock
- Earth abutment: compacted, battered slope
- Must bear load without settling or shifting
- Keep abutment top flat and level
- Anchor stringers to prevent float-out

## Suspension Bridge

- Main cables: twisted fiber or steel wire
- Towers: A-frame wood or stone piers
- Deck hangers attach to main cables
- Deck boards on hangers and walkway rails
- Requires careful tensioning and balance
- Width: 3 ft minimum for safe walking
- Maximum span: determined by cable strength

## Maintenance

- Inspect after every major flood
- Look for shifted abutments and rot
- Deck planks crack and rot first; replace
- Cables stretch; retension annually
- Mark weight limits for community
- Ice damage in winter: check in spring

## Long-Term Value

- Opens territory and enables trade
- Reduces seasonal isolation
- Log bridge buildable in one day by crew
- Investment in community connectivity
- Teaches engineering: load, tension, span
