# Root Cellar Build

## Overview

- Root cellar: earth-insulated cold storage
- Target temperature: 32-40F year-round
- Target humidity: 85-95% for most crops
- Location: north-facing hillside is best
- Depth: floor at least 4 ft below frost line
- Works because earth stays near 50F deep
- Extends food supply through entire winter

## Site and Design

- North-facing slope: minimizes sun warming
- Earth bermed on three sides if possible
- Entry door: north-facing, small to limit
  heat gain; insulated door critical
- Ventilation: two pipes, in and out
- Keep roots, fruits separate (ethylene)
- Size: 6x8 ft serves family of four

## Excavation

- Dig into hillside or excavate level site
- Excavate 8 ft deep for floor space
- Allow for wall and floor materials
- Berm excavated soil over roof afterward
- Drainage trench below floor level
- Slope floor 1/4 in per foot to drain

## Walls and Roof

- Dry-stone, mortared stone, or log crib
- Walls minimum 12 in thick stone
- Roof: heavy timbers, then planks, then
  6 in soil over waterproof bark layer
- No air gaps in roof; cold infiltrates
- Entry tunnel: 4 ft long, insulated

## Ventilation

- Two pipes: 4 in diameter each
- Low intake: outside air enters near floor
- High exhaust: warm moist air exits at peak
- Close both pipes in extreme cold
- Open for air exchange in mild weather
- Prevents CO2 buildup from produce

## Shelving and Storage

- Wood shelves; never metal (condensation)
- Potatoes: store in dark in dry sand
- Carrots, beets: pack in damp sand or moss
- Apples, pears: store apart from roots
- Cabbages: hang by root, very pungent
- Label everything; check monthly for rot

## Long-Term Value

- Eliminates refrigeration need entirely
- Doubles effective growing season value
- Reduces spoilage dramatically
- Entirely local materials to build
- 200-year-old root cellars still functional
