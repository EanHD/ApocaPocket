# Scientific Record

## Overview

- Records turn experience into knowledge
- Others can repeat your work only if
  records are complete and accurate
- Date, conditions, observations, results:
  four required elements per entry
- Bound journal: pages cannot be removed;
  prevents tampering with records
- Community records outlive individuals
- Lab notebook standard: never erase,
  draw single line through errors

## Journal Setup

- Bound, not loose-leaf
- Number all pages before first use
- Table of contents: last few pages
  reserved; fill in as you go
- Date format: ISO: 2026-02-24
- One topic per entry; cross-reference
- Index key terms for retrieval
- Store in dry location; protect from
  moisture and pests

## What to Record

- Date and time of observation
- Location: GPS, landmark, or sketch map
- Weather and environmental conditions
- What you observed (not interpreted)
- What you measured: include units
- What you did; exact procedure
- Results: numbers, descriptions, sketches
- Interpretation or hypothesis (labeled)

## Measurement Standards

- Always record units: ft, in, lb, oz
- Define your measurement method once;
  refer back to it in all future entries
- Calibration: record how your instrument
  was checked; e.g., compared to reference
- Uncertainty: note range if not exact
- Consistent units within project
- Convert to metric if sharing widely

## Data Sketches

- Diagram with labels more informative
  than description alone
- North arrow on all maps; scale bar
- Cross-section views for soil, walls
- Plant sketch: leaf, stem, flower detail
- Equipment diagram: shows setup clearly
- Number sketches; reference in text

## Community Records

- All records property of community; not
  individual; agree on this in governance
- Designate keeper of records role
- Annual summary: highlight key findings
- Archive copies in two locations
- Cross-reference agricultural records
  with weather records for patterns
- Teach recording habit to all apprentices

## Long-Term Value

- Darwin's 20 years of notes enabled
  On the Origin of Species
- Faraday's notebooks: foundation of
  electromagnetic knowledge
- Records survive individuals; knowledge
  accumulates across generations
