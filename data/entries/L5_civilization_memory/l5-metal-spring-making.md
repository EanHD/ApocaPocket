# Spring Making

## Overview

- Springs store and release energy
- Types: coil, leaf, flat, torsion
- Material: high-carbon steel (0.7-1.0% C)
- Coil springs: from wire; wound on mandrel
- Leaf springs: flat bar; heat treated
- Temper to blue for optimal spring quality
- Critical for: clocks, locks, bows, traps,
  valves, vehicle suspension

## Spring Steel Sources

- Drawn wire with high carbon content
- Old cart springs, saw blades, files
- Saber or sword blades
- Spring steel from bloomery is possible
  with careful carbon control
- Spark test: bright white bursting sparks
  indicate high carbon content
- Test small piece: quench; see if brittle

## Coil Spring Making

- Mandrel: steel rod of desired ID
- Heat wire section to bright orange
- Wrap around mandrel while hot
- Space coils evenly with wire rod spacer
- Allow to cool on mandrel
- Remove: spring will be larger than mandrel
- Cut to length with cold chisel

## Leaf Spring Making

- High-carbon flat bar; anneal first
- Shape to desired curve while hot
- Multiple leaves: same curve each
- Clamp together; normalize together
- Final curve set by bending while hot
- Must be symmetric and centered
- Punch center hole for clamp bolt

## Hardening Springs

- Heat uniformly to critical temperature
- All red-orange with no dark spots
- Quench in warm oil (never water)
- Water quench cracks coil springs
- Handle carefully after quench; brittle
- Test: tap gently; should ring not crack

## Tempering to Blue

- Polish surface bright to see color
- Heat slowly and uniformly
- Watch for oxide colors to advance:
  yellow, straw, brown, purple, then blue
- Springs: pull at blue (570F)
- Quench in oil to halt temper
- Spring should flex and recover fully
- If it bends: under-tempered; redo

## Long-Term Value

- Springs enable mechanisms and machines
- Locks, clocks, traps all require springs
- Mechanical advantage multiplied
- Any blacksmith can learn spring work
- Old springs are valuable scrap stock
