# Blacksmithing

One blacksmith equips an entire village. Axes, hoes, knives, nails. Every other trade depends on iron tools.

## Forge Construction

**Components:** Firepot (fire chamber), hearth (work surface), tuyere (air inlet), chimney.

**Simple brick forge**
- Hearth: 3×3×3 ft. Firepot: 12-16" diameter, 6-8" deep, centered on hearth.
- Fire bricks with clay mortar. Tuyere pipe enters firepot at 45° upward angle.
- Hood minimum 3 ft above fire. Chimney 6-10 ft tall for strong draft.
- Airflow: Bellows → tuyere → firepot → chimney.

## Fuel

- Bituminous coal (best): 2500-3000°F with forced air. Bank fresh coal around edges to coke.
- Charcoal (good): 2000-2200°F. Cleaner smoke. Renewable. Burns faster than coal.
- No regular wood: Too cool, too smoky, cannot sustain forging temperatures.

## Anvil

- Minimum 100-200 lbs. Hard tool-steel face. Mounted on solid stump or steel stand.
- Parts: Face (flat top), horn (curved end for bending), hardy hole (square, bottom tools), pritchel hole (round, punching).
- Improvised: Railroad rail (best), thick steel plate bolted to wood block, large granite.

## Heat Colors

- Below 900°F (black): Not visible. Too cold to forge.
- 900-1100°F (blood-dark cherry): Barely visible. Too cold. Will crack under hammer.
- 1200-1400°F (cherry red): PRIMARY FORGING RANGE. 90% of all work happens here.
- 1400-1600°F (bright cherry-orange): Heavy forging, large bends.
- 1900-2100°F (yellow): Forge welding. Flux with borax. Work fast after removing from fire.
- 2300°F+ (white with sparks): TOO HOT. Iron is burning. Stop immediately.

## Basic Techniques

**Drawing out (lengthening)**
Heat to cherry red. Strike at angle to push metal forward. Rotate 90° between blows. Reheat often.

**Upsetting (thickening)**
Heat to orange. Strike end-on into anvil face. Metal compresses and widens.

**Bending**
Heat to cherry red. Use horn or hardy hole for leverage. Use multiple heats for sharp angles.

**Punching holes**
Heat to bright cherry. Drive punch halfway through. Flip piece. Drive through from other side. Drift to final size.

**Forge welding**
Scarf ends (taper to match). Flux both with borax. Heat to yellow-white (near sparking). Strike immediately. Reheat and hammer to smooth joint.

## Hardening Steel

- Steel = iron + 0.3-2% carbon. Can harden. Wrought iron cannot.
- Harden: Heat to critical temp (orange, non-magnetic on magnet test). Quench in oil (best) or water.
- Result: Very hard but brittle. Must temper or it shatters in use.
- Temper: Polish steel to bright shine. Reheat slowly. Watch oxide colors appear:
- Pale yellow = razor and knife edge (hardest). Straw = chisel and axe. Purple = springs. Blue = maximum flex.
- Quench at desired color to stop tempering process.

## Safety

- Hot iron looks cold after glow fades. Still 500-900°F. Always use tongs. Never assume cold.
- Leather apron, long sleeves, closed-toe boots. Eye protection against sparks at all times.
- Coal and charcoal produce CO. Work outdoors or with a chimney creating strong draft. Never forge in enclosed space.
- Hammering exceeds 100 dB. Wear earplugs for sessions longer than 30 minutes.

## Beginner Projects

- S-hook (2 hrs): Draw out taper, bend over horn. Hanging pots and tools.
- Nail (30 min each): Draw out point, upset head, cut from stock. Build toward 50/day.
- Fire poker (3 hrs): Draw out point, twist middle section, bend handle loop.
- Knife blade (8-10 hrs): Draw out, shape, harden, temper. First true test of heat color knowledge.
