# Leadership Succession

## Overview

- Succession: who holds authority when
  a leader is absent, incapacitated, or dead
- Power vacuum is most violent governance
  moment; fill it with law, not force
- Must be: written, known, accepted before
  it is needed; not decided in crisis
- Test the plan: practice during routine
  absences, not just true emergencies
- Multiple contingency levels required
- Ostrom: governance rules must exist
  before they are needed

## Succession Order

- Define explicitly in constitution
- Primary: deputy or vice-chair
- Secondary: council senior member
- Tertiary: full council collective
- Emergency assembly: if all above fail
- No gap between any level
- Announce order publicly; post it
- Annual confirmation by council vote

## Interim Authority

- Interim: holds power only until proper
  succession or election completed
- Limited powers: routine operations only
- No major decisions without council
- Timeline: election within 30-60 days
- Announce interim status publicly
- Interim cannot run in succession election
  (prevents self-dealing)

## Incapacity vs Death

- Incapacity: temporary (injury, illness)
  Threshold: council declares by majority
  Reassume: when incapacitated returns;
  council vote to confirm recovery
- Death or permanent incapacity:
  Formal declaration; witnesses recorded
  Succession activates immediately
  Election scheduled within timeline

## Preventing Succession

- Only one person can hold position;
  competing claims cause violence
- Written designation beats any claim
- Witnesses: multiple independent witnesses
  to all succession documents
- Oral tradition of succession: written down
  at minimum in council minutes
- Public knowledge: everyone knows order

## Preparing Successors

- Deputy actively involved in decisions
- Not figurehead; actual practice authority
- Regular briefings: knows all ongoing work
- Access to all community records
- Meeting with key role holders
- Test: delegate real tasks; assess
- Successor must want the role; not forced

## Long-Term Value

- Planned succession = continuity of
  governance through any individual loss
- Roman interrex model: 5-day relay worked
  because it was planned and respected
- Most historical collapses trace to
  succession failure
- Write the plan today; it may never be
  needed; if needed it will save the community
