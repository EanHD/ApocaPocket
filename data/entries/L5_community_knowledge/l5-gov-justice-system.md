# Community Justice

## Overview

- Justice: resolving harm within community
- Two models: punitive (punishment focus)
  and restorative (repair focus)
- Restorative is more effective long-term
  in small communities; people must coexist
- Process legitimacy matters as much as
  outcome; unfair process = resentment
- Ostrom: conflict resolution mechanisms
  must be cheap and accessible to all
- No private revenge; channel through process

## Restorative Model

- Harm recognized; victim named
- Offender acknowledges responsibility
- Dialogue: victim, offender, community
- Agreement: what will offender do to repair
- Implementation: monitored; verified
- Record: written; signed by parties
- Community restoration: when appropriate

## Graduated Sanctions

- Minor violations: private warning
- Repeated minor: public warning; record
- Moderate harm: restitution payment
- Serious harm: labor or service penalty
- Repeat serious: temporary exclusion
- Extreme harm: permanent exclusion
  or physical sanction per constitution
- All steps require process; no shortcuts

## The Hearing Process

- Neutral facilitator or council panel
- Accused informed of charge in advance
- Evidence presented by accuser
- Accused responds; evidence presented
- Community may speak briefly
- Panel deliberates privately
- Decision announced with reasoning
- Record kept; appeal path stated

## Punitive vs

- Punitive: fine, punishment, exclusion
  Good for: deterrence, serious crimes
  Risk: escalation, ongoing enmity
- Restorative: apology, restitution,
  service, dialogue
  Good for: property crimes, disputes
  between people who must still live together
- Use both: graduated system

## Preventing Vigilantism

- Clear, fast process reduces private
  justice seeking
- Delay breeds revenge; timely hearings
  are essential (within 2 weeks)
- Community witness: prevents secret deals
- Record all settlements
- Enforce settlements; broken agreements
  escalate immediately to council
- Make legitimate process easier than revenge

## Long-Term Value

- Justice system is civilization core
- Communities without it fragment
- Restorative model keeps skilled people
  in community rather than exiling
- Trust built by fair process; not just
  outcomes
