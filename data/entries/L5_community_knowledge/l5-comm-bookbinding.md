# Bookbinding

## Overview

- Binding: assembling loose pages into
  permanent book form
- Methods: pamphlet, Coptic, case binding
- Pamphlet: simplest; single signature
- Coptic: multiple signatures; lies flat
- Case (hard cover): most durable
- All require: needle, thread, pages
- Bound records survive; loose pages scatter

## Paper and Folding

- Fold sheets in half: creates signatures
- Signature: group of nested folded sheets
- 4-6 sheets per signature typical
- Grain direction: fold parallel to grain
  or pages will not lie flat
- Burnish each fold with bone folder
- Mark page sequence before binding

## Pamphlet Stitch

- Single signature; 3-5 hole stitch
- Mark holes along spine fold
- Awl: pierce through signature
- Thread through center hole from outside
- In, out, in alternating; return through
  center hole and tie off
- Strong for small booklets up to 48 pages

## Coptic Stitch

- Multiple signatures; exposed thread spine
- Each signature linked to previous
- Three-hole variation for beginners
- End-to-end linking creates flexible spine
- Book opens fully flat: excellent for use
- Waxed linen thread: strongest
- Traditional Ethiopian and Coptic method

## Covers

- Soft cover: thick card or thin board
  wrapped in cloth or leather
- Hard cover: two boards; cloth spine
- Boards: compressed bark, split wood,
  or waste paper laminated
- Attach to text block with endpapers
- Glue: wheat paste or animal hide glue
- Press under weight while drying: 48 hr

## Binding Adhesives

- Wheat paste: cooked wheat flour + water
  Non-toxic; reversible; good for paper
- Hide glue: animal skin and bones boiled
  Very strong; rigid when dry
- Pine resin + tallow: flexible; weather
  resistant for field use
- Avoid modern PVA if not available

## Long-Term Value

- Bound books survive transport and use
- Community records need binding
- Medical, legal, and technical manuals
  in book form are preserved knowledge
- Teach alongside reading and writing
- One bookbinder serves entire community
