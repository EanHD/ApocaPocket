# Block Printing

## Overview

- Block printing: carved wood surface
  transfers ink to paper by pressure
- Each block prints one page; reusable
- Precedes movable type; still effective
- Ideal for: rules, calendars, maps, forms,
  currency notes, announcements
- Speed: 50-100 impressions per hour
- Cherry, pearwood, or maple: best blocks

## Block Preparation

- Choose close-grained, dry hardwood
- Thickness: 1 in minimum for stability
- Surface: planed flat and smooth
- Sand progressively: 100 to 220 grit
- Final surface: smooth as glass
- Grain direction: end grain for fine detail;
  flat grain for large areas

## Transferring Design

- Write or draw design on thin paper
- Reverse text and images before transfer
  (everything must be mirror image)
- Paste design face-down onto block
- Rub damp cloth: paper becomes transparent
- Trace through paper onto wood
- Remove paper; design visible on block
- Re-check reversal before cutting

## Carving

- Remove all areas that should NOT print
- Tools: V-gouge, U-gouge, flat chisel
- Cut outline first; then remove field
- Depth: 1/8 in minimum for ink clearance
- Undercut edges: prevents ink bleed
- Test print: roll ink; press paper; inspect
- Correct errors by gouge or fill

## Inking and Printing

- Ink: thick; lampblack and linseed oil
- Roll ink over block with leather brayer
- Even, thin coat; not pooling
- Lay paper; rub firmly with barren or
  folded cloth; even pressure
- Lift paper carefully from one corner
- Re-ink every 2-3 impressions

## Simple Printing Press

- Two flat boards hinged at one edge
- Block fixed to bottom board
- Paper laid on block; top board closed
- Press with weight or lever arm
- More even pressure than hand rubbing
- Consistent results important for forms
- Screw press: traditional design

## Long-Term Value

- Multiplies written communication 100x
- Consistent forms reduce errors
- Legal documents, records, currency
- Maps and diagrams reproducible
- Technology accessible without electricity
