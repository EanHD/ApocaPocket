# Community Education

## Overview

- Education: transfer of knowledge and skill
  to those who do not yet have them
- Formal school: structured; multiple
  students; one teacher; scheduled
- Apprenticeship: one-on-one; learning by
  doing alongside master practitioner
- Oral tradition: stories, songs, ritual
  carry cultural and practical knowledge
- All three needed; none alone is sufficient

## Apprenticeship Model

- Apprentice works alongside master
- Observes first; assists second;
  performs independently third
- Gradual handoff of responsibility
- Assessment: can apprentice do it safely
  and correctly without supervision?
- Duration: months to years by skill
- Contract: mutual commitment; obligations
  on both master and apprentice

## Critical Skills

- Medical: first aid, midwifery, herbal
- Agricultural: soil, seed, season
- Construction: foundation, framing, water
- Metallurgy: smithing, smelting
- Water: purification, well, irrigation
- Governance: records, law, facilitation
- Teaching itself: meta-skill; multiply
  all others

## Formal School

- Schedule: regular; predictable; daily
- Basics: reading, writing, arithmetic;
  all children by age 7-10
- Sciences: practical first; theory after
- History of community: own survival story
- Skills: rotate teachers by specialty
- Space: designated; weather-protected
- Materials: slates, chalk, paper as available

## Oral Tradition

- Pre-literacy: most knowledge was oral
- Memory techniques: rhythm, rhyme, story
- Loci method: mental location for recall
- Sung knowledge: endures longer than prose
- Recitation: group repetition cements info
- Storytelling: passes values and history
- Test: can student teach it to another?

## Record and Curriculum

- Community curriculum: what must everyone
  know? Written list; reviewed annually
- Skills map: who knows what; where are gaps
- Teaching materials: written guides help
  consistency; do not rely on memory alone
- Graduation recognition: ceremony matters
- Keep teaching record: who taught whom

## Long-Term Value

- Education is civilization's immune system
- Skills die with single practitioner;
  apprenticeship prevents this
- Literate community makes better decisions
- Children taught well become teachers
- Investment in education compounds over
  generations faster than any other resource
