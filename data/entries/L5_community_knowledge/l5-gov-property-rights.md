# Property Rights

## Overview

- Property rights: who may use, modify,
  exclude others from, and transfer resource
- Four types: private, communal, state,
  open access (no rights = tragedy)
- Tragedy of commons: without defined rights
  shared resources are over-exploited
- Ostrom: clearly defined boundaries are
  the first principle of commons governance
- Clear rights = investment motivation

## Types of Property

- Private: individual or household owns
  exclusively; typical for homes, tools
- Communal: group shares; rules govern
  use; typical for forest, water, pasture
- State or collective: community-wide;
  governed by council; typical for roads
- Open access: no ownership; no protection
  from over-use; convert to managed type

## Land Registry

- Written record: who holds what parcel
- Survey boundary: marked on ground
- Witnesses: two non-interested witnesses
  at transfer or initial assignment
- Bound registry book: permanent record
- Copy in two locations; fire protection
- Transfers recorded within 30 days
- No transfer valid without registry entry

## Dispute Resolution

- First step: direct negotiation between
  parties; written agreement if resolved
- Second step: community mediator;
  neutral third party; no authority
- Third step: council hearing with evidence
- Final: assembly vote on contested matter
- Survey re-established by neutral party
- Historical testimony: long-use rights
- Written records superior to memory

## Transfer Rules

- Sale: voluntary; written agreement;
  witnessed; registered
- Inheritance: written will or succession
  rules in constitution
- Gift: same as sale process
- Abandonment: community may claim after
  defined period (e.g., 3-5 years unused)
- Forfeiture: for serious crimes only;
  defined by law; council vote required

## Commons Management

- Define: who may use, when, how much
- Exclusion: non-members cannot use
- Allocation: fair share rules documented
- Monitoring: appointed role checks compliance
- Graduated sanctions: warnings before
  exclusion
- Annual review of allocation rules

## Long-Term Value

- Secure property rights motivate
  investment, improvement, and stewardship
- Prevents conflict from ambiguity
- Foundation for trade and contract
- Registry survives generations if maintained
- De Soto: formalized property rights are
  the basis of economic development
