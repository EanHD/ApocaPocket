# Community Trade

## Overview

- Trade: voluntary exchange of goods
  or services for mutual benefit
- Three systems: direct barter, ledger
  credit, and commodity currency
- Direct barter: no trust required; limited
- Ledger credit: requires trust and honesty
- Commodity currency: medium of exchange
- All require: agreed value standards
  and enforcement of agreements

## Direct Barter

- One party's surplus for another's surplus
- Works for: known parties, immediate trade
- Problem: double coincidence of wants
  (I need what you have AND vice versa)
- Solution: multi-party barter chain
- Record: even simple barters written down
- Prevents later disputes about terms
- Most ancient trade method; still valid

## Ledger Credit System

- Communal ledger: public record of credits
- Member provides service; earns credit
- Member receives service; spends credit
- Debit: spend more than earned; limited
- Account balance: visible to community
- Periodic settlement: zero out accounts
- Dishonest entry: dual-signature required

## Commodity Currency

- Commodity: widely valued item used as
  currency (grain, salt, metal, cloth)
- Properties of good currency:
- Divisible (can be split)
- Durable (does not rot)
- Portable (easy to carry)
- Scarce but not hoarded by few
- Salt and grain: ancient standards
- Metal coins: durable; scarce; divisible

## Trade Standards

- Weights and measures: community standard
- Reference weight: maintained by council
- Measuring vessel: reference container
- Disputes about weight: council adjudication
- Market time: regular fixed day
- Market rules: posted; enforce no fraud
- Visiting traders: same rules apply

## Recording Agreements

- Verbal trade binding but unenforceable
- Written: both parties mark or sign
- Witness: third party signs
- Registry: trade disputes resolved by
  reference to written record
- Debt instruments: written; transferable
- Default: council enforces; not violence

## Long-Term Value

- Trade multiplies community output
  through specialization
- Division of labor requires trade system
- Stable medium of exchange attracts
  outside traders and investment
- Economic complexity follows good
  trade governance
