# Semaphore Signals

## Overview

- Semaphore: two flags held at angles
  encode letters of alphabet
- Range: up to 2 miles with binoculars;
  500 m reliably with naked eye
- No materials needed beyond flags
  or improvised substitutes
- Day use only; night requires lights
- Speed: 10-20 characters per minute
- Widely used by navies and armies

## Equipment

- Two flags: 18-in square; high contrast
- Red and white: standard naval pattern
- Any two-color or contrasting flags work
- Sticks: 18-24 in handles
- Substitute: bright cloth tied to sticks
- Receiver: binoculars extend range greatly
- Practice pad: for learning positions

## Flag Positions (Clock

- Imagine clock face; sender at center
- Positions named by clock hours
- Right arm positions: 1, 2, 3 (pointing)
- Left arm positions: 7, 8, 9, 10, 11
- Both arms used simultaneously
- Each letter has unique combination
- Numeric shift signal precedes numbers

## Common Letters

- A: L-low(7), R-down(5)
- E: L-side(9), R-down(5)
- S: L-up(11), R-down(5)
- O: L-down(7), R-up(1)
- N: L-down-left(8), R-up(1)
- T: L-side(9), R-side(3)
- SOS: repeated attention signal; both
  flags overhead then down rapidly

## Sending Procedure

- Attention signal: both flags overhead
- Wait for ready signal from receiver
- Send each letter; pause between
- Receiver signals each word received
- Error signal: arms crossed overhead
- Resend last word on error signal
- End: C signal (both flags cross front)

## Receiving

- Record each letter as received
- Decode words after full word received
- Do not call for repeat too often; flows
- Signal confirmation after each word
- If message unclear at end: signal error
- Write out full message; read back

## Long-Term Value

- Works across river, valley, or open field
- No infrastructure required
- International standard: interoperable
- Complements runner network and fires
- Train relay teams for long-distance
  message passing between posts
