# Mass Casualty Triage

Mass casualty: more victims than available rescuers can treat at once. START (Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment) saves the most lives with limited resources.

## Immediate Actions

- Scene safety first. Active shooter, unstable building, fire, hazmat = wait or move victims first.
- Call for help. Give location, victim count, nature of incident.
- Assign roles: Triage Officer (tags only, does NOT treat), Treatment Team, Walking Wounded Manager.
- Gather: Tourniquets, pressure bandages, triage tags or markers, blankets, gloves.

## START Triage Protocol

**Step 1: Walking Wounded**
Yell loudly: "Anyone who can walk, move to [point to safe area]!"
All who walk = GREEN (minor). Assign one person to manage them.

**Step 2: Non-Walking Victims (30 seconds max each)**
- Breathing? NO → Open airway (head tilt-chin lift). Still no breathing → BLACK (dead). Starts breathing → RED.
- Breathing rate over 30/min → RED (respiratory distress).
- Radial pulse weak or absent → RED (shock).
- Mental status: "Squeeze my hand." No response → RED. Responds and follows commands → YELLOW.

**Triage categories**
- GREEN (Minor): Can walk. Minor injuries. Can wait hours for care.
- YELLOW (Delayed): Serious but stable. Wait 30 min to 2 hrs. Treat after all REDs stabilized.
- RED (Immediate): Life-threatening but saveable. Treat NOW.
- BLACK (Dead/Expectant): Not breathing after airway opened. No CPR in mass casualty.

**Critical rule:** No CPR in mass casualty. One CPR attempt = 2 rescuers tied up for 15+ min = 10+ other victims die.

## Timeline

**0-15 min — Rapid Triage**
- Triage Officer moves through ALL victims systematically. Tag only. Do not stop to treat.
- Treatment Team stands by until all REDs identified.

**15-30 min — RED Interventions**
- Bleeding: Tourniquet for arterial (spurting). Direct pressure for venous (oozing). Fast, move on.
- Airway: Recovery position for unconscious. Clear visible obstructions.
- Sucking chest wounds: Seal with plastic taped on 3 sides (allows air out, not in).
- Shock: Lie flat, elevate legs if no spinal injury, keep warm.

**30 min to 2 hrs — YELLOW Care and Re-Triage**
- Begin treating YELLOW tags once all REDs are stabilized.
- Re-triage ALL victims every 15-30 min. Stable → deteriorating or vice versa.

**Evacuation order (when ambulances arrive): RED → YELLOW → GREEN.**

## Fatal Mistakes

- Treating first victim seen before triaging others. REDs bleed out while you bandage a GREEN.
- Attempting CPR in mass casualty. Wastes 2 rescuers for 15+ min on uncertain outcome.
- Family member triaging their own loved ones. Emotional bias = overtriage or undertriage.
- Ignoring scene safety. Rescuer becomes victim, reducing help available.
- No incident command. Everyone does their own thing. Chaos. Missed victims.
- GREEN tag self-loads into first ambulance. Block until RED/YELLOW loaded first.
- No documentation. Cannot track patients, reunite families, or learn lessons.

## Responder Wellness

**During event**
- Numbness and dissociation are normal. They allow you to function. Use them.
- Rotate high-stress roles (triage, treatment) every 1-2 hrs. Fatigue causes fatal mistakes.
- Hydrate and eat even if not hungry. You are burning energy.

**After event**
- Flashbacks, nightmares, anger, crying are normal for the first 72 hours.
- Debrief with team. Talk through what happened. No shame in "this was horrible."
- Watch for isolation, substance use, aggression in teammates.

**Seek immediate help if:** Cannot function after 1 week, or suicidal thoughts appear.
