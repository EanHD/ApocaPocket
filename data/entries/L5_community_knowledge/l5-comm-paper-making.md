# Paper Making

## Overview

- Paper: plant fiber felted into flat sheet
- Source fibers: rags (cotton, linen),
  cattail, hemp, straw, bark, grass
- Better fiber = stronger paper
- Cotton and linen rags: best quality
- Process: ret, cook, beat, form, dry
- Product: writing surface for records,
  books, maps, messages, wrapping

## Fiber Preparation

- Rags: cut to small pieces; remove buttons
- Ret: soak in water 2-3 days to soften
- Cook: boil in lye water 2-4 hours
- Rinsing thoroughly: remove all lye
- Beat thoroughly: mortar, mallet, or
  stamp mill reduces to individual fibers
- Full beating: 2-4 hours; fibers must
  fully separate and hydrate

## Vat Setup

- Large tub or vat: at least 3x4 ft wide
- Fill with water; add pulp (1% by weight)
- Stir: distribute fibers evenly
- Mold: wooden frame with wire or screen
- Deckle: frame that sits on top of mold
  and contains the pulp sheet
- Both required for consistent sheets

## Sheet Forming

- Hold mold under water surface
- Raise mold horizontally through pulp
- Sheet forms as water drains through screen
- Shake gently side-to-side while lifting
- Interlocks fibers; key to strength
- Rest on angled board; allow water to drain
- Sheet must be uniform; check for thin spots

## Couching and Drying

- Couch: transfer wet sheet to felt or cloth
- Press to remove water: between felts
- Stack sheets and felts; press with weight
- Press 30 minutes; transfer to drying boards
- Dry in air: 24-48 hours; avoid direct sun
- Peel when completely dry
- Size: brush with dilute gelatin or starch
  to reduce ink bleed

## Paper Quality

- Rag paper: strong; holds ink well
- Unsized paper: ink bleeds; drawing only
- Test: fold; should not crack at fold
- Weight: hold to light; uniform cloud
- Defects: thin spots, bits of unbeaten
  fiber; acceptable for records

## Long-Term Value

- Paper production enables all written
  record keeping and communication
- Preserves knowledge across generations
- Community paper mill: priority build
- Rough paper for daily records;
  fine paper for permanent documents
