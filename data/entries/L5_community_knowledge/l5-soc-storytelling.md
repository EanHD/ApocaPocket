# Storytelling

## Overview

- Storytelling: structured narrative shared
  with an audience
- Functions: history preservation, value
  transmission, entertainment, identity
- Oral tradition carried civilization for
  tens of thousands of years before writing
- Still essential when literacy is low
  or materials unavailable
- Story is how humans process experience
  and share meaning

## Types of Stories

- Historical: what actually happened;
  community origin, crisis, survival
- Practical: how to do something; embedded
  in narrative for memory
- Moral: values demonstrated through
  character choices and consequences
- Mythological: meaning and worldview;
  not literally true but socially true
- Personal: individual experience;
  builds empathy and connection

## Story Structure

- Beginning: establish setting and character
- Tension: what is the problem or challenge
- Climax: moment of highest tension; choice
- Resolution: outcome of the choice
- Coda: what it means; lesson if any
- This structure is universal; found in
  every culture's oral tradition

## Memory Techniques

- Rule of three: listeners remember sets
  of three; structure stories with threes
- Repetition: key phrases repeated verbatim
- Vivid detail: sensory specifics aid memory
- Formulaic phrases: opening and closing
  rituals signal story mode to listeners
- Characters: consistent; recognizable types
- Embed facts: technical info in narrative

## Performance Elements

- Voice: vary pace, pitch, volume
- Pause: silence builds tension
- Eye contact: connect with audience
- Gesture: illustrates action
- Audience participation: call-and-response
- Callback: reference earlier element
- Practice: good storytellers rehearse

## Community Story

- Written record of oral stories
- Title, teller, date, occasion, version
- Core stories: those taught to all children
- Historical accuracy review: annually
- Update: living stories grow; record updates
- Public telling: required occasions include
  founding story, crisis stories, hero stories

## Long-Term Value

- Oral tradition preserved Maori, Aboriginal,
  and Homer across centuries without writing
- Story encodes information in brain
  more durably than list or text
- Values transmission faster than rule
- Community with shared story is cohesive
- Storytelling skill: 2 weeks to basics
