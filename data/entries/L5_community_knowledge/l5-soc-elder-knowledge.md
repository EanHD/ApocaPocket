# Elder Knowledge

## Overview

- Elder knowledge: accumulated experience
  and skill held by older community members
- Most valuable asset cannot be bought
- Types: practical skills, local ecology,
  social history, conflict memory,
  relationship networks, crisis memory
- Written records do not capture all
  of this; oral transmission supplements
- Every elder death is an irreversible loss

## Types of Elder

- Technical: how to do specific tasks
  correctly; details not in any book
- Local: which plants grow where; which
  years had floods; what soils do what
- Social: who has what skills; old conflicts;
  family relationships; trust networks
- Historical: what happened before; lessons
  from past crises; what worked and failed
- Cultural: songs, stories, rituals, values

## Knowledge Extraction

- Structured interview: not conversation
- Ask specifically: "Tell me the steps
  for doing X from the beginning"
- Record verbatim; do not paraphrase
- Ask follow-up: "What do you watch for?
  What goes wrong? What did you learn
  by making mistakes?"
- Demonstrate: video is best; written
  next best; do not rely on memory
- Repeat interview: different day, new info

## Oral Tradition

- Weekly elder storytelling session
- Stories of community history; crisis
- Young people required to attend
- Listeners retell next day to others
- Chain of transmission; not single source
- Verify facts against written records
- Correct errors promptly; myths form fast

## Recording and Writing

- Transcribe oral knowledge to written
  as soon as possible
- Elder reviews transcript; corrects errors
- Organize: by topic, not by person
- Cross-reference: same subject from
  multiple elders reveals consensus
- Photograph or sketch where words fail
- Bind in community knowledge library

## Respecting Elders

- Assign elders meaningful advisory roles
- Consult before major decisions
- Do not dismiss "old ways" without testing
- Honor with community ceremony
- Ensure elders are fed, housed, safe
- Elder who is anxious does not teach well
- Reciprocity: care for elders; they teach

## Long-Term Value

- Elder knowledge accelerates community
  recovery by decades
- Local ecology knowledge: 50-year cycles
  not visible in any book
- Conflict memory prevents repeat of
  historical mistakes
- Loss of elders in crisis is catastrophic;
  prioritize their safety deliberately
