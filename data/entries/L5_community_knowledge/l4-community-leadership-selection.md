# Community Lead

Leadership quality determines whether a community survives. Too little = chaos. Too much = tyranny.

## Leadership Models

- Democratic election: Voted in. Legitimate but slow. Best for stable groups of 30+.
- Meritocracy: Skills determine role. Efficient in crisis. Use for medical and technical leads.
- Council (3-7 people): Diverse expertise. Checks individual power. Best for complex communities.
- Rotating leadership: Prevents entrenchment. Good for stable periods and training new leaders.
- Situational: Different leaders for crisis vs daily operations. Needs clear handoff triggers.

**Recommended:** Functional domain leads (medical = merit, general = election) plus rotating council.

## Selection Criteria

Rate each candidate. Minimum thresholds vary by role.
- Domain competence: Can they do the job themselves? Do people trust their expertise?
- Fairness/integrity: No favoritism. Follows own rules. Admits mistakes. Handles resources honestly.
- Communication: Clear instructions. Listens before talking. Honest about bad news.
- Emotional intelligence: Calm under pressure. Empathetic. De-escalates conflict.
- Stamina: Reliable, consistent, bounces back from setbacks.

**Integrity is non-negotiable.** Without it, all other competencies become dangerous.

## Selection Process

- Week 1: Open nominations. Written 200-word justification required for each.
- Week 2: Review committee (5-7 people). Check credentials. Talk to 3+ references. Screen conflicts.
- Week 3: Public forum. Each candidate gets 10-min presentation + 20-min community Q&A.
- Week 4: Vote (secret ballot, ranked choice) OR consensus (require 70% approval).
- Week 5-6: Transition. Shadow outgoing leader. Document key procedures and contacts.

## Term Limits

- General leadership: 1-year term, max 2 consecutive, 1-year cooling-off period.
- Technical specialists: 2-year term, max 3 consecutive.
- Emergency leaders: Until crisis resolves, then revert to normal process.
- Councils: Stagger terms so only 1/3 rotate at once (maintains institutional knowledge).

Power concentration corrupts. Even good leaders must rotate out.

## Checks & Balances

- Recall process: 25% petition → written charges → leader responds → 60% vote to remove.
- Community veto: 40% petition triggers community vote. 60% overturns leader decision.
- Separation of powers: Never combine resource allocation + security in one person.
- Never combine arbitration/justice + general leadership (conflict of interest).

**Immediate recall grounds:** Abuse of power, resource theft, sexual harassment, intimidation.

## Succession Plan

- Identify 2-3 backups per role. Shadow current leader 1 day/month minimum.
- Leader maintains: Decision log, procedure manual, contact list, resource inventory.
- Document succession order: Primary → 24-hr backup → 7-day backup → trigger new election.
- Transition rituals matter: Public handoff, outgoing endorsement, new leader's commitment statement.

## Bad Leader Signs

- Favoritism, secrecy about decisions, refusing feedback from community.
- Resource hoarding, scapegoating others for failures, expanding own power domain.
- Intimidation, cult of personality, surrounding themselves with yes-men.
- Taking credit for others' work. Rage at criticism.

Act at first signs. Waiting until rage stage makes removal far harder.

## Key Principles

- No single best leadership model. Context and community size determine structure.
- Develop 5-10 leadership-capable people per 30-person community. Distribute capacity.
- Dissent is healthy. Leaders who punish criticism become tyrants.
- Charisma does not equal competence. Verify both independently.
- One narcissist in leadership destroys what good leaders spent years building.
