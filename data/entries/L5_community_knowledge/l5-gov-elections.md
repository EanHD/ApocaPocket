# Community Elections

## Overview

- Elections: formal selection of leaders
  by community vote
- Legitimacy: elected leaders have mandate
  from those who chose them
- Secret ballot: prevents coercion
- Term limits: prevents entrenchment
- Regular schedule: prevents indefinite rule
- Contested elections: sign of health;
  uncontested: investigate why
- Record all nominations and vote counts

## Nomination Process

- Announcement period: 30 days minimum
- Any member may nominate self or another
- Nominee accepts or declines in writing
- Brief biography or statement posted
  publicly for 2 weeks before election
- Eligibility rules defined in constitution
- Disqualifiers: clear and pre-defined
- No last-minute nominations; prevent gamesmanship

## Ballot Methods

- Plurality: most votes wins; simple
- Majority runoff: if no majority, top
  two candidates run again
- Ranked choice: voters rank all candidates;
  eliminates lowest; redistributes votes
- Approval voting: vote for all acceptable;
  most approvals wins
- Choose method before nominations open
- Write method in constitution

## Conducting the Vote

- Polling place: neutral location; observed
- Ballot: written; marks candidate name
- Fold ballot before depositing in box
- Sealed box opened publicly after polls close
- Two independent counters; reconcile
- Results announced and posted publicly
- Ballots stored until term complete

## Counting and

- Count in public; any member may observe
- Two independent tallies; must match
- Disputes: recount with new observers
- Results signed by witnesses and counters
- Post results within 24 hours
- Losing candidates sign acceptance or
  formally contest with specific evidence

## Term Limits

- Standard term: 1-2 years
- Maximum consecutive terms: 2-3
- Cooldown period: 1 term before re-running
- Term limits are democratic hygiene
- Record all terms served; enforce
- Council vacancy: special election within
  30 days; interim appointment optional

## Long-Term Value

- Regular elections maintain accountability
- Peaceful transitions prevent succession
  violence; most dangerous political moment
- Participation builds community investment
- Skills: campaign, debate, vote, accept
  results; teach to all age 12+
