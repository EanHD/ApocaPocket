# Ink Making

## Overview

- Three main types: carbon, iron gall, plant
- Carbon (lamp black): oldest; fade-proof
- Iron gall: standard European writing ink;
  deeply penetrates paper; very permanent
- Plant-based: berry, root, bark; variable
  permanence; some fade in years
- All need binder: gum arabic or gum resin
- Water or vinegar as solvent

## Carbon Black Ink

- Collect soot from oil lamp or pine torch
- Soot is near-pure carbon; very stable
- Dissolve gum arabic in warm water:
  1 part gum to 5 parts water
- Mix soot into gum solution
- Grind with muller until smooth paste
- Thin with water to desired consistency
- Store in sealed container; add water
  as needed when dries out

## Iron Gall Ink

- Oak galls: round growths on oak leaves
  caused by wasps; collect in fall
- Crush galls; steep in water 24 hours
  or boil 1 hour for stronger tannin
- Add iron sulfate (iron vitriol):
  found near iron-containing springs or
  made by dissolving iron filings in vinegar
- Reaction: darkens to black over days
- Add gum arabic binder: 1 tsp per cup
- Strain well; store in sealed dark bottle

## Plant-Based Inks

- Pokeberry: deep purple; fades without fix
- Elderberry: purple-black
- Black walnut hull: very dark brown;
  excellent durability; stains skin
- Madder root: red-orange
- Boil plant material; strain; concentrate
- Add vinegar or salt as mordant for
  longer life
- Fix on paper with alum solution rinse

## Writing Tools

- Quill: primary feather of goose or turkey
- Cut quill point with sharp knife: nib shape
- Slit nib: allows ink to flow by capillary
- Reed pen: cattail or bamboo section
- Replace quill often; nibs wear quickly
- Practice: consistent angle and pressure

## Storing Ink

- Sealed container: prevents drying
- Away from light: reduces degradation
- Iron gall: add tannin to stabilize
- Carbon ink: stable indefinitely sealed
- Plant inks: refrigerate if possible;
  or make small batches frequently
- Label: type, date, dilution

## Long-Term Value

- Written records require ink
- Iron gall ink in 1000-year-old manuscripts
- Self-sufficiency for all writing needs
- Oak galls available throughout Northern
  Hemisphere oak range
- Teach alongside paper making
