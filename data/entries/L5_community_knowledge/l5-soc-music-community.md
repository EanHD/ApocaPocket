# Music in Community

## Overview

- Music: organized sound as communal
  activity; not entertainment only
- Functions: morale, memory encoding,
  ceremony, coordination, social bonding
- Proven: synchronized group activity
  raises pain threshold and oxytocin
- All humans make music in all cultures
- Zero cost; no instruments required
- Voice is the primary instrument

## Functions of Communal

- Morale: raises spirits measurably in
  controlled trials; not opinion
- Memory: rhythm and melody lock knowledge
- Ceremony: marks births, deaths, seasons,
  victories; ritual creates meaning
- Work coordination: sea shanties,
  field songs, forge rhythms all examples
- Identity: community songs build cohesion
- Grief: communal mourning songs process loss

## Simple Instruments

- Drum: any hollow object; skin stretched
  over frame; played with hand or stick
- Flute: hollow tube with holes; wood,
  bone, bamboo all traditional
- Rainstick: tube with seeds inside
- Scraper: notched stick rubbed with rod
- Musical bow: single string; resonator
- All built from materials at hand
- Teach construction alongside playing

## Work Songs

- Match tempo to work rhythm
- Call-and-response: leader calls; group
  responds; reduces mental fatigue
- Logging, rowing, grinding, planting all
  have historical song traditions
- Compose new songs for new tasks
- Shared call-and-response: unity signal
- Even monotonous work becomes bearable

## Ceremony and Ritual

- Community calendar: songs mark each event
- Seasonal transitions: solstice, equinox
- Planting and harvest: agricultural songs
- Birth: welcome songs for new members
- Death: mourning and remembrance songs
- Assembly opening: gathering song
- Consistent repetition = community memory

## Teaching Music

- All children: singing before instruments
- Simple rhythms first; complexity builds
- No one excluded: all can clap or hum
- Weekly group practice: skill builds fast
- Song library: write down lyrics and notes
- Oral transmission: teach to children
  who will carry it forward
- Community musician role: respected role

## Long-Term Value

- Morale is a material resource
- Communities with active musical life
  have higher cooperation and resilience
- Ancient memory encoding: songs remembered
  when prose is forgotten
- Ceremony structures community time
  and creates shared meaning
