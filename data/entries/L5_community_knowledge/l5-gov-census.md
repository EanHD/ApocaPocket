# Population Census

## Overview

- Census: systematic count and inventory
  of community members and resources
- Purpose: plan food, housing, labor, medicine
- Minimum annually; ideally each season
- Household as unit of record
- Skills inventory: know who can do what
- Age structure: plan for dependency ratios
- Simple forms; not complex; compliance key

## Household Record

- Household location or dwelling number
- Head of household: primary contact
- All members: name, age, sex, status
- Status: healthy, sick, pregnant, infant
- Mobility: can they work and travel?
- Skills: each member's primary skills
- Resources: animals, tools, critical stores

## Skills Inventory

- Each adult: list top 3-5 skills
- Critical skills: medical, metallurgy,
  building, food production, teaching
- Identify gaps: what skills are missing?
- Plan apprenticeships to fill gaps
- Cross-train: no single-person knowledge
- Record where each person learned skill
- Succession: who trains next generation

## Resource Count

- Food stores: type, quantity, estimated
  duration at current consumption
- Seeds: what and how much
- Tools: major tools by household
- Livestock: type and count
- Critical infrastructure: water, shelter
- Compare to population needs
- Identify shortfalls early for planning

## Conducting the Census

- Assign census taker by neighborhood
- Standard form; consistent questions
- Visit every household; no self-reporting
- Verify against previous census
- Reconcile: births, deaths, arrivals,
  departures since last count
- Aggregate results; report to council
- Protect individual records; share totals

## Using Census Data

- Food planning: supply / population =
  months of reserves
- Labor planning: available working adults
  vs. labor demand for key tasks
- Medical: track illness rates by location
- Housing: identify overcrowding
- Defense: available able-bodied adults
- Review annually with council; plan ahead

## Long-Term Value

- Data-driven planning prevents shortages
- Skills gaps identified before crisis
- Census is the foundation of governance
- Historical comparison shows trends
- Ratio of dependents to workers is the
  key survival metric; watch it closely
