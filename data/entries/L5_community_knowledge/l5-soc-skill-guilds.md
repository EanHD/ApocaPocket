# Skill Guilds

## Overview

- Guild: organized group of practitioners
  of a shared craft or skill
- Functions: training, standards, prices,
  mutual support, knowledge preservation
- Medieval guilds: 10th-18th century;
  structured most of European economy
- Modern analog: trade associations,
  professional bodies
- Community guilds: essential after
  institutional infrastructure collapses

## Guild Structure

- Apprentice: learner; 3-7 year training
- Journeyman: qualified; works for others
- Master: fully skilled; may take apprentices
- Guild council: masters who set standards
- Guild seal: marks quality work
- Dues: provide mutual support fund
- Charter: written rights and obligations

## Knowledge Standards

- Define minimum competency for each level
- Practical test: apprentice must demonstrate
- Masterwork: journeyman produces test piece
- Standards written and published
- External test: another master examines
- Prevents knowledge degradation over time
- Documents what "good work" means

## Apprenticeship

- Term: specific duration; 3-7 years
- Master obligations: teach, house, feed
- Apprentice obligations: labor, obey, learn
- Community oversight: prevent abuse
- Early release: with master and council
  agreement; skills tested
- Abandonment: penalty unless master failed
- Record: skills achieved at each stage

## Preventing Guild

- Guilds must not block non-members
  from practicing; advise, not block
- Prices: set maximum, not minimum
- Quality standards: protect consumers
- Membership: open to qualified applicants
- Community council oversight of guild
- Dissolution if guild abuses power
- Knowledge is community property

## Cross-Guild

- Construction project: builder, metal,
  rope, and tool guilds coordinate
- Resource allocation: guilds request
  from common stores
- Conflict resolution: council mediates
- Joint projects: combine expertise
- Knowledge sharing: inter-guild training
  enriches all specializations
- Festival: guilds demonstrate skills publicly

## Long-Term Value

- Guilds prevented skill loss across
  European dark ages repeatedly
- Standards protect community from
  poor-quality critical infrastructure
- Apprenticeship multiplies skills
- Mutual support: injured members cared for
- Economic stability: quality work sustains
  trade relationships
