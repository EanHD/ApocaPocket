# Community Conflict

More communities collapse from infighting than from resource scarcity. Address conflict early.

## Conflict Types

- Resource disputes: Competition over food, water, shelter, tools. Escalates to violence fast.
- Power struggles: Ambiguous authority, leadership transitions. Threatens community stability.
- Value differences: Religion, child-rearing, justice philosophy. Hard to compromise on core values.
- Personality clashes: Communication style and habits. Usually containable but persistent.

## Prevention

- Document rules before conflict starts: resource allocation, decision authority, dispute process, consequences.
- Fair process matters more than fair outcomes. People accept bad results if the process was fair.
- Address at first sign. Path: Discomfort -> Annoyance -> Frustration -> Anger -> Rage -> Violence. Intervene early.
- Weekly community meetings. Anonymous suggestion box. Culture of "no surprises."
- If someone mentions an issue twice, it is real. Do not dismiss it.

## Resolution Ladder

**Level 1: Direct (1-on-1)**
Use for: Minor issues, first occurrence. Cool off at least 2 hours first. Private setting.
Each person speaks uninterrupted 3-5 min. Script: "When you [action], I felt [emotion] because [reason]."
Not: "You always [label]."

**Level 2: Group Discussion**
Use for: Affects multiple people, or Level 1 failed. Facilitator not involved in dispute.
Ground rules: Respect, no interrupting, focus on problem not person. Brainstorm solutions together.

**Level 3: Formal Mediation**
Use for: Escalated conflict, relationship damage, Level 2 failed.
- Opening: Mediator explains process, ground rules, time limit (2-3 hr max).
- Storytelling: Each party speaks uninterrupted 10-15 min. No rebuttals yet.
- Issue identification: List all issues raised. Prioritize easiest first.
- Interest exploration: "Why is that important to you?" Move from positions to underlying needs.
- Solution generation: Brainstorm without judgment. Look for win-win.
- Agreement: Written. Specific. Who does what by when. Follow-up check-in in 1 week.

**Level 4: Arbitration**
Use for: Mediation failed, urgent decision needed. Trusted neutral arbiter makes binding decision. Documented.

**Level 5: Separation**
Use for: Violence occurred, threats made, repeated violations, irreconcilable differences.
Expulsion requires community consensus or supermajority. Document grounds and process.

## Restorative Justice

Focus on repairing harm, not only punishing. Questions asked: Who was harmed? What do they need?
Outcome: Apology, restitution, behavior change commitment, monitoring check-ins.
Works when: Both parties willing, harm is repairable.
Does not work for: Serious violence, sexual assault, repeated violations.

## De-Escalation

- Separate people physically. Minimum 50 ft. Do not corner or crowd.
- Lower your voice. Calm slow speech is contagious.
- Acknowledge emotion. Never say "calm down." Say "I see you're upset."
- Give choices. "You can step outside with me, or we can talk here later."
- Keep exits clear. Stand at 45-degree angle. Hands visible and open.

## Warning Signs

- Cliques forming, silent treatment, passive aggression -> Facilitate within 2 days.
- Gossip spreading, resource hoarding -> Mediate within 24 hours.
- Verbal threats made -> Immediate mediation plus safety plan within 4 hours.
- Physical altercation -> Separate, then arbitration immediately.
- Weapon involved -> Separation or expulsion. No delay.

## Build Relations First

- Conflict prevention is 80% having good relationships before conflict starts.
- Shared meals, collaborative work, celebrations build relational buffer.
- Daily 5-min check-in. Weekly community meeting. Monthly social event. Quarterly retrospective.
- Train 3-5 mediators per 30-person community before you need them. Practice through role-play.

## Key Principles

- Intervene at discomfort, not rage. Early is always easier.
- Focus on interests (needs behind the demand), not positions (the demand itself).
- Document everything. Agreements, decisions, violations, resolutions.
- Cultural sensitivity: Ask what fairness means to each person. Do not assume.
