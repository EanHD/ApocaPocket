# Community Mental Health

## Overview

- Collective trauma: shared catastrophic
  experience affecting community function
- Symptoms: numbness, hypervigilance,
  sleep disruption, social withdrawal,
  aggression, substance use, despair
- Normal response to abnormal events
- Untreated: cooperation collapses;
  community becomes dysfunctional
- Simple, consistent support structures
  prevent worst outcomes

## Recognizing Trauma

- Emotional numbing: flat affect; withdrawal
- Hypervigilance: startling easily; fear
- Intrusive thoughts: flashbacks, nightmares
- Irritability and aggression increase
- Social isolation: withdrawal from group
- Physical symptoms: headaches, gut, fatigue
- Loss of meaning: "why bother" thinking

## Immediate Response

- Psychological first aid: not therapy
- Safety: reassure physical safety; basics
- Calm: reduce stimulation; quiet spaces
- Connect: restore contact with community
- Self-efficacy: give people meaningful tasks
- Hope: provide accurate information;
  realistic assessment of situation
- These five reduce acute trauma impact

## Community Structures

- Daily gathering: scheduled group assembly
- Shared meals: powerful stabilizer
- Meaningful work: assign purposeful tasks
- Rituals: predictable daily and weekly
  events reduce chaos and anxiety
- Ceremony: mark losses formally; grieve
  together; name the dead
- Storytelling circles: normalize experience

## Supporting Caregivers

- Mental health burden falls on helpers:
  medics, leaders, caretakers
- Caregiver burnout: watch for it actively
- Rotate heavy emotional labor roles
- Create no-work recovery time for helpers
- Peer support: helpers need helpers
- Leaders: must model vulnerability
  and seeking support; not stoicism

## Children and Youth

- Children feel adult distress; protect
  by maintaining normalcy not by hiding
- Maintain school or learning routine
- Play time: critical for children under 12
- Clear, age-appropriate truth about
  what happened; not graphic detail
- Reassure: community will protect them
- Children process through play and art

## Long-Term Value

- Mental health maintenance enables all
  other community functions
- Neglected trauma leads to violence,
  addiction, and authority breakdown
- Griefwork and ceremony are not luxuries;
  they are essential maintenance
- Communities that mourn together
  stay together
