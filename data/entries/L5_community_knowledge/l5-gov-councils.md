# Community Councils

## Overview

- Council: representative group making
  decisions on behalf of community
- Smaller than full assembly; more agile
- Full assembly: all adults vote on
  major questions; slower but legitimate
- Two-level: council for routine matters,
  assembly for major decisions
- Define scope clearly or scope creeps
- Ostrom: governance that matches scope
  of commons to decision-makers

## Council Structure

- Size: 5-15 members; odd numbers avoid ties
- Roles: chair, secretary, treasurer minimum
- Chair: facilitates; does not dominate
- Secretary: records all decisions in writing
- Treasurer: manages and reports finances
- Rotate roles: prevent power concentration
- Term limits: maximum 2-3 consecutive terms

## Quorum Rules

- Quorum: minimum present to conduct business
- Typical: majority of members (e.g., 6 of 11)
- No quorum: no binding decisions; reschedule
- Record quorum status at meeting start
- Emergency quorum: lower threshold;
  decisions require ratification later
- Posting meeting notices: required to count

## Voting Procedures

- Simple majority: most routine decisions
- Supermajority (2/3): significant changes
- Consensus: try first; fall back to vote
- Secret ballot: for elections and sensitive
  personnel matters
- Record votes by count; not names (usually)
- Chair votes only to break ties
- No voting by proxy unless pre-authorized

## Meeting Conduct

- Agenda published in advance
- Motions: propose, second, discuss, vote
- Point of order: rule violation called out
- Tabling: delay item to future meeting
- Time limits: prevent monopoly of floor
- Minutes: written record of decisions only
- Distribute minutes within 48 hours

## Accountability

- Open meetings: all community may observe
- Public comment period: structured input
- Annual report: council to full assembly
- Recall process: remove member by vote
- Financial records: open to all members
- Decisions reversible by assembly vote
- No council member above criticism

## Long-Term Value

- Representative governance scales to
  communities of any size
- Prevents both mob rule and autocracy
- Skills transferable to trade and treaty
  negotiations with other communities
- Democratic habits sustain themselves
