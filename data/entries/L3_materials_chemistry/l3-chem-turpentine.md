# Turpentine from Pine

## Overview

- Oleoresin from pine: turpentine plus rosin
- Turpentine = volatile solvent; boils off first
- Rosin = solid amber residue after distillation
- Used as solvent, paint thinner, disinfectant

## Tapping Pine Trees

- Slash shallow V-cut into bark in spring
- Insert collection trough below cut point
- Collect over weeks in clay or bark cup
- Remove insects and bark debris first

## Distillation Method

- Place raw resin in pot with small water
- Heat slowly; turpentine vapors rise first
- Condense vapor through cool clay tube
- Collect clear to pale-yellow liquid

## Rosin Remainder

- Brown-amber solid left in still after distill
- Harder and more brittle than raw resin
- Uses: bow rosin, pitch glue filler, flux
- Preserves and waterproofs when melted

## Uses of Turpentine

- Solvent to thin and apply pine pitch
- Paint thinner for natural pigments
- Disinfectant and antiseptic for wounds
- Lamp fuel: burns brightly and hot

## Safety

- Highly flammable; keep from open flame
- Wear gloves; avoid skin contact repeated
- Work outdoors or in ventilated space
- Store in sealed ceramic or glass container
