# Mordants for Dyeing

## Overview

- Mordant creates chemical bridge: fiber to dye
- Without mordant most colors wash out quickly
- Each mordant shifts final color differently
- Pre-mordant, co-mordant, or post-mordant use

## Alum Mordant

- Potassium aluminum sulfate; most common
- Ratio: 15-20% weight-of-fiber in alum
- Dissolve in hot water; simmer fiber 1 hour
- Brightens yellows and reds; golden tones

## Iron Mordant

- Iron sulfate or rusty water works well
- Saddens and darkens all dye colors
- Use sparingly: 2-4% weight-of-fiber
- Simmer iron scraps in vinegar to make it

## Tannin Mordant

- From oak galls, sumac, or black tea
- Required pre-mordant for cellulose fibers
- Simmer fiber in strong tea 1-2 hours
- Tannin itself dyes fiber tan-brown color

## Oak Gall Source

- Highest tannin content of any mordant
- Crush dry galls; simmer in water 45 min
- Use as pre-mordant before alum bath
- Also used in making iron gall writing ink

## Field Mordant Sources

- Alum: alunite mineral deposits near hot springs
- Iron: rust water, iron ore seeps, old nails
- Tannin: oak bark, sumac leaves, galls, tea
- Copper: blue-green mineral seep deposits
