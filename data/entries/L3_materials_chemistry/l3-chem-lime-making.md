# Lime Making

## Overview

- Limestone (CaCO3) burned above 900C
  produces quicklime (CaO) and CO2 gas
- Seashells and coral are same compound
- Slake quicklime with water for uses

## Source Materials

- Limestone: most common sedimentary rock
- Seashells and oyster shells (coastal)
- Coral: available in coastal tropical areas
- Chalk: soft limestone; works equally well

## Kiln Method

- Build stone or clay kiln with draft holes
- Alternate layers of charcoal and limestone
- Burn 12-24 hours maintaining high heat
- White powdery result is quicklime (CaO)

## Slaking Process

- Put quicklime in waterproof container
- Add water slowly and carefully to lime
- Violent exothermic reaction; stand clear
- Resulting paste = calcium hydroxide (lime)

## Mortar and Plaster

- Mortar: 1 part slaked lime, 3 parts sand
- Trowel onto joints; sets by CO2 absorption
- Lime plaster: apply in thin coats and dry
- Sets slowly over days to weeks

## Other Uses

- Nixtamalization: soak corn to unlock nutrition
- Whitewash walls for pest and rot resistance
- Hide dehairing: lime bath loosens hair
- Soil amendment: neutralize garden acidity
