# Soap Making

## Overview

- Cold process: lye reacts with fat
- No heat needed; cures 4-6 weeks
- Soft soap from potash lye (wood ash)
- Hard soap needs soda lye (salt ash)

## Materials Needed

- Wood-ash lye (potassium hydroxide)
- Animal fat (tallow) or vegetable oil
- Water
- Non-aluminum pot and wooden spoon

## Lye Strength Test

- Egg float test: egg floats halfway = ready
- Feather test: dissolves feather in minutes
- Boil down weak lye to concentrate
- Too strong: dilute with water

## Making the Soap

- Melt fat; cool to near room temperature
- Add lye water slowly to fat, not reverse
- Stir until trace (pudding consistency)
- Pour into clay molds; cover 24 hours

## Curing and Safety

- Soft soap usable in days; hard in 4-6 wk
- pH drops as saponification completes
- Tongue test: no sharp sting = safe to use
- Keep vinegar nearby to neutralize spills

## Safety and Warnings

- Wear eye protection throughout process
- Work outdoors or in ventilated space
- Lye water resembles plain water; label it
- Burns from lye may not be felt immediately
