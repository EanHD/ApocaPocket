# Charcoal Production

## Overview

- Pyrolysis: wood heated with limited oxygen
- Removes water, tar, and volatile gases
- Result: nearly pure carbon fuel material
- Burns hotter and cleaner than raw wood

## Wood Selection

- Hardwood yields denser, better charcoal
- Oak, hickory, maple are best choices
- Avoid resinous softwoods for metal smelting
- Dry wood thoroughly before using

## Mound Method

- Stack dry wood into dome shape
- Cover with earth and sod; leave vent holes
- Light from base; control air via vents
- Burn 6-24 hours; size determines timing

## Retort Method

- Place dry wood in sealed metal drum
- Drill small hole for vapor escape at top
- Build external fire around drum outside
- More consistent yield; faster process

## Process Indicators

- White smoke = moisture leaving the wood
- Blue-grey smoke = volatile gases burning off
- Clear or no smoke = charcoal process complete
- Seal all vents; allow full cool before opening

## Quality Testing

- Good charcoal rings clearly when struck
- No brown wood centers in broken cross-section
- Lightweight relative to its original volume
- Ignites easily; produces no smoke when hot
