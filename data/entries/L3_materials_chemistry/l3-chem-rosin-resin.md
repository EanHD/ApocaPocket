# Rosin and Resin

## Overview

- Resin: liquid sap from pine, spruce, or fir
- Rosin: solid residue after turpentine removed
- Rosin is harder and less sticky than raw resin
- Both used in adhesives, bow rosin, and flux

## Collecting Resin

- Tap wounds on pine, spruce, or fir trees
- V-shaped cut into live sapwood layer
- Collect drips in clay cup below cut
- Harvest in warm months for best flow

## Resin to Rosin

- Melt raw resin over low heat; filter bark
- Continue heating slowly without boiling
- Turpentine fraction evaporates off first
- Dark brown solid remaining = rosin

## Bow Rosin Use

- Apply to bow string to increase friction grip
- Spruce resin preferred for stringed instruments
- Rub cake of rosin along string length firmly
- Improves grip and reduces slipping in wet

## Adhesive Uses

- Base for pine pitch glue; add charcoal, fat
- Alone brittle; always add modifier for use
- Seal cork stoppers and container lid joints
- Adhere bark to wooden handles and shafts

## Other Applications

- Flux for soldering and metal joining
- Preservative component in lacquer finish
- Rubbed on ski or sled runners for glide
- Added to candles to raise melting point
