# Natural Salt Sources

## Overview

- Sodium chloride is essential for survival
- Body requires 1.5-3g daily minimum
- Multiple wild sources exist globally
- Quality varies; some sources have impurities

## Sea and Coast Sources

- Evaporate seawater in shallow clay pans
- Fill pan with seawater; allow solar evaporation
- Evaporation takes 2-7 days in warm sun
- Rake and collect white salt crystals formed

## Inland Mineral Sources

- Salt licks: white mineral outcrops on ground
- Salt springs: brine seeping from ground
- Salt flats: dried lake beds in arid regions
- Animal trails often converge at salt licks

## Plant Ash Salt

- Burn salt-marsh grasses or dried seaweed
- Leach ash with water; evaporate the liquid
- Contains mixed sodium and potassium salts
- Lower sodium content than sea salt

## Evaporation Technique

- Clay or stone pan holds solution best
- Boil to speed concentration if fuel available
- Stop evaporation when crystals start forming
- Collect white crystals; discard dark residue

## Quality Assessment

- White crystals = likely pure salt
- Yellow or brown = impurities present; filter
- Bitter taste = magnesium salts mixed in
- All field salt should be filtered or boiled
