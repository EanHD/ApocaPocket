# Natural Dyes

## Overview

- Most dyes require mordant to fix color
- Protein fibers (wool, silk) take dye easiest
- Plant fibers need stronger mordant pre-treatment
- Simmer dye material; strain; add wetted fiber

## Color Sources by Hue

- Yellow: goldenrod, onion skins, birch leaf
- Red/pink: madder root, pokeweed, sumac
- Blue: woad leaf, elderberry, indigo plant
- Brown/tan: walnut hull, oak bark, strong tea
- Black: iron mordant combined with tannin dye

## Dye Bath Preparation

- Simmer plant material 1 hour in water
- Strain out all plant material thoroughly
- Keep bath at 180F (82C); add wetted fiber
- Simmer fiber 30-60 min; stir occasionally

## Mordant Effects

- Alum: brightens; most common mordant
- Iron: darkens and saddens all colors
- Tannin: required pre-mordant for plant fibers
- Copper: shifts yellows and browns to green

## Fiber Preparation

- Scour (wash) fiber before mordanting
- Mordant in alum solution first; rinse well
- Wet fiber fully before entering dye bath
- Cool slowly in bath; do not wring or twist

## Lightfastness Notes

- Walnut and madder most resistant to fading
- Berry dyes fade quickly in direct sunlight
- Layering colors builds depth and longevity
- Store dyed goods away from direct sun
