# Alum Tanning

## Overview

- Alum = potassium aluminum sulfate
- Fastest tanning method; days not weeks
- Produces white, soft, pliable leather
- Found naturally near volcanic areas

## Materials Needed

- Scraped, de-haired hide
- Alum (mineral or trade source)
- Salt (prevents putrefaction during tanning)
- Water, container

## Alum Sources

- Alunite mineral deposits (white rock)
- Volcanic fumaroles deposit alum crystals
- Pickling salt supplies as trade item
- Ratio: 1 lb alum + 0.5 lb salt per gallon

## Process

- Dissolve alum and salt in warm water
- Submerge hide; stir daily
- Thin skins: 3-5 days; thick: 1-2 weeks
- Work hide while drying for softness

## Finishing

- Rinse hide in clean water after tanning
- Oil with tallow or egg yolk while still damp
- Stretch and work while drying
- Result: light-colored, flexible leather

## Limitations and Uses

- Not waterproof; avoid prolonged wetting
- Good for garments, pouches, bookbinding
- Apply beeswax layer to improve water resist.
- Can re-tan with bark after alum for hybrid
