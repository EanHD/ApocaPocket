# Salt for Preservation

## Overview

- Salt osmosis draws water from food cells
- Reduces water activity; bacteria cannot grow
- Does not sterilize; correct ratio is critical
- Combined with smoke greatly extends shelf life

## Minimum Concentrations

- 5% brine: slows spoilage at cool temperatures
- 10% brine: fermentation-safe at room temp
- 20% brine: prevents most bacterial growth
- Dry cure: minimum 3% salt by weight of meat

## Dry Curing Meat

- Rub 3-5% of meat weight in salt into all surfaces
- Layer meat in container with additional salt
- Allow 1 day per 0.5cm of meat thickness
- Rinse and dry before eating or smoking

## Brine Curing

- Dissolve salt in water until egg floats (20%)
- Submerge meat; weight to keep submerged
- Thin cuts: 1-3 days; thick roasts: 1-2 weeks
- Rinse well in fresh water before cooking

## Vegetable Fermentation

- Use 2-3% salt by weight of vegetables
- Pack tightly; submerge in own brine
- Allow lacto-fermentation at room temperature
- Ready in 3-14 days; taste for sourness

## Extending Preservation

- Smoke after curing adds 2-3x shelf life
- Hang cured meat in cool dry ventilated space
- Wrap in cloth or pack in dry salt for storage
- Check weekly; discard if green mold appears
