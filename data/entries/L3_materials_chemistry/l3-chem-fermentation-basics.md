# Fermentation Basics

## Overview

- Microbes convert sugars to useful products
- Yeast: sugar to alcohol and CO2 (anaerobic)
- Bacteria: sugar or alcohol to acids
- Mold: breaks down starches to simple sugars

## Key Conditions

- Temperature: 60-90F (15-32C) for most types
- Food source: sugars, starches, or proteins
- Moisture: at least 60% water content
- pH: starts neutral; cultures self-acidify

## Alcoholic Fermentation

- Wild yeast from fruit skins or ambient air
- Converts sugars to ethanol and CO2
- Visible bubbling indicates active ferment
- Stops when alcohol reaches about 15%

## Lacto-Fermentation

- Lactic acid bacteria preserve vegetables
- Salt (2-3%) selects for beneficial strains
- Produces vitamin-rich preserved food
- Sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles use this method

## Acetic Fermentation

- Bacteria convert ethanol to acetic acid
- Requires oxygen; keep container cloth-covered
- Produces vinegar from any alcoholic wash
- Mother culture contains live acetic bacteria

## Healthy vs Bad Signs

- Healthy: sour/tangy smell; mild bubbling
- Healthy: submerged with brine covering
- Bad: black or pink mold; foul rotten smell
- Bad: off-color or slimy texture; discard
