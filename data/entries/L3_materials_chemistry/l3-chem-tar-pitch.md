# Wood Tar and Pitch

## Overview

- Tar: dark liquid from slow pyrolysis of wood
- Pitch: tar concentrated by boiling to semi-solid
- Pine and birch yield most tar per unit of wood
- Used for waterproofing, preservation, adhesive

## Drum Production Method

- Pack green pine wood into clay-sealed drum
- Small hole drilled at base for tar to drip
- Build external fire around drum exterior
- Tar drips from hole; collect in vessel below

## Birch Bark Method

- Roll dry birch bark into tight cylinder
- Seal upper end with clay; open end faces down
- Heat the sealed upper end over fire
- Tar drips from open lower end; collect below

## Concentrating to Pitch

- Heat collected tar in open vessel over fire
- Boil off water and lighter volatile fractions
- Stir constantly; mixture thickens as it reduces
- Pour into molds while hot; hard when cooled

## Uses of Tar

- Coat wooden fence posts to resist rot
- Caulk boat and canoe seams watertight
- Waterproof leather boots and bag seams
- Antiseptic treatment for animal hoof rot

## Uses of Pitch

- Component in pine pitch adhesive formula
- Caulk canoe seams (hard, waterproof seal)
- Wrap over sinew handle grips for grip
- Flux for metal smelting and soldering
