# Natural Rubber

## Overview

- Latex: milky sap found in rubber trees
- Primary source: Hevea brasiliensis (tropical)
- Temperate alternatives: dandelion, guayule
- Raw latex coagulated to form solid rubber

## Latex Sources

- Rubber tree: tropical Americas and SE Asia
- Russian dandelion (Taraxacum kok-saghyz)
- Guayule shrub: arid southwestern regions
- Wild chicory: minor but viable latex source

## Tapping Process

- Diagonal cut in bark of rubber tree
- Cup collects dripping white latex below cut
- Harvest in morning; flow is highest then
- One tree yields 0.5-2L per day of tapping

## Coagulation Methods

- Smoke coagulation: drip latex over fire slowly
- Acid coagulation: add lemon juice or vinegar
- Form into ball or flat sheet as it solidifies
- Smoke-coagulated rubber is most durable

## Basic Vulcanization

- Mix rubber with sulfur powder (5-8% weight)
- Heat mixture to 120-150C for 15-20 minutes
- Improves strength, elasticity, temperature range
- Prevents tackiness and heat breakdown

## Field Uses

- Waterproof cloth by coating with liquid latex
- Adhesive for sealing container seams
- Elastic bindings and improvised gaskets
- Collect water from tapping wound directly
