# Bone Tool Making

## Overview

- Long bones of deer, elk, and cattle are best
- Dense compact bone for tools; spongy = useless
- Splitting, grinding, and polishing are methods
- Bone takes fine edge; holds shape when dry

## Best Bone Sources

- Leg bones (femur, tibia) of large mammals
- Ribs: flat; good for scrapers and spatulas
- Shoulder blade: flat; broad scraping tools
- Small bones: awls, needles, fish hooks

## Splitting Long Bones

- Score line down bone face with flint
- Insert wedge; tap with hammerstone
- Split produces flat splinter for shaping
- Select splinters with even compact structure

## Making an Awl

- Select thin bone splinter or rib section
- Grind point on sandstone; circular stroke
- Polish to smooth on finer stone
- Handle: wrap thick end with sinew or bark

## Making a Needle

- Very thin splinter from bird or small mammal bone
- Eye: drill with fine stone point (time-intensive)
- Grind fine point on smooth sandstone
- Polish entire surface to remove rough spots

## Making a Scraper

- Flat rib section or shoulder blade piece
- Grind one edge to slight bevel angle
- Polish working face smooth
- For hide working: edge should be dull not sharp
