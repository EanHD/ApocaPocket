# Wooden Peg Joinery

## Overview

- Pegs lock joints without metal hardware
- Green peg in dry wood: peg swells tight
- Dry peg in green wood: shrinks = loose
- Wedged tenon: self-tightening under load

## Making Wooden Pegs

- Split hardwood (oak, locust) into blanks
- Whittle round with sharp knife
- Taper one end slightly for driving
- Green hardwood pegs work best; dry before use

## Auger and Mortise

- Burn or bore round hole with fire-hardened stick
- Improvised auger: sharp flint tip, twist-drill
- Mortise: rectangular hole for tenon
- Use chisel and mallet; clean corners with knife

## Dowel Joint

- Align holes in both pieces exactly
- Slightly oversized peg driven in with mallet
- Dry peg in dry wood: glue with hide glue too
- Green peg in dry hole: no glue needed

## Wedged Tenon

- Tenon (peg) fits through mortise hole
- Drive hardwood wedge into slot in tenon end
- Wedge forces tenon to expand inside mortise
- Load tightens joint; cannot pull back out

## Practical Applications

- Timber frame: corner and ridge beam joints
- Furniture legs: table and stool construction
- Tool handles: secure head to handle shaft
- Door and gate frame: hinge post assembly
