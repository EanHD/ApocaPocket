# General Fabrication

Store-bought tools break. Fabrication skill = infinite tools from available materials.

## Fabrication Process

1. Define the problem precisely. Not "need a tool" but "pry boards apart without breaking."
2. Sketch: side, top, and front views. Note materials and dimensions.
3. Prototype: build a rough version from scrap first.
4. Test: functional, load, fatigue, and environmental.
5. Iterate: strengthen weak points, reduce weight, improve ergonomics.
6. Document: record materials, dimensions, and process in writing.

## Materials

Wood:
- Hardwood (oak, ash): tool handles, striking tools.
- Softwood (pine): construction, fire.
- Green wood: flexible for bending. Shrinks as it dries.
- Weaknesses: rots in moisture, splits along grain. Pre-drill near edges.

Metal:
- Steel: strong, rust-prone. Sharpenable to a cutting edge.
- Aluminum: light, soft, no rust. Good for containers and frames.
- Cold working: hacksaw and file. Hot working: hammer and anvil.
- Rust prevention: oil or paint immediately after shaping.

Cordage:
- Strong in tension, weak in compression. Knots reduce strength 50-70%.
- Natural (hemp, jute): rots in moisture. Synthetic (nylon): UV degrades.

Plastic:
- Heat to mold with heat gun or hot water. Cut with saw or knife.
- UV degrades over time. Paint or keep shaded for long-term use.

## Tool Selection

Wood: saw (cross-grain), knife or chisel (with-grain), axe (rough shaping).
Metal: hacksaw, file, hammer, anvil or solid steel backing.
Plastic: heat and mold, saw, sharp knife.

Substitutions:
- No saw: score deeply with knife, snap along line. Or abrasion-cut with wire.
- No drill: heat a metal rod and burn through wood. Or rotate sharp point bow-drill style.
- No clamp: cordage plus stick tourniquet method. Or weight while glue cures.

## Joining Methods

Nails: quick, but weak in tension. Pre-drill near edges to prevent splitting.
Screws: stronger, removable. Pre-drill pilot hole at 2/3 of screw diameter.
Bolts: highest load, allows disassembly. Washer under nut. Lock nut for vibration.
Lashing: no fasteners needed. Loosens over time, re-tighten regularly.
- Start and end with clove hitch. 6-8 wrapping turns. Frapping turns tighten.
Adhesive: permanent. Clean both surfaces. Thin even coat. Clamp during full cure.
Joinery (wood): mortise-and-tenon or lap joint. Strong, tool-intensive.

## Measurement

3-4-5 triangle = perfect 90 degree corner (3 units, 4 units, 5 diagonal).
Improvised level: water in clear tube (surface = horizontal). Plumb bob = vertical.
Mark waste side with X before cutting. Measure twice, cut once.

## Finishing

Wood: sand coarse to fine (80-120-220 grit). Seal with oil, wax, or pitch resin.
Metal: file all edges smooth (deburr). Light oil coat prevents rust.
Plastic: sand smooth. Flame-polish edges carefully (works on PET and acrylic).

## Testing

- Functional: does it accomplish the task?
- Load: apply stress progressively up to expected rating.
- Fatigue: does it hold up after 50-100 repetitions?
- Environmental: how does rain, heat, and cold affect it?

If it fails: identify whether material, design, or execution caused it. Fix that element.

## Quick Reference

Sequence: Define - Sketch - Prototype - Test - Iterate - Document.
Join by strength: Bolt > Screw > Nail > Lashing.
Seal wood: penetrating oil or wax, or surface varnish.
Function beats form. Prototype first, perfect later.
