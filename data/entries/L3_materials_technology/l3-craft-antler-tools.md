# Antler Tool Making

## Overview

- Antler is dense calcified bone; very tough
- Tines are naturally shaped for many uses
- Softer than stone; compresses rather than chips
- Ideal for pressure flaking (transmits force)

## Antler Sources

- Shed antler: collected in spring (no harvesting)
- Fresh from harvested deer or elk carcass
- Dry fresh antler 2-4 weeks before use
- Base of tine most dense; use for pressure tools

## Pressure Flaker

- Cut tine section 10-15cm long
- Point end on sandstone to smooth tip
- Hold with leather pad; apply controlled force
- Remove small flakes from stone edge precisely

## Tool Handle

- Antler beam with socket: fits stone tool insert
- Drill socket with stone point or fire hardening
- Wedge stone tool into socket; secure with sinew
- Pine pitch in socket before inserting tool

## Punch and Chisel

- Short blunt tine section for punching holes
- Strike with hammerstone through leather work
- Punch holes in hide for lacing
- Works for drilling holes in bone and shell

## Digging Tool

- Tine base or main beam section
- Sharp tine angle naturally suited for digging
- Loosen compact soil and roots
- Hardened tip lasts through root-cutting use
