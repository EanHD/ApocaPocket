# Sinew Cordage

## Overview

- Sinew: long back tendon of large mammals
- Strongest natural cordage material available
- Stretches when wet; returns when dried
- Used for bowstrings, lashing, and sewing

## Harvesting Sinew

- Back straps (two long tendons along spine)
- Leg tendons (thick but shorter length)
- Harvest while fresh; peel from muscle
- Dry completely before splitting and using

## Preparation

- Allow sinew to dry fully (2-3 days minimum)
- Pound dried sinew lightly to loosen fibers
- Peel and split into thin consistent strands
- Store dry; moisture reverts to sticky gelatin

## Twisting Method

- Moisten slightly with saliva before twisting
- Use same reverse-wrap as plant cordage
- Short strands splice by overlap and twist
- Keep strands thin and even for consistency

## Bowstring Use

- Twist multiple strands together for strength
- Twist opposite to strands for self-tightening
- Coat with beeswax after completion
- Test at full draw weight before final use

## Sewing Use

- Single split strand as thread for leather
- Moisten tip; push through awl hole
- Self-bonding: sinew fibers bind when dried
- Stronger than plant thread for leather sewing
