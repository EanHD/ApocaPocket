# Bark Containers

## Overview

- Bark offers natural waterproofing from tree oils
- Birch bark is finest; peels in large sheets
- Cedar and elm provide wider panels
- Simple folded forms to elaborate sewn containers

## Best Bark Species

- Paper birch: finest; peels in large flexible sheets
- Western red cedar: larger panels; easy to fold
- Elm: large tough bark; less flexible than birch
- Pine and spruce: small; good for cups and bowls

## Harvesting Bark

- Spring harvest best; bark slips from wood
- Score outline of sheet; pry gently with stick
- Work slowly to avoid tearing
- Keep damp in storage to prevent cracking

## Simple Folded Cup

- Cut rectangle of bark; score fold lines
- Fold corners up 90 degrees
- Pin corners with green twig or pine splint
- Ready immediately for cold-water use

## Sewn Container

- Cut bark panels to desired shape
- Pierce holes 1cm from edge with awl
- Sew panels together with spruce root lacing
- Seal seams with pine pitch inside and out

## Waterproofing and Use

- Seal inside seams with melted pine pitch
- Good for carrying water; do not hold over fire
- Can boil water: place hot stones inside
- Store folded flat; reshape with water
