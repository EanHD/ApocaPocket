# Small Engine Repair

## Carburetor Cleaning

Signs carb is the problem: starts with starting fluid then dies, runs only with choke, hunts and surges.

1. Drain fuel. Remove air filter and carburetor (usually 2-3 bolts).
2. Disassemble: bowl, main jet, emulsion tube, float, needle.
3. Spray all passages with carburetor cleaner. Use wire only on metal jets, not plastic.
4. Confirm all passages clear. Light should shine through main jet bore.
5. Replace gaskets and bowl O-ring. Reassemble.
6. Set mixture screw: turn in gently until seated, back out 1.5 turns. Adjust for smooth idle.

Float height: set to manufacturer spec. Wrong float height causes flooding or lean condition.
Main jet: fully clear bore is critical. Partial clog causes poor performance under load.

## Fuel Line Check

Cracked, soft, or hardened lines cause vapor lock, lean condition, or no-start.
Replace with correct ID fuel line (typically 1/4 inch ID for most small engines).
Ethanol degrades rubber. Use ethanol-resistant line on engines running E10 or E15.
Check primer bulb for cracks. Replace if it does not spring back fully when released.

## Spark Plug Gap

Standard gap: 0.028-0.030 inch for most 4-stroke small engines.
2-stroke engines: 0.020-0.025 inch.
Use feeler gauge. Bend ground electrode only to adjust.
Replace if: electrode worn, insulator cracked, heavy carbon buildup present.
Plug color: tan = good. Black = rich or fouled. White = lean or overheating.

## Recoil Starter Fix

Rope frayed or broken: disassemble housing, note spring tension direction. Feed new rope of same diameter and length. Tie figure-8 knot in handle.
Spring will not retract: remove cover, rewind spring clockwise on most engines. Apply 3-5 pre-wind turns to restore tension.
Pawls do not engage: inspect plastic pawls for wear. Replacement kit costs about five dollars.
Starter turns but does not engage engine: inspect ratchet and flywheel dogs. Replace worn parts.

## Common Failures

Runs 30 seconds then dies: float sticking or fuel tank vent clogged. Loosen fuel cap while running to confirm vent issue.
Starts cold, will not restart hot: vapor lock or ignition coil failing when hot. Let cool 15 minutes and retry.
Blue smoke: oil in combustion chamber. Worn rings or valve seals.
Black smoke: rich mixture. Dirty air filter, stuck choke, or carburetor flooding.
No spark: kill switch wire shorted, coil gap too wide (correct: 0.008-0.012 inch), or sheared flywheel key.
Hard to pull: hydrolocked cylinder (oil in bore) or seized piston. Remove spark plug and pull to clear.
