# Hide Scraping

## Overview

- Fleshing: remove muscle and fat from inside
- De-hairing: remove hair and outer grain layer
- Grain removal: required for soft tanned leather
- Smooth even hide is goal; no thin spots

## Tools Needed

- Fleshing tool: dull-edged bone or steel blade
- Scraping beam: log 20cm diameter; chest height
- Dehairing tool: blunt bone or flint scraper
- Frame for stretching fresh hide (optional)

## Fleshing Step

- Drape hide over beam, flesh side up
- Push fleshing tool at 45 degrees away
- Remove all meat and fat in long strokes
- Do not cut into hide; use blunt push only

## De-hairing Step

- Soak hide in lime water 2-4 days optional
- Or begin dry scraping with blunt bone tool
- Push hair and epidermis toward edge
- Work systematically; check for missed patches

## Grain Removal

- Work flesh side; remove membrane layer
- Consistent even strokes toward one end
- Feel for lumpy uneven spots; scrape level
- Thin spots show as translucent when held up

## Final Check

- Even translucency across entire hide
- Uniform thickness; no ridge or valley
- Soft and pliable when wet; stiff when dry
- Ready for brain tan, bark tan, or rawhide use
