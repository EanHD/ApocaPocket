# Bark Containers & Baskets

**Build time:** 30 min–8 hours. Waterproof, lightweight, durable.

## Best Barks

### Birch (Best)
- Species: paper birch, white birch, yellow birch
- Naturally 100% waterproof (resin layers), flexible, peels in large sheets
- Harvest in spring — sap running means bark peels easily
- Found: Northern US, Canada, Northern Europe, Russia

### Cedar
- Species: western red cedar, eastern red cedar
- Rot-resistant, aromatic, fibrous — good for weaving and cordage
- Harvest year-round, remove outer bark and use inner
- Found: Pacific Northwest, Eastern US

### Avoid
- Oak: too brittle when dry
- Pine: resinous, difficult to work
- Maple: not waterproof
- Willow: too thin, tears easily

---

## Birch Bark Harvesting

### Timing
- Spring (late April–June): bark peels easily
- Summer: possible but harder
- Fall/Winter: nearly impossible

### Sustainable Rules
- Never girdle the tree — harvest from one side only
- Harvest above shoulder height
- Take 2-4 square feet maximum per tree
- Tree heals in 2-5 years

### Method
1. Score a vertical line 4-6 feet tall with two horizontal cuts at top and bottom
2. Insert wooden wedge between bark and wood
3. Peel carefully around perimeter — bark releases in one sheet
4. Roll immediately inner-side out to prevent curling
5. Store rolled in cool, damp place — use within weeks

## Folded Bark Container

**Build time:** 15-30 minutes. Waterproof with pitch seal.

1. Cut 12" x 12" square of smooth bark
2. Mark 3-4" squares at each corner
3. Fold sides vertical to create box shape
4. Pinch corners, fold flaps inward with overlap
5. Stitch with spruce root or peg with wooden pins
6. Seal seams with warm pine pitch
- Weight: 2-4 oz. Knife is the only tool required.

---

## Sewn Bark Container

**Build time:** 1-2 hours. Best for long-term use.

**Materials needed:**
- Large bark sheet (2-4 sq ft), smooth, no cracks
- Spruce root or sinew for stitching
- Pine pitch + charcoal for sealing
- Willow hoop for rim reinforcement

**Steps:**
1. Lay out pattern: rectangular base with four sides
2. Score fold lines on outer surface only
3. Fold sides up along scored lines
4. Pre-drill holes with awl (1/8" holes, 1/2" from edge, 1" spacing)
5. Whip stitch or cross-stitch seams with spruce root
6. Heat pine pitch with charcoal (1:1 ratio), apply warm to all seams
7. Lash willow hoop around top edge for reinforcement
8. Allow 24 hours to cure

---

## Coiled Basket (Brief)

**Build time:** 4-8 hours. Not waterproof — use for dry goods.

- Materials: cattail leaves, cedar bark strips, or sweetgrass
- Stitch each coil to previous row with split roots or matching material
- Start with a tight center spiral, build upward
- Angle coils outward for a bowl or keep vertical for a cylinder
- Bind final row tightly and tuck end under for rim

---

## Sustainability

- One tree yields multiple containers — do not over-harvest
- Harvest from healthy trees only
- Do not return to the same tree for 5+ years
- Check local regulations before harvesting on public land

---

## Advantages

**Vs. pottery:** 1/10th the weight, doesn't shatter, builds in 30 min vs 5+ hours.
**Vs. leather:** Birch bark naturally waterproof, holds shape without a frame.
**Vs. woven baskets:** 30 min folded bark vs 6+ hours woven; birch is waterproof.
