# Stone Knapping

## Overview

- Conchoidal fracture propagates from impact
- Platform angle determines flake trajectory
- Direct percussion: hammer removes large flakes
- Pressure flaking: refines edge and shape

## Materials Needed

- Flint, chert, obsidian, or quality quartzite
- Hammerstone: smooth rounded river cobble
- Pressure flaker: antler tine or copper nail
- Leather lap pad; leather for hand protection

## Basic Percussion

- Strike platform at 75-80 degree angle to face
- Light tap first; flake pops off predictably
- Isolate platform; clear overhang before strike
- Remove one flake at a time; plan sequence

## Preparing a Platform

- Abrade platform with sandstone before striking
- Platform must be flat or slightly convex
- Remove small step fractures that block flakes
- Re-abrade between each percussion strike

## Pressure Flaking

- Use leather-wrapped copper or antler tine
- Apply steady pressure inward at 45 degrees
- Small flakes remove in controlled sequence
- Work alternate sides to maintain symmetry

## Common Problems

- Step fracture: platform angle too steep
- Hinge fracture: not enough force or bad platform
- Overshot (ran off edge): too much force used
- Solution: re-examine platform; abrade more
