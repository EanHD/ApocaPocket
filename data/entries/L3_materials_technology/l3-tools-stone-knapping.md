# Stone Knapping

**Build time:** 30 min - 2 hours. Edges sharper than steel.

## Conchoidal Fracture

- Certain stones break in predictable curved patterns, like broken glass
- Strike at angle: fracture propagates through the material
- Each strike removes a thin flake and leaves a sharp edge
- Achievable edge angle: <30 deg (sharper than most steel at 40-50 deg)
- Works on: flint, obsidian, chert, quartzite, jasper, agate

---

## Stone Materials

### Best Stones
- Obsidian: volcanic glass, sharpest edge (3 nanometers), easiest to flake  -  volcanic regions (Western US, Iceland, Mexico)
- Flint: microcrystalline quartz, very sharp, durable, strikes sparks  -  chalk/limestone areas (England, Texas, Midwest US)
- Chert: similar to flint, common in riverbeds and limestone outcrops globally
- Quartzite: metamorphosed sandstone, tough, harder to flake  -  riverbeds and mountains

### Test Stone Quality
1. Break test: strike two stones together  -  good stone shows conchoidal fracture
2. Sound test: tap with hard object  -  high-pitched ring = good, dull thud = poor
3. Visual test: consistent color, no cracks, inclusions, or layers

### Avoid
- Granite: crystalline, does not flake
- Limestone: too soft, crumbles
- Sandstone: grainy, no sharp edge
- Shale: layered, splits weakly

## Percussion Flaking

**Use:** Removing large flakes, rough shaping. **Tool:** Hammerstone (hard river cobble, 1-2 lbs).

1. Grip core securely on a leather pad
2. Identify platform: flat edge surface where you will strike
3. Strike at 70-80 deg from platform surface  -  not perpendicular
4. Strike location: 1/2" from edge (closer = larger flake)
5. Use a sharp, quick blow  -  not a hard smash

---

## Pressure Flaking

**Use:** Sharpening edges, fine detailing, notching. **Tool:** Antler tine, copper rod, or hardwood point.

1. Hold core against leather pad in lap
2. Place tool point 2-3mm from edge margin
3. Angle tool 45-60 deg from edge surface
4. Push down and forward slowly  -  use chest or shoulder as brace
5. Small precise flakes detach to refine edge

---

## Tool Types

- Knife: large flake (3-6"), pressure flake one or both edges, wrap base for handle  -  30-60 min
- Scraper: thick flake, steep edge angle (50-70 deg), for hide scraping and wood smoothing  -  15-30 min
- Arrowhead/spearpoint: thin flake (2-4"), pressure flake to point, notch base  -  1-2 hours, requires skill
- Drill/awl: small flake, pressure flake to thin pointed tip, for holes in leather/wood/bone  -  20-40 min
- Axe/adze: large core (fist-sized), percussion flake cutting edge, lash to handle  -  2-4 hours

---

## Safety Gear

- Eye protection: safety glasses or goggles  -  chips fly 10-20 feet and can blind
- Hand protection: thick leather gloves (welding gloves)  -  edges cut through thin gloves
- Respiratory: work outdoors or wear N95 mask  -  silica dust causes silicosis (irreversible)
- Work surface: thick leather pad or folded towel  -  absorbs impact, protects lap
