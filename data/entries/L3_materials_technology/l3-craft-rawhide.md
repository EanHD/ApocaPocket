# Rawhide

## Overview

- Hide scraped clean; not tanned; allowed to dry
- Dries nearly as hard as horn or thin bone
- Wet rawhide shrinks; used to secure joints
- Versatile repair material in camp and field

## Preparing Rawhide

- Flesh (remove meat) and de-hair the hide
- Stretch hide on frame while damp
- Scrape both sides to even thickness
- Dry slowly; avoid direct sun (cracks)

## Cutting When Wet

- Cut into strips while still wet and pliable
- Wet rawhide cuts with any sharp blade
- Cut in long spiral from round piece
- Store wet in water or dry and re-soak

## Lashing Applications

- Wrap wet rawhide tightly around joint
- Shrinks as it dries; locks joint very firmly
- Use for tool hafting, frame building
- Apply under tension so shrink = tighter

## Field Repair Uses

- Patch broken wooden handles (wrap and dry)
- Re-sole worn moccasins; stitch while wet
- Bind broken bow limbs as emergency fix
- Secure stone tools to handles

## Soaking and Re-use

- Soak dry rawhide in warm water 30-60 min
- Becomes pliable; can be shaped and cut
- Re-dries hard in new shape
- Can be reused multiple times
