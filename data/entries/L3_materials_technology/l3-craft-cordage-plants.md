# Cordage from Plants

## Overview

- Bast fibers in plant stems are strongest source
- Leaf fibers (yucca, iris) also useful
- Inner bark of many trees provides cordage
- Preparation differs; all twisted the same way

## Top Cordage Plants

- Stinging nettle: finest, strongest bast fiber
- Dogbane (Indian hemp): very strong; common
- Milkweed: medium strength; widely available
- Yucca leaf: excellent in arid and coastal areas

## Harvesting Stalks

- Harvest mature dry stalks in fall for best fiber
- Break stalk; peel outer skin lengthwise
- Separate inner woody core from bark fibers
- Comb fibers to align and remove short bits

## Preparing Leaf Fibers

- Cut green yucca leaf; remove saw-toothed edge
- Scrape fleshy pulp from both faces
- Rinse remaining white fiber bundles
- Dry before twisting for use

## Twisting Technique

- Hold fiber bundle; bend at midpoint
- Twist top half clockwise with fingers
- Wrap twisted half counter-clockwise around lower
- Add new fiber at splice; continue length

## Strength Ranking

- Dogbane and nettle: strongest plant cordage
- Milkweed: good medium strength
- Cattail and grass: low strength; emergency
- Rawhide: strongest natural cordage if available
