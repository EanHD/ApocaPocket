# Natural Fiber Cordage

## Overview

- Cordage strength depends on fiber and twist
- Proper twist dramatically increases strength
- All natural fibers weaken when wet
- Sinew and rawhide are strongest overall

## Strength Ranking

- Rawhide: strongest; 100% reference point
- Sinew: 85-90%; stretches when wet
- Dogbane: 70%; excellent plant cordage
- Stinging nettle: 65%; widely available
- Hemp and milkweed: 55-60%
- Cattail and grass: 20-30%; emergency only

## Fiber Properties

- Bast fiber (stem): strongest plant cordage
- Leaf fiber (yucca, iris): medium strength
- Inner bark: moderate; widely available
- Animal fiber: strongest; sinew and rawhide

## Factors Affecting

- Preparation: remove short fibers first
- Twist angle: 30-45 degrees is optimal
- Moisture: dry cordage much stronger
- Splicing: taper splices reduce strength loss

## Testing Procedure

- Always test new cordage batch before relying
- Hang weight from 1m length; test to failure
- Record result; match to expected load
- Safety factor: use 3x minimum for body weight

## Storage and Care

- Dry and store away from UV and moisture
- Inspect for rot and wear before critical use
- Re-twist loosening sections to restore strength
- Replace any section showing fraying or rot
