# Dehydration Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Dehydration begins before thirst
- Mild dehydration impairs judgment
- Severe dehydration is life-threatening
- Field assessment is reliable and fast
- Treatment priority depends on severity
- All other conditions worsen with dehydration

## Mild Dehydration

- Thirst: present and notable
- Urine: yellow, reduced frequency
- Mouth: slightly dry
- Skin: normal turgor
- Mental: slightly fatigued
- Treatment: oral fluids and rest

## Moderate Dehydration

- Strong thirst, headache, dizziness
- Urine: dark yellow, infrequent
- Mouth: dry, sticky
- Skin turgor: slow rebound (2-3 sec)
- Heart rate elevated 10-20 bpm
- Treatment: aggressive oral rehydration

## Severe Dehydration

- Urine: dark amber or none
- Mouth: very dry, no saliva
- Skin turgor: tents over 3 seconds
- Confusion, weakness, rapid pulse
- Sunken eyes, sunken fontanelle (infant)
- Treatment: IV fluids if available

## Skin Turgor Test

- Pinch skin on back of hand or forearm
- Release and observe return time
- Under 2 seconds: normal
- 2-3 seconds: moderate dehydration
- Over 3 seconds: severe dehydration
- Less reliable in elderly and obese

## Urine Color Guide

- Clear: well hydrated (may be over)
- Pale yellow: good hydration
- Dark yellow: drink more fluids
- Amber: significantly dehydrated
- Brown or no urine: emergency level

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Stage dehydration in 3
  simulated patients correctly
- Sign of mastery: Skin turgor test
  and urine color assessed in 30 sec
