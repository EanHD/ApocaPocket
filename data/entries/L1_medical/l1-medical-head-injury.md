# Head Injury Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Head injuries can appear minor then worsen
- Epidural bleed is a lucid interval emergency
- Second injury on same day can be fatal
- Monitoring is more important than treatment
- Field treatment is mainly observation
- Evacuate decision must be made early

## Concussion Signs

- Any loss of consciousness
- Confusion after impact
- Amnesia about the event
- Headache after impact
- Nausea or vomiting (once)
- Ringing in ears, blurred vision

## Signs: Evacuate Now

- Loss of consciousness over 1 minute
- Vomiting repeatedly after injury
- Worsening headache over 2+ hours
- Slurred speech developing
- One pupil larger than other
- Deteriorating mental status AVPU
- Seizure following injury

## Monitoring Protocol

- Establish AVPU at time of injury
- Recheck every 15 min for 2 hours
- Then every 30 min for next 6 hours
- Wake patient hourly overnight
- Document each check with time
- Any step back in AVPU = evacuate

## Lucid Interval Warning

- Patient may be alert after injury
- Then worsen dramatically hours later
- This is epidural bleed pattern
- Do not be reassured by early recovery
- 24-hour monitoring period minimum
- Do not leave patient alone first 24 hrs

## Activity Restriction

- No strenuous activity after concussion
- Complete rest day 1-2
- Cognitive rest also: no devices
- Return to activity only if symptom free
- No second impact: remove from field
- This is non-negotiable protocol

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Complete head injury
  monitoring protocol for 4 hours
- Sign of mastery: Identify lucid
  interval in case description
