# Heart Attack Response

Heart attacks kill 600,000 Americans yearly — the number one cause of death. Every minute of delayed treatment is more heart tissue death. Early recognition plus aspirin plus evacuation equals survival.

## Classic Symptoms

Chest pressure or squeezing — not sharp pain. Described as an elephant sitting on the chest. Lasts over 5 minutes. Does not go away with rest.

Radiation pattern:
- Left arm pain (most common, 50% of cases).
- Jaw pain (often mistaken for toothache).
- Neck or back pain between shoulder blades.

Associated symptoms:
- Cold sweat — sudden and profuse.
- Nausea or vomiting (30% of cases).
- Shortness of breath.
- Lightheadedness or dizziness.
- Sense of impending doom.

## Women's Symptoms

Women have atypical presentations more often than men. 40% of women have NO chest pain.

Common female heart attack signs:
- Extreme fatigue (unexplained).
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Shortness of breath.
- Back pain or jaw pain.

If a woman over 50 with cardiovascular risk factors has unexplained fatigue plus nausea plus shortness of breath, assume heart attack until proven otherwise.

## Heart Attack vs Angina

Angina: Chest pain lasting under 5 minutes. Relieved by rest or nitroglycerin. Predictable and stable.

Heart attack: Pain lasting over 5 minutes. NOT relieved by rest. Profuse cold sweat. Worsens over time.

Angina is a warning sign. Heart attack is blocked artery with tissue death occurring now.

## Heart Attack vs GERD

Heartburn (GERD): Pain triggered by eating or lying down. Burning sensation. Relieved by antacids within 5-15 minutes.

Heart attack: NOT triggered by food. Pressure or squeezing, not burning. Antacids provide no relief. Radiation to arm or jaw.

Many people die because they assumed it was heartburn. If in doubt, treat as heart attack.

## Emergency Care

Step 1 — Aspirin immediately:
- 325mg chewed (not swallowed whole — chewing speeds absorption).
- Effect starts in 5-10 minutes.
- Reduces death by 23% when given within 30 minutes.

Do NOT give aspirin if: known aspirin allergy, active bleeding, recent hemorrhagic stroke, severe bleeding disorder.

Step 2 — Nitroglycerin (if available):
- 0.4mg tablet or spray under tongue.
- Repeat every 5 minutes, maximum 3 doses in 15 minutes.
- Do NOT give if systolic BP is under 90, heart rate is under 50, or person has taken Viagra or Cialis in the last 24-48 hours (fatal interaction).
- Expected effects: headache, slight blood pressure drop, flushing.

Step 3 — Positioning:
- Semi-reclined at 45 degrees.
- Do NOT lay flat — increases heart workload.
- If unconscious with no pulse, lay flat and start CPR.

Step 4 — Calm and reassure:
- Keep person calm — stress increases heart workload.
- Speak slowly and calmly.
- Do not leave person alone.

## CPR Readiness

Heart attack can progress to cardiac arrest. Watch for:
- Confusion or drowsiness (hypoxia).
- Irregular pulse.
- Rapid breathing or gasping.
- Loss of consciousness.

CPR if no pulse:
- 30 compressions, 2 breaths.
- Compression depth: 2 inches (5 cm).
- Rate: 100-120 per minute ("Stayin' Alive" tempo).
- Continue until person recovers, AED arrives, or exhaustion.

## Time Is Muscle

Every minute without treatment = more heart tissue death.

- Under 1 hour: 5% mortality, minimal muscle damage.
- 1-3 hours: 8% mortality, 20-40% muscle lost.
- 3-6 hours: 12% mortality, 40-60% muscle lost.
- 6-12 hours: 20% mortality, 60-80% muscle lost.

tPA (clot-busting drug) is effective up to 90 minutes from onset for best outcomes.

## Evacuation

Evacuate immediately for: chest pressure lasting over 5 minutes, chest pain plus radiation to arm or jaw, women with atypical symptoms and risk factors, or any suspected heart attack.

Do NOT drive yourself — 25% of heart attack deaths occur while driving. Call 911 or have someone drive you.

During transport: Continue aspirin, monitor closely, be ready to start CPR.

## Risk Factors

Major factors: Age (men over 45, women over 55), smoking, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, family history, obesity, sedentary lifestyle.

Risk multiplies with each added factor. Three or more factors = 10x elevated risk.

Know your numbers: Blood pressure target under 120/80. Total cholesterol under 200. Fasting blood sugar under 100.

## Common Mistakes

- Waiting to see if it improves: Heart attacks worsen over time. Treat now.
- Assuming too young: 10% of heart attacks occur under age 45.
- Mistaking for heartburn: Many deaths result from this error.
- Ignoring women's atypical symptoms: No chest pain in 40% of women.
- Driving yourself: High risk of crash or arrest.
- Nitroglycerin with low BP: Can cause fatal hypotension.
- Swallowing aspirin whole: Delays effect by 20+ minutes. Always chew it.
