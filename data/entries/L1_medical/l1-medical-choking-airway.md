## Overview
Brain damage: 4-6 min without oxygen. Death: 8-10 min. Over 5,000 US deaths/year — most preventable.

## Recognition
**Mild (can breathe):** forceful cough, can speak, wheezing between coughs → encourage coughing, do NOT interfere
**Severe (complete obstruction):** cannot speak/cough/breathe, silent or gasping, cyanosis, hands clutching throat → intervene immediately

## Adults & Children

### Confirm & Call
- Ask "Are you choking?" — nods but cannot speak = complete obstruction
- Call 911 or send someone immediately

### Heimlich Maneuver
1. Stand behind victim; wrap arms around waist; victim leans slightly forward
2. Make fist; place thumb side ABOVE navel, BELOW ribcage, center of abdomen
3. Grasp fist with other hand; deliver quick inward and UPWARD thrusts
4. Up to 5 thrusts per cycle; repeat until object expelled, victim breathes, or goes unconscious

### If Victim Loses Consciousness
- Lower to ground carefully; start CPR immediately (compressions first)
- Before each rescue breath: look in mouth, remove object only if visible — NO blind finger sweeps
- Continue until EMS arrives or object is expelled

## Infants (<1 Year)
1. Face-down on forearm, head lower than chest, support head/jaw with your hand
2. **5 back blows** — heel of hand between shoulder blades (firm blows)
3. Flip face-up; check mouth; remove object only if clearly visible
4. **5 chest thrusts** — 2 fingers center of chest below nipple line, 1.5" depth
5. Alternate 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts until expelled, crying, or unconscious
6. If unconscious: infant CPR (30:2), check mouth before each breath cycle

## Special Situations
**Pregnant or obese:** chest thrusts — fist on center of breastbone, thrust straight backward
**Self-Heimlich:** fist above navel + other hand grasps; thrust over chair back or countertop edge
**Unconscious found:** check responsiveness, call 911, begin CPR, check mouth before giving breaths

## Post-Rescue
- Seek medical evaluation even if "feels fine" — thrusts can rupture stomach or spleen, break ribs
- Watch for abdominal pain, difficulty swallowing, or persistent coughing

## Common Mistakes
- ❌ Blind finger sweeps (can push object deeper)
- ❌ Interfering with effective coughing (mild obstruction)
- ❌ Thrusting too high (on ribcage) or too low (below navel)
- ❌ Abdominal thrusts on infants or pregnant women
- ❌ Stopping too soon — continue until successful or victim is unconscious
