# Wound Irrigation

## Why It Matters

- Bacterial load determines infection
- Wiping pushes bacteria deeper
- Pressure irrigation reduces infection
  risk by 80% vs no irrigation
- Works with clean water
- This is the most important step
  in wound care before closure

## Pressure Target

- 5-8 PSI is ideal for wound cleaning
- Achieved with 35 mL syringe + 18g tip
- Or squeeze bottle with small opening
- Bulb syringe is too low pressure
- Cup and pour is too low pressure
- Must create visible turbulence

## Improvised Irrigation

- Plastic bag with pinhole in corner
- Filled and squeezed produces good PSI
- Clear water bottle with cap poked hole
- Squeeze hard for jet stream
- Repeat until runoff is clear
- Test pressure on your hand first

## Fluid Options

- Potable water is acceptable
- Saline (1/4 tsp salt per cup water)
- Hydrogen peroxide diluted (2-3%)
- Do not use full-strength peroxide
- Iodine solution diluted 1% betadine
- No alcohol directly in wound

## Volume and Technique

- Minimum 250 mL per inch of wound
- Hold tip 1-2 cm above wound
- Angle jet to flush all recesses
- Lift wound edges and irrigate beneath
- Continue until runoff is clear
- Gentle once more after debridement

## What Can Go Wrong

- Pressure too low, debris remains
- Not enough volume used
- Skipping wound corners and pockets
- Using alcohol which damages tissue

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Improvised pressure system
  delivering 5 PSI in 60 seconds
- Sign of mastery: 250 mL clean
  irrigation of simulated wound
