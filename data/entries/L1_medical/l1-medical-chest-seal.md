# Chest Seal Technique

## Sucking Wound Signs

- Penetrating chest wound that bubbles, hisses, or foams with breathing
- Any wound between collarbone and bottom of rib cage
- Check FRONT and BACK  -  bullet may have exited through back
- Decreased or absent breath sounds on injured side
- Victim struggling to breathe, rapid shallow respirations

## Seal Application

- Have patient exhale forcefully before applying  -  pushes air out first
- Commercial vented seal (Hyfin, Asherman): press firmly, full contact
- Improvised: plastic bag, cling wrap, or credit card over wound
- Tape THREE sides only: top, left, right  -  leave bottom edge open
- Open bottom edge = flutter valve: air escapes out, cannot enter
- Seal must extend 2 inches beyond wound edges on all sides
- Seal BOTH entry AND exit wounds simultaneously if exit wound present

## Tension Pneumo Signs

- Breathing gets WORSE after sealing (not better)
- Neck veins become visibly distended
- Increasing confusion, agitation, or loss of consciousness
- Trachea shifts to one side (away from injured side)  -  late sign
- Rapid deterioration and dropping blood pressure
- Check every 2-5 minutes  -  develops within minutes if tension builds

## Burp the Seal

- Lift one corner of seal 2-3 seconds  -  trapped air rushes out
- Reseal immediately, watch for breathing to improve
- Repeat every 5-10 minutes if tension signs return
- Commercial vented seals: built-in valve handles this automatically
- No improvement after burping: needle decompression needed urgently

## Do NOT / Evacuate

- Do NOT tape all 4 sides  -  creates tension pneumothorax and kills
- Do NOT remove impaled objects  -  stabilize in place
- Do NOT lay patient flat  -  position semi-upright on injured side
- Best position: 45-60 degrees upright, leaning toward injured side
- Evacuate immediately  -  needs chest tube insertion and surgical care
