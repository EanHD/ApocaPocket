# Improvised Medications

## Why It Matters

- Pharmaceutical supply may be gone
- Some natural remedies have evidence
- Knowing limitations prevents harm
- Charcoal for poisoning is critical
- Honey for wounds has good evidence
- Do not use herbs with no knowledge

## Willow Bark

- Contains salicin, similar to aspirin
- Chew or boil white willow bark
- Pain relief and fever reduction
- Use inner bark, not outer layer
- Dose: 1-2 inches bark per cup water
- Works in 1-2 hours, lasts 4 hours
- Avoid in children: same Reye risk

## Activated Charcoal

- Adsorbs toxins before absorption
- Must be given within 1 hour ideally
- Made from burned and activated wood
- Dose: 25-50 g in water (adult)
- Do not use for acid or alkali ingestion
- Do not use for unconscious patient
- Pre-made is far more effective

## Honey for Wounds

- Raw honey is antimicrobial
- Manuka honey most evidence-based
- Apply to wound after irrigation
- Cover with light dressing
- Change daily
- Works against resistant bacteria
- Do not use on deep punctures

## Plantain Leaf Poultice

- Common lawn weed, broad flat leaves
- Bruise and apply to bee stings
- Mild anti-inflammatory topically
- Clean wound before application
- Reduce itch and minor swelling
- Not a substitute for irrigation

## What Can Go Wrong

- Willow given to children
- Charcoal given too late to help
- Honey applied to infected deep wound
- Misidentifying plant species

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify willow and plantain
  in a field or park setting
- Sign of mastery: Prepare charcoal
  suspension and dose correctly
