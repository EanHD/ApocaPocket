# Altitude Edema

## Why It Matters

- HACE and HAPE occur above 8,000 ft
- They require different monitoring
- Same root cause, different organs
- Peripheral edema (hands, face) is
  a warning sign of developing HACE
- HAPE can be silent until crisis

## Peripheral Edema

- Face puffiness on waking
- Rings tight on fingers at altitude
- Ankle and lower leg swelling
- This alone is not dangerous
- But it is a warning: slow ascent
- May indicate HACE risk

## HACE Signs

- Severe headache not responding
  to ibuprofen or acetaminophen
- Ataxia: cannot walk heel-to-toe
- Confusion and disorientation
- Drowsiness, difficulty staying awake
- Hallucinations in advanced stage
- Descend immediately: 1,000+ ft

## HAPE Signs

- Dry cough, worsening over hours
- Reduced exertion capacity
- Gurgling or crackling lung sounds
- Cyanosis: blue lips or nails
- Resting rate over 30 breaths/min
- Descend immediately: life-threatening

## Field Medications

- HACE: Dexamethasone 8 mg first dose
  then 4 mg every 6 hours
- HAPE: Nifedipine 30 mg extended
  release if available
- Medications buy time, not a cure
- Descent is the only real treatment
- Gamow bag if available buys hours

## What Can Go Wrong

- Peripheral edema ignored as minor
- Medication without descent attempted
- HAPE blamed on fitness
- HACE confusion prevents self-help

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Heel-to-toe ataxia test
  administered correctly
- Sign of mastery: Correct ID of
  HACE vs HAPE vs AMS in 3 cases
