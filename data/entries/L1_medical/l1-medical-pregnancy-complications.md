# Pregnancy Emergencies

## Preeclampsia

### Recognition
- Concerning: BP >=140/90 mmHg (two readings 4+ hours apart)
- Severe: BP >=160/110 mmHg
- Emergency: BP >=180/120 + symptoms
- Classic triad: hypertension + proteinuria + edema
- Severe headache unrelieved by medication
- Vision changes: blurred, spots, flashing lights
- Upper right abdominal pain (liver involvement)
- Sudden severe swelling of face, hands, feet
- Shortness of breath or decreased urination

### Treatment
- Mild (140-160/90-110): bed rest on left side, monitor BP every 4-6 hours, limit salt, track fetal movement, evacuate within 24-48 hours
- Severe (>=160/110 or severe symptoms): left side position, magnesium sulfate 4-6g IV if available, evacuate immediately
- Danger signs (imminent eclampsia): severe headache + vision changes, right upper quadrant pain, altered consciousness  -  evacuate now

## Eclampsia

### Recognition
- Pre-eclampsia + tonic-clonic seizures (convulsions, loss of consciousness)
- Can occur before, during, or up to 6 weeks after delivery
- 40% have no warning signs

### Management
1. Protect airway: turn on left side to prevent aspiration
2. Protect from injury: pad head, remove hazards  -  do NOT restrain
3. Time the seizure  -  >5 min = status epilepticus
4. Give magnesium sulfate 4-6g IV bolus then 2g/hr if available
5. After seizure: recovery position on left side, supplemental oxygen if available
6. Evacuate urgently  -  delivery is the only cure, 60% have multiple seizures

---

## HELLP Syndrome

- Acronym: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets
- Right upper quadrant pain, nausea, fatigue  -  10-15% have NO hypertension
- Danger signs: bleeding from nose or gums, jaundice, confusion or seizures
- Evacuate immediately  -  only cure is delivery
- Magnesium sulfate for seizure prevention if available

---

## Ectopic Pregnancy

### Recognition
- Classic triad: abdominal pain + vaginal bleeding + missed period
- Sharp one-sided stabbing pain
- Shoulder pain = ruptured ectopic (blood irritating diaphragm)
- Dizziness or fainting (internal bleeding)
- Shock signs: pale, cold, rapid pulse >100, low BP

### Emergency Management
1. Recognize shock: pale, cold, pulse >100, low BP
2. Lay flat, elevate legs 12 inches
3. Keep warm to prevent hypothermia
4. Do NOT give fluids by mouth  -  surgery likely needed
5. Evacuate urgently  -  ruptured ectopic is a surgical emergency
- WARNING Ectopic pregnancy cannot be saved. Attempting to continue is fatal.

---

## Miscarriage

- Threatened: light bleeding, cervix closed, pregnancy may continue  -  monitor closely
- Inevitable/incomplete: moderate to heavy bleeding, cervix open, tissue passing  -  evacuate, may need D&C
- Complete: all tissue passed, bleeding decreasing  -  monitor for infection signs

**Management:**
- Bleeding >2 pads/hour: evacuate immediately
- Pain: ibuprofen 600mg every 6 hours
- Infection signs: fever >100.4 degF, foul discharge, severe pain  -  evacuate
- Late miscarriage (12-20 weeks): higher bleeding risk, evacuate sooner

---

## Preterm Labor

### Recognition
- Regular contractions: >=4 per hour or >=8 per 20 minutes before 37 weeks
- Pelvic pressure, low back pain, change in vaginal discharge
- Fluid leak = premature rupture of membranes

### Management
1. Drink 500mL water  -  dehydration can trigger contractions
2. Empty bladder
3. Rest on left side and time contractions
4. Tocolytics if available (nifedipine, terbutaline) with medical supervision
5. Steroids at 24-34 weeks if available (betamethasone) for fetal lung maturity
6. Evacuate if contractions continue, fluid is leaking, bleeding, or <34 weeks

---

## Placental Abruption

- Sudden severe abdominal pain (constant, rigid abdomen) + dark red vaginal bleeding
- Decreased fetal movement
- Risk factors: trauma, hypertension, pre-eclampsia, smoking, cocaine, prior abruption
- Shock protocol: lay flat, elevate legs, keep warm  -  do NOT give fluids by mouth
- Evacuate urgently  -  fetal mortality 10-50% depending on severity

---

## Placenta Previa

- Painless bright red vaginal bleeding, 2nd-3rd trimester, no abdominal pain
- Do NOT perform vaginal exam  -  can trigger massive bleeding
- Rest, no activity, no sex, no heavy lifting
- Evacuate if bleeding exceeds 1 pad per hour
- Requires cesarean delivery  -  vaginal delivery is fatal

## Common Mistakes

- [X] Ignoring vision changes + headache  -  pre-eclampsia emergency
- [X] Assuming all bleeding is miscarriage  -  could be ectopic (life-threatening)
- [X] Skipping BP monitoring  -  pre-eclampsia can develop in 24-48 hours
- [X] Delaying evacuation for ectopic  -  ruptured ectopic = surgical emergency
- [X] Vaginal exam with placenta previa  -  triggers massive bleeding
- [X] Ignoring decreased fetal movement  -  sign of fetal distress
