# Stroke Response (FAST)

Stroke kills 2 million brain cells per minute. Treat within 3-4.5 hours. Every second counts.

## FAST Protocol

Check all four signs now.

- F  -  Face: Ask to smile. One side droops = stroke sign.
- A  -  Arm: Both arms up, eyes closed. One drifts down = stroke sign.
- S  -  Speech: "Can't teach old dog new tricks." Slurred or wrong words = stroke sign.
- T  -  Time: Note exact symptom start time. Call 911 now.

Add BE-FAST for full screen:

- B  -  Balance: Sudden dizziness or stumbling.
- E  -  Eyes: Sudden vision loss or double vision.

ANY positive sign = evacuate immediately. Do not wait.

## Stroke Types

Ischemic (87%): Clot blocks artery. Gradual onset over minutes to hours. Give aspirin.

Hemorrhagic (13%): Vessel ruptures. Sudden "worst headache of my life." DO NOT give aspirin  -  worsens bleeding.

Suspect hemorrhagic if any of these are present:
- Thunderclap headache  -  sudden and severe
- Vomiting
- Neck stiffness
- Altered consciousness early
- Onset under 1 minute

## Aspirin

Give 325mg chewed immediately ONLY if:
- FAST symptoms present
- No severe headache
- No vomiting, no neck stiffness
- Gradual onset (minutes to hours)

DO NOT give if thunderclap headache or suspected hemorrhagic stroke. Aspirin in hemorrhagic stroke kills.

## Positioning

Elevate head 30 degrees. Never lay flat  -  increases intracranial pressure and aspiration risk.

Do NOT lower blood pressure. High BP is compensatory. Allow systolic 140-180. Exception: systolic >220 with active bleeding.

## Women's Symptoms

Women have atypical presentations 40% of the time  -  no chest pain equivalent.

Common female stroke signs:
- Extreme fatigue
- Nausea or vomiting
- Shortness of breath
- Back or jaw pain
- NO obvious FAST signs

If woman over 50 with cardiovascular risk factors has unexplained fatigue + nausea + shortness of breath, assume stroke.

## Cincinnati Scale

Three tests, 0 = normal, 1 = abnormal:
- Facial droop: Both sides equal = 0. One side droops = 1.
- Arm drift: Both arms hold = 0. One arm drifts = 1.
- Speech: Clear and correct = 0. Slurred or wrong words = 1.

Score 1 = 72% stroke probability. Score 3 = 97%. Any abnormality = evacuate.

## Time Windows

- tPA window: 3-4.5 hours from symptom onset. 30% better recovery if given within 3 hours.
- Thrombectomy: Up to 24 hours for large vessel occlusion.
- Every 15-minute delay: 4% worse outcome, 30 million more brain cells lost.

## TIA

TIA (mini-stroke): Symptoms resolve within 24 hours, often within 1 hour. Still a medical emergency.

- 40% have full stroke within 90 days.
- 20% have full stroke within 48 hours.

Treat TIA exactly like a full stroke. Evacuate. Give aspirin.

## Bell's Palsy vs Stroke

Bell's palsy: Full face including forehead paralyzed. Cannot wrinkle forehead. No arm weakness.

Stroke: Only lower face affected. Forehead movement preserved. Arm or leg weakness present.

## Evacuation

Evacuate immediately for any FAST/BE-FAST sign, sudden severe headache, sudden vision loss, or sudden inability to walk.

During transport:
- Head elevated 30 degrees
- No food or drink (aspiration risk)
- Monitor consciousness closely
- Be ready to start CPR (seizures or arrest possible)

## Common Mistakes

- Giving aspirin for hemorrhagic stroke  -  worsens bleeding, increases mortality.
- Assuming it's a migraine  -  stroke mimics migraine, especially under age 50.
- Waiting to see if it improves  -  TIA resolves but 40% progress to full stroke.
- Laying patient flat  -  increases intracranial pressure.
- Lowering blood pressure acutely  -  worsens brain perfusion.
