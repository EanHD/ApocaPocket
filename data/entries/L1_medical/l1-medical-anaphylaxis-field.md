# Anaphylaxis Field

## Why It Matters

- Anaphylaxis progresses in minutes
- Epinephrine is the only first-line drug
- Antihistamines alone are not sufficient
- Second doses are often needed
- Rebound reaction occurs in 30% of cases
- Knowing triggers improves preparedness

## Anaphylaxis Signs

- Hives or flushing spreading rapidly
- Throat or tongue swelling
- Difficulty swallowing or speaking
- Wheezing or stridor
- Severe drop in blood pressure
- Combined skin + airway + cardiac

## Allergy vs Anaphylaxis

- Mild: hives only, no systemic signs
- Anaphylaxis: systemic reaction in
  2+ organ systems (skin + airway)
- Or: shock with known allergen exposure
- Or: any airway compromise
- When in doubt: give epinephrine

## EpiPen Technique

- Mid outer thigh, can be through clothing
- Hold 3 seconds after injection
- Remove and rub injection site
- Call for evacuation immediately
- Dose: EpiPen 0.3 mg adult (0.15 mg child)
- Repeat dose in 5-15 minutes if no improvement

## Positioning

- Anaphylaxis with low BP: lay flat
  legs elevated
- With airway compromise: sitting up
- Unconscious: recovery position
- Cardiac arrest: begin CPR
- Do not stand up patient with shock

## Rebound Management

- Secondary reaction at 6-12 hours
- Continue observation 24 hours
- Antihistamines (Benadryl 25-50 mg)
  for mild urticaria support
- Steroids if available and if
  prolonged reaction expected
- Do not leave patient alone

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Administer EpiPen correctly
  with correct thigh technique
- Sign of mastery: Identify anaphylaxis
  vs mild reaction in 4 scenarios
