# Sunburn Field Care

## Why It Matters

- Severe sunburn is a real emergency
- Fluid loss from large burn areas
- Sun poisoning causes systemic illness
- Desert and snow both cause rapid burn
- Reflection doubles UV exposure
- Proper care prevents infection

## Sunburn Severity

- First degree: redness, pain, warmth
- Second degree: blisters forming
- Sun poisoning: blisters + headache,
  nausea, fever, chills, dehydration
- Over 10% body area second degree:
  consider medical evacuation
- Child with blistering burn: evacuate

## First Degree Care

- Cool water or cool wet cloth on skin
- Do not use ice or ice water
- Aloe vera gel directly on skin
- Ibuprofen 400 mg for inflammation
- Increase fluid intake 1-2 L extra
- Shade and cover from further exposure

## Second Degree Care

- Cool as above
- Do not burst blisters
- Cover loosely with non-stick gauze
- Antibiotic ointment on open areas
- Increase fluids aggressively
- Ibuprofen every 6-8 hours

## Sun Poisoning Care

- Move to shade and rest
- Oral rehydration with electrolytes
- Cool damp cloths to reduce fever
- Ibuprofen for systemic inflammation
- No further sun exposure today
- Monitor for confusion or shock signs

## What Can Go Wrong

- Blisters burst and infected
- Ice applied to burn (worsens injury)
- Fluid replacement neglected
- Covering too tight, restricts movement

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Classify and treat a
  described sunburn case in 60 sec
- Sign of mastery: Estimate fluid
  replacement needed for large sunburn
