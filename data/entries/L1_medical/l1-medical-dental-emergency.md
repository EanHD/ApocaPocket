# Dental Emergencies

## Why It Matters

- Dental pain is incapacitating
- Abscess can spread to airway fast
- Simple temporary relief is possible
- Wrong treatment can worsen abscess
- Antibiotics are required for abscess
- Most dental emergencies are time-sensitive

## Identifying Abscess

- Severe throbbing tooth pain
- Swelling of gum around tooth
- Facial swelling adjacent to tooth
- Fever accompanying pain
- Foul taste in mouth
- Pus visible at gum line

## Abscess Treatment

- Warm salt water rinses every 2-3 hrs
- Do not lance abscess yourself
- Ibuprofen 600 mg for pain and swelling
- Antibiotics required:
  amoxicillin 500 mg TID x 7 days
  or metronidazole if penicillin allergy
- Do not apply heat to outside of face
- Evacuate when antibiotics available

## Lost Filling or Crown

- Temporary cement: zinc oxide paste
- Oil of cloves (eugenol) desensitizes
- Dental wax as temporary sealant
- Sugarfree gum packed in cavity
- Avoid hot/cold foods on affected tooth
- Keep area clean, gentle rinse

## Tooth Pain Only

- Clove oil on cotton in cavity
- Ibuprofen for anti-inflammatory
- Avoid extreme temperature foods
- Rinse with warm salt water
- Temporary filling material from kit
- Evacuate when able

## What Can Go Wrong

- Abscess ignored - spreads to jaw
- Heat applied externally accelerates
- Lancing abscess pushes bacteria
- Facial swelling reaches eye

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Correctly identify abscess
  vs tooth pain from description
- Sign of mastery: Temporary filling
  applied and held for 24 hours
