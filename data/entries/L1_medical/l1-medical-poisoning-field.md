# Poisoning Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Many field toxins look similar
- Treatment varies by toxin class
- Charcoal works for some, not all
- Vomiting induction can worsen some
- Time to treatment matters greatly
- Poison control is best resource

## Common Sources

- Toxic plant ingestion (berries, fungi)
- Medication overdose or error
- Carbon monoxide exposure
- Heavy metal contaminated water
- Chemical skin absorption
- Botulism from improperly stored food

## Initial Assessment

- What was ingested? When? How much?
- Is patient awake and breathing?
- Any burns in mouth or on lips?
- Pupils: dilated or pinpoint?
- Skin: dry and hot, or sweaty?
- Any odor on breath? (alcohol, almonds)

## Charcoal: When to Use

- Effective: plant toxins, medications
- Give within 1 hour of ingestion
- Dose: 25-50 g in water (adult)
- Not for: acids, alkalis, metals
- Not for: hydrocarbons (gasoline)
- Not for: unconscious patient

## Never Induce Vomiting

- Corrosive substances (acid, alkali)
- Petroleum products (gasoline, kerosene)
- Sharp objects ingested
- Any patient with altered mental status
- Any patient at risk of aspiration
- Plant toxins if >1 hour elapsed

## What Can Go Wrong

- Induced vomiting of corrosive
- Charcoal given to unconscious patient
- Aspiration during vomiting
- Under-estimating time-toxin window

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Correctly classify 5 toxins
  as charcoal appropriate or not
- Sign of mastery: Full poisoning
  assessment in under 3 minutes
