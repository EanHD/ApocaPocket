# Severe Asthma Attack

## Overview
Severe attacks can be fatal in 30-60 min without treatment. "Silent chest" (no wheezing) = no air moving = life-threatening emergency.

## Severity Assessment
**MILD:** wheezing, full sentences, HR <100 -> 2-4 puffs albuterol, monitor
**MODERATE:** loud wheeze, 3-5 word phrases, accessory muscles, HR 100-120 -> 4-6 puffs every 20 min, oral steroids
**SEVERE:** 1-2 words or silent chest, cyanosis, confused, HR >120 -> EMERGENCY: aggressive treatment + immediate evacuation

Peak flow zones: GREEN >=80% (routine) - YELLOW 50-80% (use rescue inhaler) - RED <50% (emergency)

## Inhaler Protocol
- Albuterol/salbutamol: 4-6 puffs initial, repeat every 20 min x 3 rounds (max 18 puffs/hr)
- **Proper use:** shake 5-10 sec - exhale fully - seal lips - press + inhale slowly 3-5 sec - hold breath 10 sec - wait 30-60 sec between puffs
- With spacer: 1 puff into spacer, 5-6 normal breaths per puff (better delivery)

## Oral Steroids
- Prednisone: adults 40-60 mg - children 6-11 yrs 1-2 mg/kg - children <6 yrs 1-2 mg/kg
- Give early  -  works in 4-6 hours; alternatives: methylprednisolone 40-60 mg, dexamethasone 8-12 mg

## Position & Breathe
- **Tripod position:** sit upright, lean forward, elbows on knees  -  do NOT lie flat
- **Pursed-lip breathing:** inhale 2 sec through nose - exhale 4-6 sec through pursed lips (creates back-pressure, keeps airways open)
- **Diaphragmatic:** belly rises on inhale (not chest); exhale slowly; reduces panic

## No Inhaler Options
- Strong coffee/tea (2-3 cups, 200-300mg caffeine): mild bronchodilator, effect in 30-60 min (~10-15% relief)
- EpiPen 0.3mg IM thigh: works 5-10 min, repeat after 5-10 min if needed (causes rapid HR, tremors  -  severe cases only)
- Steam inhalation: may loosen mucus (can worsen cold-air triggered asthma)

## Status Asthmaticus
Attack lasting >1 hr despite treatment: continue 6-8 puffs every 20 min - oral steroids if not given - tripod position - prepare for CPR - EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY (5-10% mortality even with ICU)

## Breathing vs CPR
- No pulse + not breathing: full CPR (30 compressions : 2 breaths)
- Pulse present but not breathing: 1 breath every 5-6 sec (10-12/min)
- Asthmatic lungs are stiff -> may need more force for rescue breaths; don't overdo (pneumothorax risk)

## Trigger Avoidance
- Fire smoke: stay upwind, cover mouth/nose with cloth
- Cold air: scarf over mouth/nose; breathe through nose; pre-treat with inhaler before sleep
- Dusty shelter: wet cloth to reduce dust; clean bedding
- Exercise: 2 puffs albuterol 15 min before activity

## When to Evacuate
- Attack not improving after 1 hr of treatment
- Blue lips/fingernails (cyanosis); confusion or drowsiness (hypoxia)
- Silent chest (no wheezing = no air movement); person collapsing

## Common Mistakes
- [X] Waiting too long to use inhaler  -  use at first sign of symptoms
- [X] Not shaking inhaler; rapid shallow breaths; lying flat during attack
- [X] Overusing rescue inhaler without steroids (doesn't address inflammation)
- [X] Ignoring "silent chest"  -  this is LIFE-THREATENING, not "getting better"
