# Electrolyte Loss

## Why It Matters

- Dehydration and hyponatremia look
  similar but have opposite treatments
- Giving water to hyponatremia patient
  causes dangerous brain swelling
- Electrolyte loss is common in field
- Endurance exertion = high risk
- Know the difference before treating

## Dehydration vs Hypo

- Dehydration: too little water
  (and electrolytes in proportion)
- Hyponatremia: too little sodium
  relative to water volume
- Dehydration: dark urine, thirst
- Hyponatremia: clear urine, nausea
- Both cause weakness and confusion
- Different cause, different treatment

## Hyponatremia Signs

- Headache, nausea, vomiting
- Confusion, irritability
- Weakness and cramping
- Swollen hands or face
- Clear or pale urine in sick patient
- History: drank large amounts of
  plain water during exertion

## Hyponatremia Treatment

- Stop all plain water intake
- Provide salty food immediately
- Crackers, chips, broth, salty snacks
- Oral rehydration salts if available
- Rest in cool shade
- Mild cases: resolves within 2-4 hrs

## Dehydration Treatment

- ORS: 1 L water, 6 tsp sugar,
  1/2 tsp salt, citrus if available
- Sip slowly if nausea present
- Avoid plain water only if severe
- Cool shade, rest, reduce activity
- IV fluids for severe cases

## What Can Go Wrong

- Hyponatremia given plain water
- Both conditions treated identically
- Urine color alone used to diagnose
- Patient restarting activity too soon

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Correctly distinguish 3
  dehydration vs hyponatremia cases
- Sign of mastery: Correct ORS
  preparation from memory
