summary: Field management of suspected spinal cord injuries with manual stabilization, log-roll techniques, and improvised immobilization to prevent permanent paralysis.
steps:
- Suspect spinal injury based on mechanism - falls >10 feet (3m), vehicle accidents, diving, blunt head/neck trauma, unconsciousness.
- Immediately stabilize head and neck in neutral position - manual inline stabilization.
- Assess neurological status - sensation, movement, pain in all extremities.
- Tell patient DO NOT MOVE - explain spinal injury risk.
- If immediate danger (fire, water), use emergency drag maintaining neutral spine alignment.
- For non-emergency moves, use log-roll technique with minimum 3-4 people.
- Apply improvised cervical collar - rolled towel, SAM splint, or rigid materials.
- Immobilize entire spine on firm surface - backboard or door.
- Monitor airway continuously - be prepared to log-roll if vomiting.
- Check distal CSM (circulation, sensation, motor) every 15 minutes.
- Maintain body temperature - hypothermia worsens outcomes.
- Evacuate with spinal precautions maintained - this requires advanced care.
warnings:
- ANY movement of unstable spinal injury can cause permanent paralysis or death.
- Absence of pain or numbness does NOT rule out spinal injury - mechanism of injury is key indicator.
- Do NOT remove motorcycle/sports helmet unless airway compromised - requires two-person technique.
- Do NOT apply excessive traction to neck - neutral position only.
- Flexion, extension, or rotation of spine is catastrophic - maintain strict alignment.
- Children have proportionally larger heads - pad under shoulders to maintain neutral.

## Overview
Secondary injury from improper handling causes permanent paralysis. Immobilize immediately  -  when in doubt, immobilize.

## Suspect Spinal Injury
- Fall >10 ft (3m), MVC, diving into shallow water
- Ejection/rollover, pedestrian struck, direct blow to head/neck/back
- Found unconscious, hanging/strangulation, blast injury
- Elderly: higher risk even with minor falls

## Signs of Injury
- Numbness/tingling or weakness in any extremity
- Loss of bowel/bladder control; priapism
- Neck/back pain; difficulty breathing without chest injury
- Altered mental status or distracting injuries + high-risk mechanism
- **Neuro check:** "Squeeze hands / push feet against mine / wiggle toes"; light touch all limbs

## Manual Inline Stab
- Approach from above victim's head
- Palms over ears, fingers along jaw/skull, thumbs at back of head
- Hold neutral  -  no flex, no extension, no rotation, no lateral bend
- Gentle firm pressure  -  do NOT pull or apply traction
- Maintain until immobilization device is applied
- Off-angle head: move gently toward neutral; stop if resistance, pain, or spasm -> immobilize in position found

## Log-Roll Technique
- Lead (head): stabilizes and calls all commands
- Rescuer 2: shoulders/torso; Rescuer 3: hips; Rescuer 4: legs (optional)
- Roll as single unit  -  shoulders and hips together, no twisting
- Place backboard behind victim, roll back onto board
- Secure at forehead, chest, pelvis, and ankles

## Cervical Collar
- Materials: SAM splint, rolled towel (4" cylinder), foam pad
- Chin to shoulders, ear to ear; wrap and secure with tape
- Must NOT compress airway  -  check carotid pulse after applying

## Full Spine Immob
- Board options: door, wide boards lashed, skis/poles lashed
- Adult: pad behind head; Child: pad under shoulders 1-2" (proportionally large head)
- Head blocks: rolled clothing or stuff sacks on each side
- Secure: forehead/collar, chest, pelvis, above knees, ankles

## Monitoring
- Every 15-30 min: neuro status, breathing, skin color, collar tightness, alignment
- If vomiting: log-roll entire board to side, clear airway, return to supine

## Helmet Removal
Remove ONLY if airway obstructed, helmet is loose, or CPR required
- Rescuer 1 supports jaw/skull from below; Rescuer 2 cuts strap, spreads sides, slides off
- Rescuer 1 transitions to inline stab as helmet clears; Rescuer 2 applies collar

## Clearing Spine
No immobilization needed ONLY IF ALL met: no high-risk mechanism - normal mental status - no intoxicants - no neck/back pain - no neuro symptoms - no midline tenderness - full ROM without pain

## Common Mistakes
- [X] Trusting "I feel fine" after high-risk mechanism
- [X] Moving victim before stabilization; log-rolling with <3 people
- [X] Not maintaining neutral alignment during movement
- [X] Pulling/tractioning neck; hyperextending to open airway (use jaw thrust)
- [X] Removing helmet unnecessarily; collar too tight
- [X] Not reassessing neuro status after any movement

**When in doubt, immobilize.**
