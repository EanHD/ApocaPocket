# Advanced Wound Closure

## WARNING

Training required. Never close contaminated wounds.
Trapping infection causes abscess or sepsis.

If medical care is reachable within 6-8 hours:
clean, pressure-dress, and transport instead.

## When NOT to Close

Do not close any of these:
- More than 12 hours old (6 hours for hands and feet)
- Animal or human bites on body or extremities
- Puncture wounds of any depth
- Contaminated: dirt, feces, embedded material
- Signs of infection: pus, red streaking, fever, odor
- Devitalized tissue visible, gray or black tissue

When in doubt, leave open and pack with gauze.

## Wound Prep

Steps before any closure:
- Irrigate with 200-500 mL clean water under hard pressure
- Use 30-60 mL syringe and 18g needle, or squeeze bottle
- Flush all surfaces until water runs clear
- Remove visible debris and non-bleeding tissue
- Apply pressure for 5 minutes to achieve hemostasis
- Pat completely dry before closure

Do NOT use hydrogen peroxide inside wound. It destroys tissue.

## Closure Methods

Steri-strips or butterfly closures:
- Low-tension, clean, shallow wounds only
- Apply perpendicular to wound, pulling edges together
- Add a strip parallel along wound edge to reinforce
- Leave in place 7-10 days

Tissue adhesive (Dermabond or clean super glue):
- Short, dry, low-tension wounds on flat skin
- Hold edges together, apply thin coat, wait 30 seconds
- Apply 3-4 coats total
- Do not use on joints, moist areas, or gaping wounds

Staples:
- Scalp, trunk, extremities. Not face.
- Evert edges with forceps, center stapler, fire
- Space 5-10 mm apart
- Remove at 7-14 days

Sutures:
- Most versatile. Use for high-tension or deep wounds.
- Simple interrupted: enter at 90 degrees, equal depth both sides
- Space sutures 5-10 mm apart
- Face: 5-0 or 6-0 nylon, remove at 3-5 days
- Scalp, trunk, limbs: 3-0 or 4-0 nylon, remove 7-14 days
- Square knot: right over left, left over right, 4 total throws

## Infection Signs

Check wound daily. Evacuate immediately if:
- Red streaking extending outward from wound edge
- Pus or foul odor developing
- Fever combined with increasing wound pain
- Redness spreading rapidly, hours not days
- Blistering or crackling sensation under skin (necrotizing)

For minor redness without spreading:
remove one suture for drainage, irrigate, pack with gauze,
change dressing daily. Watch closely for 24-48 hours.

## Aftercare

- Keep dry for 48 hours after closure
- After 48 hours: clean with soap and water, pat dry
- Apply thin coat of antibiotic ointment daily
- Elevate injured area and limit movement
- Remove sutures on schedule noted above
- After suture removal: apply steri-strips for 7 more days
