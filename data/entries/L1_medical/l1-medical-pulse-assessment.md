# Pulse Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Pulse reveals cardiac and shock status
- Location correlates with blood pressure
- Rate and quality narrow diagnoses
- Trending over time shows progression
- First step in patient assessment
- Do it every 5-15 min in sick patient

## Pulse Locations

- Radial: inner wrist, thumb side
- Carotid: neck beside trachea
- Femoral: upper inner thigh crease
- Brachial: inner upper arm (infant)
- Dorsalis pedis: top of foot
- If radial absent, check carotid next

## Normal Values

- Adult resting: 60-100 bpm
- Child: 70-120 bpm
- Infant: 100-160 bpm
- Athlete: may be 40-60 bpm normal
- Over 100 bpm: tachycardia
- Under 60 bpm: bradycardia

## Measuring Correctly

- Use index and middle finger, not thumb
- Thumb has its own pulse
- Count 30 seconds, multiply by 2
- Note if regular or irregular rhythm
- Describe quality: strong, weak, thready
- Record time alongside rate

## Quality Descriptors

- Strong: normal cardiac output
- Weak or thready: low blood pressure
- Bounding: high pressure, sepsis
- Irregular: arrhythmia possible
- Absent radial but carotid present:
  systolic BP approximately 70 mmHg

## What Can Go Wrong

- Counting own pulse instead of patient
- Too much pressure collapses vessel
- Arrhythmia makes count inaccurate
- Missing thready pulse in cold skin

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Locate and count pulse at
  3 sites in under 60 seconds each
- Sign of mastery: Correctly identify
  weak/strong/absent quality difference
