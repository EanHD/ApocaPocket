# Internal Bleeding

## Why It Matters

- No field treatment stops internal bleed
- Recognition determines evacuation speed
- Shock is preventable with positioning
- Denial is common and dangerous
- Mechanism of injury predicts risk
- Delay in evacuation is the main mistake

## High-Risk Mechanisms

- Fall from height over 15 feet
- High-speed vehicle impact
- Blunt abdominal or chest trauma
- Crush injury to torso or pelvis
- Handlebar or steering wheel impact
- Explosion or blast overpressure

## Internal Bleed Signs

- Mechanism of injury as above
- Progressive abdominal distension
- Rigidity or guarding of abdomen
- Bruising developing over hours
- Tenderness without external wound
- Signs of shock without visible blood

## Shock Signs

- Anxiety and restlessness
- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Rapid weak pulse over 100
- Rapid breathing over 20
- Thirst and nausea
- Confusion in late stage

## Field Management

- Lay flat, legs elevated if no spine risk
- Keep warm to prevent hypothermia
- No food or water by mouth
- Oxygen if available
- Frequent vitals every 5-10 minutes
- Rapid evacuation is the treatment

## What Can Go Wrong

- Waiting for more obvious signs
- Sitting patient up with low BP
- Food or fluids given before surgery
- Missing delayed shock presentation

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify internal bleeding
  risk from mechanism alone
- Sign of mastery: Shock prevention
  positioning and monitoring for 1 hr
