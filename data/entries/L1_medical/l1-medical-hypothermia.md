## Overview
Hypothermia kills when core temp drops below 95 degF (35 degC). Kills more in moderate climates via wet + wind than extreme cold. "No one is dead until warm and dead"  -  continue CPR until hospital.

## Stages
- Mild 90-95 degF (32-35 degC): shivering, cold pale skin, confusion, slurred speech, clumsiness
- Moderate 82-90 degF (28-32 degC): shivering STOPS (danger sign), blue lips, drowsy, irrational, slow pulse/breathing
- Severe <82 degF (<28 degC): unconscious, rigid, very slow/absent pulse, appears dead but may be revivable

## Protocol

### 1. Remove from Exposure
- Get out of wind, rain, snow; create windbreak if no shelter
- Get victim off cold ground (insulate from below with sleeping bag, branches)

### 2. Prevent Heat Loss
- Remove wet clothing (cut off if needed  -  wet conducts heat 25x faster than dry)
- Priority areas: head, neck, chest, groin (core loses most heat)
- Cover head (40% of heat lost through head); insulate below AND above
- Add vapor barrier (tarp/plastic) outside insulation to block wind

### 3. Handle Gently (Moderate/Severe)
- Cold heart is irritable  -  rough movement can trigger fatal cardiac arrest
- Keep horizontal; do NOT stand up, walk, massage limbs, or jostle

### 4. Rewarm by Severity

**Mild (alert, shivering):**
- Hot sweet drinks (not alcohol, not caffeine); high-energy food (chocolate, energy bars)
- Heat packs to neck, armpits, groin only (NOT directly on skin  -  wrap in cloth)
- Body-to-body heat in sleeping bag; gentle exercise if fully alert and coordinated

**Moderate (drowsy, shivering stopped):**
- Passive rewarming only  -  sleeping bag, blankets, head covering, ground insulation
- Body-to-body heat (two rescuers skin-to-skin in sleeping bag, focus on core)
- [X] Nothing by mouth - [X] No direct heat (causes afterdrop) - [X] No rubbing limbs
- Monitor pulse/breathing every 5 min; evacuate urgently on stretcher (do not walk)

**Severe (unconscious):**
- Check pulse for 60 seconds (may be very slow and weak)
- If pulse present: handle gently, insulate aggressively, immediate evacuation  -  no active rewarming
- If no pulse: start CPR; continue until hospital, exhaustion, or rigor mortis

## Key DOs and DON'Ts
- [X] Never warm extremities first (causes afterdrop  -  cold blood surges to core)
- [X] Never give alcohol (vasodilation worsens afterdrop)
- [X] Never let victim "sleep it off"  -  hypothermia progresses without treatment
- [OK] Always warm CORE first: neck, armpits, groin

## Special Situations
- Paradoxical undressing: victim removes clothes despite cold  -  prevent and redress immediately
- Immersion (cold water): 1 min cold shock - 10 min useful movement - 1 hr unconsciousness -> get out immediately
- Frostbite + hypothermia: treat hypothermia first; do not rewarm frostbitten extremities until core is warm
- Children: lose heat faster than adults  -  extra insulation critical
- Elderly: may develop hypothermia indoors; may not shiver  -  treat aggressively

## When to Evacuate
- Shivering has stopped, altered mental status, unconscious, or unable to rewarm in field

## Prognosis
- Mild: 100% survival with treatment
- Moderate: 90%+ with proper field care + evacuation
- Severe: 50-80% if CPR started immediately and continued to hospital
