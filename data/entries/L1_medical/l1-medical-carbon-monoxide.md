# Carbon Monoxide

## Why It Matters

- CO is silent and invisible
- Many survival shelter fires cause CO
- Vehicle exhaust in snow is high risk
- Symptoms mimic altitude sickness
- CO detector saves lives reliably
- All enclosed combustion is a risk

## Common CO Sources

- Generator running indoors or close
- Vehicle engine in snow-covered area
- Fire in poorly ventilated shelter
- Propane or white gas stove inside tent
- Charcoal grill in enclosed space
- Coleman stove in vehicle overnight

## Symptoms by Severity

- Mild: headache, dizziness, nausea
- Moderate: severe headache, confusion
  rapid heart rate, weakness
- Severe: loss of consciousness, seizure
- Classic: headache better outside,
  returns on re-entry to space
- Cherry red skin in late stages

## Immediate Treatment

- Remove victim to fresh air immediately
- Remove yourself safely first
- Lay victim down and keep calm
- 100% oxygen if available
- Monitor breathing and pulse
- Recovery position if unconscious
- Evacuate for hyperbaric oxygen

## Prevention in Camp

- Never use combustion inside tents
- Keep generator 20+ feet from shelter
- Crack window when running vehicle heat
- Use stove only in ventilated vestibule
- CO detector in any enclosed shelter
- Check for drift blocking exhaust

## What Can Go Wrong

- Rescuer also incapacitated on entry
- Symptoms confused with altitude or flu
- Oxygen not continued long enough
- Re-entry to source before clearing

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify CO risk in
  3 survival camp scenarios
- Sign of mastery: Scene entry
  protocol for CO with rescue
