# Near Drowning Care

## Why It Matters

- Recovery after submersion is not the end
- Secondary drowning is a real and delayed risk
- Pulmonary edema can develop hours later
- Cold water submersion has better outcomes
- Even brief submersion needs observation
- Most secondary deaths are preventable

## Immediate Rescue Care

- Remove from water, keep horizontal
- Check breathing and pulse
- Begin CPR if no pulse within 10 seconds
- Do not wait for water to clear lungs
- Position: recovery position if breathing
- Keep warm: hypothermia risk is high

## Observation Period

- Minimum 24 hours observation
- Monitor respiratory rate every 30 min
- Any coughing, wheeze, or chest pain:
  increasing observation or evacuate
- Watch for fever (pneumonia developing)
- Mental status changes = evacuate

## Secondary Drowning

- Persistent coughing after rescue
- Increasing shortness of breath
- Chest tightness hours after event
- Unusual fatigue or weakness
- Fever developing within 12 hours
- These can develop up to 24 hours later

## Cold Water Submersion

- Cold water slows metabolism
- Longer submersion times survive with CPR
- Do not stop resuscitation early
- Core temp must be above 90F to stop
- Continue until warm and no pulse
- Hospital rewarming better than field

## What Can Go Wrong

- Victim sent to sleep after recovery
- Secondary drowning missed at home
- CPR stopped too early in cold submersion
- Not watching for respiratory decline

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Set up 24-hour observation
  plan for near-drowning patient
- Sign of mastery: Identify secondary
  drowning signs in case description
