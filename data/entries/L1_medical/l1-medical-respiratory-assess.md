# Respiratory Assessment

## Why It Matters

- Breathing changes precede collapse
- Rate and pattern reveal diagnosis
- Trending over time is key data
- Many emergencies show here first
- Assess before any treatment
- Document rate and time for handoff

## Normal Values

- Adult: 12-20 breaths per minute
- Child: 20-30 breaths per minute
- Infant: 30-60 breaths per minute
- Rhythm: regular and unlabored
- Effort: no muscle use visible
- Sound: quiet, no audible wheeze

## Measuring Rate

- Count over 30 seconds, multiply by 2
- Patient should not know you are counting
- Awareness alters breathing rate
- Watch chest or abdomen rise and fall
- Count each rise as one breath
- Note if regular or irregular

## Distress Signs

- Nasal flaring in children
- Neck and shoulder muscles used
- Intercostal retractions (ribs visible)
- Leaning forward hands on knees
- Unable to complete full sentences
- Cyanosis: blue lips or fingertips

## Abnormal Patterns

- Rapid and shallow: shock, anxiety
- Slow and deep: head injury, drugs
- Cheyne-Stokes: periodic with apnea:
  severe brain or cardiac issue
- Gurgling: fluid in airway, suction
- Stridor: partial obstruction
- Wheezing: bronchoconstriction

## What Can Go Wrong

- Counting breaths incorrectly
- Missing subtle distress signs
- Not trending rate over time
- Confusing anxiety breathing with
  respiratory emergency

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Accurately count and classify
  respiratory rate in 30 seconds
- Sign of mastery: Identify 4 abnormal
  patterns from description alone
