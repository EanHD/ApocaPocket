# Frostbite Field Care

## Why It Matters

- Frostbite staging determines treatment
- Rewarm vs no-rewarm is critical choice
- Refreeze after thaw destroys tissue
- Field rewarm is possible but risky
- Most amputations come from poor care
- Correct staging = correct outcome

## Staging Frostbite

- Frostnip: numb, pale, still pliable
  No ice crystals; rewarm immediately
- Superficial: white/gray, hard surface
  soft underneath; blisters within 24 hr
- Deep: hard all the way through
  no blanch with pressure; woody feel

## Rewarm Decision

- Rewarm IF: warm shelter guaranteed
- Rewarm IF: no walking on it needed
- Do NOT rewarm IF: must walk to exit
- Do NOT rewarm IF: refreeze possible
- Walking on frozen tissue: acceptable
- Walking on thawed tissue: destroys it

## Rewarm Protocol

- Water temperature: 99-102F exactly
- Test on inner wrist first
- Immerse part in water, do not rub
- Duration: 15-30 minutes
- Process is painful, give ibuprofen
- Blisters will form: do not break

## Post-Thaw Care

- Do not rub or massage thawed tissue
- Cover with loose non-stick dressing
- Elevate limb above heart level
- Give ibuprofen 400-600 mg q 6-8 hr
- Keep warm and do not let refreeze
- Evacuate as soon as possible

## What Can Go Wrong

- Dry heat applied (fire, heating pad)
- Refreeze after field rewarm
- Blisters broken and infection follows
- Rubbing during warming process

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Correctly stage and make
  rewarm decision for 3 cases
- Sign of mastery: Maintain exact
  water temperature for rewarm
