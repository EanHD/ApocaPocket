# Diabetes Emergency

## Hypoglycemia

### Recognition
- Mild (55-70 mg/dL): shakiness, sweating, hunger, anxiety — treat within 15 min
- Moderate (40-55 mg/dL): confusion, blurred vision, difficulty speaking — treat within 5-10 min
- Severe (<40 mg/dL): seizures, unconsciousness, coma
- Warning signs: cold sweat, rapid heartbeat, intense hunger, pale skin, irritability

### Rule of 15
1. Give 15g fast-acting carbs: 4 glucose tablets, ½ cup juice, 1 tbsp honey, or 5-6 hard candies
2. Wait 15 minutes
3. If no improvement: repeat 15g carbs
4. If improved: give complex carbs (bread, crackers) to stabilize

### Severe (Unconscious)
- Do NOT give food or drink by mouth — choking risk
- Inject glucagon 1mg IM into thigh if available
- Place in recovery position on side
- Evacuate immediately
- ⚠️ Brain damage in 30-60 min, death in 2-3 hours if untreated


## Hyperglycemia

### Recognition
- 180-250 mg/dL: thirst, frequent urination, fatigue
- 250-400 mg/dL: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity breath
- >400 mg/dL: confusion, rapid breathing, altered consciousness

### DKA Signs
- Fruity/acetone breath
- Kussmaul breathing (rapid, deep)
- Severe dehydration: dry mouth, sunken eyes
- Abdominal pain
- Altered mental status progressing to coma

### Treatment
- Mild (180-250): 8 oz water hourly, avoid carbs, monitor every 2-4 hours
- Moderate/severe (>250): 16-32 oz water hourly, correction insulin if available, no exercise
- DKA: 1-2L water first hour then 500mL/hr, 0.1 units/kg/hr insulin IM, evacuate urgently
- ⚠️ DKA fatal within 24-48 hours without treatment — evacuate


## Insulin Storage

- Unopened: 28 days at room temp (59-86°F)
- Opened: use within 28 days
- Heat >86°F: use within 14 days
- Freezing destroys insulin — do not use frozen

### Storage Methods
- Evaporative cooling: wrap vial in wet cloth in shade (60-70°F)
- Zeer pot: nest small pot in larger, fill gap with wet sand (50-60°F)
- Underground: bury 2-3 feet deep in waterproof container (50-60°F)
- Avoid: direct sunlight, hot cars, metal containers

### Quality Check
- Clear (or uniformly cloudy for NPH) = good
- Clumping, crystals, or discoloration = discard
- When in doubt: use it — degraded is better than none


## Type 1 vs Type 2

- Insulin production — Type 1: none; Type 2: reduced or ineffective
- Insulin required — Type 1: always; Type 2: may not need
- Hypo risk — Type 1: high; Type 2: lower unless on insulin
- DKA risk — Type 1: high; Type 2: lower
- Survival without insulin — Type 1: days to weeks (fatal); Type 2: months to years
- Emergency priority — Type 1: extreme; Type 2: high but more flexible


## Medication Rationing

### Type 1 (Critical)
- Minimum: 0.3-0.5 units/kg/day
- Focus on basal (long-acting) insulin first: 0.2-0.3 units/kg/day
- Reduce carbs to 50-100g/day to cut bolus needs
- Accept higher sugars (180-250 mg/dL) temporarily
- ⚠️ Rationing is dangerous — high sugars lead to DKA

### Type 2
- Metformin: skip doses, focus on diet control
- Sulfonylureas: reduce dose to avoid hypos
- If on insulin: ration as above; Type 2s often survive without


## Evacuation Criteria

- Severe hypoglycemia (unconscious, seizing) with no glucagon
- DKA symptoms: fruity breath, confusion, vomiting, rapid breathing
- Blood sugar >400 mg/dL with altered consciousness
- Type 1 out of insulin and blood sugar climbing
- Unable to keep fluids down

### Evacuation Prep
- Carry 3-7 day insulin supply and syringes
- Bring backup glucose: honey, candy, juice
- Medical ID noting diabetes type
- Glucagon kit if available


## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Giving food or drink to unconscious person — choking risk
- ❌ Eating snow or cold water for hypo — slows absorption
- ❌ Exercising with high blood sugar and ketones — worsens DKA
- ❌ Storing insulin in hot car — degrades in hours
- ❌ Ignoring mild hypo symptoms — progresses to severe in 20-30 min
- ❌ Over-correcting high blood sugar — causes rebound hypo
