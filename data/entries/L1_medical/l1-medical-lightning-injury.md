# Lightning Injury Care

## Why It Matters

- Lightning CPR success rates are high
- Victim carries no charge - touch safely
- Neurological damage may be permanent
- Entry and exit wounds may be subtle
- Cardiac arrest is the primary concern
- Multiple victims: treat pulseless first

## Lightning Triage Rules

- Normal triage is reversed for lightning
- CPR priority: start with apparently dead
- Survivors can wait: they are more stable
- Cardiac arrest from lightning responds
  well to CPR vs other causes
- Automated defib effective if available

## Cardiac Care

- Check pulse immediately
- No pulse: begin CPR immediately
- Continue CPR aggressively
- AED as soon as available
- Lightning cardiac arrest often VF
- Good outcome with fast treatment

## Neuro Assessment

- Note mental status at scene
- Confusion, amnesia very common
- Paralysis may be temporary
- Hearing and vision may be affected
- Kernigs sign for spinal involvement
- Document and trend over time

## Wound Assessment

- Feathering (fern) pattern on skin
- Entry point: often head or shoulder
- Exit point: often feet or ground
  contact point
- Clothes may be burned or blown off
- Tympanic membranes often ruptured
- Inspect ears for blood or fluid

## Secondary Injuries

- Falls from strike may cause spine injury
- Blast may cause internal injuries
- Burns from clothing ignition
- Blunt trauma if thrown by strike
- Spinal precautions if unconscious

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Correct triage of 3 simultaneous
  lightning victims on scene
- Sign of mastery: CPR continued
  aggressively with neurological check
