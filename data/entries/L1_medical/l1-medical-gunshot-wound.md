# Gunshot Wound Care

## Scene Safety First

- Do NOT approach until scene is secure — active shooter: stay back
- Hot zone (active fire): tourniquet only, drag to cover, nothing more
- Warm zone (secured but unstable): minimal care, move to safety
- Cold zone (secured): full assessment and treatment
- Never become a second casualty — assess before approaching

## Stop Bleeding First

- Extremity arterial bleeding: tourniquet 2-3 inches above wound
- Deep wound: pack with hemostatic gauze (QuikClot), then pressure
- Junctional wound (groin/armpit/neck): pack tightly — cannot tourniquet
- Hold pressure minimum 3-5 minutes — do NOT lift to check
- Mark time of tourniquet on patient forehead or tape on limb

## Count All Wounds

- Completely undress patient — cut clothing, check back and sides
- Log-roll to inspect back (protect spine while rolling)
- Even number of wounds = likely through-and-through
- Odd number = bullet retained or multiple projectiles
- Seal chest wounds and pack all bleeding wounds found

## Chest Wounds

- Sucking/hissing wound: seal immediately on patient exhale
- Three-sided seal: tape top/left/right, leave bottom edge open
- Improvised seal: plastic bag or cling wrap, tape 3 sides only
- If breathing worsens after sealing: lift one corner briefly, reseal
- See chest-seal entry for full detail on tension pneumothorax

## Abdominal Wounds

- Cover exposed organs with clean moist dressing — do NOT push back
- Do NOT give food or water (surgery required)
- Apply gentle dressing only — do not pack abdominal wounds
- Surgical emergency even with small external wound entry

## Do NOT Remove

- Do NOT remove embedded bullets or fragments
- Do NOT remove impaled objects — stabilize in place with bulky dressing
- Removal can cause immediate fatal hemorrhage
- Do NOT probe wound to assess depth

## Shock Signs

- Rapid weak pulse, pale cold skin, capillary refill >2 seconds
- Confusion, restlessness, decreasing responsiveness
- Treat: lay flat, legs slightly elevated (not if chest or head injury)
- Keep warm — hypothermia worsens bleeding and shock

## Evacuate Urgently

- Field care is stabilization only — surgery is definitive treatment
- Internal bleeding kills — may be no visible external blood loss
- Exit wound usually larger and more ragged than entry wound
- Both entry and exit wounds need sealing or packing
- Golden hour: survival drops sharply beyond 60 min without surgery
