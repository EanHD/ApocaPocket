# Chemical Burns

## Why It Matters

- Chemical burns worsen without water
- Delay causes deeper tissue damage
- Acid and alkali penetrate differently
- Eye chemical burns are emergencies
- Water is the correct universal first aid
- Neutralizers cause more harm

## Acid vs Alkali Burns

- Acid burns: immediate intense pain
  Self-limiting: protein denatures fast
  Visible brown or black eschar forms
- Alkali burns: less immediate pain
  Continues to penetrate deeper
  Worse long-term tissue damage
- Both treated the same initially

## Immediate Action

- Remove all contaminated clothing first
- Use gloves or avoid direct contact
- Flush with large volumes of water
- 20 minutes minimum for acids
- 30+ minutes for alkalis
- Never use neutralizing agents

## Flush Technique

- Use flowing water, not stagnant
- Brush dry powder off before flushing
- Flush from center of burn outward
- Do not trap chemical with wrapping
- Continue even if pain temporarily worse
- Check for eye involvement simultaneously

## After Flushing

- Cover with clean moist dressing
- Do not apply any creams or oils
- Elevate burned area if possible
- Give pain medication as needed
- Keep patient warm to prevent shock
- Evacuate all significant chemical burns

## What Can Go Wrong

- Flushing too briefly (under 10 min)
- Using baking soda or vinegar
- Forgetting dry powder removal
- Not checking for eye involvement

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Initiate correct flush within
  30 seconds of chemical exposure
- Sign of mastery: 20-minute flush
  with correct technique maintained
