# Mental Status Check

## Why It Matters

- Brain is affected by nearly every
  serious illness or injury
- Mental status is most sensitive monitor
- Changes often precede other signs
- Document to show trend over time
- Consistent language needed for handoff
- AVPU is fast, GCS is more detailed

## AVPU Scale

- A: Alert, oriented, conversing
- V: responds to Voice but not alert
- P: responds to Pain only
- U: Unresponsive to all stimuli
- Document with time at each check
- Any step down = worsening

## AVPU Detail

- Alert: eyes open, answers questions
- Voice: needs prompting to respond
- Pain: sternal rub, nail press
  elicits response but not voice
- Unresponsive: no response to any
  stimulus including pain

## Orientation Test

- Person: do they know their name?
- Place: do they know where they are?
- Time: do they know day/month/year?
- Event: do they know what happened?
- Oriented x4 is normal
- Disoriented to time/place = abnormal

## Concerning Signs

- New confusion in previously alert
- Slurred speech without known cause
- Agitation or combativeness
- Eyes fixed in one direction
- Unequal pupils (check in low light)
- Repetitive questioning

## What Can Go Wrong

- Baseline not established before event
- Language barrier mimics confusion
- Intoxication confused with head injury
- GCS score calculated incorrectly

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Complete AVPU assessment
  and orientation test in 60 seconds
- Sign of mastery: Document and
  trend mental status over 3 checks
