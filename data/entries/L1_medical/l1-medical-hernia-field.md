# Hernia Field Care

## Why It Matters

- Hernias are common, often pre-existing
- Most are manageable in field
- Strangulation is life-threatening
- Field providers see this in remote areas
- Knowing when to evacuate saves lives
- Attempting reduction wrongly causes harm

## Types and Locations

- Inguinal: inner groin, most common
- Umbilical: at navel
- Incisional: through old surgical scar
- Most are pre-existing, not acute
- Exertion and straining can worsen
- Heavy pack weight can precipitate

## Reducible Hernia

- Soft, non-tender bulge
- Pushes back in when pressed gently
- Patient usually knows it is a hernia
- Goes back in when lying flat
- No fever, no severe pain
- Field management: reduce, limit strain

## Reducing a Hernia

- Patient lies flat, hips elevated
- Apply gentle constant pressure
- Do not use force
- May take 10-15 minutes to reduce
- Scrotal or abdominal ice pack aids
- Truss or supportive wrap after

## Strangulated Hernia

- Cannot be pushed back in
- Increasingly painful over hours
- Firm and tender to touch
- Nausea, vomiting developing
- Skin over hernia may darken
- No bowel sounds near hernia
- Evacuate immediately, do not delay

## What Can Go Wrong

- Forcing reduction of strangulated
- Assuming all hernias are simple
- Missing strangulation signs at night
- Continuing activity with symptoms

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Distinguish reducible vs
  strangulated from case description
- Sign of mastery: Safe reduction
  technique demonstrated correctly
