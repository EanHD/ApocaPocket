# Smokeless Fire

## Why It Matters

- Smoke reveals position at distance
- Smell carries 1+ mile in still air
- Low signature critical in some
  tactical or conflict situations
- Also better for cooking efficiency
- Dakota hole improves combustion

## Dakota Hole Design

- Dig two holes 8 inches apart
- Main hole: 12 inches deep, 8 wide
- Air hole: same size, angled to meet
- Tunnel connects both at base
- Air hole feeds oxygen from below
- Results in near-complete combustion

## Trench Fire Design

- Dig trench 12 in long, 6 in wide
- Orient trench along prevailing wind
- Fire burns in trench bottom
- Windward end is open air intake
- Cookpot sits on trench edges
- Flame stays below ground level

## Reducing Smoke Output

- Use only dry seasoned hardwood
- Small fire with max heat is best
- Hardwood coal burns nearly smokeless
- Avoid green wood entirely
- Feed small pieces continuously
- Let wood catch fully before adding

## What Can Go Wrong

- Wet soil collapses hole walls
- Air hole gets blocked by debris
- Wind direction shifts, smoke rises
- Rocky ground prevents digging

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Fire producing no visible
  smoke plume at 50 yards
- Sign of mastery: Dakota hole
  built in under 15 minutes
