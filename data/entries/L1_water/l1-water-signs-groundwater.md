# Finding Groundwater

## Why It Matters

- Surface water may not exist nearby
- Groundwater often inches below sand
- Terrain shapes show water movement
- Vegetation reveals moisture below
- Finding it can save your life
- Look for patterns, not single signs

## Terrain Indicators

- V-shaped valley bottoms hold water
- Outside bends of dry stream beds
- Base of cliff faces and rock walls
- Low points between hills
- Green strips in dry landscapes
- Dark stained rock often shows seep

## Vegetation Clues

- Willows, cottonwood: high water
- Cattails: water within 2 feet
- Arrow weed: water within 20 ft
- Saltgrass: brackish water nearby
- Bright green in yellow landscape
- Insects hovering = moisture below

## Desert-Specific Signs

- Dry watercourses: dig at bends
- Dig at base of concave banks
- Animal trails converging point
- Bird flight direction at dawn/dusk
- Bees or wasps nearby
- Green moss on north-facing rock

## Digging for Water

- Dig hole 12-18 inches at low point
- Wait 30 minutes for water to seep
- Murky water filters through cloth
- Line hole with grass to filter
- Collect seep with cup or sponge
- Always purify before drinking

## What Can Go Wrong

- Seep water is saline or mineral
- Dig too shallow, miss water layer
- Vegetation indicator misidentified
- Standing water source dried up

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify 3 groundwater
  indicators from 100 yards away
- Sign of mastery: Locate seep
  and collect 1 L within 1 hour
