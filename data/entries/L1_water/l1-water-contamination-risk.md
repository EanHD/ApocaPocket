# Water Contamination Risks

Field risk screening guide. Know what to look for
before committing to a source. Clear water is NOT
safe water  -  pathogens and chemicals are invisible.

## Visual Screening

Check these before collecting:
- **Color:** Yellow/brown = tannins or sediment.
  Green = algae. Oily sheen = chemical runoff.
- **Smell:** Sulfur, chemical, sewage, or
  strong organic smell = high risk
- **Foam:** Persistent foam = detergents or
  organic decomposition
- **Clarity:** Should be clear enough to see
  bottom in 1 ft of water. Cloudy = pre-filter first
- **Dead animals** in or near water = contamination
- **No wildlife** using source = possible toxin

## Source Risk Levels

**Lower risk (still treat):**
- Protected developed spring
- Deep covered well (10+ ft)
- Rainwater collected from clean surface
- Fast-moving stream above treeline

**Moderate risk:**
- Flowing stream with no visible settlements
  within 2+ miles upstream
- Shallow hand-dug well

**High risk:**
- Any surface water near settlements,
  farms, or roads
- Still ponds or lakes
- Slow-moving or stagnant water
- Water with visible algae growth

**Avoid entirely if possible:**
- Flood water (contains everything)
- Water downstream from mining/industrial sites
- Bright green or red-colored water (algal bloom)
- Water with dead fish or animals present

## Red Flags

**Find another source  -  do not treat:**
- Fluorescent green, blue-green, or red color
  (cyanobacteria/toxic algal bloom  -  not
  reliably neutralized by boiling or bleach)
- Strong petrochemical smell or visible oil
  (chemical contamination  -  treatment won't help)
- Dead fish or animals concentrated at source
- Obvious industrial or mining discharge nearby
- Bright orange or yellow sediment on streambed
  (heavy metal leaching from rock or mine tailings)

## Downstream Risk

Distance from human activity matters:
- Farms: contamination travels 1-2 miles
  downstream (animal waste, fertilizer)
- Settlements: contamination travels 0.5-3 miles
  (sewage, greywater, solid waste)
- Mining sites: contamination can persist
  for decades  -  entire watershed at risk
- Roads: fuel/oil runoff within 100 ft of road
  can affect adjacent streams

## Biological vs Chemical

**Biological threats** (bacteria, viruses, protozoa):
- Most common threat in field conditions
- Treatable by boiling, bleach, SODIS, or filtration
- Source: human/animal waste, decomposing organic matter

**Chemical threats** (pesticides, heavy metals, fuel):
- Cannot be treated by boiling or bleach
- Require activated carbon filtration or distillation
- Source: agriculture, industry, mining, roads
- If suspected, find a different source entirely

## When in Doubt

- Treat ALL unknown sources  -  biological risk
  is the most likely threat
- Boiling is the gold standard  -  kills everything
- If water smells or tastes wrong after treatment,
  do not drink  -  find another source
- Document sources that gave good/bad results
  for future reference
