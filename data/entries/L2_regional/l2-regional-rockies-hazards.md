# Rockies Regional

**Region:** MT, ID, WY, CO, northern NM (mountain regions, 6,000-14,000+ ft)

## Top Hazards

- Altitude sickness (HAPE/HACE): FATAL above 10,000 ft. Most underestimated killer.
- Avalanches: 100s/year in US. 90% triggered by victim or party. Season Oct-Jun.
- Lightning: Daily afternoon storms Jun-Aug. Above treeline you are the tallest object.
- Hypothermia: Year-round at elevation. Cold + wet = fast death.
- Grizzly bears: Northern Rockies (MT, WY, ID) only. Active May-Oct.

## Altitude Sickness

**AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) — Common**
- Onset: 6-24 hrs after rapid ascent above 8,000 ft.
- Symptoms: Headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness, poor sleep, breathlessness.
- Treatment: Stop ascending. Rest. Hydrate (1+ gal/day). Ibuprofen for headache.
- If worsening after 24 hrs at same elevation: DESCEND IMMEDIATELY.

**HAPE (Pulmonary Edema) — FATAL**
- Fluid accumulates in lungs. Death in 6-12 hrs without action.
- Symptoms: Worsening breathlessness, wet cough, pink frothy sputum, blue lips, gurgling chest.
- Treatment: DESCEND 1,000-3,000 ft NOW. This is the only reliable treatment.
- Oxygen/Gamow bag/nifedipine only if descent is truly impossible.

**HACE (Cerebral Edema) — FATAL**
- Brain swells inside skull. Death in 12-24 hrs without action.
- Symptoms: Severe unrelenting headache, ataxia (loss of coordination), confusion, seizure, coma.
- Ataxia test: Draw a 3-ft line. Walk heel-to-toe. Stepping off = HACE until proven otherwise.
- Treatment: DESCEND IMMEDIATELY. Even at night. Even in a storm. Carry if necessary.

**Prevention**
- Above 8,000 ft: Increase sleeping elevation no more than 1,000-1,500 ft per day.
- Rest day every 3,000 ft gained (sleep same elevation two nights).
- Drink 1+ gallon water daily. No alcohol (suppresses respiration).
- Acetazolamide (Diamox) 125-250mg twice daily if available (start 24 hrs before ascent).

## Avalanches

- 25-30 deaths/year in US. 90% triggered by victim or their party.
- Survival rates: 93% if dug out in 15 min. 30% at 35 min. Under 3% at 130 min.
- Danger slope: 30-45°. Lee (downwind) slopes are wind-loaded and most dangerous.
- Red flags: 12+ inches new snow in 24 hrs, wind loading, rapid warming, whumpfing sounds, shooting cracks.
- Sparse trees = past avalanche path. Avoid.

**If caught in avalanche**
- Jettison pack, poles, skis. Swim/fight toward edge of slide.
- As avalanche slows: Cup hands over face, create air pocket. Expand chest.
- Thrust one arm toward surface. Do not panic. You have 15-30 min of air.

**Companion rescue**
- Mark last-seen point. All survivors switch beacon to SEARCH.
- Follow beacon signal to within 1-2 ft. Probe to pinpoint. Dig from downhill side.
- Clear airway FIRST. Do NOT leave to get help (victim will be dead when you return).

**Required every time:** Avalanche beacon (tested), probe, shovel. All three. No exceptions.

## Lightning

- Rockies pattern: Clear mornings → clouds build 11 AM → storms strike 2-4 PM → clear by evening.
- Rule: Be OFF summits and ridges by 2 PM latest. Descend before storm arrives.
- Warning signs: Towering cumulus, darkening sky, wind increase, temperature drop.
- Immediate danger: Hair stands up or buzzing sounds. Drop metal tools. Move NOW.
- Lightning position: Crouch on balls of feet, feet together, hands over ears, head down.
- Spread group 50+ ft apart. Do not touch others (conducts electricity).
- Safer locations: Dense forest interior (uniform tree height), valley bottoms (not flood zone).
- Death traps: Summits, ridges, isolated trees, open fields, cave entrances, water.
- After last thunder: Wait 30 full minutes before resuming.

**If someone struck:** Safe to touch immediately. CPR if needed (lightning stops heart, can restart).

## Grizzlies

- Range: Glacier, Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Cabinet-Yaak ecosystems only.
- Grizzly ID vs black bear: Grizzly has shoulder hump, dished concave face, 2-4 inch light claws.
- Most dangerous: Sow with cubs (May-Jul), bears on carcass (defending food).
- Prevention: Talk loudly while hiking. Groups of 4+ have no recorded attacks.
- Bear spray: 10+ oz in hip holster (not pack). Practice drawing. Check expiration.
- Food storage: Bear canister or hang 200 ft from camp. Cook 100 ft from sleeping area.

**Grizzly charges:** Most are bluffs. Stand your ground. Deploy spray at 30 ft (2-sec burst).
**Defensive attack (surprised bear):** Play dead flat on stomach. Hands laced behind neck. Legs spread. Silent. Wait 10+ min after bear leaves before moving.
**Predatory attack (stalking, prolonged):** FIGHT BACK. Target eyes and nose. Never stop.

## Mountain Lions

- Attacks rare. Victims are typically children, solo hikers, trail runners.
- Prevention: Groups, keep children close, avoid dawn/dusk, do not run.
- If you see one: Stop. Face it. Look big. Speak firmly. Back away slowly. No running.
- If attacked: FIGHT BACK aggressively. Target eyes and nose. Do NOT play dead.
