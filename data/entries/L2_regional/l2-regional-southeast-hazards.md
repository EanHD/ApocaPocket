# Southeast US Hazards

Region: VA NC SC GA FL AL MS LA AR TN KY

## Seasonal Risks

Jan-Mar: Tornadoes (SE tornado alley peak). Flash flooding. Unexpected hard freezes.
Apr-May: Severe storms and tornadoes. River flooding. Heat beginning to build.
Jun-Nov: Hurricane season. Peak Aug-Oct. Daily afternoon thunderstorms. Extreme heat.
Dec: Cold snaps. Flooding. Tornado risk continues year-round in deep South.

## Top 5 Hazards

### 1. Hurricanes

Category 1: 74-95 mph winds. Trees and power lines down. Days to weeks without power.
Category 3+: catastrophic. Storm surge 9-12 feet. Inland flooding extends 100+ miles.
Decision window: 72 hours when watch is issued. Evacuate Cat 3+ coastal zones. No exceptions for mobile homes.
After storm: floodwater is contaminated. Six inches of fast water knocks an adult down.

### 2. Flooding

Flash flood: 0-6 hour warning. Move to high ground immediately when watch is issued.
Riverine flood: days of warning. Monitor NWS river gauge forecasts.
Turn Around Don't Drown: 2 feet of water floats most vehicles. Do not drive through.
Water treatment: rolling boil 1 minute, or 8 drops unscented bleach per gallon, wait 30 minutes.

### 3. Heat

Heat index 103 degF or higher is dangerous for all outdoor activity.
Heat stroke: hot dry skin, confusion, rapid pulse. Cool immediately by any means. Medical emergency.
Work limit: 20 minutes maximum in direct sun above 100 degF. Rest in shade. Drink 1 quart per hour.
High humidity blocks sweat evaporation. Danger starts at 85 degF with 90% humidity during exertion.

### 4. Venomous Snakes

Southeast has 6 venomous species: copperhead, cottonmouth, timber rattler, eastern diamondback, pygmy rattler, coral snake.
Copperhead: most common bite. Active Apr-Oct. Rocky areas, wood piles, leaf litter near water.
Coral snake rule: "Red touches yellow, kill a fellow. Red touches black, friend of Jack."
Treatment: immobilize bitten limb below heart level. No tourniquet, no suction. Get to hospital.

### 5. Fire Ants

Disturb mound = mass attack in seconds. Each ant stings repeatedly. Venom injected with each sting.
Anaphylaxis risk: carry epi-pen if prior sensitivity known. Benadryl for mild reactions only.
Check sleeping area and shake all clothing before wearing when camping outdoors.
Flood behavior: fire ants form living raft. Can cover anything in standing floodwater.

## Shelter Priorities

Tornado: interior room, lowest floor, away from all windows.
Flooding: elevated structure. Know your floor height relative to local flood stage.
Heat: shade plus ventilation. Damp cloth on neck and wrists reduces core temperature fast.
Hurricane: designated shelter or interior ground-floor room. Never shelter near water.

## Water in SE

Surface water is abundant but contaminated after any storm event. Filter and treat all sources.
Well water: check for floodwater over wellhead before use. Disinfect entire system first.
Post-hurricane: municipal water may be off 1-4 weeks. Store 1 gallon per person per day minimum.
Rainwater collection: filter through cloth, then boil or chemical treat before drinking.

## Food in SE

Game: deer, wild hog, turkey, squirrel, rabbit, catfish, bass, crawfish available year-round.
Edible plants: muscadine grape, blackberry, persimmon, pawpaw, pokeweed shoots (cooked only).
Avoid: water hemlock (deadly, found near water), all unidentified mushrooms.
Wild hogs: year-round, dense in lowlands. Snare or hunt near water sources and wallows.
