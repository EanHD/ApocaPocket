# Rocky Mountains Regional

Region: MT ID WY CO NM mountain zones. Elevation 6,000-14,000+ feet.

## Temperature Reality

Daily swing: 30-50 degF. Hot afternoon, below freezing at night. True even in July at 10,000 feet.
Always pack for freezing temperatures above 9,000 feet regardless of morning forecast.
Lapse rate: 3.5 degF colder for each 1,000 feet of elevation gain. 14,000 ft is 28 degF colder than 6,000 ft.
Carry every trip: full sleep system, rain shell, insulating layer, and extra food for any delay.

## Altitude Sickness

AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness): headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness. Onset 6-24 hours above 8,000 ft.
HACE (cerebral edema): confusion, loss of coordination, altered consciousness. Life-threatening.
HAPE (pulmonary edema): shortness of breath at rest, wet cough, gurgling in chest. Can be lethal in hours.
Treatment for any altitude illness: DESCEND IMMEDIATELY. Even 1,000-2,000 feet can save a life.
Prevention: ascend no more than 1,000 feet per day above 8,000 feet. Rest day every 3,000 feet gained.
Never ascend if symptomatic. Never sleep at a higher altitude than your current symptoms allow.
Ibuprofen 600 mg every 8 hours reduces AMS severity. Acetazolamide (Diamox) for prevention if prescribed.

## Avalanche Risk

Prime conditions: 24-48 hours after 12 or more inches of new snow. Wind-loaded slopes. Peak Feb-Apr.
Avoid: bowl terrain, convex roll-overs, lee slopes at 30-45 degrees after new snow or wind event.
Natural triggers include cornices falling, rapid warming, and rain on existing snowpack.
If caught: swim toward surface. Create air space at face immediately when motion stops.
Beacon: only useful if others in your party also carry one. Probe and shovel are required.
Survival rate: 93% if found within 15 minutes. Drops to 30% after 35 minutes. Dig fast.

## Flash Floods

Desert canyons and mountain drainages: highest risk Jun-Sep (monsoon season).
A storm 10 miles away can send a wall of water through a dry canyon with no local rain visible.
Warning signs: roaring sound upstream, rapidly rising water smell, debris floating in stream.
Escape route: move perpendicular to drainage and uphill immediately. Do not wait.
Never camp in drainage bottoms or dry washes. Camp 20 or more feet above the high-water mark.

## Wildlife

Black bear (most Rockies): make noise while hiking. Store all food in bear canister. No food in tent.
Grizzly bear (MT, WY, ID): more aggressive. Bear spray mandatory. Play dead in a defensive attack.
Mountain lion: stand tall, make noise, throw objects if it approaches. Never run from a mountain lion.
Moose: dangerous when cornered or with calves. Give 100 or more feet of space at all times.
Rattlesnakes: common below 8,000 feet in warm months. Watch foot placement on rocks and logs.

## Mountain Rules

Never begin summit ascent after 10 AM. Afternoon thunderstorms build daily Jun-Aug.
Lightning caught above treeline: crouch low, feet together, away from ridges and lone trees.
Cold water crossings: snowmelt streams run 35-40 degF. Hypothermia risk even in summer. Unbuckle pack straps before crossing.
UV intensity: 50% more than sea level at 12,000 feet. Severe sunburn in 15 minutes without protection.
