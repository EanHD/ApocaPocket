# Alaska Regional Survival

Region: All Alaska. 663,300 sq mi. Coastal rainforest, interior taiga, arctic tundra, subarctic.

## Climate Zones

Interior (Fairbanks): subarctic continental. Summer highs 80-90 degF. Winter lows -40 to -60 degF. Extreme swings.
Arctic (North Slope): tundra. Summer 40-50 degF. Winter -20 to -50 degF. Permafrost year-round. Near-zero sun Nov-Jan.
Southcentral (Anchorage): maritime influence. Milder winters. Heavy snow possible. Seismic and volcanic risk.
Southeast (Juneau, Sitka): coastal rainforest. Year-round 30-60 degF. 80-200 inches of rain per year.
SW and Aleutians: wind-dominated. Dense fog. Williwaws (sudden violent gusts). Highly unpredictable.
Daylight: Interior has 21.8 hours of sun on June 21. Near zero hours sun Nov-Jan. Plan navigation accordingly.

## Subsistence Food

Salmon: five species. Run Jul-Sep depending on river system. Net or dip net if licensed. Dry or smoke for storage.
Moose: primary large game. 800-1,400 lbs. One animal yields 400+ lbs of meat. Hunt in Sep-Oct.
Caribou: massive herds in interior and arctic. Follow known migration routes. Fat is critical for cold-weather calories.
Berries: blueberry, crowberry, salmonberry, cloudberry. Peak Aug-Sep. High caloric density.
Ptarmigan: abundant, easily snared, year-round. Plumage turns white in winter.
Fish under ice: Arctic char, grayling, northern pike, burbot catchable through ice year-round.
Spring plants: fireweed shoots, cow parsnip (cooked only), spruce tips (vitamin C source).
Toxic: water hemlock (deadly, grows near water), death camas, wild parsnip look-alikes. Learn before foraging.

## Dog Sled vs Machine

Snowmachine (snowmobile):
- Speed: 20-60 mph. Better for long distances with cargo.
- Requires fuel. Fails mechanically at extreme cold (-40 degF and below).
- Always carry a survival bag and basic tools. Never travel alone by snowmachine.

Dog team:
- Self-fueling. Dogs eat fish, moose, commercial kibble stored at caches.
- Speed: 5-10 mph typical. Up to 100 miles per day for experienced teams.
- Reliable at any temperature. Dogs will not cross uncertain ice willingly.
- Cannot be stranded by mechanical failure. Preferred for remote multi-day travel.

Rule: snowmachine for speed and shorter runs. Dog team for extreme cold or extended remote travel.

## Bush Planes

90% of Alaska communities have no road access. Bush plane is ambulance, supply chain, emergency response.
Emergency use: most pilots will respond to a genuine emergency request via radio.
Air-to-air radio: 122.8 MHz or 123.3 MHz (CTAF). Transmit on 121.5 MHz in extreme emergency.
Ground signals (visible from air): X = need medical help. V = need food and water. Arrow = travel direction.
Make signals at least 10 feet across in an open clearing. Use rocks, logs, or stamped snow.
If injured and waiting for rescue: stay near shelter. Signal regularly. Conserve energy between signals.

## Emergency Contacts

Alaska State Troopers: 911 where cell service exists. VHF or HF radio relay in bush.
Coast Guard District 17 (Southeast AK marine): 907-463-2000.
AFRCC Rescue Coordination Center: 1-800-420-7230 (Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson).
Satellite communicator (SPOT, Garmin inReach): works statewide. Register and test before any trip.
ELT (Emergency Locator Transmitter): activate only in genuine emergency. 406 MHz monitored 24/7.
File a float plan with a trusted contact before every trip. Include route, return date, and destination contact.
