# Windmill Basics

## Windmill Types

- Post mill: whole structure rotates to face wind
- Tower mill: only cap rotates
- Smock mill: octagonal wooden tower
- Horizontal axis: traditional European type
- Vertical axis (Savonius): simpler, any wind dir
- Farm wind pump: multiple blades, familiar type

## Wind Requirements

- Minimum working wind: 4 m/s (8 mph)
- Optimal: 8-12 m/s (18-27 mph)
- Too much wind: feather sails or stop
- Site: hilltop, coastal, open plain
  Avoid: valley bottoms, forest hollows
- Wind mapping: observe grass and tree bend

## Sail Design

- 4-6 sails most common
- Cloth sails on wooden frame (traditional)
- Sail area: more area = more power in light wind
- Pitch angle: 15-20 degrees to wind
- Sails turn into wind via tail vane or manual
- Reef (reduce) sails in high winds

## Power Output Estimate

- Wind power = 0.5 x air density x swept area x V3
- Practical: 3m diameter mill, 8 m/s wind
  = approximately 200-500 watts
- Enough for: grinding grain 2-4 hours
  pumping water 50-100 liters/hour
- Intermittent: store mechanical work
  (raise water to tank when wind blows)

## Gear Reduction

- Wind shaft turns at 20-40 RPM
- Millstone needs 120-150 RPM
- Gear up 4-6:1 ratio
- Wooden bevel gears traditional
- Wood teeth in wood wheel: easy to replace
- Lantern pinion: wooden pegs in iron frame

## Mill Construction

- Stone base with iron axle bearing at top
- Rotating wooden cap holds sail shaft
- All structural wood: traditional, sufficient
- Main shaft: large-diameter hardwood log
- Bearings: iron in wooden housing
- Total height: 8-15m typical

## Safety Shutoff

- Automatic: fantail turns sail out of wind
- Manual: brake on main shaft
- Brake band: iron on wooden drum
- Pull brake in storm immediately
- Sails can fold: reef cloth to reduce area
