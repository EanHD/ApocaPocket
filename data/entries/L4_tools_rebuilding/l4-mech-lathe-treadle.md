# Treadle Lathe

## Vs Pole Lathe

- Pole lathe: reciprocating (alternating direction)
  Cut only half the time
- Treadle lathe: continuous rotation
  Cuts on every stroke
- Flywheel maintains momentum between strokes
- Faster production, better finish quality
- More complex to build

## Drive Mechanism

- Foot treadle connects to crank on flywheel
- Push treadle down: crank rotates
- Flywheel stores energy through dead center
- Drive belt from flywheel to lathe shaft
- Lathe shaft: between-center working zone
- Pulley on shaft: various speeds possible

## Flywheel Design

- Heavy = more momentum = smoother rotation
- Diameter: 60-100cm typical
- Material: heavy wood, or wooden spokes
  with iron rim for maximum weight
- Mass at rim is most effective
- Axle: smooth iron pin in wood bearings
- Weight: 15-50 kg for good momentum

## Headstock/Tailstock

- Headstock: driven end, connects to belt
- Tailstock: adjustable support, other end
- Both have pointed centers for work piece
- Center-to-center distance = max work length
- Standard: 80-120cm between centers
- Tool rest: adjustable height and position

## Belt Options

- Leather belt: traditional, works well
- Round leather (lace): for small lathes
- Flat leather: for large pulleys
- Cord: twisted fiber rope works
- Belt tension: tight enough to not slip
  Loose enough to not overload bearings

## Speed Control

- Change pulley size to change speed
- Large flywheel pulley / small lathe pulley = fast
- Equal pulleys = 1:1 ratio
- Different pulley sizes: step lathe speeds
- Slow speed: roughing large diameter work
- Fast speed: finishing small spindles

## Building Sequence

- Build frame and bench first
- Fit flywheel and crank mechanism
- Test drive mechanism before adding lathe
- Add headstock and tailstock last
- Set tool rest height: on center of work
