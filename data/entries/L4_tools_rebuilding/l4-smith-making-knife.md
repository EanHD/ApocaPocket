# Making a Knife

## Stock Removal

- Start with file, saw blade, leaf spring
- Remove metal by grinding to desired shape
- No forge required for stock removal
- Files: already high carbon steel, hardened
  Anneal first if grinding is very difficult
- Leaf spring: often 5160 spring steel

## Annealing (Softening)

- Heat to bright orange (950C)
- Bury in sand or ash to cool very slowly
- Overnight cooling is ideal
- Now steel is soft and easy to grind
- Skip this step if working a soft blank
- Never quench to anneal - hardens instead

## Layout and Grinding

- Draw blade outline on steel with scribe
- Mark center line on blade edge
- Grind profile with angle grinder or file
- Shape: point, edge bevel, tang (handle)
- Flat grind: simple, strong geometry
- Hollow grind: requires wheel, very sharp
- Go slow, do not overheat the steel

## Avoiding Heat Damage

- Keep blade cool with water dips
- Stop if metal turns blue at edge
  (temper color = too hot)
- Blue steel is soft - must reharden area
- Grind in short passes
- Keep blade moving, do not linger in one spot

## Hardening

- Heat entire blade evenly to non-magnetic
  (magnet test: blade no longer attracted)
- Non-magnetic = correct hardening temp
- Quench edge-down in canola oil (warm oil)
- Plunge straight down, move back-forth gently
- Do not quench water (too fast, cracks)
- Blade should now be glass-hard and brittle

## Tempering

- Sand off any scale from quench
- Polish to bright metal (see color easier)
- Heat gently in oven or on metal plate
- Watch for color creeping from spine to edge
- Straw yellow at edge = general purpose
- Light blue at edge = flexibility/toughness
- Edge should reach straw before full blue

## Handle and Finishing

- Drill handle hole(s) in tang
- Shape handle wood, antler, or bone
- Pin or epoxy handle scales to tang
- Final edge: file or stone to 20 degrees
- Strop on leather for final sharpness
