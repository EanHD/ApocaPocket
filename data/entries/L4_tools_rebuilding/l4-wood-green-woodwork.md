# Green Woodworking

## Why Work Green Wood

- Green wood cuts like soft cheese vs dry wood
- Drawknife, axe, froe work 3-5x faster
- No electricity required at any stage
- Leg-and-rung chairs: joints lock as wood dries
- Fresh timber readily available post-collapse
- All traditional pre-industrial woodwork

## Shrinkage Principle

- Wood shrinks as moisture content decreases
- Shrinkage strongest across grain
- Minimal shrinkage along grain
- Green tenon in dry mortise: tenon shrinks, loose
- Dry tenon in green mortise: mortise shrinks ONTO
  tenon as it dries = mechanical lock, no glue

## What to Make

- Windsor chairs: bent backs, turned legs
- Ladder-back chairs: all rived and shaped
- Baskets: thin riven splints
- Handles: rive, shape, dry
- Bowls: rough-turn green, dry, finish-turn
- Fences and structures: posts and rails

## Sourcing Green Wood

- Fallen trees: use within weeks before drying
- Coppice stools: harvested on rotation
- Any freshly cut timber
- Spring and summer: wood at highest moisture
- Winter wood: drier but still workable
- Keep cut wood in shade to slow drying

## Tools for Green Work

- Froe: riving/splitting
- Axe: rough shaping
- Drawknife + shave horse: rapid shaping
- Spokeshave: curves and detail
- Pole lathe: turning while green
- Brace and bit: drilling (easier in green wood)

## Coppicing

- Cut hardwood to stump at ground level
- Multiple straight shoots grow from stump
- Harvest every 7-15 years per species
- Oak, hazel, ash, willow all coppice
- Stool lives indefinitely (hundreds of years)
- Produces poles, handles, fuel, baskets

## Drying After Shaping

- Slow dry: store in shade, air circulation
- Cover end grain (tape, mud) to slow end drying
- Do not force-dry with heat (splits)
- Furniture parts: dry slowly before assembly
- Handles: dry under tension (bow, clamp)
