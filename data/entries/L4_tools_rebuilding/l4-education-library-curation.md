# Library Curation

**One library = civilization restart. No library = each generation re-learns from scratch.**

## Medicine Books

- Where There Is No Doctor (Werner)  -  diagnostics, treatments, surgery with illustrations; 90% of lay medical knowledge
- Where There Is No Dentist (Dickson)  -  extraction, filling, pain management; dental infection = life-threatening
- Emergency War Surgery (NATO Handbook)  -  trauma, amputations, wound management; worst-case medicine
- The Merck Manual (Professional Edition)  -  comprehensive disease reference, symptoms, treatments
- PDR for Herbal Medicines + Peterson Field Guide to Medicinal Plants  -  no pharma = plant medicine is primary care

## Agriculture Books

- Encyclopedia of Country Living (Emery)  -  farming, gardening, animal husbandry, food preservation; 900+ pp
- Seed to Seed (Ashworth)  -  seed saving, pollination, harvest, storage; genetic preservation critical
- Permaculture: A Designer's Manual (Mollison)  -  sustainable food systems beyond short-term survival
- Four-Season Harvest (Coleman)  -  year-round food production, cold frames, season extension
- Ball Complete Book of Home Preserving  -  canning, fermenting, drying, smoking

## Engineering Books

- Machinery's Handbook (30th ed.)  -  2,800 pp: metalworking, machining, materials, gears, math
- The Way Things Work (Macaulay)  -  visual mechanical principles; extrapolate to new inventions
- The Art of Electronics (Horowitz & Hill)  -  restore electricity = force multiplier for everything
- Engineering in the Ancient World (Landels)  -  proven low-tech: Roman cranes, Greek water screws
- Build Your Own Metal Working Shop from Scrap (Gingery, 7 vols)  -  rebuild machine tools from salvage

## General Reference

- Offline Wikipedia (Project Kiwix, ~100 GB compressed)  -  6+ million articles; store on multiple hard drives
- Encyclopedia Britannica (32 vols, 15th ed.)  -  survives power grid failure; paper endures indefinitely
- The Federalist Papers  -  governance and law for restarting functional society
- Guns, Germs, and Steel (Diamond)  -  why societies succeed or fail; avoid repeating history's mistakes

## Preservation

Ideal: 60-70 degF constant, 30-50% RH, zero light (UV destroys organic materials).
Achievable: underground storage (caves, cellars), sealed ammo cans or waterproof totes + silica gel packs.

Pest threats: silverfish, cockroaches, rodents, termites  -  all eat paper and bindings.
Prevention: seal entry points, sticky/snap traps, lavender/cedar sachets, diatomaceous earth at perimeter.

Fire protection: store duplicate copies in separate buildings miles apart. Sand smothers fire without destroying books. Sprinklers damage books  -  use sand or early intervention only.

## Organization

Dewey Decimal simplified for restart scenario:
- 1XX: General knowledge (reference, philosophy, history)
- 2XX: Survival (medicine, food, water)
- 3XX: Building (engineering, construction, tools)
- 4XX: Food (agriculture, preservation, cooking)
- 5XX: Skills (crafts, trades, manufacturing)
- 6XX: Community (governance, law, social organization)

Card catalog: one 3x5 index card per book  -  author card, title card, subject card. Each contains call number, author, title, brief description. Filed alphabetically by drawer. Find any book in 30
seconds without power.

## Copy & Reproduce

Hand copying: 1-2 pages/day per scribe. Assign rotating scribes, proofread against original, distribute copies to allied communities.
Woodblock printing: carve page (mirror image) into wood block; ink and press paper. One block = one page; cannot rearrange type.
Gutenberg press (movable type): screw press + cast lead type + linseed oil ink = 200-500 pages/day (250x hand copying). High construction difficulty; exponential payoff.

## Digital Storage

- 1 hard drive holds 1 million books compressed; instant duplication; full-text search
- Vulnerabilities: EMP (use Faraday cage), bit rot (refresh every 5-10 yr), power dependency
- Best practice: 3+ copies, separate locations, offline only, open formats (PDF/TXT/HTML), test yearly
- Hybrid system (digital + physical) = best resilience

## The Librarian Role

- Curates and prioritizes acquisition (what to save first)
- Organizes for findability  -  accessible knowledge = usable knowledge
- Trains others to use the collection
- Monitors conditions and repairs damage
- One well-organized library = 1,000x knowledge multiplier over oral tradition alone
