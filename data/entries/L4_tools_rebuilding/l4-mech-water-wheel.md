# Water Wheel Design

## Types of Water Wheels

- Undershot: paddles hit moving current
  Efficiency 20-30%, needs only current
- Breastshot: water hits mid-height
  Efficiency 50-60%, needs moderate head
- Overshot: water falls from above into buckets
  Efficiency 60-80%, needs 2m+ head
- Pitchback: overshot variant, reversing
- Turbine: enclosed, highest efficiency

## Choosing Wheel Type

- Undershot: gentle stream, low banks
- Overshot: high banks with drop, best power
- Calculate available head (vertical drop)
  Less than 0.5m: undershot only
  0.5-3m: breastshot or overshot
  3m+: overshot most efficient

## Wheel Construction

- Diameter: 3-5m typical
- Width: 0.5-2m depending on flow
- Buckets: sheet metal or carved wood
  Bucket shape: curved, 20-30% of diameter
- Frame: timber or stone pillars
- Axle: hardwood log or iron shaft

## Power Output

- Power = flow rate x head x efficiency
- 1 liter/second x 1m head x 80% = 8 watts
- 10 liters/second x 2m head = 160 watts
- Enough for: grinding grain, sawing,
  lifting water, powering bellows
- Small stream (50L/sec) at 2m head = 800W

## Millpond and Sluice

- Millpond: reservoir upstream, steady flow
- Sluice gate: controls flow to wheel
- Build millpond when stream flow variable
- Dam size: enough to run 4-8 hours of milling
- Let pond refill overnight
- Sluice: simple sliding gate in race

## Power Transmission

- Wheel axle to gear or pulley
- Wooden gears adequate for millstone drive
- Drive shaft to separate building if needed
- Millstone: grindstone on horizontal shaft
- 1 overshot wheel drives 1-2 millstones
- 5-8 RPM wheel: gear up to 120 RPM stone

## Maintenance

- Check bearings weekly for wear
- Grease axle bearings with animal fat
- Replace damaged paddles or buckets
- Clear debris caught in wheel
- Inspect dam for seepage after each rain
