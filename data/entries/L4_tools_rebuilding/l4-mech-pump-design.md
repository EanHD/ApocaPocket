# Hand Pump Design

## Types of Hand Pumps

- Suction (lift) pump: creates vacuum above water
  Atmospheric pressure pushes water up
  Maximum: 7-8 meters of suction head
- Force pump: piston pushes water under pressure
  Works at any depth, delivers under pressure
- Pitcher pump: common suction pump design
- Deep well: force pump with long rod

## Suction Pump

- Upstroke: piston rises, creating low pressure
- Inlet valve opens: water enters from below
- Downstroke: piston descends, inlet valve closes
- Outlet valve opens: water exits above piston
- Each stroke delivers one cylinder volume
- Priming: must fill with water to start

## Materials

- Cylinder: tight-fitting for piston
  Wood, PVC pipe, bamboo internodes
- Piston: leather cup seal works well
  Wrap leather over wood disk, form cup
- Valves: flat leather flap over hole
  Opens one direction, closes other
- Handle: lever arm for force advantage

## Wood Pump Build

- Bore cylinder from log (auger, heated rod)
- Smooth interior with oiled rag
- Leather piston expands when wet, seals
- Inlet and outlet valve bores drilled
- Leather flap valves cut, placed over holes
- Top and bottom end caps secured with pins

## Bamboo Pump

- Large internodes for cylinder
- Bore through joint with pointed stick
- Bamboo piston: smaller diameter wrapped
  with cloth or leather for seal
- Connect joints with clay or tar seal
- Very fast to construct, limited life span
- Excellent emergency or temporary water lift

## Deep Well Pump

- Pump body at water level (bottom of well)
- Long rod from piston to surface
- Operator works lever connected to rod
- Water exits through separate pipe
  (not through piston this time)
- Can deliver water to height above pump
- Used in mines since Roman era

## Priming the Pump

- Fill cylinder with water before first stroke
- Pour water into pump body from top
- Work handle rapidly: creates suction
- If no water appears: check all joints for air
- Air leaks at joints prevent suction
- Hemp fiber and pitch seal all joints
