# Charcoal Forge

## Why Charcoal

- Available anywhere wood grows
- No mining, no supply chain
- Burns hotter than wood alone
- Lower sulfur than coal (better for steel)
- Historically the universal forge fuel
- Can be made on-site from hardwood

## Making Forge Charcoal

- Use hardwood (oak, hickory, maple best)
- Cone pit or retort method
- Slow-burn without air: pyrolysis
- Target: black shiny char, not white ash
- Good forge charcoal rings like glass
- Crumbles to powder: over-burned
- See biochar entry for production method

## Simple Forge Designs

- Brake drum forge: car drum as firepot
  Set in sand or clay, connect air pipe below
- Clay box forge: build walls with clay/brick
  Leave air hole in bottom for pipe
- Stone and clay: stack stone, clay mortar
  Firepot center, air pipe from side
- Metal plate: weld box, drill tuyere hole

## Air Supply Options

- Hand bellows: traditional, no power
- PVC pipe + check valve bellows: cheap
- Hair dryer (battery): quick setup
- Shop vac: too much air (use valve)
- Foot bellows: hands-free option
- Goal: steady controlled airstream

## Tuyere Design

- Tuyere: air inlet pipe to fire
- Should enter from side or below
- Angle slightly upward into fire
- Pipe: iron, not plastic
- Size: 2-3cm diameter for small forge
- Clinker trap below tuyere ideal

## Managing Charcoal Fire

- Charcoal burns fast, replenish often
- Mound charcoal over and around steel
- Keep steel in reducing zone
  (below top of fire, not in flames)
- Oxidizing zone (above fire): causes scale
- Cover with fresh charcoal before blasting air
- Steel should be surrounded, not exposed

## Forge Maintenance

- Clean clinker (ash glass) regularly
- Clinker smothers fire and sticks to steel
- Remove with tongs when fire cool
- Clay and brick repairs: easy with clay
- Metal forges: weld cracks closed
