# Manual Lathe

## Pole Lathe Principle

- Work piece spins with foot pedal push
- Rope wraps around work piece
- Rope attached to flexible overhead pole
- Press pedal: piece spins toward you (cut)
- Release pedal: pole springs back, reverses
- Cutting happens only on the forward stroke

## Pole Lathe Build

- Two uprights with mortise-and-tenon structure
- Poppets (uprights) hold work piece
- Centers: pointed metal rods in poppets
- Adjustable gap for different lengths
- Spring pole overhead: 3-4m flexible wood
  or a long springy branch works
- Foot treadle: plank on pivot at one end

## Bow Lathe

- Portable version with hand-held bow
- Bow holds rope that wraps the work
- Helper pumps bow, operator cuts
- Or single operator: one hand cuts,
  other hand operates bow
- Slower than pole lathe but fully portable
- Used since ancient Egypt

## Tool Rest and Tools

- Tool rest: horizontal bar at working height
- Gouge: curved blade for hollowing bowls
- Skew chisel: angled blade for detail
- Parting tool: cutting off completed piece
- All tools ground with flat bottom edge
- Rest against tool rest, pivot to cut

## Green vs Dry

- Green wood: easy to cut, warps as dries
- Turn green, rough shape, let dry, finish turn
- Dry wood: stable, harder to cut
- Bowls: turn green for speed, accept warp
- Furniture parts: dry wood for stability
- Handles: turn green, dry under tension

## Centering the Work

- Find center of each end with diagonal lines
- Mark center with awl
- Points must be exactly opposite
- Off-center = wobble and vibration
- Check by spinning by hand before cutting
- Should feel smooth, not jerky

## Basic Turning Sequence

- Mount between centers tightly
- True up outside to cylinder (gouge)
- Shape profile from center outward
- Part off with parting tool
- Clean up center hole (cup chuck method)
- Sand or burnish with shavings while spinning
