# Inclined Plane

## Principle

- Inclined plane (ramp): trade distance for force
- MA = ramp length / rise height
- Long gentle ramp = high MA = less force
- Short steep ramp = low MA = more force
- Total work done is the same either way
- Choice: more force or more distance

## Calculating MA

- Ramp length 3m, rise height 1m: MA = 3
- 300kg load: need 100kg force on ramp
- Ramp 6m, same height: MA = 6
  Need only 50kg force
- Gradient 1:4 = every 1m up, 4m along
- Gentler ramp: more material, but easier task

## Friction Effect

- Real ramp: friction opposes movement
- Rough surface: needs extra force for friction
- Smoother surface: closer to theoretical MA
- Grease or water on surface: reduces friction
- Rollers: great friction reduction
- Logs as rollers: ancient standard method

## Rolling vs Sliding

- Rolling an object uses 20x less energy
  than sliding same object
- Wheel and axle is best: persistent rolling
- Log rollers: move them from back to front
- Round poles as rollers: move heavy stones
- Used for pyramid construction
- Still the most practical method without wheels

## Building a Ramp

- Materials: planks, earth, stone, log
- Secure base: ramp must not slide under load
- Support under ramp: prevent flexing/collapse
- Width: wider is more stable
- Surface: rough enough for traction
  but smooth enough to slide or roll

## Practical Ramp Uses

- Loading heavy items to wagon or platform
- Moving stone or timber into position
- Building access to elevated foundations
- Gradual slope for wheeled loads
- Slope for gravity water flow
- Access for disabled or injured

## Screw Principle

- Screw = inclined plane wrapped around cylinder
- Thread pitch = very shallow inclined plane
- Very high MA (hundreds to one)
- Used in screw press, jack screw, vise
- Each turn: tiny movement with great force
