# Gears and Ratios

## Gear Ratio Basics

- Gear ratio = driver teeth / driven teeth
- Driver: the input gear (powered)
- Driven: the output gear (load)
- More teeth on driven = slower, more torque
- Fewer teeth on driven = faster, less torque
- 20-tooth driver, 40-tooth driven = 2:1 ratio
  Output: half speed, double torque

## Calculating Output

- Speed ratio = driver teeth / driven teeth
- Torque ratio = driven teeth / driver teeth
- Example: 10-tooth driving 40-tooth
  Speed: 10/40 = 0.25 (1/4 input speed)
  Torque: 40/10 = 4x input torque
- Power conserved (minus friction losses)

## Gear Train

- Chain of gears multiplies ratio
- Compound gear: two gears on same shaft
  First pair reduces, second pair reduces more
- Total ratio = product of all individual ratios
- Mill gear trains: 5-10 RPM wind shaft
  to 120-150 RPM millstone
- Achieved through compound reduction

## Wooden Gears

- Teeth (cogs) mortised into rim
- Wood teeth: hornbeam, apple, hard maple
- Replace worn teeth without whole gear
- Tooth form: flat-topped (simple to make)
- Proper tooth profile: involute (complex)
- For low-speed: simple flat teeth work

## Lantern Pinion

- Traditional small gear (pinion)
- Two discs with round pins between them
- Pins engage teeth of larger gear
- Easy to make: drill holes, drive round rods
- Wears evenly, easy to replace pins
- Standard in medieval and colonial mills

## Bevel Gears

- Change rotation axis by 90 degrees
- Wind shaft (horizontal) to millstone (vertical)
- Wood bevel gears carved by hand
- Template: transfer same tooth spacing around
- Beveled teeth must be angled correctly
- Test fit slowly before full-speed running

## Lubrication

- All wooden gears: grease with animal fat
- Reduces wear and noise
- Reapply every 50-100 hours of operation
- Metal gears on wooden: metal wears wood
  Put wood against metal (replaceable)
