# Axe Head Forging

## Overview

- Axe is among the most complex forge projects
- Requires: drifting, forge welding, tempering
- Master these skills on simpler pieces first
- Result: a working axe that outlasts any human
- Good axe steel: 1045-1080 medium-high carbon

## Starting Material

- Round bar or square bar, 4-5cm diameter
- Or cut from thick plate steel
- Length: 15-20cm for typical felling axe
- Weight target: 1-2 kg finished head
- Mild steel body, high-carbon bit preferred
- Or use all medium-carbon for simplicity

## Poll Forming

- Heat to orange, draw out and shape
- Form rough axe shape: thicker at center
- Thin toward bit (cutting edge end)
- Poll (back) remains thick for poll use
- Work hot, large hammer to move metal fast
- Check symmetry frequently

## Drifting the Eye

- Eye = handle hole
- Punch starter hole with round punch
- Use eye drift (oval tapered tool)
- Drive drift from both sides alternately
- Eye shape: tapered oval larger at top
  (wedge locks handle in)
- Work hot, frequent reheats

## Forging the Bit Bevel

- Bit = cutting edge of axe
- Draw out thin from body toward edge
- Work metal toward cutting edge
- Bevel: 20-25 degree angle each side
- Edge should be 3-5mm thick before hardening
- Symmetric grinding is critical

## High-Carbon Bit Weld

- For longest edge life: weld in harder steel
- Split bit end at center, open for insert
- Insert piece of high-carbon (file steel)
- Forge weld closed around insert
- Edge steel hardened, body stays tough
- Technique used in all traditional axes

## Heat Treatment

- Heat bit area to non-magnetic (body stays cool)
- Quench edge in warm oil
  (water cracks unless done precisely)
- Temper to purple-blue (tough for impact)
- Grind to final bevel
- Strop and polish cutting edge

## Handle Fitting

- Select straight-grain ash or hickory handle
- Test fit: head should be tight on shoulder
- Drive wooden wedge into kerf of handle top
- Metal wedge cross-wise on top of wood wedge
- Test: strike hard surface, head must not fly
