# Making Nails and Bolts

## Nail-Making Overview

- Simplest and most useful smithing product
- Traditionally paid apprentice's first work
- Round or square shank with tapered point
- Head formed by upsetting cut rod
- Stock: square rod 5-8mm for common nails
- Once learned, very fast to produce

## Nail-Making Steps

- Heat end of square rod to orange
- Draw out point: 4-6 cm taper
- Cut at nail-length above taper
  (leave in hardy tool notch)
- Do not separate yet: leave 3mm attached
- Reheat head end
- Insert into header hole, upset head
  by striking exposed end
- Twist free: nail complete

## Making a Header Tool

- Drill or punch hole in thick plate
- Hole slightly smaller than nail shank
- Countersink top of hole slightly
- Support plate in hardy hole
- Nail inserts from below, head formed above
- Make header holes for different nail sizes

## Bolt Making

- Similar to nail but with threaded end
- Form bolt blank: draw out, shape head
- Threading: use tap and die set
- Alternatively: cut threads with triangular file
  (difficult, time-consuming, but works)
- Annealed mild steel cuts threads easily
- Bolt blank must be round and consistent

## Threading Without Dies

- Wrap wire around bolt blank to check pitch
- File thread form around circumference
- Very slow but produces working threads
- Nut: punch or drill hole, file to fit
- File-cut threads: 2-4 hours per bolt
- Use for non-critical applications

## Batch Production

- Heat multiple nail blanks at once
- Work quickly through sequence
- Use chain to carry multiple pieces
- Iron nail production: 100-200 per hour
  (skilled colonial-era smith)
- Beginner target: 10-20 per hour
- Build header templates for consistency

## Wooden Peg Option

- Difficult to make by hand
- Wooden pegs (trunnels) substitute in timber
- Wedged wooden pegs work for furniture
- Reserve metalwork for structural fasteners
