# Steel Quenching

## How Hardening Works

- Steel heated above critical temperature
  reorganizes its crystal structure
- Rapid cooling (quench) freezes structure
  in martensite phase = very hard
- Slow cooling returns to soft pearlite
- The faster the quench, the harder the steel
- Harder quench = more distortion risk

## The Magnet Test

- Heat steel past critical temp = non-magnetic
- Touch magnet to steel while heating
- When magnet no longer attracts: right temp
- Temperature: ~760-800C (cherry-orange)
- Critical temperature varies by steel alloy
- Higher carbon steel: slightly lower temp

## Quench Media

- Brine (water + salt): fastest, most cracks
  Use only for mild steel
- Water: fast, cracks high-carbon steel
- Canola/vegetable oil: slower, less distortion
  Best for high-carbon and tool steel
- Motor oil: similar to vegetable oil
- Air: slowest, used for air-hardening steels
  (D2, H13 tool steels)

## Quench by Steel Type

- Mild steel (re-bar, railroad): brine or water
- High carbon (files, springs): warm oil
- Spring steel (leaf spring, 5160): warm oil
- Unknown steel: try oil first (safer)
- Warm oil (50C) reduces distortion further

## Quench Technique

- Heat steel to non-magnetic
- Pull from fire smoothly
- Plunge edge or tip first into quench
- Move back and forth slowly (prevents steam)
- Do not remove until cool enough to touch
- Blade should be glass-hard and brittle

## Checking Hardness

- File test immediately after quench
- File should skate off without biting
- File bites: not hard enough
  Causes: wrong steel, wrong temp,
  wrong quench, decarburization (scale)
- Correct: high-pitched ring when tapped

## Preventing Distortion

- Quench edge-down for blades
- Keep movement smooth and consistent
- Warm oil reduces thermal shock
- Symmetric quench (even sides)
- Clamp between steel plates while cooling
  (extreme measures for flat stock)
