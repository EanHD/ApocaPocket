# Steam Bending Wood

## Why Steam Bending

- Bent parts are stronger than sawn curves
- Grain follows bend, not cut across it
- No wood wasted cutting away waste
- Complex curves achievable
- Traditional for: Windsor chairs, boats,
  barrels, tool handles, sleds, skis

## Best Woods for Bending

- White oak: excellent, traditional
- Ash: very good, springy, resilient
- Elm: good, strong bends
- Hickory: excellent for handles
- Cherry, walnut: moderate
- Pine, cedar: poor (compression cracking)
- Use straight-grained, knot-free wood

## Steam Box Construction

- Any airtight box with steam inlet
- PVC pipe: cheap, easy (2m section, capped)
- Wood box: insulates better, lasts longer
- Steam inlet at lower end
- Vent hole at far end (prevent pressure)
- Rest work on dowels (steam circulates all sides)
- Size: just large enough for workpieces

## Generating Steam

- Any boiling water source works
- Camp stove under old pot
- Pipe from pot to steam box
- Seal connection with wet cloth
- No pressure needed: just flow
- Boiling pot of 2L handles 1-2 pieces

## Steam Time Guide

- Rule: 1 hour per 25mm (1 inch) of thickness
- 25mm stock: 1 hour minimum
- 12mm stock: 30 minutes
- Thicker: stack several 25mm pieces
  instead of one thick piece
- Wood should feel limp when ready
- Remove and bend immediately

## Bending Form

- Make form the exact inside radius needed
- Stronger than desired: wood springs back
- Overbend 10-15% to account for springback
- Secure form to bench or floor
- Have clamps ready before removing from steam
- One person bends, another clamps

## Sequence and Timing

- Prepare form, clamps ready at station
- Remove wood from steam with gloves
- 30-60 seconds to complete bend
  (wood hardens as it cools)
- Clamp immediately, leave 24-48 hours
- Remove from form, allow to fully dry
