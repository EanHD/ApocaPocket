# Rope Brake System

## The Capstan Principle

- Rope wrapped multiple turns around post
- Each wrap multiplies holding friction
- Formula: T_hold = T_load x e^(u x turns)
- e=2.718, u=friction coefficient
- 3 wraps of rope around post: hold 1000kg
  with only 100kg hand force
- More wraps: exponentially more holding

## How Many Wraps?

- 1 wrap: minimal friction, load moves easily
- 2 wraps: significant friction
- 3 wraps: holding most loads safely
- 4-5 wraps: controlling very heavy loads
- Rope must be rough enough to grip post
- Post must be smooth enough to not cut rope

## Materials

- Post: smooth hardwood, iron pipe, capstan barrel
- Rope: natural fiber (hemp, manila) or synthetic
- Rough rope on smooth post: ideal
- Natural fiber: higher friction than synthetic
- Keep rope and post dry for best friction
- Wet natural rope: expands, more friction

## Snubbing on a Bollard

- Cleat or bollard on dock: same principle
- Figure-8 around cleat: immediate hold
- Add more turns to increase friction
- Standard marine practice
- Applicable to any heavy control situation

## Rope Brake on Wheel

- Wrap rope around wooden drum or axle
- Tension one end: brakes rotation
- Slack: drum rotates freely
- Used in mills to stop and start
- Simple pedal-operated brake
- Friction heats rope: check regularly

## Log Moving

- Post driven in ground
- Rope from log to post (3 turns)
- Pull free end: log moves toward post
- Advance post, repeat
- Move large logs with minimal equipment
- Traditional method requires only rope and post

## Safety Practices

- Never stand in line with loaded rope
- Rope can snap under high tension
- Check rope for wear before heavy use
- Never wrap hands in rope under load
- Control end: always at end away from load
