# Drawknife Use

## What is a Drawknife

- Two-handled blade pulled toward body
- Shaves wood rapidly along the grain
- Works best on green (freshly cut) wood
- Essential tool of chair-maker and wheelwright
- Makes handles, legs, spindles, spokes
- Imprecise shaping faster than any other tool

## Shave Horse

- Work-holding bench for drawknife use
- Foot pedal clamps work piece
- Both hands free to use drawknife
- Work piece at comfortable working angle
- Build from any 4 pieces of wood
- Central pivot, dumbhead holds work
- Essential companion to the drawknife

## Grip and Posture

- Bevel down: more control, slicing cut
- Bevel up: aggressive, fast removal
- Hands at ends of handles
- Pull toward body with whole arm motion
- Lean body weight into cut
- Keep wrists loose for feel

## Reading the Wood

- Always work downhill of grain
- Feel grain direction with first cut
- Grain going wrong: flip piece, work other end
- Knots deflect cut: approach from both sides
- Green wood: shaves like cheese
- Dry wood: harder, requires sharper blade

## Common Uses

- Chair legs and spindles (round over)
- Tool handles (octagonal, then rounded)
- Paddle, oar, and spoon blanks
- Shingle thinning
- Bark removal from logs
- Any rapid stock removal task

## Shaping Sequence

- Rive blank close to finished size first
- Drawknife to rough shape
- Spokeshave for finer curves
- Scraper or sandpaper for finish
- Each tool removes less and refines more
- Work in order: riving > drawknife > spoke

## Making a Drawknife

- Flat bar steel, 30-40cm blade length
- Bend ends at 90 degrees for handles
- Grind bevel on one side only
- Harden the edge, temper to straw
- Fit wooden handle tubes to each tang
- Simple handle: wrap with cord
