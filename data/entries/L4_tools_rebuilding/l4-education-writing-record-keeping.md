# Writing & Records

Writing is the dividing line between stagnant and explosive civilization. Record everything.

## Paper Making

**Materials (best to usable)**
- Cotton/linen rags: Highest quality. Historical standard 1400-1800s.
- Mulberry or other tree bark inner layer: Traditional Asian paper.
- Hemp, flax, cattail, tall grasses: Coarser but functional.

**Tools needed:** Large vat, mold and deckle (frame with screen), felts or cloths, press (boards and weights), drying surface.

**Process**
- Collect and clean plant material. Cut to 2-4 inch pieces.
- Soak 24-48 hours to soften. Optional: ret in standing water 5-14 days for softer fiber.
- Boil 2-6 hours with alkali (wood ash lye or 1 tbsp lime per gallon water). Rinse thoroughly.
- Beat wet fibers with mallet on flat stone for 30-60 minutes until pulpy.
- Dilute pulp in large vat: 1 cup pulp per 2 gallons water. Stir well.
- Dip mold and deckle vertically into vat. Level underwater. Lift straight up. Drain 10-30 seconds.
- Couch (transfer to felt): Remove deckle. Flip mold onto felt. Press gently. Lift mold away.
- Press stack of felt-paper layers with 50-200 lb weights for 1-4 hours.
- Hang or lay flat to air dry 12-24 hours.
- Optional sizing: Dip in gelatin solution (1 tbsp per cup hot water). Re-dry. Prevents ink bleeding.

## Natural Inks

**Carbon black (simplest, permanent)**
- Soot from fire-blackened pots plus gum arabic plus water. 1:1:3 ratio. Strain through cloth. Permanent.

**Iron gall (historical standard, lasts 1000+ years)**
- Crush oak galls. Steep in water 2-7 days.
- Soak rusty iron nails in vinegar 1-2 weeks to create iron solution.
- Mix 1 cup gall extract plus 1/4 cup iron solution plus 1 tbsp gum arabic.
- Dark purple-black. Turns darker over time. Lasts millennia. Slightly corrosive over centuries.

**Berry ink (temporary, practice only)**
- Crush blackberries or elderberries. Strain. Add salt plus vinegar. Fades over time.

## Writing Tools

- Quill (best): Large bird feather (goose, turkey). Harden in hot sand. Strip barbs from lower end. Cut nib at 45 angle. Split tip for flex. Re-cut nib every 1-3 days.
- Reed pen: Hollow bamboo or river cane. Cut at angle. Split tip slightly. Stiffer than quill. More durable.
- Bone stylus: Pointed bone tip. For wax tablets or soft clay. Reusable surfaces.
- Brush: Bound animal hair on bamboo or wood handle. Asian calligraphy style.

## Record Keeping

**Births, deaths, marriages**
Format: Date / Name / Parents or Spouse / Event / Notes. Critical for genealogy and inheritance decisions.

**Transactions (trade, debt, ownership)**
Date, parties involved, exchange terms, two witnesses, marks or signatures. Prevents disputes.

**Food inventories**
Harvest totals by type. Estimated months of supply at current consumption rate. Plan for scarcity.

**Technical knowledge**
Write procedures, recipes, and formulas immediately after first success. Do not rely on memory.

**Historical record**
Events, decisions, lessons learned. Format: date, what happened, what was tried, what worked.

## Teaching Literacy

- Phase 1 (2-4 weeks): Teach 5-10 letters and sounds. Blend into words. 15-30 min daily practice.
- Phase 2 (2-6 months): 500-word core vocabulary. Flashcards. Write each word 10 times.
- Priority words: water, fire, food, danger, help, safe, plant, harvest, tools, directions, numbers.
- Phase 3 (3-12 months): Read practical manuals and instructions. Student-written texts for peer reading.
- Phase 4 (ongoing): Daily journal. Letters. Technical procedure documentation.

**Time to basic literacy:** 6 months to 2 years depending on age and practice intensity.

## Why Records Survive

- Roman concrete formula: Lost 500 CE. Not rediscovered until 1800s.
- Damascus steel technique: Lost 1700s. Still debated today.
- Greek fire: Recipe lost. Cannot fully replicate.

All three lost for the same reason: masters died, writing was not prioritized, or libraries were destroyed.
