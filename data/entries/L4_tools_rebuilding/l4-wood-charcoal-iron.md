# Wood and Metal Combo

## Why Combine Materials

- Wood: light, workable, available, compressive
- Iron/steel: hard, tensile, durable at edges
- Each material used where it performs best
- Traditional tools used both always
- Wheel, cart, plow, mallet: all composite
- Greater sum than either alone

## Wheel Construction

- Wooden spokes: compression under load
- Iron tire: holds rim under tension
- Wood hub: large, iron bushing at center
- All-wood wheels wear and spread
- Iron tire: prevents wear, holds shape
- Shrink-fit iron tire: heated, fits over wood,
  cools and compresses wheel tight

## Axe and Hammer Design

- Wood handle: absorbs shock, light, replaceable
- Steel head: hard, holds edge
- Eye fitting: mechanical lock (no adhesive)
- Iron collar (ferrule) prevents handle splitting
- This design optimal for impact tools
- Cannot be improved without electronics

## Metal Joinery

- Draw-bolt: long iron pin through joint
- Iron strap across weak wood joint
- Bent-over nail holds lap joints
- Wedge + iron strap: timber frame joint
- Iron ring on hub prevents splitting
- All smithed in forge, fitted to wood

## Wood Plane Design

- Wooden body: light, warm to handle
- Iron cap iron and blade: the cutting work
- Mouth tightly fitted to reduce tear-out
- Body: beech or apple traditional
- Cut mortise for blade angle (45 degrees)
- Wedge holds blade firm in body

## Barrel and Cooperage

- Staved wooden container held by iron hoops
- Hoops: drive on hot, cool to tighten
- Or cold-drive hammer on hoop
- Tight barrel is watertight without sealant
- Iron hoops last indefinitely
- Wooden staves eventually need replacement

## Composite Plow

- Wooden beam, handles, moldboard
- Iron share (cutting tip)
- Iron landside (furrow wall)
- Easily repaired: replace iron parts at forge
- Moldboard replaceable from sheet iron
- Traditional plow: 50+ years service
