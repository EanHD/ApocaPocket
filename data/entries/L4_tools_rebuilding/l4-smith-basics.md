# Blacksmithing Basics

## Core Concepts

- Heat steel to working temperature
- Work it with hammer while hot
- Cool in various ways for different properties
- Repeat as needed
- All other blacksmithing skills build on this
- Key: read heat color before striking

## Heat Color Guide

- Black heat (<200C): no working
- Blue (300C): tempering range
- Purple/brown (250-300C): tempering
- Straw yellow (220C): hard tools
- Bright cherry red (850C): general forging
- Orange (950C): best forging heat
- Yellow-white (1100C+): forge welding
- Never hit at black heat (cracks)

## Forge Setup

- Any combustion forge: coal, coke, charcoal
- Air supply: hand bellows, electric blower
- Anvil: any large steel mass (railroad iron)
- Hammer: 1-1.5 kg cross-peen for beginners
- Tongs sized to hold your stock
- Slack tub (water barrel) nearby

## Basic Operations

- Drawing out: lengthen by hammering long axis
- Upsetting: shorten by hammering end-on
- Bending: over anvil horn or with hardy
- Punching: drive punch through hot metal
- Cutting: hardy or chisel tool
- Twisting: grip with tongs, rotate while hot

## Hammer Technique

- Hold hammer near end of handle
- Let hammer weight do the work
- Wrist snap at impact point
- Strike on flat face, not edge
- Even blows, consistent rhythm
- Move metal with each blow, not just mark it

## Fire Management

- Start with small fire, build slowly
- Coke or charcoal: mound around steel
- Blast air until fire is white-hot underneath
- Insert steel, check color every 30 seconds
- Clinker (slag): dig out and remove
- Let fire burn clean between heats

## Anvil Technique

- Work over center of face
- Use horn for curves
- Hardy hole for cutting tools
- Pritchel hole for punching
- Anvil should ring, not thud (thud = cracked)
