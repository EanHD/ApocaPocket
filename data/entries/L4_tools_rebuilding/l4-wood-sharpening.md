# Sharpening Tools

## The Sharp Standard

- A truly sharp tool shaves arm hair
- Reflects light: uniform mirror bevel
- Cuts wood with almost no pressure
- Most tools in use are not truly sharp
- Learn sharpening before all else
- Dull tools cause most woodworking accidents

## The Two Surfaces

- Back (flat side): must be perfectly flat
- Bevel (angled side): must be consistent angle
- Edge is where these two surfaces meet
- Both surfaces must be polished for sharp edge
- Hollow in back from factory: do not remove
- Use only heel and toe of hollow

## Grit Sequence

- Coarse (120-240 grit): repair damage, reshape
- Medium (400-600 grit): remove coarse scratches
- Fine (1000-2000 grit): approaching sharp
- Very fine (4000-8000 grit): mirror polish
- Strop (leather): align and polish final edge
- Can skip grits if each removes previous marks

## Flattening the Back

- Place iron flat on stone (back down)
- Move in figure-8 or circular motion
- Check: light should not reflect from near edge
- Any light on back = high spot
- Keep flat: do not rock the iron
- Only needs to be done once per tool

## Grinding the Bevel

- Freehand: 25-35 degrees for most tools
- 25 degrees: chisels, plane irons
- 30-35 degrees: harder work, more durable
- Consistent angle is everything
- Use honing guide for learning
- Freehand: let fingers maintain angle

## Stropping

- Leather strop: flat piece of thick leather
- Apply polishing compound (rouge, green)
- Pull edge backward across strop
  (bevel side down, pull away from edge)
- Alternate back and bevel: 5-10 passes each
- Aligns microscopic edge teeth
- Test: should catch fingernail distinctly

## Field Sharpening

- Small slip stone in pocket
- 10 strokes on fine side: restores edge
- Any smooth stone (silica): works adequately
- Wet with water or spit
- Work in circular or straight strokes
- Good enough to finish the day's work
