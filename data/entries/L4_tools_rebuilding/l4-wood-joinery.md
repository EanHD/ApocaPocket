# Wood Joinery

## Why Wood Joinery

- Stronger than nails and screws for most uses
- Requires no metal hardware
- Self-tightening as wood moves seasonally
- Traditional furniture lasts centuries
- No factory tools required
- The knowledge is the infrastructure

## Mortise and Tenon

- Most important joint in woodworking
- Tenon: rectangular projection on one piece
- Mortise: rectangular hole in mating piece
- Tenon: 1/3 thickness of stock
- Cut tenon with saw, pare with chisel
- Mortise: drill waste, chop walls with chisel
- Fit: tenon should push in firmly by hand

## Dovetail Joint

- Strongest joint for drawers and boxes
- Wedge-shaped pins interlock with tails
- Cannot be pulled apart in one direction
- Angle: 1:8 for hardwood, 1:6 for softwood
- Layout with bevel gauge and marking knife
- Cut tails first, scribe pins from tails
- Patience required: tight fit takes practice

## Half-Lap Joint

- Simplest structural joint
- Both pieces notched halfway through
- Overlapped flat, lock flush on both faces
- Mark: set marking gauge to half thickness
- Cut shoulders with saw, remove waste
- Check fit: pieces must sit perfectly flat
- Good for frames, light structures

## Dado Joint

- Groove across grain
- Accepts shelf or divider
- Width = thickness of shelving piece
- Depth = 1/3 of total thickness
- Cut with saw kerfs, chisel waste between
- Router plane cleans bottom flat
- Sliding fit is correct (not tight)

## Wedged Joints

- Drawbore: pin through offset mortise/tenon
  Pulls joint tight as pin is driven
- Wedge tenon: saw kerf in tenon end,
  drive wedge when assembled
- Fox-wedged: blind wedged (expands in mortise)
- All wedge methods: no glue required
- Traditional timber framing standard

## Glue Options

- Hide glue (traditional): reversible, strong
  Make from animal hides boiled in water
- Flour paste: adequate for light work
- Pine pitch: waterproof, primitive
- Modern PVA: if available, very good
- Properly fitted joints hold without glue
