# Repairing Tools

## Why Repair Matters

- New tools unavailable post-collapse
- Good steel is scarce, reuse it always
- Most "broken" tools are fixable
- A repaired tool is as good as new
- Teaches understanding of tools
- Prolongs community tool inventory

## Loose Handle Repair

- Soak wooden handles in linseed oil
  for 24 hours to swell wood fibers
- Drive wedge further into kerf
- Cross-wedge at 90 degrees to first
- Metal ring collars prevent splitting
- Replace handle: see handle-making entry
- Test: tool should not wobble at all

## Mushroomed Heads

- Overuse causes steel to peel/mushroom
- Flying steel chips are dangerous
- Anneal the head (heat orange, cool slow)
- Grind or file mushroomed section flat
- Reharden if needed (impact tools stay soft)
- Hammer over anvil to re-form tight head

## Edge Repair

- Chipped edge: grind back to clean metal
- Grind bevel carefully (do not overheat)
- Nicked blade: work out nick with grinder
- Blunt: just sharpen, no forging needed
- Major damage: forge-weld new edge section
- Keep cool in water while grinding

## Re-forging Bent Tools

- Heat bend area to working temperature
- Straighten over anvil with light blows
- Check with straight edge repeatedly
- Tool should be straighter than eye can check
- Mild steel: straightens cold sometimes
- If cracks appear: discard, use as stock

## Cracked Wooden Handles

- Small cracks: boil in water, dry, oil with
  linseed oil
- Large cracks: wrap tightly with wire
  or hose clamp
- Split completely: replace handle entirely
- Hang repaired tools head-down in oil
  overnight for conditioning

## Repair Priority

- Triage by replacement difficulty
- Cutting tools: repair first
- Impact tools: repair second
- Handles: always replace when needed
- Keep a stock of replacement handles
  pre-shaped and oiled for immediate use
