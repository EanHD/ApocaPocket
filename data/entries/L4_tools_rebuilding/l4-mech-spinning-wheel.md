# Spinning Wheel

## How Spinning Works

- Fibers (wool, flax) drafted thin by hand
- Twist inserted: fibers lock together
- Twist creates thread/yarn
- More twist = stronger, thinner thread
- Less twist = softer, weaker
- Spinning wheel inserts twist mechanically

## Wheel Components

- Great wheel: large drive wheel (hand-turned)
- Flyer and bobbin assembly: insert twist and wind
- Mother-of-all: holds flyer assembly
- Drive band: from great wheel to whorl
- Footman: treadle to crank connection
- Treadle: foot pedal drive

## Drive Ratio

- Ratio = drive wheel circumference / whorl circumference
- Large wheel, small whorl = high ratio
- High ratio = many twists per foot treadle
- Typical ratio: 10:1 to 20:1
- Higher ratio: thinner, more twisted thread
- Adjust by changing whorl size

## Flyer/Bobbin

- Flyer: U-shaped arm with hooks
- Bobbin: cylinder inside flyer
- Both rotate, but at slightly different speeds
- Difference in speed winds yarn onto bobbin
- Hooks: shift yarn to fill bobbin evenly
- Key: tension differential between flyer/bobbin

## Construction Materials

- Frame: any hardwood (oak, maple, cherry)
- Wheel: thin, light (spoke and rim)
- Spindle: hardwood or metal
- Bearings: wood-on-wood with leather liner
- All joints: mortise and tenon
- Drive band: cord, leather, or linen thread

## Wool Preparation

- Shear sheep (hand shears work)
- Wash fleece in hot water with soap
- Dry flat, do not wring
- Card wool: combs transfer fiber to rolag
- Rolag: fluffy roll ready to spin
- Or flick card individual locks

## Fiber Varieties

- Wool: easiest to spin, felts easily
- Flax (linen): more difficult, wet spinning
- Cotton: very short fiber, hardest to spin
- Nettle fiber: similar to flax, wild source
- Milkweed: possible but very short
- Plant fibers: cold water processing
