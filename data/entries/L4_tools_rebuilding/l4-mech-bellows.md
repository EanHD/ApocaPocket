# Bellows Construction

## Types of Bellows

- Simple squeeze bellows: two boards, leather
  Delivers air only on squeeze stroke
- Double-acting bellows: valved chambers
  Delivers air on both push and pull
- Great bellows: large forge bellows
  Overhead mounted, lever-operated
- Foot bellows: hands-free operation
- Box bellows: Japanese design, very efficient

## Bellows Build

- Two oval boards: top and bottom
- Leather side: riveted to both boards
- Top board hinged at narrow end (nose)
- Air enters top chamber on expansion
  through top inlet valve
- Air exits at nose on compression
- Inlet valve: leather flap over hole
  (opens inward, closes under pressure)

## Making the Valve

- Cut round hole in board (5-8cm diameter)
- Cut leather circle 3x hole size
- Rivet at one side only
- Leather lifts inward on intake
- Air pressure from inside seals it on output
- Two valves: one each board for double-acting

## Nozzle Design

- Narrow brass or iron tube at nose
- Directs air to precise location in fire
- 2-3cm inner diameter
- Angle: slightly downward into fire
- Can be removed for cleaning
- Length: reach comfortably from bellows to fire

## Leather Selection

- Heavy chrome-tanned leather: most durable
- Vegetable-tanned: traditional, soaks oil well
- Condition with neatsfoot oil or tallow
- Treat before assembly and annually
- Dry leather cracks: keep oiled
- Thickness: 2-3mm for good longevity

## Assembly Sequence

- Cut both boards to identical oval shape
- Bevel meeting edges of leather
- Rivet leather to bottom board first
- Fit nose piece with taper and rivet
- Rivet top board to leather
- Install valves after assembly
- Test: steady airflow, no leaks

## Box Bellows

- Rectangular wooden box with sliding piston
- Piston has valve opening
- Box has outlet valve
- Delivers air on push stroke only
- Very easy to build, less leather needed
- Japanese traditional forge bellows design
