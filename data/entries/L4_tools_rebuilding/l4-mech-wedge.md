# Wedge Mechanics

## Wedge Principle

- Wedge = two inclined planes back to back
- Converts linear striking force to
  perpendicular splitting/lifting force
- Mechanical advantage = wedge length / width
- Long thin wedge: high MA, slow entry
- Short fat wedge: low MA, fast entry
- Both used for different purposes

## Force Calculation

- 15-degree wedge: MA approximately 4x
- 10-degree wedge: MA approximately 6x
- 5-degree wedge: MA approximately 12x
- Sharper angle = more mechanical advantage
- Sharper angle = harder to drive in
- Choose angle for material and purpose

## Log Splitting Wedges

- Wider angle (30 degrees): fast entry
- Used for splitting firewood
- Steel wedge driven with sledge
- Soft wood: one wedge usually sufficient
- Hard wood: may need 2-3 wedges
- Start in existing check (crack) in log

## Lifting Wedges

- Thin wedges for raising heavy objects
- Slide under, drive in to lift slowly
- Multiple wedges to raise building corners
- Adjust level by changing wedge depth
- Traditional foundation leveling method
- Hardwood wedges adequate for most uses

## Making Metal Wedges

- Forge from mild steel bar
- Draw out point, taper to desired angle
- Cross-section: rectangular or triangular
- Harden steel for long-term use
- Mild steel: will mushroom over time
- High carbon: holds edge better

## Making Wooden Wedges

- Hardwood: oak, hickory, locust
- Grain running along wedge length
- Cut from riven blank (straight grain)
- Useful for: timber framing, barrel
  assembly, tool handles, temporary lifts
- Replace when mushroomed or split

## Wedge Applications

- Splitting logs and stone
- Lifting foundations for leveling
- Securing handles in tool heads
- Tightening joinery (fox wedge)
- Adjusting blade angle in wooden planes
- Securing heavy loads during transport
