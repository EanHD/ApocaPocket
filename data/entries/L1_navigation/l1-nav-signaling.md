# Signaling for Rescue

## Why It Matters

- Rescuers search with eyes and ears
- You must create contrast to stand out
- Signals work when you cannot walk
- Passive signals work while you sleep
- Rule of 3 is universal distress code
- Signaling is a learnable skill

## Mirror Flash Method

- Any shiny surface works
- Angle toward aircraft or searcher
- Punch hole in mirror to aim through
- Flash every few seconds: 3 flashes
- Visible up to 10 miles in sun
- Most effective signal available

## Audio Signals

- Whistle 3 blasts = distress
- Pause, repeat every few minutes
- Whistle carries 3x further than voice
- Voice: yell SOS or HELP in groups of 3
- Strike metal on metal for sound
- Aircraft may circle if they hear you

## Ground-to-Air Signals

- SOS in open ground with rocks/logs
- Each letter at least 10 feet wide
- X means need medical help
- Arrow points to direction traveled
- Use contrast: dark on light or reverse
- Stomp patterns in snow work well

## Color and Movement

- Bright cloth on highest point
- Wave orange or red above head
- Movement catches the eye better
- Signal only when aircraft is visible
- Conserve flares for confirmed contact
- Do not use flares blindly at night

## What Can Go Wrong

- Mirror aimed too low, misses aircraft
- Whistle exhausted before rescue
- Ground signal too small from altitude
- Signaling at wrong moment

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Flash mirror signal to
  moving aircraft 3 times
- Sign of mastery: Ground SOS
  legible from 200 ft altitude view
