# Terrain Reading

## Why It Matters

- Terrain tells you where you are
- Map terrain = ground terrain
- Ridgelines always drain predictably
- Knowing terrain = knowing water
- Essential for off-trail navigation
- Reading ground = reading the map

## Key Terrain Features

- Ridge: high ground, falls both sides
- Valley/drainage: water flows through
- Saddle: low notch between two peaks
- Spur: subsidiary ridge off main ridge
- Draw: small drainage between spurs
- Peak: highest enclosed contour rings

## Reading Contour Lines

- Lines close together = steep slope
- Lines far apart = gentle slope
- V-shapes pointing uphill = drainages
- V-shapes pointing downhill = spurs
- Closed rings = hilltop or depression
- Index lines (bold) are labeled

## Ground-to-Map Matching

- Identify 2-3 visible high points
- Match shape to contour rings on map
- Find where ridgelines run on map
- Count drainages to locate self
- Water always goes to lower contours
- Confirm with compass bearing to peaks

## Moving by Terrain

- Follow ridgelines for clear travel
- Cross drainages at narrowest points
- Saddles are natural passes
- Avoid crossing draws in rain season
- Climb spurs to reach ridges
- Descend drainages to find water

## What Can Go Wrong

- Two similar-shaped drainages confused
- Old map misses new features
- Confidence without verification
- Contour spacing scale misread

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify your location on
  topo map by terrain features only
- Sign of mastery: Predict next
  terrain feature before seeing it
