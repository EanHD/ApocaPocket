# Star Navigation

## Why It Matters

- Compass can be lost or broken
- Stars are always available on clear nights
- Polaris is always within 1 degree of north
- Works anywhere on Earth
- Accuracy within 2-3 degrees
- Must practice before relying on it

## Polaris (N Hemisphere)

- Find Big Dipper (7 stars, ladle shape)
- Two stars at end of ladle bowl
- These are the pointer stars
- Draw line through them outward
- Distance: 5x the gap between them
- That star is Polaris (North Star)

## Confirming Polaris

- Polaris is not the brightest star
- It is barely above average brightness
- It does NOT move across the sky
- All stars rotate around it
- Watch for 20 min - stays fixed
- Standing below it: you face north

## S Cross (S Hemisphere)

- Find Southern Cross (4 stars, cross)
- Two pointer stars beside it (bright)
- Extend the long axis of cross
  4.5 times its own length
- Foot of that line is south celestial
- Drop perpendicular to horizon = south

## Star Movement Method

- Plant stick in ground vertically
- Mark tip of any star shadow
- Wait 15 minutes, mark again
- First mark to second mark = east
- Opposite end is west
- Perpendicular = north/south

## What Can Go Wrong

- Confusing Polaris with other stars
- Clouds obscure key reference stars
- Light pollution in urban settings
- Southern Cross confused with False Cross

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Locate Polaris from scratch
  in under 60 seconds
- Sign of mastery: Navigate 1 km
  at night using stars only
