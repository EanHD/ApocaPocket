# Sun Compass Method

## Why It Matters

- Works anywhere sun is visible
- No equipment needed for shadow tip
- Reliable to within 5-10 degrees
- Should be first-choice compass backup
- Fast enough for immediate navigation
- Works any time of day with sun

## Shadow Tip Method

- Push 3 ft stick into level ground
- Mark tip of shadow with rock: Mark 1
- Wait 15-20 minutes
- Mark new shadow tip: Mark 2
- Draw line from Mark 1 to Mark 2
- This line runs west (M1) to east (M2)
- Stand perpendicular: north is ahead

## Confirming Results

- Northern hemisphere: sun arcs south
- Southern hemisphere: sun arcs north
- Midday shadow is always shortest
- Shortest shadow direction = north (NH)
- Shadow lengths confirm accuracy
- Recheck after 30 min of travel

## Watch Method NH

- Hold watch horizontal, face up
- Point hour hand at the sun
- Bisect angle between hour hand
  and 12 oclock position
- That bisecting line points south
- Opposite is north
- Works in Northern Hemisphere only

## Watch Method SH

- Point 12 oclock mark at the sun
- Bisect angle between 12 and hour
- Bisecting line points north
- Works in Southern Hemisphere only
- Off by up to 20 deg near equator

## What Can Go Wrong

- Overcast sky blocks sun entirely
- Daylight saving time throws watch off
- Stick not vertical - skewed results
- Near equator around equinox - less accurate

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Determine north to within
  15 degrees in under 5 minutes
- Sign of mastery: Shadow tip and
  watch agree within 10 degrees
