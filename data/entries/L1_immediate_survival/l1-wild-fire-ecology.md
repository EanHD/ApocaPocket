# Fire Ecology

## Immediate Post-Fire
- Ash: potassium-rich, fertilizes next year
- Roasted seeds: found in ash layer
- Exposed roots: easier to dig and find
- Dead fish from heated streams: collect now
- Rodents fleeing fire: hunting opportunity

## Year 1 Regrowth
- Fireweed: first plant to return, edible
- Dandelion and thistle: abundant
- New grass: deer and elk congregate
- Raspberry cane: rapid early colonizer
- Morel mushrooms: boom year 1-2 post-fire

## Year 2-5 Resources
- Dense berry patches: blackberry, service
- Young aspen: browse for deer, elk
- Cavity-nesting birds increase: eggs
- Shrub layer replaces grass
- Root crops more accessible: soil loose

## Hazard Awareness
- Snags: burned standing dead trees fall
- Wind: topples snags without warning
- Hot spots: buried coals persist weeks
- Soil hydrophobic layer: flash flood risk
- Avoid camping under standing snags

## Navigation in Burned
- Landmarks changed or destroyed
- Trail markers may be burned
- Compass: rely more heavily post-fire
- Check for hotspots: prod soil with stick
- Map changes quickly year over year

## Soil Recovery Signs
- Earthworms return: soil health good
- Nitrogen-fixing plants: alder, clover
- Mycorrhizal fungi return in year 3-5
- Indicator of stable camp site return
- Nutrient flush: best foraging window
