# Building Structural

Entering a compromised building is the number 1 cause of death in post-disaster environments.
60-70% of post-earthquake deaths occur after the initial event from secondary collapse, not the quake itself.

When in doubt, mark it RED. No resource inside is worth your life.

## Safety Categories

GREEN: safe to enter normally. Re-inspect after any aftershock or further degradation.
YELLOW: limited entry only. 15 minutes maximum. Evacuation plan before entering. Exit immediately at any new crack or sound.
RED: do not enter under any circumstances.

## 5-Point Assessment

1. Lean: stand 50-100 feet away. Hold a pen vertically as a plumb reference.
Any visible lean? 1-2 degrees = caution. 3-5 degrees = YELLOW. Over 5 degrees = RED (collapse imminent within hours).

2. Foundation: walk the perimeter. Cracks wider than 1/4 inch = YELLOW or RED depending on direction.
Horizontal cracks = serious. Gaps between foundation and walls = RED. Water pooling against foundation = undermining.

3. Walls: hairline cracks (less than 1/16 inch) = normal. Cracks over 1/4 inch = YELLOW.
X-pattern cracks (two diagonals crossing) = RED. Bulging or bowing over 2 inches = RED. Horizontal cracks = RED.

4. Roof: roofline should be straight and level. Sag over 6 inches (U or V shape) = RED. Missing sections = RED.

5. Hazards: gas smell (rotten eggs) = evacuate immediately, no sparks, no phones near building.
Sparking electrical = avoid area. Chemical odor = evacuate and move upwind.
Downed power lines = stay 35+ feet away.

## Interior Checks

Floor: tap with crowbar. Hollow sound = stop. Test each step near doorway before committing full weight.
If floor gives: spread weight, lie flat, crawl to wall, then to exit.

Ceiling: sagging or wet staining = falling hazard. Stay near walls and doorways under damaged ceilings.
Stairs: test edge of first step with half weight before descending. One person at a time. Handrail must be firm.

## Earthquake Damage

Soft-story collapse: first floor compressed, upper floors tilted or intact. Total collapse imminent. RED.
Shear cracks: diagonal cracks at 45 degrees in walls from seismic twisting. Over 1/4 inch wide = RED.
Foundation offset: visible shift between foundation sections over 1 inch = RED.

Aftershock warning: damaged buildings are more vulnerable to aftershocks than to the original quake.
If building is YELLOW, minimize time inside and exit at first sign of shaking.

## Fire Damage

Wood structures: surface char (0-1 inch deep) = cosmetic, likely safe.
Char over 3 inches deep or over 50% of member thickness = RED.
Test: poke charred beam with screwdriver. Flakes off cleanly to reveal solid wood = possibly safe.
Crumbles or feels spongy = RED.

Steel: any visible warping or drooping = permanently weakened = RED.
Concrete: spalling (chunks missing, rebar exposed) = RED. Pink or red discoloration = high heat = YELLOW.

## Environmental Hazards

Gas: rotten egg smell. Walk out (no running, no static sparks). Move 200+ feet upwind.
No phone, flashlight, or any spark source near building.
Electrical: all downed lines are live. Stay 35+ feet away. Water plus electricity = electrocution.
Chemicals: unknown containers = assume toxic. Burning eyes or dizziness on approach = exit immediately.

## Decision Flowchart

1. Building leaning over 3 degrees? YES = RED.
2. Wide cracks (over 1/4 inch) in foundation or walls? YES = RED.
3. Roof sagging over 6 inches or partially collapsed? YES = RED.
4. Gas smell, chemical smell, or sparking electrical? YES = RED.
5. X-pattern cracks, bulging walls, or soft-story collapse visible? YES = RED.
6. Moderate cracks (1/8 to 1/4 inch), minor sag, or water damage? YES = YELLOW.
7. Minor cosmetic damage only? = GREEN (still monitor for changes).

## Marking System

Mark assessed buildings with spray paint on door or wall near the entrance.
GREEN box labeled INSPECTED: safe to enter normally.
YELLOW box labeled LIMITED ENTRY: 15 min max, 2-3 people, evacuation plan required before entry.
RED box labeled UNSAFE: do not enter. Add specifics inside box (LEANING, GAS LEAK, FIRE DAMAGE).
Include assessment date and your initials.

## Common Mistakes

- Running in for one quick item: even 30 seconds = real collapse risk.
- Trusting a building that survived the initial event: aftershocks and degradation cause delayed collapse.
- Going up to get valuables on upper floors: upper floors are the highest risk zone.
- Entering despite gas smell: one spark = explosion. No exceptions.
- Assessing alone: buddy system. One watches from outside, one enters.
- Re-entering after initial exit: each entry increases collapse risk.
- Assuming concrete buildings are safe: fire-damaged concrete is severely weakened.
