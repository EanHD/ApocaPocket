# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Cold nights even in summer: 40-50F
- Dehydration in dry air at altitude
- Flash floods in wash channels
- UV radiation at high elevation
- Gila monster and rattlesnake bites
- Wide open terrain: disorientation

## Water Sources
- Seeps below canyon rock layers
- Tinaja (rock potholes): shaded spots
- Follow dry wash to water table
- Animal tracks and birds circle water
- Morning frost on rocks: collect it
- Deeper soil in wash beds holds water

## Food Sources
- Pinyon pine nuts: high calorie
- Mormon tea plant: mild stimulant
- Cholla cactus buds: roast to eat
- Jackrabbit: abundant, easy to catch
- Joshua tree fruit: edible when ripe
- Yucca flowers and pods: edible

## Shelter Priorities
- Shade midday is the top priority
- Rock overhang: common in cliffs
- Reflective surface to block sun
- Windbreak needed for cold nights
- High ground is warmer at night

## Navigation
- Mountain ranges visible 50+ miles
- Basin and range: predictable pattern
- US-395, US-50: main cross routes
- Dry lake beds often have roads
- Powerlines cross from city to city

## Evacuation Routes
- Move at night toward known roads
- I-15, I-40: southern corridor
- US-50, US-6: central Nevada routes
- Follow powerlines or pipelines
