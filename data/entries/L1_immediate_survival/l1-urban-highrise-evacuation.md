# High-Rise Evacuation

Evacuate early. Once stairwells fill (300+ people), descent slows to 10-15 minutes per floor.
A 10-minute delay can cost 1-2 hours in stairwell crush conditions.

## When to Evacuate

Evacuate now: fire alarm, visible or smelled smoke, earthquake felt strongly, explosion,
structural damage, civil unrest reaching building, official evacuation order.

Shelter in place: active shooter outside your floor (lock door, barricade, silence phone).
Hazmat release outside building (seal door gaps, turn off HVAC). Tornado or high winds (interior room, low floor).

## 60-Second Pre-Evac

1. Touch your door before opening (15 seconds). Hot = fire on your floor or above.
If hot, do not open. Seal door gaps with wet towels. Go to window. Call 911. Wait for rescue.
Cool or warm = proceed.

2. Grab go-bag only if pre-staged at door (15 seconds max). Otherwise leave immediately.

3. Knock on 2-3 adjacent doors as you go (10 seconds). Do not wait for response.

4. Shoes on. Door closed but unlocked (firefighters need access). Move.

## Stairwell Descent

Never use elevators. Power fails during emergencies. Elevators recall to lobby in fires.

Body position: right hand on railing, face forward, knees slightly bent, small controlled steps.
Pace: 10-15 seconds per floor under normal conditions.

Descent time: 10 floors = 2-3 min. 20 floors = 5-7 min. 30 floors = 8-12 min. 40 floors = 12-18 min.
Add 50-100% time if 100+ people in stairwell.

Smoke in stairwell: drop to hands and knees (air cleaner near floor). Wet cloth over nose and mouth.
Descend 1-2 floors and reassess. If smoke worsens, stop and retreat upward.
Temperature above 140 F in stairwell: retreat immediately. Flashover imminent.

Crowd crush in stairwell: cross arms over chest, elbows out. Move diagonally toward wall.
If falling, curl and protect head, get to wall, yell I am down.

Locked stairwell door: try the floor below. Do not waste time forcing it.
Ground-floor exit door must open to exterior by law.

## Floor Strategy

High floors (30+): start immediately, pace yourself. Rest only on landings.
Drink water every 10 floors. Fatigue is real at this distance.

Mid floors (10-29): steady pace. Watch for crowds merging from floors above.
Low floors (2-9): controlled pace. Do not sprint. Falls near the finish line pile people up.

Ground floor: assess lobby before exiting. Smoke through lobby doors = find alternate exit.
Crowded exit = look for side doors or service entrances.

Once outside: move 200+ feet from building. Never re-enter for any reason.

## Alternate Exits

Roof (only if fire is below and all stairwells are smoke-filled):
- Break lock if necessary. Close door behind you (contains smoke).
- Signal for rescue with bright cloth or flashlight. Spell SOS with debris.
- Stay away from edges. Lie flat when helicopter approaches (rotor wash = 60+ mph wind).

Window descent (last resort, life-threatening fire in your room only):
- Tie rope to radiator, pipe, or bolted furniture. Test by hanging full weight.
- Bedsheets tied with square knots hold ~200 lbs. Test each knot.
- 1-floor drop (10-12 ft): hang from edge, land on balls of feet, knees bent, roll sideways.
- 2-floor drop (20-24 ft): 50-70% survival rate. 3+ floors = likely fatal.
- Only attempt if fire is already in your room and rescue is not coming.

## Special Populations

Mobility impaired: areas of refuge on each floor are designed for this purpose.
Call 911 from refuge area. Give exact floor and location. Wait for firefighter rescue.
Do not use elevators under any circumstances.

Children under 5: carry them (faster than walking, reduces panic).
Children 5-12: hold hands. Say: we are going down the stairs to get outside.
Elderly: offer your arm for stability, do not pull. Rest 30-60 seconds on landings.

## Calling for Help

Call 911 once: state building address, your floor number, and what is happening. Then hang up.
Text family: evacuating building name from floor X. Texts use less bandwidth than voice calls.
If trapped: call 911 with exact location. Bang rhythmically on wall (3 bangs, pause, repeat). Use whistle.

## Common Mistakes

- Using the elevator: power fails, it recalls to lobby, or fills with smoke.
- Grabbing belongings (laptop, wallet): 60 seconds max, go-bag only, or leave it.
- Opening a hot door: backdraft or fire in hallway. Seal door and call 911 instead.
- Sprinting down stairs: falls cause pile-ups. Controlled pace only.
- Going up to roof immediately: only as last resort when fire blocks all stairwells.
- Re-entering the building: never, for any reason, including pets.
- Ignoring the alarm (assuming false): treat every alarm as real.
- Using phone for anything except 911 and emergency contacts.
