# Improvised Tools

## Why It Matters

- The right tool multiplies capability
- Gear fails; knowledge does not
- Primitive tools built civilizations
- Found materials are everywhere
- Making tools burns less energy
  than struggling without them

## Cutting Tools

- Flint, obsidian, quartz: knap edge
- Broken glass: wrap one end in cloth
- Aluminum can lid: good scraper
- Split bamboo: sharp edge blade
- Clam or mussel shell: scrapers
- Car mirror glass: last resort edge

## Containers and Vessels

- Large bark sheet: fold and pin sides
- Plastic bag from trash: immediate use
- Clay hollowed out: fire-harden it
- Half-buried rock: collect rain in it
- Hubcap or car door panel: water trough
- Green bamboo sections: sealed cups

## Improvised Cordage

- Twisted inner bark fibers (basswood)
- Plant stem fibers (nettle, dogbane)
- Paracord inner strands each = 50 lb
- Shoelaces and zip ties as connector
- Duct tape rolled into rope
- T-shirt strips twisted into line

## Fishing and Trapping

- Safety pin bent = fishhook
- Thorn lashed to stick = hook
- Bent aluminum = large hook
- Snare from wire or thick cordage
- Bottle trap: cut top, invert inside
- Weir from sticks in shallow water

## What Can Go Wrong

- Improvised blade with no handle wrapping
- Bark container leaks before use
- Plant cordage not tested before trusting
- Over-engineering instead of action

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Make functional cordage
  from plant fiber in 20 minutes
- Sign of mastery: Build 5 tools
  from found materials in 2 hours
