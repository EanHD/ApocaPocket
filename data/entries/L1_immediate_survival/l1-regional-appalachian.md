# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Dense fog: navigation near zero
- Black bears: active spring-fall
- Copperhead and timber rattler bites
- Tick-borne illness: Lyme and RMSF
- Hypothermia: wet spring and fall
- Flooding creeks after heavy rain

## Water Sources
- Springs abundant in limestone areas
- Small streams: filter and boil
- Seeps at base of rock outcrops
- Rain catchment: reliable year-round
- Avoid valley bottom stagnant pools

## Food Sources
- Ramps (wild leek): early spring
- Pawpaw: late summer fruit
- Black walnuts: fall, calorie dense
- Deer and turkey: common year-round
- Morel mushrooms: spring
- Persimmons: ripen after first frost

## Shelter Priorities
- Ridgelines: wind and lightning risk
- Mid-slope position is ideal
- Hemlock stands: dry ground under
- Rock overhangs: common and dry
- Debris hut: abundant leaf material

## Navigation
- Ridgelines run NE-SW generally
- Appalachian Trail: white blaze marks
- Streams flow east to Atlantic
- Hollows often hold farms and roads
- Powerline cuts visible from ridges

## Evacuation Routes
- Follow streams to valley roads
- AT corridor spans GA to ME
- US-19, US-23: main ridge roads
- Rail lines follow river valleys
