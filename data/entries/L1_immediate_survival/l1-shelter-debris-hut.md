# Debris Hut

## Why It Matters

- No tools or gear required
- Works in temperate forest conditions
- Properly built survives 20F nights
- Leaf insulation matches sleeping bag
- Builds body heat by afternoon
- Humans built these for millennia

## Frame Construction

- Find ridgepole 9-10 ft long
- Prop one end on stump 4 ft high
- Other end rests on ground
- Ribbing sticks lean along sides
- Angle ribs to 45 degrees
- Width: just wider than shoulders

## Filling the Frame

- Add lattice sticks to hold debris
- Pile leaves, duff, pine needles
- Layer must be arm depth thick
- Pack densely, not loosely
- Cover ridge fully to apex
- Sides, ends, roof all covered

## Door Plug

- Collect armful of dry leaves
- Stuff into entrance after entering
- Compact door plug fills gap
- Test by pushing with arm - resists
- No gaps = warm; gaps = cold
- Re-stuff if you feel drafts

## Interior Bed

- Pile leaves 12+ inches inside
- Test by punching down - resilient
- Add dry moss or grass on top
- Curl body into leaf cocoon
- Less space inside = warmer
- Small fire near (not inside) entrance

## What Can Go Wrong

- Leaf layer too thin - loses 70% value
- Ridge pole too short, cannot fit
- Wet leaves compact, lose R-value
- Entrance gap allows cold air in

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Build full debris hut in
  3 hours before dark
- Sign of mastery: Warm through
  night at 30F with no sleeping bag
