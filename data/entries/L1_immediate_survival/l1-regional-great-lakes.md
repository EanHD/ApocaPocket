# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Lake-effect blizzards: wall of snow
- Thin ice: lakes freeze unevenly
- Hypothermia: cold water immersion
- Dense fog over open water
- Spring ice breakup: very unstable
- Cold water drowning risk year-round

## Water Sources
- Great Lakes: largest freshwater supply
- Treat lake water: filter and boil
- Streams and rivers: filter all
- Snow melt: safe if away from roads
- Wells: abundant in rural areas

## Food Sources
- Walleye and perch: year-round catch
- Whitefish: great for smoking
- Wild blueberries: July-August
- Cattail: roots and pollen edible
- Deer: high population near lakes
- Morel mushrooms: spring

## Shelter Priorities
- Avoid lake shore: wind and exposure
- Dense forest breaks wind well
- Birch bark: fire starting and shelter
- Snow trench: quick winter shelter
- Ice fishing shelters: emergency use

## Navigation
- Lakes are huge visible landmarks
- Rivers flow toward the lakes
- I-90, I-94: main E-W routes
- US-2 and US-41: northern routes
- Ferry routes cross major lakes

## Evacuation Routes
- Shoreline roads circle each lake
- US-2 and US-41: northern routes
- Ferry to islands if road cut off
- Snowmobile trails useful in winter
