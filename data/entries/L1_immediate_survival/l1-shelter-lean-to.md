# Lean-To Shelter

## Why It Matters

- Fastest shelter to construct
- Works with tarp or natural materials
- Reflects fire heat back at you
- Good visibility and access to fire
- Requires minimal structural skill
- Foundation for more complex builds

## Frame Setup

- Two uprights, 7 ft apart
- Each upright 5-6 ft tall
- Crossbar lashed horizontally
- Angled back poles lean to ground
- Angle: 45 degrees from vertical
- Length: at least body-width plus 2 ft

## Tarp Version

- Tie ridgeline to two trees
- Drape tarp over ridgeline
- Stake back edge to ground
- Stake side edges to angles
- Front remains fully open to fire
- Adjust angle for rain shedding

## Natural Version

- Lean branches on crossbar densely
- Start from ground, layer upward
- Overlap like shingles for rain
- Add dry leaf layer over branches
- Thickness arm-depth for warmth
- Plug sides with leaf walls

## Fire Reflector Pairing

- Build fire 4-6 ft from open front
- Reflector wall behind fire (logs)
- Heat bounces back into shelter
- Angle shelter slightly downward
- Sleep with feet toward fire
- This setup beats any sleeping bag

## What Can Go Wrong

- Wind direction shifts in night
- Too steep angle sheds rain inside
- No reflector - heat dissipates
- Uprights not anchored - collapses

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Functional lean-to with
  tarp in under 15 minutes
- Sign of mastery: Warm and dry
  through a rainy 40F night
