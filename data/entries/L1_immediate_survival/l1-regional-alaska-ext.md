# Extended Alaska Survival

## Key Hazards
- Polar bear: most dangerous predator
- Grizzly bear: common statewide
- Hypothermia: wind chill is severe
- Breakup ice: May river ice unstable
- Moose attacks: more per yr than bears
- Frostbite in minutes at -40F windchill

## Permafrost Awareness
- Do not dig near surface for water
- Ground unstable during summer thaw
- Tussock fields: ankle injury risk
- Water pools on frozen substrate
- Fire may sink into thawed ground

## Water Sources
- Snow: melt before eating if possible
- Rivers: filter and treat year-round
- Do not eat yellow or orange snow
- Glacier runoff: clean but very cold
- Summer tundra ponds: always treat

## Food Sources
- Moose, caribou, Dall sheep: key
- Salmon: July-Sept run in rivers
- Crowberries and cloudberries: fall
- Spruce tips: spring, vitamin C rich
- Arctic char: clear cold streams
- Ptarmigan: year-round at treeline

## Shelter Priorities
- Quinzhee snow shelter: 1 hour build
- Spruce bark and boughs: insulation
- Wind is bigger threat than cold
- Vapor barrier layers prevent wicking
- Never sleep directly on snow

## Evacuation Routes
- Alaska Range: few passes, well-known
- Dalton, Parks, Richardson Highways
- Rivers: main travel corridors
- Signal aircraft: highest priority
