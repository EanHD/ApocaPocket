# Mental Health Crisis

Mental health emergencies kill as surely as physical trauma. When there is no 911, you are the intervention.

## Triage

**Assess immediately:**
- Danger to self? Suicidal statements, self-harm, refusing food/water
- Danger to others? Threats, weapons, aggression
- Medical cause? Head injury, fever, intoxication, dehydration

## Panic Attack

**Act immediately:**
1. Move to quiet, calm space. Dim lights. Sit or lie down.
2. Say: "You are having a panic attack. It feels like dying but you are not. This passes in 10-20 minutes. I am staying."
3. Do not say: "Calm down" or "You are fine." Both escalate.

**Box breathing, count aloud:** 4 counts in, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Repeat 5-10 cycles.

**5-4-3-2-1 grounding:** Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.

**Physical grounding:** cold water on face or hands activates dive reflex, slows heart rate.

Do not leave until symptoms subside (10-30 min).

## Suicidal Crisis

**Remove means immediately:** weapons, rope, cords, medications, sharp objects.

**Ask directly:** "Are you thinking about killing yourself?"
- Asking does not plant the idea. It gives permission to talk.
- Specific plan + means + timeline = highest risk.

**Listen without arguing.** Validate: "I hear you. You are in pain. I am glad you told me."

**Safety contract:** "Promise me you will not hurt yourself for the next 24 hours. If you feel this way, come find me first."

**24/7 supervision for 72 hours:**
- Never alone. Rotate caregivers every 2-4 hours.
- Remove privacy temporarily. Hard but necessary.
- Give short-term purpose: "I need your help tomorrow morning."

**Watch for:** sudden calm after deep depression. A decision may have been made.

## Psychosis

**Recognize:** hallucinations, delusions, incoherent speech, bizarre behavior.

**Check medical causes first:** sleep deprivation, dehydration, fever, head injury, drug use or withdrawal.

**Communicate:**
- Speak slowly, low voice, use their name often
- Do not argue with delusions. Say: "I do not hear it, but I believe you do."
- Validate feelings, not the delusion: "You seem frightened. I am not here to hurt you."
- Offer choices for a sense of control: "Would you like to sit inside or outside?"

**Environment:** quiet, dim, uncrowded, no sharp objects nearby.

**Basic needs:** offer water frequently, simple food, remind about bathroom. Dehydration worsens all symptoms.

## Depression Shutdown

**Recognize:** will not move or speak, not eating or drinking, staring blankly.

**Physical care first:** put water to lips, offer simple food, move to warmth and shelter.

**Communicate:**
- Speak to them, not about them while they are present
- Narrate your care: "I am putting a blanket on you. Water is here."
- No demands or urgency. Pressure worsens it.

**Micro-goals:** "Can you drink this sip?" Celebrate if they do. Build slowly.

## Violent Outburst

**Your safety first:** maintain distance, position near exit, do not corner them, call for help.

**De-escalate:**
- Stay calm. Your calm is contagious.
- Hands visible and open. Relaxed posture. Neutral face.
- Speak slowly: "I am not here to hurt you. Tell me what is wrong."
- Offer choices: "You can stay here or go outside. You decide."

**If armed:** back away slowly while talking. Do not grab for the weapon.

**Restraint only as absolute last resort:** life in immediate danger, multiple people required, position on side not face-down.

## Post-Crisis

**Hours 1-24:** never alone, water, food, sleep, warmth.

**Days 2-3:** gradual return to routine, light tasks, social contact, debrief when ready.

**Week 1-2:** daily check-ins, assigned meaningful role, reduced workload, watch for relapse. Most relapses occur days 2-7.

## Fatal Mistakes

- Dismissing suicidal threats as attention-seeking. Most attempts follow warnings.
- Leaving suicidal person alone even for a minute. Hanging takes 5 minutes.
- Arguing with psychosis. Causes escalation and destroys trust.
- Restraining prematurely. Causes violence rather than preventing it.
- Shaming: "Man up." Worsens depression and destroys trust.
- Ignoring medical causes. Fever or head injury can mimic psychosis.
- No post-crisis monitoring. Most relapses peak at days 2-7.
- One caregiver for 72 hours. Burnout causes missed warning signs.
