# Urban Water Sources

Urban disasters cut water supply but buildings hold 40-100+ gallons of extractable water. Act within 24-48 hours of water loss before contamination spreads.

Average person needs 1 gallon per day minimum. A 40-gallon water heater supplies a family of 4 for 10 days.

## Water Heater

Primary source. 30-80 gallons of pre-heated, clean water. Location: basement, utility closet, garage, or laundry room. Cylindrical tank, 4-5 feet tall.

Extraction steps:
- Turn off power or gas: Electric = flip breaker labeled Water Heater. Gas = turn knob to Pilot or Off.
- Turn off cold water inlet valve (top of tank, blue marking). Turn clockwise until tight.
- Attach garden hose to drain valve at the bottom of the tank.
- Open drain valve counterclockwise.
- Open the T&P (pressure relief) valve on the side near the top. Lift the lever. This breaks vacuum and allows water to flow.
- Discard first gallon (sediment). Remaining water is clean.
- Fill and label all containers.

Drainage takes 30-60 minutes total.

Capacity: 30-40 gal small, 40-50 gal standard, 50-80 gal large.

## Toilet Tanks

Safe only if no chemical cleaners are present. Never use toilet bowls — fecal contamination.

Check before using:
- Clear water, no chemical smell: safe.
- Blue or colored water, slimy, cloudy, or strong smell: skip.

Bail water with cup or ladle into clean container. Purify before drinking: boil 1 minute or filter. Multiple toilets = 3-6 gallons total.

## Pipe Drainage

Water sits in vertical pipes between floors. Gravity drains it down when a vacuum break is created at the top.

Steps:
- Open the highest faucet in the building (top floor or roof). This lets air in.
- Open the lowest faucet (basement or ground floor).
- Collect water from lowest point: bathtub or large sink.
- Allow 10-20 minutes to drain fully.

Yield: Multi-story building = 5-15 gallons. Single-story home = 1-3 gallons.

## Other Sources

Ice maker or freezer ice: 5-10 lbs = 0.5-1 gallon. Safe — made from tap water before the disaster.

Canned vegetables and fruit: 2-4 oz liquid per can. Safe to drink directly.

Hot tub or pool: Not safe for drinking. Use for flushing toilets and washing only.

Radiator water: Assume contaminated. Non-potable only.

## Water Treatment

Water heater water: Safe as-is. Optionally boil 1 minute for extra safety.

All other sources must be purified before drinking.

Boiling: 1 minute at sea level. 3 minutes above 6500 feet elevation.

Bleach treatment (use only unscented plain bleach):
- 1 quart water: 2 drops.
- 1 gallon water: 8 drops (1/8 tsp).
- 5 gallons water: 1/2 tsp.
- Stir, wait 30 minutes. Should smell faintly of chlorine. If not, repeat dose and wait 15 more minutes.

Filter cloudy water through cloth first to remove sediment, then boil or treat chemically.

## Extraction Priority

- First: Water heater (40-50 gal, cleanest, easiest).
- Second: Toilet tanks (1-2 gal each, purify first).
- Third: Pipe drainage (5-15 gal, moderate effort).
- Fourth: Ice maker or freezer (0.5-1 gal, safe).
- Fifth: Canned goods liquid (minimal but safe).
- Last resort: Hot tub (extensive purification required).

Skip entirely: Toilet bowls, radiators, flood water.

## Container Storage

Use food-grade plastic (HDPE #2 or PET #1) or glass canning jars. Avoid milk jugs (protein residue grows bacteria) and any container that previously held chemicals.

Fill to the top to minimize air and bacterial growth. Label with date and source. Store in a cool, dark place. Shelf life: 6-12 months properly sealed.

## When to Skip a Source

Skip any water that is brown or black, has a strong chemical smell, has an oily sheen on the surface, has dead animals nearby, or appears mixed with flood water.

Contaminated water can kill in hours. Dehydration takes 3 days. If in doubt, skip the source.

## Evacuation Decision

Do not attempt extraction if the building structure is compromised, fire or gas is present, or flooding is entering the building.

Water heater extraction takes 30-60 minutes. Only attempt if safe to stay that long. Take what you can carry and move to safety.

## Common Mistakes

- Drinking from toilet bowl: Use tank only, and purify it.
- Not turning off heater power before draining.
- Waiting more than 48 hours to extract water.
- Forgetting to open T&P valve: Water will not flow without it.
- Filling containers halfway: Fill to top to prevent bacterial growth.
- Not labeling containers: Always record date, source, and treatment method.
