# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Blizzards: nor'easters bring 24 in+
- Ice storms: coats everything in ice
- Dense boreal forest: easy to get lost
- Black bears: common in rural areas
- Lyme disease: tick-borne, very common
- Spring flooding from snowmelt rivers

## Water Sources
- Rivers and streams: abundant
- Springs in glacial till soil: clean
- Maple sap: available in early spring
- Rainwater catchment: reliable
- Filter and boil all surface water

## Food Sources
- Blueberries and raspberries: summer
- Hickory and beech nuts: fall
- Trout in cold streams: year-round
- White-tailed deer: most rural areas
- Fiddlehead ferns: early spring
- Ramps and wild garlic: spring woods

## Shelter Priorities
- Hemlock groves: dry and wind-blocked
- Stone walls: found throughout region
- Old barns and farmhouses: common
- Debris hut: leaves abundant in fall
- Snow trench if stranded in winter

## Navigation
- Appalachian Trail: white blazes
- Roads are irregular grid: use map
- Rivers flow generally east to ocean
- Rock walls mark old property lines
- Ski resorts visible from ridges

## Evacuation Routes
- I-95 corridor: main NE artery
- State routes parallel rivers
- Train routes connect major cities
- Ferry service in coastal areas
