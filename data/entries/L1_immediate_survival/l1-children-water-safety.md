# Child Water Safety

## Drowning Recognition

Drowning is SILENT. No splashing. No calling for help.
Watch for:
- Head low in water, mouth at or below the surface
- Head tilted back, mouth open, eyes glassy or closed
- Body vertical, little or no leg kick
- Arms pushing down on water, not waving
- Hair over forehead, no response to voice
If you see any of these: act NOW. You have 30-60 seconds.

## Supervision Rules

- Touch supervision: within arm's reach for children under 5. Always.
- Active supervision: eyes on the child at all times near water. No phone.
- Designated watcher: one adult's only job is watching. Rotate every 15 min.
- One adult per child under 3 in any water.
- Count heads before, during, and after every water activity.
- Child is missing: check the water first.

## Water Hazard Rules

Any standing water is a drowning hazard.
- Buckets, coolers, rain barrels: drain or cover when not in use
- Flooded areas after disaster: treat as open water, no children near it
- Bathing containers: empty immediately after use
- Natural bodies of water: rope off or fence the shoreline if possible
Never leave a child alone near water for any reason.

## Drowning Prevention

Use all layers — none alone is enough:
- Barriers: fence with locked gate, pool cover when not supervised
- Flotation: properly fitted PFD on non-swimmers near open water
- Swim skills: teach children to float on their back as early as possible
- Rules: no running, no pushing, no unsupervised water access
- Survival float: back float + call for help = core skill for any child

## Immediate Response

1. Do not enter the water unless trained for rescue swimming.
2. Throw a rope, pole, or flotation device first.
3. Call for help loudly while assisting.
4. Once out of water: check for breathing.
5. Not breathing: begin CPR — 30 compressions, 2 breaths. Repeat.
6. Continue until they breathe or help arrives.
7. All near-drowning victims need medical evaluation even if they seem fine.
