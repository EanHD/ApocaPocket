# Urban Navigation

Without GPS, average people get lost within 1-2 miles of familiar territory. Plan routes, identify cardinal directions, and know your landmarks before moving.

## Pre-Move Planning

Determine destination: exact address or intersection plus a landmark description.
Estimate time: urban walking = 2-2.5 mph (obstacles, navigation stops). 5 miles = 2-2.5 hours.

Map sources (best to worst): paper road map, printed internet map, hand-drawn from memory.
If no map: follow major streets toward city center. Ask locals if safe.

Plan three routes:
- Primary: main roads, direct, well-marked.
- Alternate 1: parallel streets, 1-2 blocks over.
- Alternate 2: completely different approach from a different major street.

## Cardinal Directions

Shadow stick method (accurate to 5-10 degrees, 15 min setup):
1. Place vertical stick in ground.
2. Mark the shadow tip with a stone.
3. Wait 15 minutes. Mark the new shadow tip.
4. Line between marks = West (first) to East (second).
5. Perpendicular to that line = North-South.

Quick sun method (rough, instant):
- 6-10 AM: sun = East, shadow = West.
- 10 AM to 2 PM: sun = South, shadow = North.
- 2-6 PM: sun = West, shadow = East.

Analog watch method: point hour hand at sun. Halfway between hour hand and 12 = South.

North Star at night (Northern Hemisphere):
1. Find the Big Dipper.
2. Draw line through the two outer edge stars of the cup.
3. Extend line 5x the distance between those stars.
4. Bright star at end = Polaris (true North, accurate to 1 degree).

Street grid: many US cities run cardinal. Numbered streets often increase heading North or East.
Walk one block and note if address numbers increase or decrease to determine your direction.

Satellite dishes: in Northern Hemisphere, dishes point roughly South toward the equator.

## Distance Estimation

Pace count: walk a known 100-meter distance and count paces (left foot only).
Average adult = 100 paces per 100 meters. Count paces during travel. 1,000 paces = ~1 km.

Time method: flat pavement = 3 mph (20 min/mile). Urban obstacles = 2-2.5 mph (24-30 min/mile).
Heavy load (40+ lbs) = 1.5-2 mph.

Blocks: US city block = ~1/10 mile (100-150 meters). 10 blocks = ~1 mile.

## Navigation Techniques

Major street following: stick to wide, well-marked roads with fewer turns and more landmarks.
Turn toward destination when cross-streets provide opportunity.

Landmark chaining: identify a visible landmark 0.5-1 mile ahead. Walk to it.
Upon arrival, identify the next landmark. Repeat. Always have a visual target.

Dead reckoning: walk a known cardinal direction for an estimated distance.
Re-check heading every 15 minutes. Humans drift 15-20 degrees off course without correction.

Backtracking markers: chalk or spray arrows on buildings and poles. Mark direction of travel.
Remove markers on return trip to prevent others from tracking your route.

## Route Hazards

Blocked routes: detour at most 0.5 miles from primary route. Return to primary as soon as possible.
If all 3 routes blocked, shelter in place for 1-2 hours and reassess.

Night navigation: walk 1-1.5 mph. Use stars. Minimize flashlight use. Stay on major streets.
Smoke: wet cloth over nose and mouth. Detour upwind of smoke source.

Disorientation: stop immediately. Sit for 5-10 minutes. Re-orient with sun or street signs.
Backtrack to last known position if needed. Do not keep walking when lost.

## Group Navigation

Point person system:
- Navigator reads map and makes route decisions.
- Point walks 5-10 feet ahead and watches for hazards.
- Rear guard watches behind for anyone following.
- Vulnerable members (children, elderly, injured) walk in the middle.

Hand signals: fist up = stop. Point = direction. Tap head = look and listen.
Rest discipline: group walks at slowest member pace. Breaks every 60-90 minutes.

## Stop and Shelter

Stop navigation and find shelter if:
- Injured (twisted ankle, exhaustion, cuts).
- Night falls with no safe destination visible.
- Weather turns severe.
- All 3 planned routes are blocked.
- Threat level increases significantly.

Wait up to 8 hours in temporary shelter. Reassess, rest, then continue or return home.
