# Toxic Plant Avoidance

## Why It Matters

- Contact plants disable survivors
- Ingestion plants can be fatal
- Children mistake berries for food
- Burning toxic plants damages lungs
- Several are widespread and common
- Learn these 4 before wilderness trips

## Poison Ivy/Oak/Sumac

- Ivy: 3 leaflets, waxy, variable shape
- Oak: 3-7 lobed leaflets like oak
- Sumac: 7-13 leaflets on red stem
- All cause severe blistering rash
- Rule: leaves of 3, let it be
- Urushiol oil persists on gear years

## Hemlock (Poison)

- Musty unpleasant smell when crushed
- Purple-spotted hollow stems
- White flower clusters (umbel shape)
- All parts toxic, no safe amount
- Do not confuse with carrot or parsley
- ID before foraging any white umbel

## Water Hemlock

- Hollow jointed stems with chambers
- Grows in wet areas, streams, marshes
- Most acutely toxic plant in NA
- Causes severe seizures
- White flower clusters near water
- Never eat any white-flowered umbel

## Nightshade Species

- Deadly nightshade: black berries
- Bittersweet: red or orange berries
- Jimsonweed: white trumpet flower
- All parts toxic, especially berries
- Green tomato-like fruit when young
- Attractive but extremely toxic

## Exposure Response

- Rinse skin with cold water fast
- Do not rub, spreads urushiol oil
- Wash all clothing and gear
- Treat rash with hydrocortisone cream
- Evacuate for any ingestion event

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Identify 4 toxic plants
  from 10 feet away in your region
- Sign of mastery: Spot and avoid
  all 4 on a 5-mile trail walk
