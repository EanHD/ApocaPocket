# Literacy Circles

## Why Literacy

The human brain requires intellectual stimulation. Without it, we deteriorate.

- Lack of engagement accelerates dementia onset by 5-8 years
- Reading 30 min reduces cortisol 68%
- Literacy circles 2x per week reduce depression risk 38%
- A community that stops learning dies mentally

## With Books Available

**Weekly reading circle (90 min):**
- Opening 5 min: gather, recap last session
- Reading 20-30 min: take turns reading aloud, pause to clarify vocabulary
- Discussion 30-40 min: what happened, why, what does it mean for us
- Reflection 10 min: key takeaways, questions to ponder
- Closing 5 min: set next assignment

**Book selection priority:**
1. Survival knowledge: medicine, agriculture, building
2. Philosophy and psychology: meaning, resilience, ethics
3. Fiction: entertainment, empathy, imagination

**Reading aloud is inclusive.** Non-readers can participate. Shared experience builds social bonds.

**Practice oral recitation:**
- Key texts memorized as backup if paper is lost
- Weekly recitation circles to keep memory sharp
- Test each other for accuracy

**Materials when paper is scarce:** bark, animal hide, clay tablets, cloth. Writing tools: charcoal sticks, plant inks, carved styli.

## When Literacy Lost

Rebuild before all who remember are gone. Teach phonics from scratch.

**Phase 1: Letter-sound associations (2-4 weeks)**
- Teach in high-frequency order: s, a, t, p, i, n first
- Show letter, say sound (not letter name), practice 10-20 times, write it
- 10-15 min daily
- Materials: dirt or sand to write in, charcoal on bark, carved clay

**Phase 2: Blending sounds into words (4-8 weeks)**
- Sound out CVC words: cat, dog, sit, run, bed
- Add digraphs: sh, ch, th
- Add blends: st, bl, gr
- 20-30 min daily, 10-20 words per session

**Phase 3: Sentences and comprehension (8-16 weeks)**
- Simple sentences: âThe cat sat on a mat.â
- Sight words to memorize: the, is, of, and, a, to, in, it, you, that
- Create simple illustrated readers using known words
- 30-40 min daily

**Phase 4: Writing (concurrent)**
- Trace, copy, dictation, then composition
- First exercises: name, family names, common words, short sentences

## Core Vocabulary

Covers 80-90% of everyday communication. Teach 5-10 new words per week.

**Categories:**
- People, body parts, nature, food, shelter (nouns)
- Walk, run, eat, drink, sleep, build, cook, hunt, gather (verbs)
- Big, small, hot, cold, wet, dry, good, bad (descriptors)
- I, you, he, she, we, in, on, at, and, but, if, because (function words)

Use words in sentences. Point to objects. Act out verbs. Context builds memory.

## Group Learning Methods

**Socratic method:** ask open questions, students discover, no lecture.
- What happened? Why did the character act this way? What would you do?

**Shared reading:** one person reads aloud, pass to next, pause to discuss. All skill levels included.

**Memorization contest:** select a passage, one week to memorize, recitation night, celebrate. Preserves critical texts and builds confidence.

## Children First

- Neuroplasticity makes children faster learners
- Ages 4-6: oral language, songs, rhymes, letter recognition through play
- Ages 7-10: systematic phonics, 20-30 min daily, reading aloud to them
- Ages 11+: independent reading, writing, teach others

**Keep it engaging:** stories with adventure and humor, word games, praise progress.

## Warning Signs

- No literacy activity, increasing boredom and apathy
- Children not being taught
- Books neglected or damaged
- No intellectual discussion in the community

**Consequences:** cognitive decline, depression from boredom, knowledge dies with elders.

**Response:** start a literacy circle, even small. Short sessions. Make it social with food and celebration.

## Mission

1. If books exist: start a reading circle now
2. If no books: write down critical knowledge to preserve it
3. If literacy is lost: teach phonics, rebuild from scratch
4. Prioritize children: they carry knowledge to the next generation
5. Make it social: regular gatherings build habit and bonding

**A community that stops learning is in its final generation. Teach it. Use it. Preserve it.**
