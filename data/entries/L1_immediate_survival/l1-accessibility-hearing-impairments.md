# Hearing Impairments

## Visual Alert Systems

Replace sound-based alerts with visual cues:
- Red = danger/stop. Green = safe. Yellow = caution. Be consistent.
- Flashlight signal: 3 short flashes = emergency.
- Use mirrors at entry points to extend field of view.
- Signal flags or bright cloth at perimeter: move if threat detected.
- Assign a sighted partner for every watch rotation.

## Tactile Alert Signals

Agree on these signals and practice them in advance:
- Two shoulder taps = attention needed
- Two taps + point = look in that direction
- Firm grip on arm = stop moving
- Both hands on shoulders = get down now
- Rope tied to wrist while sleeping = wake-up alert

## Communication Backup

- Keep pen and notepad or a whiteboard in the common area.
- Pre-agreed gesture set: simple, consistent, practiced.
- Flashcards for core messages: help, danger, water, food, go.
- Face the person directly. Do not cover your mouth.
- If they lip-read: clear lighting, speak at normal pace.
- Use text/SMS if any devices are operational.

## Safe Positioning

Deaf and hard-of-hearing persons cannot rely on sound for awareness.
- Seat them facing the main entry at all times.
- Position them next to a sighted partner.
- Include them in watch rotation using pre-agreed visual signals.
- Brief them on hazards at every new location.
- During movement: sighted guide beside them, light grip on arm.
