# Regional Survival

## Key Hazards
- Alligators: all freshwater bodies
- Hurricane season: June to November
- Heat and humidity: illness risk fast
- Flooding: little high ground exists
- Venomous snakes: water moccasin
- Lightning: most strikes in US here

## Water Sources
- Freshwater springs: many, scattered
- Rainfall: 50-60 inches per year
- Avoid swamp water without treatment
- Reverse osmosis near coastline
- Coconut water: coastal emergency use

## Food Sources
- Fish: abundant in lakes and coast
- Sabal palm heart: edible inner core
- Feral pigs: common statewide
- Clams and oysters: tidal flats
- Sea grapes: coastal scrub areas
- Prickly pear cactus: dry areas

## Shelter Priorities
- Concrete block structures only
- Avoid mobile homes in any storm
- Elevated ground: rare; use bridges
- Mosquito protection is critical
- Shade first; cooling is survival

## Navigation
- Flat terrain: sky is main cue
- Waterways crisscross the state
- Major roads run N-S: I-75, I-95
- I-4 crosses central east-west
- Coastline is a constant reference

## Evacuation Routes
- I-75, I-95 north from peninsula
- Contraflow initiated for storms
- Leave 48 hours before landfall
- Coastal routes flood first: go inland
