# Cold Water Survival

## Why It Matters

- 60F water is dangerous within 1 hr
- 32F water incapacitates in 3 min
- Panic causes more deaths than cold
- Correct actions multiply survival time
- Most people drown from incapacitation
- Not from core temperature alone

## 1-10-1 Rule

- 1 minute: cold shock response peak
  (hyperventilation, gasp reflex)
- Stay calm, do not thrash
- 10 minutes: swimming incapacitation
  (hands, arms lose function)
- Use first 10 min to get to object
- 1 hour: hypothermia unconsciousness
  in average adult in 50F water

## Cold Shock Phase

- Gasp and hyperventilate is normal
- Do NOT panic and thrash
- Hold on to anything nearby
- Control breathing within 60 sec
- Keep head above water first priority
- Wait for breathing to normalize

## HELP and Huddle

- HELP: Heat Escape Lessening Position
- Pull knees to chest, arms over chest
- Reduces heat loss 50% vs treading
- Huddle: group facing inward, sides touching
- Increases survival time for group
- Do not use if boat or object nearby

## Rescue Priority

- Reach, throw, row, go (in that order)
- Rescuer in water = two victims
- Throw rope or line first
- Wade with pole if safe
- Last resort: swim rescue with buoy
- Professional rescuers for submersion

## What Can Go Wrong

- Removing clothing to swim faster
- Thrashing in cold shock phase
- Swimming long distances, exhausting
- Leaving HELP position to wave

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Demonstrate HELP position
  and hold it calmly for 5 minutes
- Sign of mastery: Full cold shock
  breathing control in 60 seconds
