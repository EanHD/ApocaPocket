# Wildfire Smoke Safety

## What This Covers
Protect yourself when wildfire smoke reaches your area.

## AQI Thresholds
- 0-50: Good. Normal activity OK.
- 51-100: Moderate. Sensitive groups limit outdoor time.
- 101-150: Unhealthy for sensitive groups. Stay inside.
- 151-200: Unhealthy. Everyone stay inside.
- 201+: Hazardous. Do not go outside.

## What Smoke Does
- PM2.5 particles penetrate deep into lungs.
- Causes inflammation, reduced lung function.
- Worsens heart and respiratory conditions.
- Effects can last days after exposure.

## Shelter in Place
- Close all windows and doors.
- Seal gaps with wet towels or tape.
- Turn off HVAC — set to recirculate only.
- Run air purifier with HEPA filter if available.
- Stay in interior rooms away from outside walls.

## Masks That Help
- N95/KN95: Filters PM2.5. Only effective option.
- Surgical mask: Minimal protection. Not enough.
- Cloth mask: No protection from smoke particles.
- Fit matters: seal tight to face, no gaps.

## High-Risk Groups
- Pregnant women and unborn babies.
- Children — lungs still developing.
- Elderly (65+).
- Anyone with asthma, COPD, heart disease.
- Take extra precautions at lower AQI thresholds.

## In Your Car
- Keep windows fully closed.
- Set AC/heat to recirculate (NOT fresh air intake).
- Check dashboard recirculate button — it matters.

## Emergency Signs
- Chest tightness or pain.
- Shortness of breath at rest.
- Wheezing or persistent cough.
- Gray or blue lips or fingernails — call 911 now.

## Evacuate When
- AQI exceeds 200 and shelter is inadequate.
- You cannot breathe comfortably indoors.
- Embers or flames are visible near structure.
- Authorities issue evacuation order.

## Recovery
- Most smoke events last 1-7 days.
- Drink water — helps clear airways.
- Avoid exercise until AQI returns below 100.
- See a doctor if symptoms persist after smoke clears.
