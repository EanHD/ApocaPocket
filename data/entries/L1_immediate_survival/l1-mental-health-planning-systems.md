# Planning Systems

Spend 10% of your time planning to make the other 90% effective.
Reactive = survive. Proactive = thrive.

## SMART Goals

Vague goals fail. Specific goals succeed.
SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

Vague: get better at foraging.
SMART: identify and safely harvest 10 local edible plants by end of month. 1 hour practice every 3 days.

Before setting any goal, verify:
- What exactly will you do, and by when?
- How will you know you succeeded?
- Do you have the resources and time required?
- Why does this matter right now?

## Goal Hierarchy

Mission (multi-year): establish sustainable, secure community.
Yearly goals: grow 6 months of food. Build defensible perimeter. Train 5 people in first aid.
Quarterly goals: Q1 clear and prepare soil. Q2 plant and irrigate. Q3 harvest. Q4 repair.
Monthly goals: break quarterly into weekly phases with clear deliverables.
Weekly goals: break monthly into daily tasks.

If you complete all weekly goals you hit monthly. All monthly = quarterly. Cascade up.

## Priority Matrix

Sort tasks by urgency and importance:
- Urgent plus Important: DO NOW. Injury, fire, immediate threat.
- Not Urgent plus Important: SCHEDULE. Training, maintenance, skill development. This is where growth happens.
- Urgent but Not Important: DELEGATE or minimize. Routine admin.
- Neither: ELIMINATE. Ruthlessly.

The trap: living in crisis mode. The fix: invest time in prevention to reduce future crises.

## Daily Planning

The night before (10 min):
- Review what got done today and what did not.
- Brain dump everything for tomorrow.
- Mark your top 3 must-do tasks.
- Assign tasks to specific time blocks.
- Gather tools or materials needed for tomorrow.

Rule: if you complete your top 3 tasks, the day is a success. Everything else is bonus.

Time blocking example:
- 0700-1000: Priority 1 (highest-value task of the day).
- 1000-1200: Priority 2.
- 1300-1500: Priority 3.
- 1500-1700: Routine tasks.
- 1900-2000: Planning and documentation.

Realistic expectations: things take 2x longer than estimated.
Plan for 4-6 hours of productive work per day, not 12.

## Weekly Planning

Sunday review (1 hour):
- What went well? What did not?
- What are next week 3-5 priorities?
- Assign big tasks to specific days.
- Identify resource needs and gather early.
- What could go wrong? Prepare contingencies.

## Contingency Planning

For each critical system, identify single points of failure.
Rate each by likelihood (low/medium/high) times severity (minor/serious/catastrophic).

Example for water supply:
- Primary: stream 500 meters north.
- Plan B: rainwater collection (tarp and cistern).
- Plan C: well location already scouted.
- Trigger: if stream drops below 6 inches, activate Plan B immediately.

Rule: two is one, one is none. Three is comfortable.

## Resource Allocation

Daily energy split:
- 70% immediate needs (food, water, shelter, security today).
- 20% short-term projects (next week and month).
- 10% long-term investment (skills, tools, infrastructure).

Adjust for threat level: high threat = 90% immediate. Stable environment = 50/30/20 split.

Energy by time of day:
- High energy (morning): hardest tasks first.
- Medium energy (midday): steady ongoing work.
- Low energy (evening): routine tasks, planning, documentation.

Rest is productive time. Recovery enables future performance.

## Group Coordination

Daily stand-up (10 min, whole group):
1. What I did yesterday.
2. What I am doing today.
3. What is blocking me.
Keep it brief. Note blockers. Address them after the meeting, not during.

Weekly planning meeting (1 hour):
1. Wins: what went well?
2. Challenges: what struggled?
3. Next week priorities.
4. Assignments: who does what by when.

Document decisions in writing. Memory fails and people disagree on what was said.
Assign by skill, preference, and capacity. Rotate roles to prevent bottlenecks.

## Quick Reference

Daily: top 3 priorities, time block, gather materials, execute.
Weekly: review wins and losses, set priorities, assign tasks, identify resource needs.
Goal check: Specific? Measurable? Achievable? Relevant? Time-bound?
Priority: Urgent plus Important = now. Not Urgent plus Important = schedule (that is how you grow).
Resources: 70% today, 20% this month, 10% future.
