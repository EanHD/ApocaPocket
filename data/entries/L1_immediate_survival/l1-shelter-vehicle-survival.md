# Vehicle as Shelter

## Why It Matters

- Vehicle is better shelter than open air
- Far easier to spot for rescuers
- Contains emergency equipment
- Retains heat with minimal effort
- Most survival deaths happen away
  from the broken-down vehicle
- Stay with vehicle unless 100% sure

## Heat Management

- Run engine max 10 min per hour
- Crack window slightly while running
- Check exhaust clear of snow first
- Body heat + small candle heats cab
- Use all available insulation/clothing
- Cover windows to retain heat

## Signaling from Vehicle

- Raise hood or trunk to signal
- Tie bright cloth to antenna
- Turn hazard lights on briefly
- Flash headlights at aircraft
- Arrange rocks/debris in SOS nearby
- Keep phone charged with car power

## Water and Food

- Check for any food in car first
- Floor mats, leather = chew source
  in extreme scenario (calories)
- Melt snow in container, do not eat
  snow directly - cools core temp
- Radiator fluid is toxic, avoid
- Windshield washer toxic, avoid

## Conserving Battery

- Use interior light minimally
- Do not leave radio on drain
- Short engine runs only
- Keep phone in inner pocket
  to preserve battery in cold
- Prioritize starting over charging

## What Can Go Wrong

- Exhaust blocked by snow drift
- CO builds up in sealed car
- Wandering from car in panic
- Running out of fuel on first night

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: 24-hr stay in vehicle with
  no engine, using body heat only
- Sign of mastery: Temp above 40F
  in car at dawn after cold night
