## Start Here

Three primitive shelter types  -  choose based on time, materials, and conditions.
- **Debris Hut:** Best insulation, slow to build (3-6 hrs), cold/wet climates
- **Lean-To:** Fast (1-2 hrs), good rain shed, needs fire in front for warmth
- **A-Frame:** Moderate (2-3 hrs), balanced wind+rain, no fire required
Build BEFORE dark  -  all take longer than expected.
Site check first: no widow-makers overhead, no low-ground flooding risk.

## Debris Hut

Best choice for cold nights  -  body heat warms the tight space.
1. Find or cut ridge pole 8-10 ft long.
2. Rest one end on ground, prop other end on stump/fork 3 ft high.
3. Lean ribs (sticks) against both sides of the ridge pole.
4. Pile leaves, grass, bark, or debris 2-3 ft thick over entire frame.
5. Fill interior floor with 4+ inches of dry leaves for ground insulation.
6. Crawl in feet-first; plug entrance with debris bundle or pack.
Tight fit is intentional  -  body heat warms a small space in minutes.

## Lean-To

Fastest build; open front pairs with a fire for heat reflection.
1. Lash or wedge a horizontal ridge pole between two trees, 4-5 ft high.
2. Lean poles against ridge at 45 deg angle to form the roof slope.
3. Layer bark, branches, then leaves from bottom up (shingle style).
4. Build a fire 4-6 ft in front  -  reflected heat warms the interior.
5. Optional: add side walls by leaning branches at steep angles.
Less insulation than debris hut  -  not ideal for below-freezing without fire.

## A-Frame

Enclosed on three sides; moderate insulation without requiring fire.
1. Plant two forked sticks in ground, 6-7 ft apart along centerline.
2. Lay ridge pole across the forks (waist to chest height at entry end).
3. Lean poles along both sides from ground to ridge, like tent ribs.
4. Cover frame with bark slabs, then branches, then deep leaf layer.
5. Leave one short end open as entrance; stuff a debris door to block it.
Seal gaps  -  small enclosed volume holds body heat well.

## Materials Guide

**Best insulation (dry):** Dead leaves, dry grass, pine needles, cattail fluff
**Best structure:** Straight poles 1-2 in diameter, green wood bends, dead wood rigid
**Avoid:** Wet leaves (cold, compress), live pine boughs (resin sticks, heavy)
**Thickness guide:** 2 ft minimum coverage for rain shed; 3 ft for overnight warmth
**Ground layer:** Never skip  -  4+ inches of dry debris under sleeping area
Shake all materials before use  -  insects and small animals nest in debris piles.
