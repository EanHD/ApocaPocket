# Tree Pit Snow Shelter

## Why It Matters

- Fastest snow shelter to make
- Tree does most of the work
- Dense conifers trap air heat
- Natural overhead protection
- Minimal energy expenditure
- Perfect when exhausted and cold

## Finding the Right Tree

- Large old conifer (spruce or fir)
- Wide dense canopy at base
- Deep snow well around trunk
- No large dead branches above
- Sheltered from wind direction
- Multiple trees together = better

## Improving the Pit

- Clear snow from pit floor down
- Expose ground or duff layer
- Build up snow walls on open sides
- Break dead lower branches for
  floor insulation bedding
- Deepen pit if not body-depth

## Using the Space

- Crawl in from downhill side
- Block entrance with pack or snow
- Sit or lie on insulated layer
- Use trunk as backrest for heat
- Overhead branches shed snow load
- Canopy reduces radiant heat loss

## Optional Roof

- Cut branches from canopy edges
- Lean across pit opening
- Pack snow on branches for roof
- Leave vent gap at top of lean
- This significantly raises warmth
- Even a partial roof helps

## What Can Go Wrong

- Snow load dumps off branches
- Pit too shallow, no wind block
- Tree not dense enough - open above
- Wet snow makes tree less effective

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Locate and improve pit
  for overnight use in 20 min
- Sign of mastery: Sleep through
  a night in below-zero temps
