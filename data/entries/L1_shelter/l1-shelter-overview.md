# Shelter Overview

Shelter is priority 3 after bleeding and breathing.
In cold or wet weather it becomes priority 1 — you
can die of exposure in under 3 hours.
Match shelter type to your situation and materials.

## Time vs Warmth

**15-30 min — Emergency warmth:**
Debris pile, hollow log, leaf pile.
Not shelter — buys time while you build.

**1-2 hrs — Functional shelter:**
Lean-to, A-frame, tarp setup.
Rain and wind protection. Needs insulated floor.

**2-4 hrs — Good shelter:**
Debris hut, wickiup dome.
Warmer than a tent. Lasts days to weeks.

**2-4 weeks — Permanent shelter:**
Log cabin. Lasts years. Requires axe, dry site.

## Decision Guide

**In the wilderness, no gear?**
→ Primitive Shelter Types (debris hut first)

**Have a tarp or emergency blanket?**
→ Tarp Shelter Setups (8 configurations)

**In heavy snow or alpine environment?**
→ Snow Cave Construction (stays above 0°C)

**In your car?**
→ Using Vehicle as Shelter (fastest option)

**Car + winter conditions?**
→ Vehicle Winter Survival

**In a city or building?**
→ Urban Emergency Shelter

**Long-term camp (weeks)?**
→ Wickiup Dome Shelter or Log Cabin

## The Floor Rule

**Ground steals heat 25x faster than air.**

Build your floor insulation first, always.
Target R-5 minimum (6"+ dry leaves/debris).
See: Ground Insulation for R-value guide.

Without floor insulation, any shelter fails
in cold weather — you will lose heat faster
than your body can produce it.

## Site Selection

1. Drainage — never build in a low spot
2. Wind protection — behind a hill or trees
3. Not under dead branches (widowmakers)
4. Not in a dry riverbed or flood zone
5. Close to building materials and water
6. Visible to rescuers (if signaling)

See: Shelter Site Selection for full guide.

## Thermal Stack

**Layer these to stay alive:**
1. Floor insulation (R-5 minimum)
2. Wind block (walls, tarp, windbreak)
3. Rain protection (roof or cover)
4. Body insulation (dry clothing layers)
5. Heat source (fire, body heat)

**Missing any layer = heat loss.**
Debris hut does all 5 with natural materials.
