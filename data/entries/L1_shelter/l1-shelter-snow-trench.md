# Snow Trench Bivouac

## Why It Matters

- Can be dug in 20-30 minutes
- Blocks lethal wind exposure
- Usable with minimal tools
- Best option in open flat terrain
- When time/energy too low for dome
- Trench is faster than quinzhee

## Site Selection

- Choose location with deep snow
- Avoid avalanche runout zones
- Not at base of corniced ridges
- Mark location with poles or skis
- Place in natural wind shadow
- Orient trench parallel to wind

## Digging the Trench

- Trench: body length plus 12 in
- Width: shoulder width plus 6 in
- Depth: 3 feet if possible
- Stack snow blocks on windward edge
- Creates additional windbreak wall
- Dig deeper if in extreme cold

## Covering the Trench

- Lay ski poles or sticks across top
- Cover with tarp, poncho, or branches
- Weight edges with snow blocks
- Leave one end partially open
  for ventilation
- Snow block roof is best if possible
- Test cover holds weight before sleep

## Insulation Inside

- Line bottom with pack, branches
- Sleeping pad is critical here
- Sleeping bag stays dry in trench
- Hat and insulating layers on
- Curl to conserve body heat
- Partner trench together if 2 people

## What Can Go Wrong

- Trench too narrow to turn over
- Cover collapses under wet snow
- Wind shifts, trench faces wrong way
- Drowning in spindrift if not covered

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Functional trench with cover
  in 30 minutes or less
- Sign of mastery: Warm through
  a night above -20C in trench
