# Earth Berm Shelter

## Why It Matters

- Ground 6 inches deep stays 50-55F
- Earth is the best natural insulator
- Wind cannot penetrate earth walls
- Lasts indefinitely with maintenance
- Fire inside stays sheltered
- Best for multi-day or base camp

## Site Selection

- Gentle slope for drainage
- Deep soil, not rocky ground
- Southern exposure for sun
- Away from drainage channels
- Check for high water table first
- Natural hillside is easiest start

## Cut and Bank Method

- Cut into hillside 4-5 ft deep
- Bank soil on 3 sides as walls
- Use cut soil to build windward berm
- Walls should reach shoulder height
- Angle berm top away from interior
- Compact walls by stomping

## Roof Construction

- Lay heavy poles across walls
- Add brush and leaf layer next
- Pack 6 inches of soil on top
- Leave vent gap at ridge
- Entrance faces away from wind
- Door plug of leaves or brush

## Drainage Management

- Dig channel around perimeter
- Slope floor toward entrance
- Raise sleeping area 4 inches
- Inspect after heavy rain
- Drain water before it enters
- Gravel floor layer helps

## What Can Go Wrong

- Roof collapses under soil weight
- Rain fills dugout from seepage
- Rodents and insects move in
- Vent blocked causes poor air

## Practice Benchmark

- Goal: Functional dugout shelter
  in 4-6 hours solo
- Sign of mastery: Interior stays
  within 15F of ground temp outside
