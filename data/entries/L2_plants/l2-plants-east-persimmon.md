# Wild Persimmon

## Identification

- Medium tree 30-60 ft; dark blocky bark
- Alternate oval glossy leaves
- Small yellow-orange round fruits 1-2 in
- Calyx (leaf cap) persists on fruit
- Fruit hangs on bare branches into winter

## The Frost Rule

- Before frost: EXTREMELY astringent (tannins)
- After hard frost: sweet, jelly-like
- Or wait until fully soft and wrinkled
- Test: press fruit; fully soft = ready

## Where to Find

- Open woodlands, roadsides, forest edges
- Common in eastern and southeastern US
- Fruit hangs November through February
- One of best winter wild foods available

## How to Harvest

- Ripe: falls freely or shakes off easily
- Collect from ground after frost
- Gather in quantity when available
- Orange-yellow color is fully developed

## Preparation

- Eat raw when fully soft; scoop out seeds
- Seeds are hard; 4-8 per fruit
- Dry sliced for long storage
- Traditional: dry and grind to persimmon flour

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin A: 270% DV per 100g
- Vitamin C, thiamine, iron
- Natural sugars for quick energy
- ~127 cal per 100g ripe fruit

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Distinctive blocky dark bark
- Calyx remaining on fruit = persimmon
- Confirm: orange fruit on bare winter tree
