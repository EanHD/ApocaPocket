# Wild Violets

## Identification

- Heart-shaped leaves with scalloped edges
- Purple, blue, white, or yellow flowers
- 5 irregular petals; distinctive violet shape
- Low growing rosette in forest/lawn
- Grows from rhizome; often in colonies

## Where to Find

- Forest floors, meadow edges, lawns
- Moist to well-drained soils
- Early spring flowers; leaves year-round
- Eastern US and Pacific Northwest common

## How to Harvest

- Pinch young leaves from center rosette
- Pick fresh flowers for immediate use
- Harvest in quantity; very abundant
- Take no more than half any one patch

## Preparation

- Flowers: eat raw; mild, pleasant taste
- Young leaves: raw in salads
- Older leaves: high mucilage; use in soups
- Leaves boiled: thickens soup naturally (mucilage)

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C: leaves 3x higher than oranges
- Vitamin A: high in young leaves
- ~30 cal per 100g fresh leaves
- Anti-inflammatory flavonoids in flowers

## Year-Round Season

- Spring: best leaves and flowers
- Summer: leaves get tougher but still edible
- Fall: may reflower; leaves persist
- Winter (mild areas): leaves still accessible

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Heartshaped leaves + purple flower = safe
- Spurge nearby; check for milky sap on stems
- Violets: no milky sap; clear when broken
