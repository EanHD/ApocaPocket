# Sea Purslane

## Identification

- Thick succulent stems and leaves
- Oval to spatula-shaped fleshy leaves
- Pink-purple star-shaped small flowers
- Grows in dense mats on beaches
- Salty taste; slightly crunchy texture

## Where to Find

- Beaches, tidal flats, salt marshes
- Coastal areas SE US, Gulf Coast, Caribbean
- Year-round in mild climates
- Upper beach and salt marsh edges

## How to Harvest

- Pinch stems and leaves by hand
- Harvest young tender growth
- Rinse with freshwater if available
- Take from clean beach areas only

## Preparation

- Eat raw: salty, crisp, refreshing
- Rinse to reduce salt content
- Boil briefly in freshwater to reduce salt further
- Add to any soup or stew

## Salt Content Benefit

- In heat: electrolyte replacement value
- Provides sodium when fresh water plentiful
- Do not consume when dehydrated or low water
- Salt content is a feature, not just a warning

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C; beta-carotene
- Sodium, potassium balance
- Omega-3 fatty acids (like purslane)
- ~16 cal per 100g

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes in coastal habitat
- Salty succulent in beach zone = sea purslane
- Glasswort (Salicornia): also edible; jointed
- Sea rocket nearby: also edible; different leaf
