# Stinging Nettle

## Identification

- Serrated opposite heart-shaped leaves
- Square stem, covered in fine stinging hairs
- Grows 3-7 ft tall in dense patches
- Painful sting on skin contact when raw
- Dark green, vibrant color in spring

## Where to Find

- Moist disturbed soil, stream banks
- Forest edges and clearings
- Low to mid elevations
- Spring: best harvest March-May (PNW)

## How to Harvest

- Use gloves; pinch top 4-6 leaves only
- Harvest before plant flowers for best taste
- Young tops are most tender and least fibrous
- Bag immediately; sting neutralizes when crushed

## Preparation

- Boil 2-3 min neutralizes all sting
- Use boiling water for tea; high vitamin C
- Saute in fat like spinach
- Add to soups, stews as nutritional boost

## Nutritional Value

- Iron: 1.6mg per 100g cooked
- Protein: 2.7g per 100g cooked
- Vitamins A, C, K in large amounts
- Calcium higher than most wild greens

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Dead nettle (Lamium): no sting, square stem
- Wood nettle (Laportea): similar sting
- Both edible; no deadly lookalikes
- Confirm: stinging hairs = true nettle

## Tea and Medicine

- Pour boiling water over fresh leaves
- Steep 5 min for mineral-rich tea
- Traditional use: iron deficiency anemia
- Anti-inflammatory for joint pain relief
