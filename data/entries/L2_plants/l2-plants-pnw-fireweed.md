# Fireweed

## Identification

- Tall single stem to 5-8 ft at maturity
- Bright magenta/pink 4-petal flowers
- Long narrow seed pods that split open
- Leaves alternate, long and narrow
- Veins on leaves loop and reconnect

## Where to Find

- Burns, clearcuts, disturbed areas
- Roadsides, avalanche chutes
- First colonizer after fire; forms large stands
- Throughout PNW from coast to subalpine

## Seasonal Harvest

- Spring: young shoots (best edible stage)
- Early summer: leaves before flower
- Summer: flowers edible raw
- Summer: pith from inside hollow stalk

## How to Harvest

- Snap young spring shoots like asparagus
- Pinch young leaves from top of plant
- Extract pith: split stalk, scrape inside
- Flowers: pull from stem and eat raw

## Preparation

- Young shoots: boil 3-5 min, eat like asparagus
- Leaves: wilt in hot water, use in soup
- Pith: eat raw, slightly sweet and nutritious
- Flowers: raw in trail mix or infused in water

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamins A and C concentrated in leaves
- ~40 cal per 100g young shoots
- Antioxidants in flowers (beta-carotene)
- Widely used by First Nations peoples

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Unique: magenta 4-petal flowers
- Looping leaf veins are distinctive
- One of safest plants to ID in PNW
