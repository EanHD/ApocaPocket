# Pine Tree Edibles

## Identification Check

- True pine: needles in bundles of 2-5
- Bark smells resinous when scratched
- Cones with scales; no fleshy berry
- Yew NEVER has bundled needles
- Never eat yew; red berries = fatal

## Pine Needles Use

- Strip needles from young green twigs
- Steep in hot water 10 min for tea
- Vitamin C: prevents scurvy in wilderness
- White pine (P. strobus): best-tasting
- DO NOT steep in boiling water (destroys vit C)

## Inner Bark Use

- Scrape outer rough bark from young tree
- Expose white to pale inner bark (cambium)
- Scrape in long strips with stick or shell
- Best in spring when sap flowing

## Preparing Inner Bark

- Eat strips raw: sticky, slightly sweet
- Dry and grind to fibrous flour
- Boil for emergency starchy porridge
- Mix 20-30% with other flour sources

## Pine Seeds Use

- Open mature cones near fire; seeds fall
- Small seeds (most pines): eat directly
- Pinyon pine (P. edulis): large seeds, high fat
- Crack with rocks; inner seed meat edible

## Nutritional Value

- Needles: vitamin C 5x oranges per weight
- Inner bark: starch, some minerals; ~100 cal/100g
- Seeds (pinyon): 673 cal/100g; 14g protein
- Pollen (spring): edible, protein-rich

## Yew Warning  -  CRITICAL

- Yew (Taxus): looks somewhat like pine
- Yew: solitary flat needles NOT bundled
- Yew: RED fleshy berry (aril)
- Yew seeds/bark/needles = FATAL; no antidote
