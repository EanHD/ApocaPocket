# Elderberry PNW

## Identification

- Compound leaf: 5-9 serrated leaflets
- Flat-topped flower clusters (white)
- Blue-black berry clusters in fall
- Hollow pithy stems; woody shrub
- Sambucus nigra/cerulea: PNW species

## Where to Find

- Moist roadsides, stream banks
- Forest edges, disturbed areas
- Low to mid elevations in PNW
- August-September berry harvest

## CRITICAL Species Check

- Blue/black berries: S. cerulea, S. nigra
- Red berries: S. racemosa — DO NOT EAT
- Red elderberry causes severe GI upset
- Blue berries in large flat-topped clusters

## How to Harvest

- Cut entire berry cluster stem
- Harvest only deep blue-black clusters
- Do not eat berries raw off the bush
- Leaves/stems/bark are all toxic

## Preparation

- MUST cook berries before eating
- Boil 10+ min destroys toxic glycosides
- Strain out seeds if making syrup
- Traditional syrup: berry + water + honey

## Nutritional Value

- Cooked berries: vitamin C, B6, iron
- Antioxidant-rich; immune support
- ~73 cal per 100g cooked
- Safe in quantity when properly cooked

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Water hemlock (Cicuta): NOT a shrub
- Poison hemlock: no compound leaves
- Elderberry is shrub/small tree with pith
- Always confirm hollow pithy stem
