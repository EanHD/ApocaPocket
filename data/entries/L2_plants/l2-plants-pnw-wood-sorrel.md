# Wood Sorrel

## Identification

- Three heart-shaped leaflets (shamrock)
- Small yellow or white 5-petal flowers
- Seed pods elongated, cucumber-shaped
- Sour lemon taste when chewed
- Folds leaves downward at night

## Where to Find

- Forest floors, shaded stream banks
- Common in disturbed moist areas
- PNW forests: year-round in mild areas
- Ground-level; often carpets the floor

## How to Harvest

- Pinch leaves and stems from plant
- Take small amounts; plant is delicate
- Harvest leaves, flowers, and pods
- All above-ground parts edible

## Preparation

- Eat raw as trail nibble (small amounts)
- Add to salad for lemon flavor boost
- Simmer briefly as pot herb (reduces oxalates)
- Squeeze juice into water as flavoring

## Oxalic Acid Caution

- Oxalic acid binds calcium, blocks absorption
- Small trail amounts are completely safe
- Do not eat bowls-full as primary food
- Boiling reduces oxalic acid content

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C: significant in small amount
- Antioxidants, organic acids
- Caloric value low; use as flavoring
- Water content high; mild hydration

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Clover (Trifolium): round leaflets, no sour
- Clover is also fully edible; safe confuse
- Sour taste = definitive wood sorrel ID
- No dangerous lookalikes in PNW
