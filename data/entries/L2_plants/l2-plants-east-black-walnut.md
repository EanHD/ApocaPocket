# Black Walnut

## Identification

- Large tree 50-100 ft; ridged dark bark
- Compound leaves: 15-23 leaflets, alternate
- Round green-yellow husk fruit in fall
- Husk has distinctive walnut smell when rubbed
- Hard corrugated nut inside thick husk

## Where to Find

- Floodplains, rich bottom soils
- Woodland edges and roadsides
- Eastern US; common in midwest
- Nuts ripe: September-November

## How to Harvest

- Gather fallen nuts from ground in fall
- Wear gloves: husks stain black permanently
- Step on husk to remove outer hull
- Dry nuts before cracking to improve flavor

## Processing the Nuts

- Cracking: require hard rock or rock vs rock
- Shell is extremely hard; needs real force
- Pick nut meat out with stick or fingers
- Dry nuts last months in cool dry storage

## Preparation

- Eat nut meat raw; rich, oily, intense flavor
- Roast lightly in coals for better taste
- Grind to paste for high-calorie fat source
- Mix nut paste with other foods as binder

## Nutritional Value

- 618 calories per 100g nut meat
- Fat: 59g (mostly polyunsaturated)
- Protein: 24g per 100g
- Omega-3, magnesium, phosphorus, B6

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for walnut
- Butternut (J. cinerea): also edible, lighter
- Compound leaves + round husk = walnuts
- Walnut smell from crushed husk is definitive
