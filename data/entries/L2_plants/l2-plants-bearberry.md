# Bearberry

## Identification

- Low trailing evergreen ground cover
- Small leathery spoon-shaped leaves
- Small pink-white urn-shaped flowers
- Bright red round berries; persistent
- Leaves stay green through winter

## Where to Find

- Boreal forest, open rocky areas
- Dry sandy acidic soil; full sun
- Circumboreal; very widespread
- Year-round plant; berries persist all winter

## How to Harvest

- Pick red berries from trailing stems
- Year-round availability is significant value
- Berries are dry and mealy; high seeds
- Better cooked than raw

## Preparation

- Eat raw: very mealy, grainy, bland
- Best boiled or mixed into other foods
- Dry and grind to mix in porridge
- Primarily medicinal use

## Urinary Tract Use

- Leaves contain arbutin (antibacterial)
- Steep dried leaves for UTI treatment
- Requires alkaline urine to activate
- Drink with alkaline foods for best effect

## Making Leaf Tea

- Dry leaves in sun or low heat
- Steep 1 tsp dry leaves 15 minutes
- Use for urinary tract infection support
- Drink 3x daily during infection

## Nutritional Value

- Berries: minimal calories; mostly filler
- Vitamin C in berries
- Emergency food when nothing else available
- Winter berries important in deep snow terrain
