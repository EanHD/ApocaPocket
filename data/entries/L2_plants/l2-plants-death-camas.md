# Death Camas  -  DEADLY

## Death Camas ID

- Species: Zigadenus venenosus (meadow), Z. paniculatus (foothill), Toxicoscordion fremontii
- Flowers: CREAM to WHITE (occasionally yellowish), star-shaped, 6 tepals with greenish/yellow gland at base; raceme or panicle; blooms May - July
- Leaves: grass-like, basal, V-shaped cross-section, bluish-green waxy coating, 8 - 20 inches long
- Bulb: onion-like, 1 - 2 inches, dark outer coating, NO ONION SMELL
- Habitat: wet meadows, prairies, open slopes  -  SAME habitat as edible camas
- Height: 1 - 2 feet

## Edible Camas ID

- Species: Camassia quamash
- Flowers: DEEP BLUE to BLUE-PURPLE; rare white variants exist  -  avoid white specimens; raceme; blooms April - June
- Leaves: grass-like, basal, similar to death camas but greener
- Bulb: onion-like, 1 - 2 inches, NO ONION SMELL
- Height: 1 - 2 feet; wet meadows and prairies

## Critical Distinction

- **FLOWER COLOR is the ONLY reliable field distinction when in bloom**
- Death camas: white/cream/yellowish flowers
- Edible camas: deep blue-purple flowers
- **IF FLOWERS ARE NOT BLUE, DO NOT HARVEST**
- When NOT flowering: bulbs, leaves, and habitat are nearly IDENTICAL  -  cannot reliably distinguish
- Traditional foragers ONLY harvested camas during/after flowering to confirm identity by color

## Poisoning Symptoms

- Onset: 30 minutes to 3 hours after ingestion
- Early: excessive salivation, nausea, vomiting, burning mouth/throat, abdominal pain
- Cardiac: slow irregular heartbeat, low blood pressure, difficulty breathing, weakness, tremors
- Late: severe bradycardia, heart block, seizures, coma, cardiac arrest
- Duration: symptoms persist 24 - 48 hours even with supportive treatment

## Field Treatment

- NO SPECIFIC ANTIDOTE  -  call emergency services immediately
- If ingestion under 30 min and patient conscious: induce vomiting or activated charcoal 1 g/kg body weight
- Lay patient down, keep calm  -  minimize cardiac stress
- Monitor pulse continuously  -  cardiac arrest is the primary danger; CPR if needed
- Hospital care critical for cardiac monitoring and supportive treatment

## Safe Harvest Protocol

1. Visit site April - June when flowering; confirm blue flowers on specific plants
2. Mark confirmed plants with GPS, stakes, or photos
3. Return in summer or fall to harvest bulbs from marked locations ONLY
4. If any white-flowered plants are nearby  -  skip the entire area
5. When in doubt, discard it  -  no meal is worth your life

## Range & History

- Range: western North America from British Columbia to California, east to Montana and New Mexico; some eastern NA populations
- Most common: foothills and mountain meadows; Z. venenosus is most widespread species
- History: caused human and livestock deaths throughout recorded history; Indigenous peoples harvested camas ONLY during flowering  -  settlers who skipped this step often died
