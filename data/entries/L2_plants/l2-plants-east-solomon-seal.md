# Solomon's Seal

## Identification

- Arching stem 1-3 ft; leaves alternate
- Leaves clasp stem in two rows
- Dangling white-green bell flowers underneath
- Blue-black berries in fall (mildly toxic)
- Rhizome: knobby with circular seal scars

## The Seal Scar Test

- Rhizome has round seal-like marks
- Each scar = one year of growth
- Scars distinguish from false Solomon's seal
- Root is knobby, white inside, starchy

## Where to Find

- Moist deciduous forest understory
- Rich soil on slopes and stream banks
- Eastern US and Pacific Northwest
- Spring harvest of new shoots April-May

## How to Harvest

- Young shoots: snap when 4-6 in tall
- Rhizome: dig in fall or spring
- Take roots only from large colonies
- Shoots harvested same as asparagus

## Preparation

- Shoots: boil 5-10 min, eat like asparagus
- Rhizome: boil or roast 15-20 min
- Rhizome raw has bitter taste; cooking required
- Leach rhizome in water to reduce bitterness

## Nutritional Value

- Rhizome: starchy carbohydrate energy
- ~150 cal per 100g cooked rhizome
- Vitamins and minerals in young shoots
- Traditional food of many eastern tribes

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- False Solomon's seal (Maianthemum): edible shoots
- False variety: berries in terminal cluster (red)
- False variety berries mildly toxic in quantity
- Confirm: flowers hang underneath leaf (true)
