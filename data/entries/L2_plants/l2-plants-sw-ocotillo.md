# Ocotillo

## Identification

- Tall spiny wands 10-20 ft, no true branches
- Spines 1-2 in on all stems
- Small oval leaves appear after rain
- Brilliant red-orange tubular flowers at tip
- Gray-green stems when dry; leafs out quickly

## Where to Find

- Sonoran and Chihuahuan Deserts
- Rocky desert slopes and flats
- Flowers: March-June (after rain)
- Common landmark plant in SW desert

## Water Source Method

- Soak flowers in water 10-15 min
- Produces sweet pink drink
- Traditional Tohono O'odham practice
- Not enough for full hydration alone

## How to Harvest

- Knock flower clusters from tips with stick
- Collect fallen blooms from ground
- Wear eye protection; thorns can snap
- Only harvest blooms; stems not food

## Preparation

- Eat flowers raw: sweet, slightly waxy
- Soak in water for flower drink
- Dry flowers for long-term tea storage
- Traditional: grind dried flowers to paste

## Other Survival Uses

- Stems used for water-tight living fences
- Wands planted = live fence grows with rain
- Thorny branches for camp perimeter defense
- Stems contain some moisture when fresh

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes; unmistakable form
- Distinctive: tall spiny wands + red tips
- No other plant looks like ocotillo
- One of safest desert plants to identify
