# Wild Mulberry

## Identification

- Tree 30-70 ft; rough gray scaly bark
- Leaves: mitten-shaped, lobed OR simple oval
- Variable leaf shapes on same tree
- Aggregate elongated berries; red-black
- Milky sap when leaf petiole broken

## Where to Find

- Woodland edges, stream banks, roadsides
- Often near human habitation (originally cultivated)
- Eastern US; Red mulberry (M. rubra) native
- Berries ripe: June-July

## Drop Cloth Harvest

- Shake branches over tarp or clothing
- Ripe berries fall easily with little force
- Collect from tarp and eat or dry
- Most efficient large-harvest technique

## Identifying Ripe Fruit

- Deep red-black color throughout berry
- Red or white berries: not fully ripe
- Ripe: sweet, comes off easily
- Unripe: sour/astringent, resist pulling

## Preparation

- Eat raw immediately; soft and juicy
- Dry on flat surface; sun or low heat
- Boil into trail jam quickly
- Dry crushed into fruit leather

## Nutritional Value

- ~43 cal per 100g fresh
- Vitamin C, resveratrol, iron
- Anthocyanins: high antioxidant
- Short shelf life fresh; dry immediately

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for true mulberry
- Aggregate elongated berry shape = distinctive
- Pokeweed berries: NOT on trees, different plant
- Confirm: tree + elongated aggregate berry
