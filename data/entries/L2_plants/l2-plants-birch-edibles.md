# Birch Tree Edibles

## Identification

- White papery peeling bark (most species)
- Triangular to oval double-toothed leaves
- Drooping catkins in spring
- Yellow-gold fall color
- Betula papyrifera: paper birch, most common

## Spring Sap Harvest

- Tap in spring when buds just opening
- Drill or bore 1-2 inch hole in trunk
- Insert hollow tube or stick spout
- Collect 1-2 quarts per day per tap

## Using Birch Sap

- Drink fresh: light sweet taste, nutritious
- Boil down to syrup (40:1 ratio)
- Contains xylitol (natural sweetener)
- Minerals, B vitamins, antioxidants

## Inner Bark Use

- Scrape cambium (white inner layer)
- Spring is best; more tender and moist
- Eat raw or dry and grind to powder
- Add to soups for thickening/nutrition

## Catkins and Leaves

- Spring catkins: green, edible raw
- Young leaves: eat raw; mildly resinous
- Dried leaves: tea for urinary tract
- Catkin tea: astringent, pleasant taste

## Nutritional Value

- Sap: sugars, xylitol, minerals
- Inner bark: starch, some vitamins
- Leaves: flavonoids, vitamin C
- Low overall calorie but critical vitamins

## Firewood Note

- Birch bark burns even when wet
- Excellent fire starter material
- Strip small pieces of outer bark only
- Do not ring-strip; leaves tree intact
