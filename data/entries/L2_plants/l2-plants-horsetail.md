# Horsetail

## Identification

- Hollow jointed green stems; bamboo-like
- Whorls of thin green branches at joints
- Rough gritty texture (high silica)
- No flowers; reproduces by spores
- Two forms: sterile green; fertile brown cone

## Where to Find

- Moist disturbed areas, stream banks
- Roadsides and disturbed soil
- Nearly universal; every continent
- Spring: fertile brown stalks emerge first

## Edible Young Shoots

- Spring: edible brown fertile shoot tip
- Pull off brown papery sheath sections
- Inner core of young stalk edible raw
- Best when only 2-4 inches tall

## Horsetail Tea

- Simmer green stems 15-20 minutes in water
- Do not steep; boiling releases silica
- Drink 1 cup per day maximum
- Mild mineral taste; slightly astringent

## Medicinal Properties

- Silica: highest silica content of any plant
- Supports hair, nail, and bone strength
- Diuretic: increases urine output
- Traditional: kidney and urinary support

## Abrasive Uses

- Dried stems used to scour pots and tools
- Historically used as sandpaper substitute
- Silica content makes it genuinely abrasive
- Bundle and rub on metal to clean

## Caution

- Raw plant contains thiaminase (B1 destroyer)
- Cooking or making tea neutralizes this
- Avoid eating raw in large amounts
- Safe as tea and cooked young shoots
