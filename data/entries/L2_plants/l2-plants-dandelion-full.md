# Dandelion Full Use

## Identification

- Toothed basal leaves in rosette
- Hollow milky stem; single yellow flower
- Puffball seed head when mature
- Deeply lobed leaves point toward center
- Milky white sap when stem broken

## Edible Parts by Season

- Spring: tender young leaves (least bitter)
- Spring: flower buds before opening
- Summer: flowers in full bloom
- Fall/Winter: roots (most bitter but starchy)
- Year-round: roots, though quality varies

## Leaves  -  Greens Use

- Young: mild enough to eat raw
- Mature: bitter but nutritious; boil 5 min
- Change boiling water once to reduce bitterness
- Use in soups for nutrition boost

## Flowers  -  Direct Use

- Eat raw petals; pull from green base
- Batter and fry (camp luxury)
- Steep in water for flower tea
- Buds: pickle in vinegar for capers substitute

## Root Coffee Starch

- Roast root dry over fire until dark brown
- Grind roasted root for coffee substitute
- Raw root: starchy, bitter, edible
- Root is most nutritious in fall

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin A: 338% DV per 100g greens
- Vitamin K, C, calcium, iron, potassium
- Leaves: 45 cal per 100g
- Root: inulin fiber, starch, bitter glycosides
