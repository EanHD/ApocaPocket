# Miner's Lettuce

## Identification

- Round disc leaf surrounding flower stem
- Small white-pink 5-petal flowers
- Basal leaves spoon-shaped, fleshy
- Bright green, succulent texture
- Distinctive: stem appears to pierce leaf

## Where to Find

- Moist shaded areas, disturbed soil
- Coast to low mountains in PNW
- February to May peak season
- Often forms large dense patches

## How to Harvest

- Pinch whole stem below disc leaf
- Take no more than 1/3 of any patch
- Harvest before flowers open for best taste
- Young plants most tender and mild

## Preparation

- Eat raw immediately as salad green
- Mild flavor; good bulk in trail meal
- Wilt quickly; eat within hours of harvest
- Chop fine and add to other foraged items

## Nutritional Value

- High vitamin C: ~33mg per 100g
- Vitamins A, B; iron, calcium
- Low calorie but high micronutrient
- Named for Gold Rush miners who ate it

## Seasonal Notes

- Spring ephemeral; disappears by summer
- Best Feb-April in PNW lowlands
- Find on north-facing slopes in heat
- One of earliest spring greens available

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Disc-around-stem is unique feature
- Navelwort (Umbilicus): similar disc, edible
- Confirm: fleshy disc + white flowers
