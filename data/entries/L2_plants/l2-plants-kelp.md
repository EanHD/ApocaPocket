# Kelp and Seaweed

## Edible Species

- Bull kelp (Nereocystis): PNW; large fronds
- Sea lettuce (Ulva): bright green; worldwide
- Dulse (Palmaria): red-purple; N. Atlantic
- Nori (Porphyra): used for sushi; edible
- All wash up on beaches after storms

## Identification

- Kelp: long brown rubbery fronds with bulb
- Sea lettuce: bright translucent green sheets
- Dulse: red-maroon flat fronds
- All lack roots; holdfast attaches to rock

## Where to Find

- Rocky coastlines and beaches
- High tide line: fresh washed-up seaweed
- Best: kelp attached to rocks at low tide
- Storm beaches: large quantities available

## How to Harvest

- Fresh from water or recently stranded
- Avoid dry crumbly old seaweed
- Rinse in ocean water first
- Take bull kelp stipe; sea lettuce sheets

## Preparation

- Eat raw after rinse: salty, mineral taste
- Dry in sun 1-2 days for storage
- Boil in freshwater: reduces salt and softens
- Add dried to any soup for minerals

## Nutritional Value

- Iodine: critical thyroid function
- Iron, calcium, magnesium (concentrated)
- Vitamin B12 in some species (rare for plants)
- ~45 cal per 100g dry weight

## Iodine Warning

- High iodine can disrupt thyroid function
- Limit intake to a few small portions weekly
- More important to avoid iodine deficiency
- Coastal survival: seaweed prevents goiter

## Toxic Algae Warning

- Red tide/blue-green algae blooms: avoid
- Blooms: discolored water, fish die-offs
- Do not harvest during visible algae bloom
- Stick to fresh clear ocean conditions
