# Wild Hazelnut

## Identification

- Shrub 5-15 ft; thicket-forming
- Alternate double-toothed leaves
- Catkins in spring before leaves
- Nut in leafy husk cluster 2-3 together
- Round brown nut 1/2-3/4 inch diameter

## Where to Find

- Woodland edges, forest understory
- Stream banks, thicket areas
- Eastern US and Great Plains woodland margins
- Nuts ripe: August-September

## Timing the Harvest

- Must harvest before squirrels/birds take all
- Check every few days from late August
- Green husks: not quite ready
- Brown husk with loose nut: harvest now

## How to Harvest

- Pull nut clusters from branches
- Husk is leafy; pull back to find nut
- Shell is thin; cracks between rocks easily
- Harvest large amounts while available

## Preparation

- Crack and eat raw immediately
- Roast briefly: improves flavor significantly
- Grind to paste: high-calorie fat source
- Dry uncracked: store months without spoiling

## Nutritional Value

- 628 cal per 100g nut meat
- Fat: 61g; high monounsaturated
- Protein: 15g per 100g
- Folate, vitamin E, manganese high

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Leafy husk clusters in pairs/threes = hazelnut
- Beaked hazelnut: long beak on husk; same nut inside
- Both fully edible; no toxic confusion
