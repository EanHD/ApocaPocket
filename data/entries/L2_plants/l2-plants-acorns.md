## Oak Identification
- Trees 30-100+ ft tall; leaves alternate
- White oak group: rounded lobes, no bristle tips
- Red oak group: pointed lobes with bristle tips
- White oak: light gray scaly bark
- Red oak: dark ridged/furrowed bark
- All oaks produce edible acorns after leaching

## Acorn Varieties
- White oak (Q. alba): large, warty cap, low tannin — BEST
- Bur oak (Q. macrocarpa): very large, fringed cap, sweet
- Northern red oak (Q. rubra): large, flat cap, very bitter
- Pin oak (Q. palustris): small, round, bitter (red group)
- Live oak (Q. virginiana): small, pointed, moderate tannin
- Prefer white oak group; all are edible after leaching

## Harvest Season
- September-November when acorns fall naturally
- Collect fresh-fallen acorns from ground
- Best immediately after falling; before animals or rot
- One mature oak produces 50-500+ lbs per year
- Mark productive trees for annual return

## Selecting Acorns
- Choose: brown (ripe), heavy, shiny
- Reject: green (unripe), holes (weevils), mold, floaters
- Float test: good acorns sink; hollow/rotten float
- White oak acorns preferred; fewer hours to leach

## Nutritional Value
- 387 cal per 100g dried flour
- Carbohydrates: 41g (starch-rich)
- Fat: 24g (mostly unsaturated)
- Protein: 7g; fiber: 9g
- Calcium, potassium, magnesium, B vitamins
- Dietary staple for many indigenous peoples worldwide
