# Wild Plum

## Identification

- Thorny shrub to small tree 5-25 ft
- White 5-petal flowers in early spring
- Round to oval red-yellow-purple fruit
- Alternate simple leaves, serrated
- Often forms thickets along streams

## Where to Find

- Stream banks, woodland edges, draws
- Great Plains; very common in river valleys
- Also found throughout eastern US
- Fruit ripe: July-September by region

## How to Harvest

- Pick ripe fruit by hand; soft and colored
- Shake branches over tarp to collect
- Ripe: comes off easily, sweet smell
- Gather in quantity; prolific in good years

## Preparation

- Eat raw: sweet-tart flesh around pit
- Spit pit; do not crack or eat
- Dry whole: remove pit first, lay in sun
- Boil and strain for fruit juice

## Cyanide Warning

- Prunus pits contain amygdalin (cyanide source)
- Never crack or grind pits
- Wilted or damaged leaves: DO NOT USE
- Fresh or fully dried leaves safe as tea in small amounts

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C, A, potassium
- ~46 cal per 100g flesh
- Sugar: quick energy when ripe
- Dried plums last months; calorie-dense

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Chokecherry (P. virginiana): smaller berries
- Chokecherry flesh edible; pit also toxic
- All Prunus fruits have edible flesh, toxic pits
- Confirm: stone fruit with smooth skin + thorny shrub
