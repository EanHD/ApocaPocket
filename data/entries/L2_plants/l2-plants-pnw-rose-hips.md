# Wild Rose Hips

## Identification

- Thorny canes with arching habit
- Compound leaves: 5-9 leaflets, serrated
- Pink 5-petal flowers in summer
- Red-orange oval hips after frost
- Hips remain on canes through winter

## Where to Find

- Forest edges, roadsides, open slopes
- Throughout PNW from coast to mountains
- Hips ripe: August onward; best after frost
- Old fields and shrubby clearings

## How to Harvest

- Pick red to orange-red hips by hand
- Ripe: slightly soft; deep red color
- Winter hips: wrinkled but still nutritious
- Pinch off stem and flower end

## Preparation

- Eat flesh raw; avoid seeds and hairs
- Slice in half; scoop out seeds
- Boil hips and strain through cloth for tea
- Dry whole hips; grind later for tea

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C: 400-500mg per 100g fresh
- 20x more vitamin C than oranges
- Vitamin A, iron, calcium present
- Critical anti-scurvy winter food source

## Winter Availability

- One of few fruits accessible in winter
- Remain on canes after leaves drop
- Frost improves sweetness; best late fall
- High priority winter foraging target

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for rose hips
- All Rosa species produce edible hips
- Thorns + compound leaves + red hip = rose
- Hawthorn berries similar; also edible
