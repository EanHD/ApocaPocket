# Lamb's Quarters

## Identification

- Mealy white-gray powder on young leaves
- Diamond to triangle shaped leaves
- Small green flower spikes, no petals
- Leaves alternate; lower leaves often lobed
- Metallic sheen on undersides of leaves

## Where to Find

- Disturbed soil, roadsides, gardens
- Agricultural fields and edges
- Universal weed; nearly worldwide
- Spring to fall; best spring-early summer

## How to Harvest

- Pinch young tops (4-6 inches)
- Harvest before flowers develop
- Leaves larger lower on plant but tougher
- Large patches make efficient harvest

## Preparation

- Young leaves: eat raw in salad
- All leaves: boil 3-5 min like spinach
- Seeds: harvest in late summer, grind to flour
- Substitute for spinach in any recipe

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin A: 580% DV per 100g cooked
- Calcium, vitamin C, iron, B vitamins
- Protein: 4.2g per 100g cooked
- Among highest-nutrition wild greens available

## Seeds as Food

- Mature seeds in fall: small and black
- Rub through hands to thresh from stalks
- Grind to dark flour for pancakes/porridge
- Related to quinoa; similar nutritional value

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes when young
- Mealy coating is distinctive
- Nightshade leaves: no white mealy coating
- Confirm: white powder on leaves = lamb's quarters
