# Wild Clover

## Identification

- 3 oval leaflets with pale chevron marking
- Round flower heads: white, red, or pink
- Low growing to 12 in; very common
- Sweet nectar smell from flowers
- Trifolium repens (white) most common

## Where to Find

- Lawns, fields, roadsides, meadows
- Nearly worldwide in temperate climates
- Spring through fall abundance
- Year-round in mild winter areas

## How to Harvest

- Pinch flower heads and leaves by hand
- Harvest flowers when fully open
- Pull leaves from stems; eat whole
- Seeds: rub seed heads into bag

## Preparation

- Flowers: eat raw; sweet
- Flowers: steep in hot water for floral tea
- Leaves: raw in salad or boil briefly
- Seeds: dry roast and grind to flour

## Sprouting Method

- Soak seeds overnight; drain
- Rinse twice daily; sprout in 3-5 days
- Sprouts: much more digestible than raw seeds
- Excellent protein and vitamin source

## Nutritional Value

- Protein in seeds: ~24% by dry weight
- Flowers: trace sugars, vitamins
- Sprouted seeds: vitamins B, C, E
- Greens: folate, vitamins K and C

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Wood sorrel (Oxalis): heart-shaped leaflets
- Wood sorrel is also edible; sour taste
- No dangerous lookalikes for true Trifolium
- Medick (Medicago): small crescent pods; also edible
