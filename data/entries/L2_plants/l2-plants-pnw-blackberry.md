# Wild Blackberry

## Identification

- Thorny canes, trailing or arching
- Leaves: 3-5 leaflets, serrated
- White 5-petal flowers in spring
- Aggregate berry: clustered drupelets
- Ripe berry: deep black, pulls off easily

## Species in PNW

- Himalayan (R. armeniacus): huge patches
- Trailing (R. ursinus): native, smaller
- Cutleaf (R. laciniatus): deeply lobed leaf
- All three fully edible when ripe

## Where to Find

- Roadsides, clearcuts, forest edges
- Himalayan: lowland disturbed areas
- Trailing: open forest understory
- Peak harvest: July-September PNW

## How to Harvest

- Wear long sleeves; thorns are serious
- Ripe = black, pulls off with no force
- Red or purple = not ripe yet; bitter
- Morning harvest when cool and firm

## Preparation

- Eat raw; excellent fresh nutrition
- Dry for trail use (dehydrate at low heat)
- Boil with water for trail jam (no pectin)
- Leaves: dry for astringent tea

## Nutritional Value

- ~43 cal per 100g fresh berries
- High vitamin C, manganese, fiber
- Anthocyanins: anti-inflammatory
- Leaves: tannins useful for diarrhea

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for true blackberry
- Nightshade berries are NOT aggregate
- Pokeweed berries cluster but are smooth
- Confirm: aggregate drupelets = blackberry
