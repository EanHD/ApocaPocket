# Mountain Sorrel

## Identification

- Kidney-shaped to round leaves
- Reddish stem base; fleshy leaves
- Small red-green flower spikes
- Low growing; 4-12 in tall
- Sour lemon taste when chewed

## Where to Find

- Alpine and subalpine rocky areas
- Above 6000 ft elevation in western US
- Snowmelt areas; rocky slopes
- Summer presence after snow melts

## Anti-Scurvy Value

- Vitamin C critical above tree line
- Mountain sorrel: 240mg vitamin C per 100g
- Fresh leaves eaten prevent scurvy
- Key food for arctic and alpine survival

## How to Harvest

- Pinch leaves from plant
- Small amounts at a time; plant is delicate
- Harvest regularly throughout summer
- Flowers and seed pods also edible

## Preparation

- Eat raw: refreshing sour trail nibble
- Steep in water for vitamin C drink
- Add to soup for flavor and nutrition
- Moderate amounts only due to oxalates

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C: 240mg per 100g (very high)
- Vitamin A, iron, calcium
- ~30 cal per 100g
- Priority plant in alpine scurvy prevention

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes in alpine zone
- Sour taste is definitive field test
- Round kidney leaf shape distinctive
- Dock (Rumex): also edible, also sour; taller
