# Saguaro Fruit

## Identification

- Iconic column cactus; up to 40+ ft tall
- Ribbed trunk; arm branches develop after 75yr
- Large white flowers on arm tips in May
- Red fruit 2-3 inch oval at branch tips
- Fruit splits open revealing red pulp/black seeds

## Where to Find

- Sonoran Desert: Arizona, Baja California
- Rocky desert slopes below 4000 ft
- Fruit ripens late June-July
- Traditional Tohono O'odham harvest time

## How to Harvest

- Traditional: long pole (saguaro rib) to knock
- Fruit falls; collect from ground quickly
- Fallen fruit: check for mold before eating
- Harvest early morning before birds get them

## Preparation

- Eat red pulp raw on the spot
- Seeds edible; high fat, grind to paste
- Boil pulp to concentrate into syrup
- Dry pulp into fruit leather for storage

## Nutritional Value

- Fruit: ~47 cal per 100g fresh
- Seeds: ~500 cal per 100g; 30% fat, 14% protein
- Vitamin C in fresh fruit
- Seeds: complete protein with essential amino acids

## Making Syrup

- Boil pulp in water; reduce by half
- Strain through cloth to remove seeds
- Continue reducing to thick syrup
- Very sweet; use as energy supplement

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes; unmistakable shape
- Only cactus this tall in Sonoran Desert
- Protected species: harvest respectfully
- Do not damage cactus to reach fruit
