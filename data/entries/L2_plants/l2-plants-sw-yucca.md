# Yucca Plant

## Identification

- Stiff sword-shaped leaves with sharp tip
- Tall central flower stalk 3-10 ft
- Cream white bell-shaped flowers in clusters
- Banana-shaped green/brown fruit pods
- Rosette growth form at base

## Where to Find

- Desert grasslands, rocky slopes
- Southwest US, Great Plains margins
- Flowers: May-July depending on species
- Year-round plant; fruit harvested July-Sept

## Edible Parts

- Flowers: edible raw; mild, slightly sweet
- Fruit: edible when young and green
- Seeds: edible roasted or ground to flour
- Growing tip/bud: roast before flowers open
- Roots: boiled (contain saponins)

## How to Harvest

- Flowers: pick fresh white blooms by hand
- Fruit: harvest when firm and green
- Bud: cut central stalk before opening
- Root: dig carefully; wash and boil

## Preparation

- Flowers: eat raw or toss in hot coals
- Fruit: boil or roast 15-20 min
- Bud: roast in coals 30 min; eat like cabbage
- Root: boil 30+ min; changes flavor significantly

## Survival Uses

- Leaves: split for strong cordage/rope
- Leaf fibers: weave into basket or mat
- Root lather: soap substitute in water
- Sharp leaf tip + fiber: needle and thread

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Agave (Agave spp): very similar
- Agave also edible with same methods
- Both safe; no dangerous confusion
- Confirm: bell flowers = yucca; spike = agave
