# Spicebush

## Identification

- Woodland shrub 6-12 ft tall
- Small yellow flowers before leaves in spring
- Alternate oval leaves; spicy when crushed
- Bright red oval berries in fall
- Crushed twig: spicy allspice-cinnamon smell

## Where to Find

- Moist rich deciduous forest understory
- Streambanks and floodplain forest
- Common indicator of quality hardwood forest
- Eastern US; most common Appalachian states

## How to Harvest

- Berries: pick red ripe oval berries in fall
- Twigs: snap pencil-size fresh twigs anytime
- Leaves: harvest green before berries appear
- All parts smell spicy when crushed

## Preparation

- Berries: dry then grind as allspice replacement
- Twigs: boil in water for spicy tea
- Leaves: steep fresh in hot water for tea
- Dried berries: preserve in dry container months

## Culinary Uses

- 1:1 substitute for allspice in recipes
- Flavor wild game meat, soups, stews
- Mix with other plant teas for flavor
- One dried berry = significant flavor contribution

## Nutritional Value

- Primarily used as flavoring, not calories
- Antioxidants in berries
- Antimicrobial compounds in bark oils
- Traditional medicinal: fever, body ache

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes in eastern US
- Spicy smell from any crushed part = definitive
- Sassafras: similar habitat; also edible
- Sassafras: mitten-shaped leaves, different form
