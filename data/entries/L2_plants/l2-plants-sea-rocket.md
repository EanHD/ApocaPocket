# Sea Rocket

## Identification

- Fleshy lobed leaves on beach/dune sand
- Small purple-pink 4-petal flowers
- Rocket-shaped seed pods when mature
- Grows directly in beach sand above tideline
- Thick succulent stems; sprawling habit

## Where to Find

- Sandy beaches above high tide line
- Coastal dunes; Atlantic and Pacific coasts
- Year-round in southern areas
- Common on open beaches; easy to find

## How to Harvest

- Snap leaves from stems by hand
- Young leaves milder than mature
- Seed pods edible when young
- Take only what you need; exposed plant

## Preparation

- Eat raw: pungent mustard-horse radish bite
- Very strong; use as flavoring not bulk food
- Boil briefly: reduces pungency
- Mix with bland foods to balance

## Mustard Properties

- Glucosinolates: anti-microbial compounds
- Vitamin C; vitamin K
- Seed pods: grind as mustard substitute
- Traditional coastal emergency food

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C, vitamin K
- Glucosinolates (cancer-protective)
- ~25 cal per 100g
- Strong flavor means small amounts used

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes in beach habitat
- Rocket-shaped seed pods = distinctive
- Purple 4-petal flowers on beach = sea rocket
- Confirm: sandy beach habitat + mustard taste
