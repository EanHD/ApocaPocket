# Crowberry

## Identification

- Low evergreen mat-forming shrub
- Tiny needle-like leaves; crowded on stem
- Small black-purple shiny berries
- Looks like heather with dark berries
- Forms large mats on tundra and bog

## Where to Find

- Tundra, heathland, coastal bog
- Circumboreal and arctic distribution
- Berries ripe: August-September
- Persist under snow; accessible winter

## Winter Foraging

- Berries survive under snow intact
- Scrape snow; find frozen crowberries
- Frozen berries can be eaten as is
- Major calorie source in arctic winter

## How to Harvest

- Strip ripe berries by hand from mat
- Collect into container; easy in large patches
- Ripe: shiny black, slightly soft
- Large mats can yield significant volume

## Preparation

- Eat raw: bland, slightly bitter, seedy
- Mix with other foods to improve palatability
- Dry for winter storage
- Boil into thin sauce with water

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C: 30mg per 100g
- Anthocyanins (antioxidants)
- Low calorie: ~20 cal per 100g
- High water content but vitamin value real

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Needle-like leaves + black berries = crowberry
- Mountain cranberry (Vaccinium): red berries; edible
- Both species often found in same mats
