# Common Plantain

## Identification

- Oval to round basal leaves in rosette
- Prominent parallel veins on leaf underside
- Tall narrow flowering spike in summer
- Plantago major: broad leaf, rounded
- Plantago lanceolata: narrow leaf, ribbed

## Where to Find

- Lawns, roadsides, disturbed soil
- Compacted soil preferred
- Grows nearly worldwide; very common
- Year-round in mild climates

## How to Harvest

- Young leaves: pull from rosette center
- Best before flowering spike appears
- Older leaves: fibrous but still edible
- Harvest whole plant including roots

## Preparation

- Young leaves: eat raw, slightly bitter
- All leaves: boil 5-10 min as pot herb
- Seeds: shake from spike into bag; edible
- Boiled leaves: use in any green recipe

## First Aid Use

- Chew leaf to pulp and apply to wound
- Anti-inflammatory; reduces bee sting pain
- Apply mashed leaf to blister or cut
- Traditional wound dressing worldwide

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamins A, C, K in young leaves
- Calcium and iron present
- Seeds: similar to psyllium; fiber-rich
- ~27 cal per 100g leaves

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Parallel veins are distinctive
- Lily of the valley has parallel veins too
- Lily of valley: forest; NO flower spike
