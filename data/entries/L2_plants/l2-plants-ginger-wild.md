# Wild Ginger

## Identification

- Low-growing ground cover; 6-8 in tall
- Two large heart-shaped hairy leaves per stem
- Small maroon-brown cup flower near ground
- Rhizome just below soil: aromatic ginger smell
- Distinctive: maroon flower hides at base

## The Smell Test

- Scratch rhizome with fingernail
- Strong ginger-like spicy earthy smell
- This confirms wild ginger identity
- Smell is distinctive and unmistakable

## Where to Find

- Rich moist deciduous forest
- Shaded stream banks and north slopes
- Eastern US and Pacific Northwest
- Forms dense ground cover patches

## How to Harvest

- Carefully scrape soil; expose rhizome
- Take small sections; leave most intact
- Rhizomes are thin and horizontal
- Harvest sparingly from large patches only

## Preparation

- Use as ginger substitute in tiny amounts
- Add one small piece to tea or soup
- Strong flavor; very little needed
- Not eaten in quantity; flavoring only

## Medicinal Use

- Carminative: relieves gas and bloating
- Add small piece to tea for digestion
- Traditional uses for nausea and stomach
- Chew tiny piece for stomach settling

## Caution

- Aristolochic acid: known nephrotoxin
- Do NOT use regularly or in large amounts
- Safe as occasional flavoring
- Never use as a medicinal supplement in quantity
