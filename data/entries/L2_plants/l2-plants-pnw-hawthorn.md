# Hawthorn Berries

## Identification

- Thorny tree or shrub; thorns 1-3 in long
- Leaves: lobed, toothed, alternate
- White flower clusters in spring
- Berries: red to red-orange, pea-sized
- Each berry contains 1-5 hard seeds

## Where to Find

- Forest edges, roadsides, open woods
- Low to mid elevations in PNW
- September-November berry harvest
- Berries persist into winter on branches

## How to Harvest

- Wear leather gloves to avoid thorns
- Harvest berry clusters in fall
- Ripe: deep red, slightly soft to touch
- Can still harvest after frost; sweetens

## Preparation

- Eat flesh raw; spit out seeds
- Boil berries, strain through cloth
- Berry paste: boil + press through sieve
- Dry berries for winter storage

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamins C and B-complex in flesh
- Polyphenols, flavonoids, antioxidants
- ~52 cal per 100g flesh
- Mealy texture; high in dietary fiber

## Medicinal Properties

- Traditional heart tonic herb
- Increases blood flow to heart muscle
- Use berries and young leaves for tea
- Steep 1 tbsp dried berries 10 min

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No deadly lookalikes in PNW
- Washington hawthorn also fully edible
- Confirm: thorns + lobed leaves + red berry
- All Crataegus species safe to eat
