# Biscuit Root

## Identification

- Compound leaves: finely divided, carrot-like
- Small yellow or white flower clusters
- Taproot: carrot-shaped to round, starchy
- Grows in open rocky/grassy areas
- Celery-parsley smell when leaves crushed

## Where to Find

- Dry open slopes, sagebrush country
- Columbia Plateau and east Cascades
- Spring flowering: March-May
- Often on south-facing rocky outcrops

## Carrot Family Danger

- Poison hemlock: purple-blotched stem
- Water hemlock: in wet areas, chambered root
- Lomatium: dry rocky areas, no purple spots
- RULE: Avoid carrot-family roots unless expert

## How to Harvest

- Dig taproot in early spring or fall
- Use digging stick; root can be 8-12 in deep
- Wash thoroughly before any preparation
- Harvest from dry open rocky areas only

## Preparation

- Eat raw: starchy, slightly sweet
- Roast in coals: becomes soft, bread-like
- Dry and pound to flour for cakes
- Traditional biscuit: roast + flatten

## Nutritional Value

- High starch carbohydrates: ~300 cal/100g dry
- Major caloric staple for Plateau tribes
- Some protein and minerals
- Energy-dense survival food

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum)
- Water hemlock (Cicuta douglasii)
- Both deadly; both in carrot family
- Without expert ID: do not harvest root
