# PNW Huckleberry

## Identification

- Small deciduous shrub 1-5 ft tall
- Oval leaves with fine teeth, alternate
- Berries: round, blue-black or red, 6-10mm
- Single berry per stem attachment point
- Tart sweet flavor, no toxic look-alikes

## Where to Find

- Subalpine slopes 3000-6000 ft elevation
- Clearcuts and burned forest areas
- Mid to late August peak harvest
- Cascade and Olympic mountain ranges

## How to Harvest

- Pick individual ripe berries by hand
- Ripe = deep color, comes off easily
- Strip comb method for large patches
- Handle gently; they crush easily

## Preparation

- Eat raw immediately; high nutrition
- Dry on flat surface for preservation
- Boil with water for trail jam
- Calorie-dense snack food on the move

## Nutritional Value

- ~57 cal per 100g fresh
- High anthocyanins, vitamin C, manganese
- Quick energy carbohydrates
- Anti-inflammatory compounds

## Storage Methods

- Eat fresh same day for best nutrition
- Dry: spread on flat rock in sun 2 days
- Dried berries last months in dry bag
- Mash and dry as fruit leather sheet

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Baneberry (Actaea rubra): red cluster
- Nightshade (Solanum): clusters, hairy
- Huckleberry: single berry per stem
- When in doubt: single berry = safe check
