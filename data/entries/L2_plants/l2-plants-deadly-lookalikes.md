# Deadly Plant Lookalikes

More people die from eating poisonous lookalikes than from starvation while foraging. When in doubt, throw it out.

Use multiple identification features, not just one. 90% certain = 100% uncertain. Do not eat it.

## Wild Carrot vs Hemlock

Wild carrot (Daucus carota) — SAFE:
- Root: White to orange with carrot smell. Single taproot.
- Stem: Green, hairy, solid.
- Leaves: Finely divided, fern-like, hairy.
- Flowers: White flat-topped cluster. Tiny dark purple flower at center.
- Height: 1-3 feet.

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum) — DEADLY:
- Toxin: Coniine alkaloids. Paralyzes muscles and causes respiratory failure.
- Fatal dose: Small handful of leaves or roots. Death in 3-6 hours. No antidote.
- Root: White, no carrot smell. Musty, mousy odor.
- Stem: PURPLE SPOTS or blotches, hollow, smooth, hairless.
- Leaves: Finely divided, hairless, shiny.
- Flowers: White umbel cluster. No central dark flower.
- Height: 4-10 feet.

Key identifier: Purple-spotted stem = POISON. Never harvest white-rooted plants with purple-spotted stems.

## Onion vs Death Camas

Wild onion or garlic (Allium spp.) — SAFE:
- Bulb: White to pink, layered.
- Leaves: Grass-like.
- Smell: STRONG ONION OR GARLIC ODOR when crushed.
- Flowers: Pink, white, or purple, clustered ball at top.

Death camas (Toxicoscordion spp.) — DEADLY:
- Toxin: Zygacine alkaloids. Heart failure and respiratory arrest.
- Fatal dose: 1-2 bulbs. Death in 2-8 hours.
- Bulb: White to tan, layered, dark outer coat.
- Leaves: Grass-like, solid.
- Smell: NO ONION ODOR.
- Flowers: White to cream, greenish tint, arranged linearly along stem.

Key identifier: No onion smell = not onion = do not eat. Always crush and smell before harvesting bulbs.

## Elderberry vs Hemlock

Elderberry (Sambucus spp.) — SAFE cooked:
- Plant type: Woody shrub, 5-20 feet.
- Stem: Pithy center (soft white pith when cut).
- Leaves: Pinnate, 5-11 serrated leaflets in opposite pairs.
- Berries: Dark purple to black when ripe, clustered.
- Note: Raw berries cause nausea. Cook before eating.

Water hemlock (Cicuta spp.) — DEADLY:
- Toxin: Cicutoxin. Violent seizures and respiratory failure.
- Fatal dose: One mouthful of root. Death in 15 minutes to 3 hours.
- Rated most violently toxic plant in North America (USDA).
- Plant type: Herbaceous, 3-6 feet (not woody).
- Stem: Hollow, chambered root crown, purple streaks.
- Root: Tuberous, white. Yellow liquid when cut — critical identifier.
- Leaves: Pinnate, alternate (not opposite pairs).
- Flowers: White umbel cluster.

Key identifier: Woody shrub with pithy stem = elderberry. Hollow stem with chambered root crown = water hemlock. Never harvest roots from white-flowered plants in wet areas without absolute certainty.

## Mushrooms

Mushroom ID is extremely difficult. Do not forage mushrooms without expert training. Every edible mushroom has poisonous lookalikes.

Death cap (Amanita phalloides) — DEADLY:
- Toxin: Amatoxins. Destroys liver and kidneys.
- Fatal dose: One mushroom (50g). No antidote. Survival rate under 10% without transplant.
- Death timeline: Symptoms start 6-16 hours after eating. False recovery at 24-48 hours. Organ failure and death at 3-7 days.
- Features: Green to yellowish cap. White gills free from stem. Skirt-like ring on stem. Bulbous base with cup-like volva (sac).

Destroying angel (Amanita bisporigera) — DEADLY:
- Same toxin as death cap.
- Features: Pure white cap. White gills. Ring on stem. Volva at base.

Mushroom rules:
- If white gills + ring + volva at base = never eat. These are Amanita features.
- Never eat a mushroom unless 100% certain — not 99%.
- Spore print required for any ID attempt.
- Cook all wild mushrooms (some edibles are toxic raw).

Better rule for beginners: Do not eat wild mushrooms at all. The risk is not worth it.

## Safe Foraging Rules

The certainty rule: 100% certain = safe. 90% certain = uncertain = do not eat.

Use at least 4 identification features:
- Leaf shape and arrangement
- Stem characteristics (color, texture, hollow vs solid)
- Smell when crushed
- Root structure
- Flower or seed details

If poisoning is suspected:
- Stop eating immediately.
- Save a plant sample for identification.
- Drink water to dilute.
- Evacuate to medical care.
- Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222 (US).

Do not induce vomiting without guidance — some toxins cause more damage coming up.

## Foraging Checklist

Before harvesting:
- I can name this plant by common and scientific name.
- I have confirmed 4+ identifying features.
- I have ruled out all poisonous lookalikes.
- The plant matches multiple field guides.
- I know which part is edible and how to prepare it.
- If any doubt exists, I will not eat this plant.
