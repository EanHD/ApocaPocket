# Pawpaw Fruit

## Identification

- Small understory tree 15-30 ft
- Large tropical-looking leaves 6-12 in
- Dark purple maroon flowers in spring
- Green yellow fruit 3-6 in; banana shaped
- Soft when ripe; gives to finger pressure

## Where to Find

- Rich bottomland forest, stream banks
- Forms dense colonies (clonal patches)
- Ohio River valley, Appalachian piedmont
- Fruit ripe: late August-October

## How to Harvest

- Ripe: falls from tree, or gentle shake
- Soft to the touch = ready to eat
- Firm fruit: pick and ripen 2-3 days
- Check ground under tree daily when ripe

## Preparation

- Cut in half; scoop flesh with fingers
- Remove large brown seeds (not edible)
- Eat fresh on the spot; best flavor raw
- Mash pulp for camp pudding

## Nutritional Value

- Calories: ~80 per 100g
- Protein: 1.2g per 100g (high for fruit)
- Vitamins A, C; magnesium, iron, potassium
- Healthy fats; amino acid profile unusual for fruit

## Shelf Life Warning

- Falls from tree when fully ripe
- Ripe fruit lasts 1-2 days max
- Cannot be stored; eat immediately
- Mash and dry as leather for days-longer storage

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes in eastern US
- No other tree has large tropical leaves + purple flowers
- Distinctive smell: sweet tropical when ripe
- Unripe: green and firm; wait for softness
