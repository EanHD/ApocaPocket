# Yarrow

## Identification

- Finely divided feathery gray-green leaves
- Flat-topped white flower clusters
- Strong aromatic smell: spicy-herbal
- Stems and leaves very finely hairy
- Grows 1-3 ft tall in open areas

## Where to Find

- Roadsides, meadows, open woods
- Disturbed soil worldwide; very common
- Spring through fall bloom
- Dry to medium soil; full sun

## Wound First Aid Use

- Crush fresh leaves into poultice
- Apply directly to wound or cut
- Yarrow is styptic: stops bleeding
- Tie in place with cloth or bark strip

## Fever Tea

- Steep handful of fresh or dry leaves
- Boil water; pour over leaves; steep 10 min
- Drink hot to induce sweating
- Traditional fever-breaking method worldwide

## Other Medicinal Uses

- Chew fresh leaves for toothache pain
- Anti-inflammatory for joint swelling
- Antimicrobial compounds in leaves
- Insect repellent: rub leaves on skin

## Edible Notes

- Young leaves edible raw (bitter)
- Tea is primary use; not caloric food
- Flowers edible raw; mild flavor
- Not a significant calorie source

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum)
- Hemlock: purple-blotched hollow stems
- Yarrow: solid stems, no purple blotches
- Key: yarrow smells strongly spicy-herbal
