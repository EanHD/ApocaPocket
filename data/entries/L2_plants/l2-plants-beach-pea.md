# Beach Pea

## Identification

- Trailing vine on beach sand and dunes
- Pea-like compound leaves with tendrils
- Purple-pink pea-shaped flowers
- Flat pea pods 1-2 in long
- Coastal beaches; often near driftwood

## Where to Find

- Sandy beaches and coastal dunes
- Northern Atlantic and Pacific coasts
- Summer flowering; pods develop summer-fall
- Common along upper beach margins

## Safe Harvest Rules

- Young green pods: SAFE in moderation
- Peas inside young pods: SAFE small amounts
- Mature dry seeds: DO NOT EAT as main food
- Never rely as primary caloric source

## The Lathyrism Warning

- Mature dried seeds contain ODAP toxin
- ODAP causes irreversible limb paralysis
- Only triggered by prolonged heavy consumption
- Occasional small amounts are safe

## How to Harvest

- Pick young green pods when 1-2 inches
- Eat pods like snap peas while small
- Do not wait for pods to mature and dry
- Stop eating when running low on other food

## Preparation

- Young pods: eat raw or boil 5 min
- Taste like mild garden peas
- Shell older pods and boil peas briefly
- Supplement to other food; not staple

## Nutritional Value

- Protein in peas; carbohydrate
- Vitamin C in fresh pods
- ~50 cal per 100g fresh pods
- Coastal supplement; not primary survival food

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Wild vetch (Vicia): similar; check habitat
- Most coastal Lathyrus = edible young pods
- Confirm: beach habitat + pea flowers + tendrils
