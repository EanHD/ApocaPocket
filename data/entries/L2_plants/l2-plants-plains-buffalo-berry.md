# Buffalo Berry

## Identification

- Thorny shrub 6-15 ft tall
- Silver-gray scaly leaves; opposite
- Tiny yellow flowers in spring
- Red to orange small berries in fall
- Berries in clusters along branches

## Where to Find

- Streambanks, coulees, hillsides
- Northern Great Plains and Rocky Mountain foothills
- Common North Dakota through Montana
- Berries ripe: August-September

## How to Harvest

- Beat or shake branches into tarp below
- Wear eye protection; thorns at branch tips
- Harvest large amounts at peak ripeness
- Berries sweeten after frost; wait if possible

## Preparation

- Eat raw: very tart, high in seeds
- Best cooked or after frost sweetens
- Boil with water and strain for juice
- Dry for long-term storage

## Traditional Use

- Lakota/Plains tribes: major fruit food
- Mixed with pemmican and dried meat
- Boiled into sauce for dipping dried meat
- Dried cakes of mashed berries stored for winter

## Nutritional Value

- Very high vitamin C (anti-scurvy important)
- Iron and calcium
- Malic acid (tartness) has antimicrobial effect
- ~40 cal per 100g; low calorie density

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Silver-gray leaves distinctive
- Russian olive (Elaeagnus): similar; also edible
- Confirm: silver scaly leaves + red berries
