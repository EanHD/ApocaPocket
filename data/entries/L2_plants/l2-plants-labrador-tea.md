# Labrador Tea

## Identification

- Evergreen shrub 1-3 ft tall
- Narrow leaves; edges curl under (revolute)
- Rust-brown woolly underside on leaves
- White flower clusters in spring
- Strong aromatic smell when leaves crushed

## Identification Key

- Leaf underside: rust-brown woolly fuzz
- This fuzz is distinctive and definitive
- Edges rolled under to underside
- Aromatic smell; slightly medicinal

## Where to Find

- Boreal forest bogs and muskegs
- Wet acidic peaty soils
- Northern US, Canada, Alaska
- Year-round; evergreen plant

## How to Harvest

- Pick 10-15 leaves from several plants
- Use mature dark green leaves
- Do not strip all leaves from one plant
- Year-round harvest possible

## Making Labrador Tea

- Steep 5-8 dry leaves in hot water
- 10 minutes; do NOT boil (increases toxicity)
- Strain; drink warm
- Mild pleasant aromatic flavor

## Survival Value

- Hot drink: critical morale in cold survival
- Vitamin C prevents scurvy
- Warming and stimulating effect
- Traditional northern peoples used daily

## Caution

- Ledol in leaves: toxic in large amounts
- Moderate use (1-2 cups daily) is safe
- Do not boil; keep as steep
- Bog-rosemary (Andromeda): no woolly underside

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Bog-rosemary (Andromeda polifolia): TOXIC
- Bog-rosemary: smooth white underside
- Labrador tea: rust-brown woolly underside
- Check underside of leaf every time
