# Desert Willow

## Identification

- Shrubby tree 10-25 ft; often multi-trunk
- Long narrow willow-like leaves
- Orchid-like pink-purple-white flowers
- Long thin seed pods 4-12 in (not edible)
- Sweet floral smell when in bloom

## Where to Find

- Desert washes and dry streambeds
- Indicates subsurface water presence
- Chihuahuan and Sonoran Deserts
- Blooms: May-September after rain

## Water Indicator Value

- Desert willow roots reach water table
- Presence indicates water within 10-30 ft
- Dig in sandy wash below tree for water
- More reliable water indicator than cactus

## How to Harvest

- Pull fresh flowers directly from branches
- Harvest only fully open blooms
- Flowers available during long bloom season
- Small harvest: take 10-20% of blooms

## Preparation

- Eat flowers raw as sweet trail nibble
- Steep fresh flowers in water for tea
- Mild sweet floral flavor; pleasant
- Dry flowers for later use in tea

## Nutritional Value

- Minimal caloric value in flowers
- Primarily used for flavor and morale
- Traditional use: Chiricahua Apache food
- Flowers contain antifungal compounds

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Oleander (Nerium): highly toxic
- Oleander: thick dark leaves, not narrow
- Oleander flowers: similar colors but milky sap
- Always check: no milky sap = desert willow
