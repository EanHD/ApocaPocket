# Prairie Wild Onion

## Identification

- Grass-like round hollow stems
- Small pink-white flower cluster on top
- Underground bulb: layered like onion
- Strong onion smell from any crushed part
- Allium textile: widespread Plains species

## Smell Test Critical

- Crush leaf, stem, or bulb between fingers
- True wild onion: STRONG onion/garlic smell
- Death camas: NO onion smell
- RULE: if no onion smell, drop it immediately
- No exceptions; this test is mandatory

## Death Camas Danger

- Death camas (Anticlea elegans/Zigadenus)
- Same habitat, same time, same look
- Death camas: cream white flowers, no smell
- Wild onion: pink-white flowers, strong smell
- Any doubt: do not harvest

## Where to Find

- Dry prairie grasslands, rocky soil
- Open slopes and hilltops
- Great Plains; spring and fall harvest
- Also common in SW and Rocky Mountain areas

## How to Harvest

- Dig bulb from soil with stick
- Pull leaves to find stem, trace to bulb
- Wash bulb; peel outer papery skin
- All above-ground parts are edible too

## Preparation

- Eat raw: strong flavoring for any food
- Boil or roast bulb: milder flavor
- Use leaves chopped in any cooked dish
- Dry for storage; flavor concentrates

## Nutritional Value

- Vitamin C, sulfur compounds, flavonoids
- Low calorie but high flavor value
- Important flavoring to make survival food palatable
- Sulfur compounds may have antimicrobial properties
