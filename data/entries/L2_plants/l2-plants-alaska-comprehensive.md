# Alaska Edible Plants

**Alaska foraging reality**: Short season (May-September). Focus on berries (calories), greens (vitamins), roots (carbs).
**Safety**: Cross-reference l2-plants-deadly-lookalikes.md. Some edibles have toxic lookalikes.

---

## Berries

**Blueberry** (Vaccinium spp.)  -  July-September:
- Shrub 6"-3' tall. Small oval leaves. Blue-black berry with powdery bloom and 5-pointed crown.
- Pick when fully blue. Yield: 1-3 lbs/hr. Eat raw, dry, or cook with meat.

**Salmonberry** (Rubus spectabilis)  -  June-August:
- Thorny shrub 3-6'. 3-leaflet compound leaves. Orange to red raspberry-shaped fruit.
- Very fragile  -  eat soon after picking. Young peeled shoots edible in spring.

**Cloudberry** (Rubus chamaemorus)  -  August-September:
- Low herbaceous plant 3-6". Maple-like lobed leaves. Golden-amber when ripe (red = unripe).
- Highest vitamin C of any Alaskan berry (4x oranges). Grows in tundra and bogs.

**Crowberry** (Empetrum nigrum)  -  August onward:
- Mat-forming shrub 2-6". Tiny needle-like leaves. Black shiny berries persist into winter.
- Bland flavor. Natural freeze storage  -  harvestable all winter under snow.

**Lingonberry** (Vaccinium vitis-idaea)  -  August-October:
- Low evergreen shrub 3-6". Bright red tart berries. Sweeter after first frost.
- Contains benzoic acid  -  natural preservative. Lasts months unrefrigerated.

## Greens

**Fireweed** (Chamerion angustifolium)  -  May-August:
- 3-6' tall. Pink-purple flower spikes. Edible parts: young shoots (May-June, peel and eat raw/cooked) and young leaves (before flowering, cook like spinach).
- Rich in vitamins A and C. Boil 5-10 min to reduce bitterness.

**Dandelion** (Taraxacum officinale)  -  May-September:
- Jagged toothed leaves in rosette. Bright yellow flower.
- Young leaves: eat raw (spring) or boil (change water 2x for older leaves).
- Roots: roast and grind as coffee substitute. High vitamin A, calcium, iron.

**Fiddleheads** (Ostrich fern)  -  May-June only:
- Tightly coiled young fronds covered in brown scales.
- **WARNING**: Bracken fern is toxic. Only harvest if positive ID confirmed.
- MUST cook  -  never eat raw (destroys thiamine). Boil 10-15 min, change water once.

## Roots and Tubers

**Wild Potato / Eskimo Potato** (Hedysarum alpinum):
- **ROOTS SAFE. SEEDS DEADLY.** See l2-plants-alaska-wild-potato.md for full details.
- Pink pea-like flowers. Harvest spring or fall. Eat raw (crunchy, sweet) or boil.

**Wild Rhubarb** (Polygonum alaskanum)  -  June-August:
- **STALKS EDIBLE. LEAVES TOXIC** (oxalic acid, causes kidney damage).
- Large 8-12" heart-shaped leaves  -  remove all leaf material before cooking.
- Cook stalks with sweetener (very tart). Do not eat raw.

**Cattail** (Typha latifolia)  -  multiple seasons:
- Tall aquatic plant. Brown cylindrical flower spike.
- Shoots (spring): white inner core, eat raw or cooked.
- Pollen (June): high protein, mix with flour.
- Roots (fall): starchy, ~100 cal/cup. Boil, roast, or dry and grind to flour.

## Toxic Plants

- **Baneberry** (red or white berries): cardiac arrest.
- **Death Camas** (white bulb, no onion smell): paralysis and death.
- **Water Hemlock** (white flowers, chambered roots): seizures and death.
- **Monkshood** (purple hood-shaped flowers): heart failure.
- **Wild Sweet Pea / Hedysarum mackenzii seeds**: paralysis (ODAP toxin).

**Rule**: If you are not 100% certain, do not eat it.

---

## Seasonal Summary

- May-June: Fireweed shoots, fiddleheads, dandelion greens, cattail shoots.
- June-August: Salmonberries, fireweed leaves, wild rhubarb stalks, wild potato (spring).
- August: Blueberries, cloudberries, wild potato (fall harvest).
- August-October: Crowberries, lingonberries.
- Year-round: Cattail roots (fall best), crowberries (persist under snow).

---

## Calorie Reality

Plants alone will not sustain you long-term. Active survival requires 2,000-3,000 calories/day. Realistic foraging provides 200-400 calories/day from plants.

**Calorie priorities**: cattail roots (100 cal/cup) > wild potato (80 cal/cup) > berries (45-70 cal/cup).
**Supplement plants with meat, fish, and animal fat.**
