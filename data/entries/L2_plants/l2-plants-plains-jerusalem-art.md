# Jerusalem Artichoke

## Identification

- Tall sunflower relative; 5-10 ft tall
- Rough hairy stems and leaves
- Small yellow daisy-like flowers in fall
- Tubers at root: knobby, potato-like
- Leaves: alternate, long-stalked, rough

## Where to Find

- Disturbed soil, roadsides, stream banks
- Open sunny areas; often forms large patches
- Great Plains and eastern US
- Tubers best: fall after frost and spring

## How to Harvest

- Dig with stick after flowers die back
- Tubers 2-4 inches; irregular knobby shape
- They spread; find one, find many
- Leave some tubers to regrow next year

## Preparation

- Eat raw: crispy, slightly sweet, nutty
- Boil 10-15 min; becomes soft
- Roast in coals for best flavor
- Slice thin and dry for storage

## Nutritional Value

- Inulin: prebiotic fiber (not digestible starch)
- ~73 cal per 100g raw
- Iron, potassium, thiamine present
- Less calorie-dense than potato due to inulin

## Gas Warning

- Inulin ferments in gut; causes significant gas
- Start with small amounts; body adapts
- Boiling reduces inulin content slightly
- Mix with other foods to moderate effect

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Common sunflower (H. annuus): also edible seeds
- Tall rough sunflower leaves are distinctive
- No dangerous lookalikes for sunchoke
- Confirm: tall sunflower + knobby tubers
