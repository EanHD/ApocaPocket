# PNW Cattail

## Identification

- Brown hot-dog shaped seed head
- Flat strap-like leaves 3-6 ft tall
- Grows in standing or slow water
- Green pollen spike above brown head
- Strong herbal smell when crushed

## Where to Find

- Marshes, pond edges, slow streams
- Lowland areas below 3000 ft
- Year-round availability of some part
- Common throughout PNW wetlands

## Seasonal Harvest Guide

- Spring: green shoots, pollen, new stalks
- Summer: pollen spike, green seed heads
- Fall/Winter: starchy rhizomes in mud
- Pollen: bright yellow, shakes off easily

## How to Harvest

- Pull young shoots from base underwater
- Shake pollen into bag when bright yellow
- Dig rhizomes with stick in shallow water
- Peel outer leaves to reach white core

## Preparation

- Shoots: eat raw or boil like asparagus
- Pollen: use as flour supplement (25%)
- Rhizomes: pound in water, strain starch
- Green heads: boil and eat like corn cob

## Nutritional Value

- Rhizome starch: 266 cal per 100g dry
- Pollen: high protein ~18%, minerals
- Shoots: vitamins A, B, C; potassium
- One of highest-yield wild carb sources

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- Yellow flag iris (Iris pseudacorus)
- Iris leaves fan-shaped, not flat strap
- Iris has NO brown cylindrical head
- Iris rhizome is poisonous if eaten
