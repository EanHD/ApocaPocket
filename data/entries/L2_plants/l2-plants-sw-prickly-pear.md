# Prickly Pear Cactus

## Identification

- Flat paddle-shaped stem segments (pads)
- Large spines plus tiny glochid hair spines
- Yellow to orange flowers on pad edges
- Red-purple egg-shaped fruits (tunas)
- Common ground-level to 6 ft shrub

## Where to Find

- Desert grassland, rocky slopes
- Sagebrush and juniper zones
- Throughout SW US; very common
- Fruit ripe: late August-October

## Glochid Removal

- Glochids are tiny barbed hair-spines
- Embedded in skin cause days of pain
- Burn off with open flame before handling
- Or tongs + thick gloves + scrub with brush

## How to Harvest

- Pads (nopales): tongs + long knife
- Slice off young tender pads spring-summer
- Fruit: knock off with stick into bag
- Never touch with bare hands until cleaned

## Preparation

- Pads: char skin off in fire or peel raw
- Slice pads and boil or roast
- Fruit: peel thick skin, eat raw flesh
- Fruit juice: squeeze into water for drink

## Nutritional Value

- Pads: ~16 cal per 100g; vitamins C, B6
- Fruit: ~41 cal per 100g; vitamin C, magnesium
- Water content 85-90%: hydration value
- Mucilage in pads binds toxins in gut

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes; unique paddle shape
- Cholla cactus: cylindrical, not flat pads
- Both are edible with proper prep
- Confirm: flat pads = prickly pear
