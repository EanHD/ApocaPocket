# Mayapple

## Identification

- Distinctive large umbrella-like leaves
- Two leaves per stem; 6-12 in across
- Single white flower under leaves in May
- Small green fruit under leaf canopy
- Ripe fruit: yellow-green, soft, August

## TOXIC PARTS  -  CRITICAL

- Roots: HIGHLY TOXIC (podophyllotoxin)
- Leaves: TOXIC
- Unripe green fruit: TOXIC
- Seeds inside fruit: TOXIC
- ONLY ripe yellow fruit flesh: edible (small amount)

## Identifying Ripe Fruit

- Color: yellow-green, fully yellowed
- Texture: completely soft; almost mushy
- Size: small apple, 1.5-2 in
- Taste test tiny piece: tropical, sweet
- If at all firm or green: do not eat

## How to Harvest

- Wait until fully yellow and very soft
- September in most of range
- Check under leaves in August-September
- Twist gently; ripe fruit falls off

## Preparation

- Scoop out flesh; avoid seeds
- Eat small amount raw (few fruits max)
- Large amounts even of ripe fruit may cause GI upset
- Not worth the risk in true emergency

## Nutritional Value

- Small caloric contribution; not worth risking
- Vitamin C in ripe fruit
- Risk-reward: very poor for emergency use
- Better to find safer alternatives nearby

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for mayapple itself
- Unique umbrella leaf form
- Risk here is eating wrong part of the plant
- RULE: if not fully soft yellow fruit, do not eat
