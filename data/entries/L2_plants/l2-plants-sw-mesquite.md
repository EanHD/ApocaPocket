# Mesquite Pods

## Identification

- Small thorny tree 10-30 ft; scrubby form
- Bipinnate feathery leaves, bright green
- Long tan-yellow seed pods 4-8 inches
- Pods hang in clusters; sweet smell
- Velvet mesquite and honey mesquite common

## Where to Find

- Desert washes, dry riverbeds
- Bajadas and alluvial fans
- Below 5500 ft elevation in SW
- Pods ripe June-August depending on location

## How to Harvest

- Shake or beat pods from branches
- Gather fallen dry pods from ground
- Pods should rattle when dry and ripe
- Test: bend pod = flexible (good) vs brittle

## Preparation

- Eat pod flesh raw; sweet, slightly fibrous
- Do NOT eat hard seeds directly
- Grind whole dry pods to flour (metate/rock)
- Mesquite flour: 25-50% mix with other flour

## Nutritional Value

- Protein: 13% by weight in pod flour
- Sugar content: 25-30%; immediate energy
- Calcium, magnesium, zinc, iron
- Low glycemic despite sweetness: 25 GI

## Storage

- Dry pods store months in dry container
- Grind to flour; store in sealed container
- Pound pods first on rock to break down

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Confirm: bipinnate leaves + sweet pods
- Other pod trees in SW are mostly edible
- Desert willow pods: NOT same; ornamental
