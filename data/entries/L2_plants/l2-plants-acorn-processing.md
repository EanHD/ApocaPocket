## Shelling
- Crack shell with rock, nutcracker, or knife butt
- Remove inner nut (kernel); discard caps and shells
- Remove brown skin: blanch 1-2 min, rub off
- Discard wormy or hollow kernels
- Chop or grind kernels before leaching

## Cold Water Leaching
- Place ground meal in cloth bag
- Submerge in stream or bucket; change water 2-4x daily
- White oak: 1-3 days; red oak: 5-7 days
- Taste: no bitterness = done
- Preserves nutrients; makes better flour

## Hot Water Leaching
- Boil crushed acorns in large pot 15 min
- Drain dark water; add fresh water; repeat
- White oak: 2-4 boils; red oak: 6-10 boils
- Water runs clear and taste is mild = done
- Faster but gelatinizes starch; better for mush

## Drying and Storage
- Spread leached meal on cloth or screen
- Sun-dry 3-5 days or oven at low heat
- Dry completely before grinding or storing
- Store in airtight container; lasts 6-12 months

## Making Acorn Flour
- Grind dried meal with two stones or mortar
- Sift through cloth; regrind coarse bits
- Goal: fine cornmeal to flour texture
- Mix 25-50% with wheat flour for baking

## Cooking Uses
- Acorn mush: simmer wet leached meal with water
- Flatbread: mix flour with water; bake on hot rock
- Pancakes, breads, muffins (needs binder; no gluten)
- Thicken soups and stews
- Roasted acorns: leach first, then roast
