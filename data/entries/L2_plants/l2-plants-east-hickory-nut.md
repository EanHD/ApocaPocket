# Hickory Nuts

## Identification

- Large tree; shagbark = peeling plate bark
- Compound leaves 5-9 leaflets, alternate
- Round to oval nut in 4-part husk
- Husk splits open at maturity in fall
- Shagbark (C. ovata): best tasting species

## Best Species to Target

- Shagbark (C. ovata): shaggy peeling bark
- Shellbark (C. laciniosa): similar, bigger nut
- Mockernut (C. tomentosa): smaller but good
- Bitternut (C. cordiformis): yellow bud; avoid

## Where to Find

- Mixed hardwood forests, ridgelines
- Well-drained soils east of Rockies
- Nuts ripe: September-November
- Often intermixed with oaks

## How to Harvest

- Gather fallen nuts from ground
- Hull opens naturally; collect after opening
- Shaking tree may loosen ripe nuts
- Carry away in quantity before squirrels take all

## Processing

- Crack with rock on hard surface
- Shell thick; requires firm repeated strikes
- Extract nut meat; sweet and oily
- Dry 1-2 weeks improves flavor

## Nutritional Value

- 657 cal per 100g nut meat
- Fat: 64g; high monounsaturated
- Protein: 13g; good amino acid profile
- Hickory oil: render from crushed nuts + water

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes for hickory
- Taste test eliminates bitter species
- Ohio buckeye nuts are TOXIC; not compound leaf
- Hickory: compound leaves + husked nut = safe
