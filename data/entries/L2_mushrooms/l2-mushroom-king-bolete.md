# King Bolete/Porcini

## Identification

- Brown convex cap; bun-shaped, 3-12 in
- Underside: sponge-like pores (not gills)
- White to yellow pore surface
- Thick bulging white-brown stem
- Reticulate (net-like) pattern on stem

## Bolete Safety Rules

- RULE 1: Pores must be white to yellow
- RULE 2: Flesh must NOT stain blue
- RULE 3: Stem must have net pattern
- RULE 4: No red pores on any surface
- Violate any rule = do not eat

## Blue Staining Test

- Slice flesh; watch for 30-60 seconds
- True porcini: white flesh; stays white
- Blue stain = bitter at minimum; potentially toxic
- Red pores or red stem base = toxic species

## Where to Find

- Under spruce, fir, pine trees
- Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountains
- Late summer through fall after rains
- Often partially buried in duff

## How to Harvest

- Cut at base cleanly with knife
- Trim base; check for maggots inside
- Heavy and dense; worth the effort
- Brush clean; pores hold dirt

## Preparation

- Slice and saute: nutty, rich, meaty
- Dry for long-term storage (excellent dried)
- Add to any soup or stew for depth
- Classic Italian porcini pasta and risotto

## Nutritional Value

- Protein: ~5g per 100g fresh
- Selenium, copper, potassium, phosphorus
- ~34 cal per 100g fresh
- Dried: concentrated nutrition

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Satan's bolete (B. satanas): red pores TOXIC
- Bitter bolete (Tylopilus): bitter taste, similar
- RULE: white/yellow pores + no blue = safe
