# Chicken of Woods

## Identification

- Bright orange-yellow shelf/bracket on trees
- Multiple overlapping fan-shaped caps
- Upper surface: orange to yellow-orange
- Lower surface: tiny pores (not gills)
- Soft moist texture when young; firm when old

## Finding It

- Growing on living or dead trees/logs
- Oak preferred in eastern US
- Conifer in western US; can cause reactions
- Visible from distance due to bright color

## Age and Quality Check

- Young and fresh: bright color, soft, moist
- Old: faded, tough, dry, white-orange
- Harvest ONLY young tender portions
- Edge of shelf is youngest and most tender

## How to Harvest

- Cut outer 2-3 inches from shelf edge
- Leave inner tough portions on tree
- Brush clean; minimal water
- Heavy producer; can yield pounds

## Preparation

- Cut into strips; texture like chicken
- Saute in fat with onion (if available)
- Boil for 10 min before eating
- Dry for preservation; rehydrates to soup

## First-Time Caution

- Try a small amount first time
- Wait 2 hours; check for reactions
- Some people react to certain tree hosts
- Avoid on locust, eucalyptus trees

## Nutritional Value

- Protein: ~5g per 100g fresh
- Good source of selenium and potassium
- ~33 cal per 100g fresh
- One of best protein mushrooms

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes when young
- Sulfur shelf (another name for same)
- Giant polypore: gray-brown, not orange
- Bright orange shelf = chicken of woods
