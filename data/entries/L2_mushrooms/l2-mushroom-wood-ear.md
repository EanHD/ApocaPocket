# Wood Ear Mushroom

## Identification

- Ear-shaped to irregular rubbery bracket
- Brown to dark brown, gelatinous texture
- Slightly translucent in wet conditions
- Smooth or finely hairy upper surface
- Grows in clusters on dead elder, beech

## Where to Find

- Elder, beech, and other dead hardwood
- Year-round in mild conditions
- After rain: plumps up dramatically
- Dry weather: shrinks to dry brown crisp

## Dry vs Fresh

- Dry: small, hard, brittle, brown disc
- Wet: plumps 5-7x original size, gelatinous
- Rehydrate dried wood ear in water 30 min
- Both dry and fresh are edible

## How to Harvest

- Peel or cut from wood
- Wash under water to remove grit
- Harvest fresh specimens; avoid very old
- Dry excess harvest immediately for storage

## Preparation

- Raw: rubbery, mild; acceptable in emergency
- Boil 5 min in water; softens slightly
- Adds texture and nutrition to any soup
- Classic in Asian cooking; very versatile

## Drying for Storage

- String or spread on flat surface
- Dries within 1-2 days in air
- Dried wood ear lasts months in dry bag
- Rehydrate: soak in water 20-30 minutes

## Nutritional Value

- Iron: 185mg per 100g dried (exceptional)
- Protein: ~8g per 100g dried
- Vitamins B2, B3; calcium
- Blood-thinning properties (polysaccharides)

## Lookalikes — DANGER

- No dangerous lookalikes
- Rubbery ear shape on dead elder = wood ear
- Witches butter (Tremella): yellow; also edible
- Confirm: brown ear shape on dead hardwood
