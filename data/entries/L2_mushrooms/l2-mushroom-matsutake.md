# Matsutake

## Identification

- White to tan-brown convex cap 3-8 in
- Firm white gills; partial veil visible
- Thick white stem with shaggy lower portion
- Grows at base or near pine roots
- CRITICAL: strong spicy cinnamon-like odor

## The Odor Test

- Crushed: intense spicy cinnamon-like aroma
- No other forest mushroom smells like this
- Smell is definitive identification feature
- Mild or no smell = different species; stop

## Where to Find

- Pine, fir, and hemlock forest
- Pacific Northwest: Oregon, Washington, BC
- Under trees directly at root zone
- Fall: September-November peak season

## How to Harvest

- Brush away leaf litter carefully
- Twist and lift; minimal soil disturbance
- Take only mature specimens; leave buttons
- Replace leaf litter to protect mycelium

## Preparation

- Clean with dry brush; avoid water
- Slice thin; cook lightly to preserve aroma
- Broil or grill: intense flavor development
- Dry for storage; fragrance concentrates

## Nutritional Value

- Protein: ~3g per 100g fresh
- Ergothioneine antioxidant (unique to mushrooms)
- Iron, copper, selenium
- ~31 cal per 100g

## Lookalikes  -  DANGER

- Tricholoma nauseosum: unpleasant smell
- Tricholoma pardinum: toxic; less spicy
- Amanita species: confirm no ring/volva
- RULE: strong spicy cinnamon smell = matsutake
